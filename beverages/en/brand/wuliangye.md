# Wuliangye

Wuliangye is a [strong-aroma baijiu](ref:strong-aroma) brand under [Sichuan Yibin Wuliangye Group Co., Ltd.](ref:producer), named directly for five grains: sorghum, rice, glutinous rice, wheat and corn. Brand and product are not the same layer: the brand carries the name, series and identity system, while the product carries strength, volume and price.

## Definition and position

Wuliangye is first a brand name and only then a particular bottle. It belongs to the strong-aroma route of [baijiu](ref:baijiu) and comes from Yibin, Sichuan. Calling every strong-aroma spirit from Yibin "Wuliangye" is a common misreading; origin, aroma type and brand are three different dimensions.

This brand covers brand structure and how to check identity, while the [product page](ref:drink) covers basic flavour and process. The two overlap, but their purposes differ: the brand page answers what the name covers and how to verify it, while the product page answers what a bottle roughly tastes like.

## Relationship with the producer

The brand is held within the [group](ref:producer) system, while production and sales sit with the listed platform. The group handles industrial resources and brand strategy, the listed company handles production and disclosure, the brand handles names and series, and the product handles specification and price. Responsibilities differ, and so do the data scopes.

For Wuliangye, begin with the product line, then the producer named on the label, and finish with the market it is sold in; that order keeps the account grounded.

## Brand and product system

The core product usually means the eighth-generation Wuliangye (Puwu), commonly 52% ABV in 500 mL, with lower-strength versions such as 39% ABV also available. Series products cover different price bands under names such as Wuliangchun and Wuliangtequ. Core and series products share the brand endorsement but differ in body design and price position.

Series products are not "inferior goods", and the core product is not the "only genuine one". They serve different occasions and budgets. When buying, read the specific product name, producer and specification on the bottle rather than only the three characters "Wuliangye".

## Origin and raw materials

Yibin lies in southern Sichuan, where the Min and Jinsha rivers meet before joining the Yangtze. The humid valley climate suits open [qu making](ref:qu-making) and long pit fermentation. Within the map of [China](ref:china)'s baijiu regions, Yibin is an important strong-aroma node.

The five grains each have a role: sorghum provides the frame, rice and glutinous rice add sweetness and roundness, wheat brings qu aroma and fermentation power, and corn adds body. The proportions are not a fixed formula and differ by product line. Multi-grain is not automatically better than single-grain; it is a style route, not a quality grade.

## Process and flavour

Strong aroma uses solid-state mud-pit fermentation, continued mash recycling and mixed steaming and boiling. The longer a pit is used continuously, the more stable the microbial ecology in the pit mud and the more mature the pit aroma. New spirit is stored by grade and round, then blended to unify style; blending balances aroma, body and finish rather than simply adding spirits.

The aroma is led by pit, grain and sweet notes, with a mellow sweet entry, an opening mid-palate and a clean finish. At 52% ABV the heat is stronger, while 39% ABV is softer; strength affects entry and aroma release but does not establish a quality ranking.

## Identity and labels

The producer, product name, aroma type, strength, volume and standard on the label are the basics for checking identity. The product standard for strong-aroma baijiu is commonly GB/T 10781.1, but the specific product still follows the bottle. Anti-counterfeit marks, channels and batch information also matter.

Counterfeits and look-alike names exist, and judging risk from packaging similarity alone is unreliable. Official channels, checkable anti-counterfeit measures and clear labels are more reliable than price. Gift boxes and channel-specific editions should also be checked against the label.

## History and narrative

Common public milestones are: the distillery was formally named Yibin Wuliangye Distillery in 1959, the group was restructured in 1998, and the listed arm was listed in the same year. Earlier workshop history mostly belongs to local gazetteers and corporate narrative, and specific years need archival support.

"Old pit" and "old method" carry both physical and process meaning and brand rhetoric. Company history is not product age, and brand age is not time in the bottle. When reading historical narrative, separate checkable milestones from legendary material.

## Buying and drinking

The traditional serve is neat in a small cup at room temperature. At the table it suits savoury, sauce-flavoured and mildly spicy Sichuan dishes such as twice-cooked pork, camphor-smoked duck, braised dishes and braised snacks; too much heat amplifies the alcohol and too much sweetness covers the pit aroma. Banquets and gifting are common occasions, but drinking should be moderate.

In mixed drinks, 52% ABV significantly changes the balance and sugar and acid need rebalancing. Ice is not traditional and lowers aroma release while diluting the body; for a lighter impression, a lower-strength version suits better than a lot of ice.

## Common misconceptions

First, treating origin as the brand. Not every strong-aroma spirit from Yibin is Wuliangye.
Second, treating series products as fake. They are official product lines with different positions.
Third, assuming "five grains" is necessarily better. Multi-grain is a style route, not a grade.
Fourth, treating "old pit" as product age. Pit age and liquid age are different things.
Fifth, judging authenticity from packaging or price. Anti-counterfeit checks, channels and labels are more reliable.
