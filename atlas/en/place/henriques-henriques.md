# Henriques & Henriques

Henriques & Henriques, usually H&H, stands in Câmara de Lobos west of Funchal, at Avenida da Autónoma 10. Many guides place it in Funchal, but the cellar is in the neighbouring town, 15 to 20 minutes by car. There is no museum route like Blandy's: this is a working ageing, bottling and sales cellar, with a short, dense visit and a tasting that starts with the three-year wines.

## A modern cellar in Câmara de Lobos

The cellar is at number 10, with a clear frontage and offices and shop at the entrance. Câmara de Lobos is a fishing town, its bay painted by Churchill, with black lava rocks and boats. You can drive from Funchal or take a bus; parking is limited, so arrive early in season. The coordinates, about 32.64986, -16.97900, come from the winery's published degrees-minutes-seconds; navigate to "Henriques & Henriques".

The present cellar was built in 1992 and connects to the headquarters and shop; winemaking is at Quinta Grande, where grapes are pressed, fermented and fortified before the wine is brought here to age. Pipes, tanks, pallets and shelves are visible, which makes it easier to see how a Madeira house works. The visit covers the ageing area and tasting room, not the vineyards.

## 1850: a company named after a family

In 1850 João Gonçalves Henriques founded the company. His family had lived in Câmara de Lobos for generations and knew local vineyards and merchants. After his death in 1912, his sons João Joaquim and Francisco Eduardo formed a partnership that gave the firm its name. In 1968 the last Henriques died and the company passed to close colleagues and friends.

The history is bound up with island disasters: oidium in 1852 and phylloxera in 1872 hit the vineyards; the family assets were consolidated as a company in 1913, and exports began in 1925. Unlike Blandy's seven generations, the H&H family line ended in 1968. Official material calls it one of the oldest genuinely Madeiran houses, a claim of pride rather than an independent ranking.

## Own vineyards and the role of Tinta Negra

H&H says it is the only Madeira company with its own vineyards, growing Verdelho and Terrantez among other varieties, and running a winemaking centre at Quinta Grande. "Only" is the house's own description; other shippers may rely on long-term growers, so definitions differ. What is clear is that H&H takes part from growing and harvest to fermentation, ageing and bottling.

The main white grapes are Sercial, Verdelho, Bual and Malvasia; Tinta Negra is the blank canvas, sweet if fermentation stops early, dry if it continues, with fortification timing deciding residual sugar and alcohol. The range runs from three-year wines to 20 years, a single-harvest Verdelho 2007 and a 50-year-old Tinta Negra, tracing the route from fast to slow.

## The tasting room and the three-year benchmark

The visit centres on the ageing area: rows of old casks, a warm room, caramel and nuts in the air. The tasting follows, with the three-year range as the benchmark at a symbolic charge. Three-year wines are mostly Tinta Negra, aged by estufagem: amber, caramel, orange peel and roasted nuts, lighter than old Madeira but still high in acidity.

For a first impression, try a ten-year Verdelho or Bual: the first medium dry with citrus and spice, the second medium sweet with caramel and fig. Above that, 15 and 20 year olds gain complexity from slow canteiro oxidation: coffee, tobacco, old wood and sea salt. The Verdelho 2007 and the 50-year Tinta Negra are collector's bottles. Taste dry to sweet, young to old; sweet styles need only 14 to 16 °C.

## Walking in without a booking

The rules are relaxed for Madeira: open Monday to Friday, 09:00–13:00 and 14:30–17:30. Parties of fewer than 10 do not need a booking; only larger groups must arrange one. The cellar closes at weekends, on holidays and for two weeks at Christmas, so check the site before travelling. Telephone +351 291 941 551. Explanations are mainly in Portuguese; ask by email in advance if you need English.

The visit is short, focused on the ageing area and tasting, not a half-day of vineyards and lunch. The tasting charge is symbolic and the wines poured depend on the day; the shop sells the full range. Cruise days and high season are busier, while a weekday morning is quieter. `operating_status: verified` means it is trading, but weekend closures are real.

## A contrast with Blandy's

Put H&H beside Blandy's and two Madeira narratives appear. Blandy's, founded in 1811 in central Funchal, is a seven-generation family story with a museum, cooperage and three tour tiers: ideal for a first visit. H&H, founded in 1850 in the fishing town, lost its family line in 1968 and feels like a workplace, with a three-year tasting and lower prices: better once you know the basics. Both share high acidity, oxidation and heat ageing; they differ in setting and pace.

In practice, visit [Blandy's](ref:blandy) in the morning, then drive to Câmara de Lobos in the afternoon and stop at Cabo Girão; or reverse the order. For background, read [Madeira](ref:madeira), [Madeira wine](ref:madeirawine) and [fortified wine](ref:fortified), then [residual sugar](ref:sugar) and [fermentation](ref:fermentation) to see how one grape can yield a row of different sweetness levels.
