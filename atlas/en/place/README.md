# Places

- [A Ginjinha](a-ginjinha.md) — The standing-room ginjinha counter at Largo de São Domingos 8 in Lisbon, tracing its brand to 1840; a small glass of sour-cherry liqueur with a cherry steeped in it is the city’s shortest drinking ritual.
- [À la Mort Subite](a-la-mort-subite.md) — A historic café at 7 Rue Montagne-aux-Herbes Potagères in Brussels, moved to this address in 1928; its name comes from the 421/pitjesbak games of National Bank regulars, and the wood-panelled interior and neo-Louis XVI facade survive.
- [Al Bicerin](al-bicerin-torino.md) — A café on Piazza della Consolata in Turin, opened in 1763 and moved into Carlo Promis's building in 1856; known for the layered bicerin and its pur e fiur, pur e barba and ‘n poc ‘d tut variations, and closed on Wednesdays.
- [Allegrini](allegrini.md) — A Valpolicella family winery in Fumane whose history reaches back to the sixteenth century; Corvina, dried grapes and Amarone are the core, and visits range from a 45-minute tasting to a 3-hour vineyard experience, all by appointment.
- [American Bar at The Savoy](american-bar-savoy.md) — The classic American Bar inside The Savoy in London, opened in 1893 and known for The Savoy Cocktail Book, Ada Coleman's Hanky Panky and its Art Deco room.
- [Antico Caffè Greco](antico-caffe-greco.md) — A historic café at 86 Via dei Condotti in Rome, opened in 1760 and once frequented by Goethe, Byron and Gogol; it was evicted on 8 October 2025 after a long lease dispute.
- [Antinori nel Chianti Classico](antinori-nel-chianti-classico.md) — The Antinori family's winery at Bargino, south of Florence, inaugurated in October 2012 and designed by Archea Associati; the building is sunk into the hillside and combines gravity-flow winemaking, a spiral staircase, cellar tours and the rooftop restaurant Rinuccio 1180.
- [Appleton Estate](appleton-estate.md) — A sugar estate and distillery in the Nassau Valley, St Elizabeth, Jamaica, established in 1670 with the earliest recorded legal distillation in 1749; Joy Spence joined in 1981, became the industry’s first female Master Blender in 1997, and the visitor centre opened the same year.
- [Ardbeg Distillery](ardbeg-distillery.md) — A single malt Scotch whisky distillery on the south coast of Islay, licensed by John MacDougall in 1815 and known for its heavily peated style. The visitor centre offers tours, tastings and the Old Kiln Cafe; booking ahead is advised.
- [Augustiner-Bräu](augustiner-braeu.md) — Munich's oldest surviving brewery, tracing its origins to the Augustinian monastery of 1328 and now run by the family firm Augustiner-Bräu Wagner KG; its Landsberger Straße site preserves a nineteenth-century brewing complex.
- [Avenue de Champagne, Épernay](avenue-de-champagne.md) — The 1.5 km champagne avenue in Épernay, lined with Moët & Chandon, Mercier, Perrier-Jouët and other houses. Around 110 km of cellars and some 200 million bottles lie beneath it; it forms part of the 2015 UNESCO listing.
- [Bar High Five](bar-high-five.md) — A classic cocktail bar in a Ginza basement in Tokyo, founded in 2008 by Hidetsugu Ueno and known for its no-menu custom drinks, hand-carved clear ice and Japanese bar etiquette.
- [Bar Lupin](bar-lupin.md) — A classic basement bar in Ginza 5-chome, Tokyo, opened in 1928; its Japanese-ash L-shaped counter dates from 1935, and the seats of Osamu Dazai, Ango Sakaguchi and other writers remain central, along with the Golden Fizz.
- [Bell in Hand Tavern](bell-in-hand-tavern.md) — A historic tavern on Union Street in Boston, founded in 1795 by town crier Jimmy Wilson; the present building is a mid-19th-century brick structure, and the house's claim to be the oldest continuously operating tavern in the United States is qualified in the text.
- [Bemelmans Bar](bemelmans-bar.md) — A classic hotel bar on the ground floor of The Carlyle at 35 East 76th Street, New York. Opened in 1947, it is surrounded by Ludwig Bemelmans' hand-painted Central Park murals and is known for nightly piano, a late jazz trio, classic cocktails and a real dress code.
- [Beringer Vineyards](beringer-vineyards.md) — A historic winery in St. Helena, Napa Valley, founded in 1876 by the Beringer brothers and known for its hand-dug caves, the 1884 Rhine House and Private Reserve Cabernet Sauvignon.
- [Biondi-Santi Tenuta Greppo](biondi-santi.md) — A historic estate at Greppo, northeast of Montalcino, which produced the first wine officially labelled Brunello di Montalcino in 1888; known for 100% Sangiovese Grosso, the BBS11 clone, old vines and large Slavonian oak, EPI took over in 2017, and the Riserva has been made only about 38 times.
- [Bisol](bisola-valdobbiadene.md) — Bisol1542 in Valdobbiadene, a family that has farmed Glera on the Prosecco heartland slopes since 1542; its cellar is cut into the rock, and visits and tastings run Monday to Saturday, with Cartizze and Rive as the keys to the steep-slope terroir.
- [Blandy's Wine Lodge](blandys-wine-lodge.md) — A Madeira wine ageing lodge and visitor site in central Funchal, founded by John Blandy in 1811 and run by the Madeira Wine Company; known for canteiro attic ageing, its cooperage and the four Madeira styles, with tours every day.
- [Bodegas López de Heredia](bodegas-lopez-de-heredia.md) — A historic winery in Haro's Barrio de la Estación, Rioja, founded in 1877 by Rafael López de Heredia. It is known for Viña Tondonia, very long barrel ageing, sandstone cellars and its own cooperage.
- [Bodegas Lustau](bodegas-lustau.md) — A Sherry house at Calle Arcos 53 in Jerez de la Frontera, tracing its origins to an 1896 almacenista family business; known for six 19th-century cathedral-like cellars, the Almacenista range and the VORS Sacristy, with visits by reservation.
- [Champagne Bollinger](bollinger.md) — A family-owned Champagne house in Aÿ founded in 1829 by Athanase de Villermont, Joseph Bollinger and Paul Renaudin; about 180 hectares, 85% Grand and Premier Cru, over 60% Pinot Noir, known for old oak, a million reserve magnums and Special Cuvée, La Grande Année and R.D.
- [Botín](botin.md) — A family restaurant at Calle de Cuchilleros 17 in Madrid, opened in 1725 and often cited by Guinness World Records as the world's oldest restaurant; known for wood-fired roast suckling pig and lamb, and for its Hemingway connection.
- [Brasserie Lipp](brasserie-lipp.md) — The century-old brasserie at 151 Boulevard Saint-Germain, opened in 1880 by the Alsatian Léonard Lipp; after Marcelin Cazes took over in 1920 it became a literary and political meeting place, was listed as a historic monument in 1989, and still serves choucroute and profiteroles.
- [Bruichladdich Distillery](bruichladdich-distillery.md) — A Victorian distillery on the shore of Loch Indaal, Islay, built in 1881 by the Harvey brothers; restarted in 2001 by Mark Reynier, Simon Coughlin and Jim McEwan, acquired by Rémy Cointreau in 2012 and B Corp certified in 2020. It makes unpeated Bruichladdich, heavily peated Port Charlotte, super-peated Octomore and The Botanist gin.
- [Buena Vista Café](buena-vista-cafe.md) — A 1916 bar at the Hyde and Beach corner in San Francisco, at the Powell-Hyde cable-car terminus; Stanton Delaplane and Jack Koeppler popularized the American Irish coffee here in 1952, using 48-hour matured cream and a six-ounce glass.
- [Buffalo Trace Distillery](buffalo-trace-distillery.md) — A working bourbon distillery in Frankfort, Kentucky, on a site occupied since the 1775 Leestown settlement and expanded by E.H. Taylor Jr. into the O.F.C. distillery in 1870; renamed Buffalo Trace in 1999, now a National Historic Landmark with free tours, tastings and parking that must be reserved online.
- [Bushmills Distillery](bushmills-distillery.md) — An Irish whiskey distillery in the village of Bushmills, County Antrim, Northern Ireland, which dates its licence to 1608 and calls itself the world's oldest licensed whiskey distillery; it still triple distils in copper pot stills and runs booked tours, a gift shop and the 1608 Bar.
- [Café A Brasileira](cafe-a-brasileira.md) — The Art Nouveau café at Rua Garrett 120–122 in Chiado, Lisbon, opened in 1905 by Adriano Telles; Fernando Pessoa was a regular, and a bronze statue of him sits outside.
- [Café Central](cafe-central.md) — A historic literary café in Vienna, opened in 1876 and known for its coffee, cakes and intellectual clientele.
- [Café De Dokter](cafe-de-dokter.md) — The tiny bar at Rozenboomsteeg 4 in Amsterdam, founded in 1798 by a surgeon; about 18 square metres, run by the same family’s seventh generation, cash only and no reservations.
- [Café de Flore](cafe-de-flore.md) — A literary café in Saint-Germain-des-Prés, Paris, opened in the late 19th century and known for its intellectual clientele.
- [Café de la Paix](cafe-de-la-paix.md) — The café facing the Opéra Garnier, opened in 1862 in the same year as the Grand Hôtel; its Napoleon III decoration, historic-monument status, Maupassant, Hugo and Zola guests and the 1948 This Is Paris recording make it a literary and musical anchor of the opera quarter.
- [Café Hawelka](cafe-hawelka.md) — A family-run café at Dorotheergasse 6 in Vienna, opened by Leopold and Josefine Hawelka in 1939; it became a postwar meeting place for writers and artists and is known for Buchteln baked in the evening.
- [Café Hoppe](cafe-hoppe.md) — The historic tavern at Spui 18–20 in Amsterdam, officially selling drink there since 1670; “standing Hoppe” and “sitting Hoppe” face each other across the doorway, and no music is played.
- [Café Majestic](cafe-majestic.md) — The Art Nouveau café at Rua de Santa Catarina 112 in Porto, opened on 2 December 1922 and designed by José Pinto de Oliveira; marble facade, Flemish mirrors and conservatory, listed as a public-interest building in 1983 and reopened after restoration in 1994.
- [Le Procope](cafe-procope.md) — A café-restaurant in Saint-Germain-des-Prés, Paris, opened in 1686 by the Sicilian Francesco Procopio dei Coltelli and often called the oldest café in Paris; Voltaire, Rousseau and Diderot were regulars, and it still serves coffee and traditional French food.
- [Café Pushkin](cafe-pushkin.md) — A Russian restaurant at 26A Tverskoy Boulevard in Moscow, opened in 1999 by Andrei Dellos in a Baroque mansion; the name comes from the fictional “Café Pouchkine” in Gilbert Bécaud’s 1964 song Nathalie, and it is not a century-old institution.
- [Café Sacher](cafe-sacher.md) — The café on the ground floor of Hotel Sacher at Philharmoniker Straße 4 in Vienna, where the Sachertorte was created in 1832; the Original Sacher-Torte and the red-velvet room are the core, and booking is advised.
- [Café Slavia](cafe-slavia.md) — A historic café at Národní 1012/1 in Prague, opposite the National Theatre, opened in 1884 and remodelled in Art Deco style in the 1930s; it was associated with Rilke, Kafka, Havel and dissident circles.
- [Café Sperl](cafe-sperl.md) — A traditional coffee house in Vienna's Mariahilf district, opened in 1880 and known for its preserved 19th-century interior, marble tables, Thonet chairs, billiard tables and Sperltorte.
- [Caffè Florian](caffe-florian.md) — A historic café on Piazza San Marco in Venice, opened in 1720 and one of Europe’s longest-running cafés.
- [Caffè Gilli](caffe-gilli.md) — The café at the corner of Piazza della Repubblica in Florence, opened by the Gilli family in 1733, moved near the new square in 1893 and settled on the ground floor of Palazzo Levi in 1917; dark woodwork, mirrors and Ezio Giovannozzi’s female portrait medallions, renovated and reopened in 2025.
- [Caffè San Carlo](caffe-san-carlo-torino.md) — A café on Piazza San Carlo in Turin, opened in 1822 and closed in 1837 for “subversive activity”; known for Giuseppe Leoni's facade, the Morgari/Borra neoclassical interior, the 1851 Chinese Room and its Risorgimento clientele, reopened by the Costardi brothers in 2022.
- [Camparino in Galleria](camparino-in-galleria.md) — A 1915 bar in the northern arcade of Milan's Galleria Vittorio Emanuele II, opened by Davide Campari on the family's 1867 Caffè Campari; known for Campari and soda, Liberty decoration, the cellar-fed soda system and its 2019 restoration.
- [Cantillon Brewery](cantillon-brewery.md) — A spontaneous-fermentation lambic brewery in Anderlecht, Brussels, founded in 1900 and home to the Brussels Gueuze Museum; weekdays allow self-guided visits, while Saturdays are tour-only and must be booked online.
- [Carlsberg: Beer, a Laboratory and a Garden](carlsberg-brewery.md) — The old Carlsberg brewery in Valby, Copenhagen, was founded by J.C. Jacobsen in 1847; today Home of Carlsberg (formerly Visit Carlsberg) uses an exhibition, old cellars, stables and tastings to tell the family story, scientific discoveries and lager brewing.
- [The Carousel Bar](carousel-bar-hotel-monteleone.md) — A 1949 revolving bar inside Hotel Monteleone in New Orleans, with 25 seats, a rotation of about 15 minutes, and Vieux Carré, Sazerac and live piano as its signatures.
- [Casa Herradura: The Horseshoe Hacienda of Amatitán](casa-herradura.md) — A tequila distillery at the Ex-Hacienda San José del Refugio in Amatitán, Jalisco, with roots in 1870; known for clay-dome ovens, open-air wild-yeast fermentation and its own American white oak barrels, with visits by reservation for adults only.
- [Casa Labra](casa-labra.md) — The century-old tavern at Calle de Tetuán 12 beside Madrid’s Puerta del Sol, opened in 1860 and the secret founding place of the PSOE on 2 May 1879; the Molina family took over in 1947, and fried cod strips and cod croquettes are served at a zinc counter.
- [Castello di Brolio](castello-di-brolio.md) — The Ricasoli family castle at Gaiole in Chianti, documented as family property as early as 1141; Baron Bettino Ricasoli set down the Chianti formula in 1872, and the estate is now known for castle visits, gardens, the Ricasoli Collection and the flagship Castello di Brolio wine.
- [Catena Zapata](catena-zapata.md) — A family winery in Luján de Cuyo, Mendoza, founded in 1902 and known for the revival of Malbec and high-altitude terroirs in the Andean foothills; the pyramid building and Adrianna vineyard anchor the visit, which requires booking, and the official coordinates are 33°10′4.264″ S, 68°54′14.522″ W.
- [Changyu Wine Culture Museum](changyu-wine-culture-museum.md) — China's first specialist wine museum, at 56 Dama Road, Zhifu District, Yantai, Shandong. It opened in 1992 on the old Changyu plant, is a national first-class museum and a 4A tourist attraction, and was expanded in 2002 and 2007.
- [Château Cheval Blanc](chateau-cheval-blanc.md) — Cheval Blanc in Saint-Émilion, where Jean-Jacques Ducasse bought the core plots in 1832 and assembled about 39 hectares from Figeac within twenty years; today it has 42 hectares in 56 parcels, 55% Cabernet Franc, was first ranked Premier Grand Cru Classé A in 1954, withdrew from the 2022 classification in 2021, and opened a Portzamparc-designed cellar in 2011.
- [Château de Laubade](chateau-de-laubade.md) — A single-estate Bas-Armagnac property in Sorbets, Gers, built in 1870 with 80 hectares in one block, known for tawny sand soils, a continuous still built in 1974, some 3,000 casks and estate-bottled vintage Armagnac.
- [Château de Meursault](chateau-de-meursault.md) — A wine estate in Meursault, Burgundy, with a history reaching back to the 11th century. The present Italianate château dates from the 18th century and was renovated in 2024; the estate farms about 67 hectares and its cellars span the 12th to the 19th century. Visits and tastings are available by reservation.
- [Château d’Yquem](chateau-dyquem.md) — A sweet-wine estate on the hill above Sauternes, the only Premier Cru Supérieur in the 1855 classification; its roughly 100 hectares are mostly Sémillon, concentrated by botrytis, and picking usually takes five to ten manual passes; the Sauvage family established it in 1593, LVMH bought it in 1999, and Pierre Lurton took over management in 2004.
- [Château Haut-Brion](chateau-haut-brion.md) — A Graves First Growth in Pessac-Léognan, the only First Growth in the 1855 classification outside the Médoc; its vineyard history reaches back to the first century, the name Haut-Brion first appears in 1521, the Pontac family bought it in 1533, and it has belonged to the Dillon family since 1935.
- [Château Lafite Rothschild](chateau-lafite-rothschild.md) — First-growth Pauillac estate with 112 hectares of vines, classified in 1855 and acquired by Baron James de Rothschild in 1868. The official visit page says visits are suspended until January 2028 for renovation.
- [Château Latour](chateau-latour.md) — A First Growth of the Médoc in Pauillac, documented back to the Saint-Maubert tower of 1331; the vineyard covers 96.5 hectares, including a 47-hectare Enclos about 300 metres from the Gironde estuary, and since 1993 it has belonged to François Pinault’s Artémis, which stopped selling through en primeur from 2012.
- [Château Margaux](chateau-margaux.md) — A First Growth of the Médoc in the village of Margaux, Bordeaux, and one of the four First Growths in the 1855 classification; the estate covers about 265 hectares, and its 1810–1815 neo-Palladian main house stands alongside a new cellar designed by Norman Foster and opened in 2015, with the Mentzelopoulos family in charge since 1977.
- [Château Miraval](chateau-miraval.md) — A Provençal rosé name in the Correns valley, with vineyards laid out on dry-stone terraces and certified organic farming; the estate itself is closed to the public, and the wines are sold through Famille Perrin's tasting room and selected retailers.
- [Chateau Montelena](chateau-montelena.md) — An 1882 estate in Calistoga, Napa Valley, whose 1973 Chardonnay placed first among whites at the 1976 Judgment of Paris; known for its stone chateau, Chinese garden and Jade Lake, and age-worthy Estate Cabernet Sauvignon, it has been owned by Michel Reybier since 2008.
- [Château Mouton Rothschild](chateau-mouton-rothschild.md) — A first growth in Pauillac, bought by Baron Nathaniel de Rothschild in 1853 and renamed, classified a second growth in 1855 and promoted to first growth in 1973. Since 1945 every vintage has carried an artist-designed label, and the Museum of Wine in Art opened in 1962.
- [Château Pétrus](chateau-petrus.md) — Pétrus in Pomerol, its 11.4 hectares on the commune’s highest blue-clay plateau, 100% Merlot since 2010, with a single grand vin, no second wine and about 30,000 bottles a year; there is no château-style main house and no 1855 classification, the Moueix family bought a majority in 1969, and it does not open to the public.
- [China Liquor Culture City](china-liquor-culture-city.md) — A liquor-culture museum in Maotai Town, Renhuai, Guizhou, built by the Moutai Group between 1993 and 1997; it covers about 30,000 square metres, contains seven galleries including the Hall of Wine Origins, the Hall of Brewing Technology, the Hall of Customs and the Moutai Hall, and is a national 4A scenic area and, since 2017, the Guizhou Moutai Wine Museum.
- [China Yellow Wine Museum](china-yellow-wine-museum.md) — A museum devoted to Chinese yellow wine at 557 Xia Da Road in Shaoxing's Yuecheng District, beside Guangxiang Bridge. Opened in 2007 with investment from Guyuelongshan, it covers about 30,000 square metres of site and 16,000 square metres of building, with displays on wine history, custom, technique, Huadiao decoration and an underground cellar.
- [Çiçek Pasajı](cicek-pasaji.md) — An arcade on İstiklal Avenue in Beyoğlu, Istanbul, opened in 1876 as the Cité de Péra; after a partial collapse in 1978 it reopened in 1988, and today it holds meyhanes, restaurants and beer houses rather than a single bar.
- [La Cité du Vin](cite-du-vin.md) — Wine-themed museum on the Bacalan quays in Bordeaux, opened in 2016 and designed by XTU architects. Its 3,000 m² permanent exhibition uses six themes and 18 modules to tell the global story of wine.
- [Cloudy Bay](cloudy-bay.md) — A Marlborough Sauvignon Blanc estate on Jacksons Road in Blenheim, first vintage 1985; its cellar door opens seven days 10:00–17:00, with the Saku restaurant and booked tastings, and it remains a clear entry point to Wairau Valley style and Marlborough's export story.
- [Viña Concha y Toro](concha-y-toro.md) — A historic Maipo Valley winery in Pirque founded in 1883 by Melchor Concha y Toro; core labels include Casillero del Diablo, Marques de Casa Concha and Don Melchor, and the Centro del Vino opened for its 140th anniversary with a 22-hectare park, Bodega 1883 and the Casillero del Diablo experience.
- [La Coulée de Serrant](coulee-de-serrant.md) — A 7-hectare monopole AOC in Savennières, France, planted by Cistercian monks in 1130 and now run by the Joly family, biodynamic since 1984; it produces Coulée de Serrant, Clos de la Bergerie and Les Vieux Clos from Chenin Blanc.
- [CVNE](cvne.md) — A historic winery in Haro's Barrio de la Estación, founded in 1879 by the Real de Asúa brothers as Compañía Vinícola del Norte de España; known for the Cune, Viña Real and Imperial brands, American-oak ageing and a still family-run old winery, with visits by appointment.
- [Dalwhinnie Distillery](dalwhinnie-distillery.md) — A single malt distillery in the village of Dalwhinnie in the Scottish Highlands, founded as Strathspey in 1897 and sitting 1,164 ft (355 m) above sea level; officially described as one of Scotland's highest and coldest distilleries, known for heather honey, gentle smoke and worm tub condensers.

- [Darroze](darroze.md) — A family Armagnac merchant in Roquefort, Landes, formally established by Francis Darroze in 1974; rather than farming on a large scale it selects wines across Bas-Armagnac, and is known for the single-estate, single-vintage, unblended Collection Unique, matured in cellars at Roquefort and Labastide-d’Armagnac.
- [De Halve Maan Brewery](de-halve-maan.md) — A sixth-generation family brewery in central Bruges, taken over by the Maes family in 1856; its tour climbs to a rooftop view, and the story links Brugse Zot and Straffe Hendrik to a 3.3 km underground beer pipeline.
- [Delmonico's](delmonicos.md) — A legendary restaurant at 56 Beaver Street in Lower Manhattan, opened as an early American fine-dining house in 1837, rebuilt on its present site in 1890–91, and known for Delmonico Steak and several classic dishes.
- [Demel](demel.md) — The imperial confectioner and café at Kohlmarkt 14 in Vienna, founded in 1786; Rococo interiors, a celebrated window and the “K.u.k.” tradition, with no reservations accepted.
- [Den Gyldene Freden](den-gyldene-freden.md) — A historic restaurant at Österlånggatan 51 in Stockholm's Old Town, traditionally opened in 1722; the painter Anders Zorn bought it in 1919 and left it to the Swedish Academy, and it is known for Swedish home cooking and literary memory.
- [Fortaleza: Relighting the 1873 Workshop](destileria-fortaleza.md) — A family tequila distillery at Calle Tabasco 153 in the town of Tequila, Jalisco, still using the old brick oven, tahona, wooden fermentation vats and two original copper pots; relaunched in 2005 and open by reservation with a tasting in the underground cellars.
- [Domaine de la Romanée-Conti](domaine-de-la-romanee-conti.md) — A Burgundy grand cru estate in Vosne-Romanée centred on the 1.81-hectare Romanée-Conti and 6.06-hectare La Tâche monopoles, with about 28.6 hectares all grand cru; the 1942 civil company is co-owned by the de Villaine and Leroy/Roch families, and there are no public visits.
- [Maison Joseph Drouhin](domaine-drouhin.md) — A family-owned Burgundy house founded in Beaune by Joseph Drouhin in 1880. Its headquarters at 7 Rue d'Enfer sit above cellars with Roman castrum foundations and medieval remains; the estate farms about 100 hectares and offers daily cellar tours and tastings, with a retail Oenothèque at Place du Général Leclerc.
- [Domaine Drouhin Oregon](domaine-drouhin-oregon.md) — Founded in 1987 by the Drouhin family of Burgundy in the Dundee Hills of Oregon, bringing Burgundian growing and winemak…
- [Domaine Huet](domaine-huet.md) — A family estate in Vouvray, France, founded in 1928 by Victor Huet and Gaston Huet, known for 30 hectares of Chenin Blanc on the Haut-Lieu, Le Mont and Clos du Bourg parcels and for biodynamics since 1988; visits and tastings must be confirmed first.
- [Domaine d'Ognoas](domaine-ognoas.md) — A Bas-Armagnac estate at Arthez-d'Armagnac in the Landes, with a seigneurie dating to the late 11th or early 12th century and departmental ownership since 1905. Some 650 hectares, a continuous still, rows of cellars and free tastings make it an unusually public estate for the appellation.
- [Domaine Weinbach](domaine-weinbach.md) — A family estate in Kaysersberg, Alsace, founded on a Capuchin monastery site in 1612 at the foot of the Schlossberg Grand Cru, known for the Clos des Capucins, biodynamic farming and Riesling and Pinot Gris; visits are by appointment.
- [Domaines Ott](domaines-ott.md) — A long-established Provence rosé house with three estates — Château de Selle, Clos Mireille and Château Romassan. The visitable cellar is Château de Selle in Taradeau, open on weekday mornings with a 45-minute guided tour that must be booked.
- [Dr. Loosen](dr-loosen.md) — A family estate at St. Johannishof in Bernkastel-Kues on the German Mosel, built around ungrafted old vines, blue, red and grey slate and Großes Gewächs Riesling; visits and tastings must be confirmed through official channels first.
- [Dukes Bar](dukes-bar.md) — A small hotel bar inside Dukes Hotel in St James's, London, dated by the house to 1908 and known for its tableside Dukes Martini, frozen glasses and no-ice serve.
- [The Eagle and Child](eagle-and-child.md) — A historic pub on St Giles' in Oxford, famous as a meeting place of the Inklings; closed since 2020, with restoration approved in 2025 and no current pub service.
- [Egon Müller](egon-muller.md) — A family estate at the Scharzhof in Wiltingen, in Germany's Saar valley, held by the same family since 1797 and known for Riesling from the blue-slate slopes of the Scharzhofberg and for extremely concentrated botrytised sweet wines; the official page offers only contact details, so visits must be confirmed first.
- [El Floridita](el-floridita.md) — A legendary bar in Old Havana, founded in 1817 and known for Hemingway, the frozen daiquiri and its seafood bar.
- [Els Quatre Gats](els-quatre-gats.md) — The Modernista café at Carrer de Montsió 3 in Barcelona, opened on 12 June 1897 by Ramon Casas, Santiago Rusiñol and Miquel Utrillo; Picasso frequented it and exhibited there, it closed in 1903 and reopened in the 1980s.
- [Employees Only](employees-only.md) — A West Village speakeasy-style cocktail bar opened in 2004, known for white-jacketed bartenders, martinis and Manhattans, late-night food and its "Psychic" storefront.
- [The Eyrie Vineyards](eyrie-vineyards.md) — A pioneer winery of the Willamette Valley founded by David Lett in 1965; its 1970s Pinot Noir brought Oregon internation…
- [Fattoria Fèlsina](fattoria-felsina.md) — A family estate at Castelnuovo Berardenga, east of Siena, bought by the Poggiali family in 1966; known for Sangiovese clones, Rancia and Fontalloro, tufa cellars and olive oil, and a representative estate on the south-eastern edge of Chianti Classico.
