# Drinks

[返回English目录](../README.md)

- [Penfolds Grange](australia-penfolds-grange.md) — The flagship wine of Penfolds in Australia, first made by Max Schubert in 1951 from mostly Shiraz with a little Cabernet Sauvignon, aged in American oak.
- [Erguotou](beijing-erguotou.md) — A traditional Beijing baijiu process and product category, named for the spirit taken from the second distillation pot; light-aroma and direct, commonly 43% to 56% ABV.
- [Chimay Blue](belgium-chimay-blue.md) — A dark Trappist ale from Scourmont Abbey in Belgium, 9% ABV and refermented in the bottle for about 21 days; known for dried fruit, caramel, cocoa and spice, with the 75 cl bottle also called Grande Réserve.
- [Lindemans Framboise](belgium-lindemans-framboise.md) — A raspberry lambic from the Lindemans family brewery in the Pajottenland, Belgium, made on a spontaneously fermented lambic base with raspberries added. Commonly 2.5%–4% ABV in 250 mL or 750 mL bottles; the style is sweet and differs from traditional dry lambic.
- [Orval](belgium-orval.md) — The single Trappist ale made at Orval Abbey in Belgium, commonly 5.2% ABV in 330 mL bottles, dry-hopped and fermented again in the bottle. Known for floral, citrus and dry notes with a light acidity, its character changes with bottle age.
- [Rochefort 10](belgium-rochefort-10.md) — A dark Trappist ale from Abbaye Notre-Dame de Saint-Rémy in Belgium, 11.3% ABV in 33 cl bottles; the number 10 refers to an old Belgian wort-density unit, not alcohol, and the beer is known for roasted, coffee, chocolate and spice notes.
- [Westmalle Tripel](belgium-westmalle-tripel.md) — A pale Trappist tripel from Westmalle Abbey in Belgium, commonly 9.5% ABV in 330 mL bottles and fermented again in the bottle. Known for banana, citrus, spice and a dry bitterness, it is a benchmark of the tripel style.
- [Westvleteren 12](belgium-westvleteren-12.md) — A dark quadrupel brewed by the Sint-Sixtus Abbey in Vleteren, West Flanders, Belgium, usually 10.2% vol in 330 mL and bottle-conditioned; sold only in limited quantities at the abbey, it shows dark fruit, caramel and spice and is one of the scarcest Trappist beers.
- [Changyu Jiebaina](changyu-jiebaina.md) — A dry red wine series from Changyu, first made in Yantai in 1931, commonly 750 mL with about 12%-13% ABV; Jiebaina is a product-series name, not a grape variety.
- [Almaviva](chile-alma-viva.md) — A Bordeaux-style red from Puente Alto in Chile's Maipo Valley, founded in 1997 by Viña Concha y Toro and Baron Philippe de Rothschild SA; sixty-five hectares of Andean alluvium, a Cabernet Sauvignon-led blend and the EPU second wine explain its difference from Opus One.
- [Crystal Jiannanchun](crystal-jiannanchun.md) — The core strong-aroma product of Jiannanchun, commonly called Crystal Sword in the market; usually 52% ABV in 500 mL and made in Mianzhu, Sichuan. This page covers raw materials, process, specification and buying at product level.
- [Bacardí Carta Blanca](cuba-bacardi-carta-blanca.md) — A white rum made from molasses, fermented, distilled and charcoal-filtered, commonly 40% ABV, and a base for Mojitos and Daiquiris.
- [Havana Club 7](cuba-havana-club-7-year-old.md) — A Cuban rum brand; molasses-based rums are blended and aged, commonly 40% ABV, with vanilla, caramel and tropical-fruit notes.
- [Pilsner Urquell](czech-pilsnerurquell.md) — A pale lager from Plzeň, Czechia, first brewed in 1842 with soft water, Saaz hops and triple decoction, about 4.4% ABV.
- [Dongjiu Baicaoxiang](dongjiu-baicaoxiang.md) — A high-end Dongjiu product in the Dong aroma type, commonly 54% ABV in 500 mL, made with herbs in qu, dual small and large qu fermentation and double-mash steam distillation; the Dong aroma type was formalised by a Guizhou local standard in 2008.
- [Aloxe-Corton](france-aloxe-corton.md) — A village appellation at the northern end of the Côte de Beaune, Burgundy, France, with about 118.5 hectares of village-level red and only 1.4 hectares of village-level white; the commune also contains part of Corton, the Côte de Beaune's only red-led grand cru, and of the white Corton-Charlemagne. Reds are structured and earthy, whites are scarce and come mostly from grand cru parcels, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Alsace Gewürztraminer](france-alsace-gewurztraminer.md) — A Gewürztraminer white wine from the Alsace region of France, labelled by variety and available as dry, late-harvest (VT) and botrytised selection (SGN) styles. Known for lychee, rose, ginger and cinnamon aromas, with low acidity and bold aroma; commonly 750 mL, dry styles about 12.5%–14% vol.
- [Alsace Riesling](france-alsace-riesling.md) — A Riesling white wine from Alsace, France, mostly dry, with high acidity and clear aromatics of citrus, white flowers and flint, developing a petrol note with age. Alsace labels wines by variety and has Grand Cru, late-harvest and botrytised designations; commonly 750 mL at about 12%–13.5% vol.
- [Arbois](france-arbois.md) — One of the Jura's earliest appellations, recognised on 15 May 1936 and covering 12 villages and about 766 hectares; all five Jura varieties are permitted, reds account for roughly 70% of Jura red output, and the area is known for the coexistence of vin jaune, vin de paille and ouillé whites.
- [Bandol](france-bandol.md) — An appellation on the Mediterranean coast of Provence in France, best known for red wines based on Mourvèdre, with a legal minimum of 50% Mourvèdre and at least 18 months in oak; the reds are structured with dark fruit and spice and also age well, and the appellation makes rosé and a little white wine, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–14.5% vol.
- [Bourgueil](france-bourgueil.md) — A Cabernet Franc red wine appellation in the Touraine area of the Loire Valley, France, on the right bank of the Loire west of Tours; recognised on 31 July 1937, with roughly 1,200–1,400 hectares divided between gravel terraces and clay-limestone slopes, the former supple and early-drinking, the latter firm and long-lived, plus about 4% rosé, commonly 750 mL at about 12%–13.5% vol.
- [Brouilly](france-brouilly.md) — A cru-level red wine from southern Beaujolais, France, made from Gamay, with vineyards circling Mont Brouilly across six villages. It is the largest of the ten crus, known for light fruit and relatively early drinking, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Chablis](france-chablis.md) — A dry white wine from the Chablis appellation in northern Burgundy, France, made only from Chardonnay and known for Kimmeridgian limestone soils and a lean, mineral style. Commonly 750 mL at about 12%–13% vol, with Petit Chablis, Chablis, Premier Cru and Grand Cru tiers.
- [Chambolle-Musigny](france-chambolle-musigny.md) — A village appellation in the Côte de Nuits of Burgundy, France, making red wine from Pinot Noir; the village holds two grands crus, Musigny and Bonnes-Mares, with premiers crus led by Les Amoureuses, and the style is delicate and floral, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Champagne](france-champagne.md) — A sparkling wine appellation in the Champagne region of northern France, centred on Reims and Épernay; made from Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Pinot Meunier by the traditional method of in-bottle second fermentation, known for high acidity, fine bubbles and citrus, apple and brioche notes, commonly 750 mL at about 12%–12.5% vol.
- [Chartreuse](france-chartreuse.md) — A herbal liqueur made by the Carthusian order in Voiron, France, based on a recipe said to contain about 130 plants; green is 55% ABV and yellow 40% ABV.
- [Château-Chalon](france-chateau-chalon.md) — The vin jaune of the Château-Chalon appellation in the Jura region of France, made only from Savagnin and aged oxidatively sous voile in old barrels for at least 6 years 3 months; it shows walnut, beeswax, curry and saline notes in contrast with ouillé topped-up Jura whites, commonly in a 620 mL narrow-necked bottle at about 13.5%–15% vol.
- [Château Cheval Blanc](france-chateau-cheval-blanc.md) — A red wine from Saint-Émilion in Bordeaux, France, on the border with Pomerol, with about 39 hectares planted mainly to Cabernet Franc and Merlot. It was ranked Premier Grand Cru Classé A in the 1955 Saint-Émilion classification and withdrew from that classification in 2021; commonly 750 mL at about 13%–14% vol.
- [Château d'Yquem](france-chateau-dyquem.md) — A botrytised sweet white wine from Sauternes in Bordeaux, rated Premier Cru Supérieur in the 1855 classification and controlled by LVMH since 1999; made from Sémillon and Sauvignon Blanc, commonly 375 mL or 750 mL at about 13-14% vol.
- [Château Haut-Brion](france-chateau-haut-brion.md) — A classified growth in Pessac-Léognan, Bordeaux, ranked first growth in the 1855 classification and the only estate on that list outside the Médoc; a Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot-led blend, commonly 750 mL at about 13% ABV, with vintage and batch to be checked on the label.
- [Château Latour](france-chateau-latour.md) — A first growth at the southern end of Pauillac whose grand vin comes only from the 47-hectare Enclos, planted with vines averaging about 60 years old and usually over 90% Cabernet Sauvignon; 100% new oak, 22–25 months in barrel, and no en primeur sales since 2012.
- [Château Margaux](france-chateau-margaux.md) — A Margaux first growth and the only estate to score 20/20 in the 1855 classification; 265 hectares with about 87 hectares of red vines and 12 of white, a grand vin near 89% Cabernet Sauvignon and a selection of roughly 40%, aged 21 months in new French oak.
- [Château Mouton Rothschild](france-chateau-mouton-rothschild.md) — A Pauillac estate ranked second growth in 1855 and raised to first growth in 1973, the only promotion in the list's history; its grand vin is about 81% Cabernet Sauvignon, averages 44-year-old vines and is aged around 20 months in new oak, with artist-designed labels since 1945.
- [Pétrus](france-chateau-petrus.md) — A Merlot red wine from Pomerol in Bordeaux, France, with a vineyard of about 11.4 hectares on the eastern plateau of Pomerol planted almost entirely to Merlot. Pomerol has no official classification, and Pétrus is known for clay and iron-rich subsoils, low yields and long ageing potential; commonly 750 mL at about 13%–14% vol.
- [Château Lafite Rothschild](france-chateaulafiterothschild.md) — A Pauillac first growth in Bordeaux, based on Cabernet Sauvignon; classified as a first growth in 1855 and purchased by the Rothschild family in 1868.
- [Châteauneuf-du-Pape](france-chateauneuf-du-pape.md) — A red wine from the Châteauneuf-du-Pape appellation in the southern Rhône, France, based on Grenache and often blended with Syrah and other varieties. Full-bodied and high in alcohol, known for ripe black fruit, Provençal herbs and leather. The appellation permits more than ten varieties and sets a minimum of 12.5% vol; commonly 750 mL at about 14%–15.5% vol.
- [Chénas](france-chenas.md) — A cru-level red wine from the village of Chénas in northern Beaujolais, France, made from Gamay. Its vineyards were cleared from old oak woodland, and the cru is the smallest of the ten; on granite it gives a tighter style than Fleurie and drinks earlier than Moulin-à-Vent, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Chignin-Bergeron](france-chignin-bergeron.md) — A white-wine geographical name in the Savoie region of France, made only from Roussanne, known locally as Bergeron; the vineyards lie on south or south-east facing limestone slopes around Chignin, and the style shows white flowers, apricot, honey and almond with lower acidity than Jacquère, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Chinon](france-chinon.md) — A Cabernet Franc red wine appellation in the Touraine area of the Loire Valley, France, on both banks of the Vienne upstream of its confluence with the Loire; recognised on 31 July 1937, with about 2,400 hectares across 26 communes, soils ranging from valley gravel to clay-limestone slopes and flint clays, plus rosé and a little Chenin white, commonly 750 mL at about 12%–13.5% vol.
- [Chiroubles](france-chiroubles.md) — The highest of the ten Beaujolais village crus, with vineyards climbing to about 400 metres on granite sand; the lightest body and the most outward floral scent among the crus, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13% vol.
- [Cornas](france-cornas.md) — A Syrah red wine appellation in the Northern Rhône Valley, France, on steep slopes on the west bank of the Rhône north of Valence; small in output and firm in style, with black olive, black pepper, violet and smoky notes, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–14% vol.
- [Corton](france-corton.md) — A grand cru appellation on the hill of Corton in the Côte de Beaune, Burgundy, France, making red wine from Pinot Noir and white wine from Chardonnay and the only red grand cru of the Côte de Beaune; reds are known for structure, earth and red fruit, while the whites and Corton-Charlemagne are known for nut, honey and mineral notes, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Côte de Brouilly](france-cote-de-brouilly.md) — A cru-level red wine from the slopes of Mont Brouilly in southern Beaujolais, France, made from Gamay. It takes only the hillside and not the foot of the hill; blue porphyry and granite give clearer acidity and minerality, and the area is far smaller than neighbouring Brouilly, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Côte de Nuits-Villages](france-cote-de-nuits-villages.md) — A village appellation in the southern Côte de Nuits, Burgundy, France, with vineyards in villages including Brochon, Comblanchien, Corgoloin and Prémeaux-Prissey; mainly red wine from Pinot Noir with some white wine from Chardonnay, known for red fruit, earth and earlier drinking, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Côte-Rôtie](france-cote-rotie.md) — A red wine from the Côte-Rôtie appellation at the northern end of France's Rhône Valley, based on Syrah with a permitted touch of Viognier, grown on steep terraced slopes around Ampuis and known for black pepper, violet, olive and smoky notes. Commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–14% vol.
- [Côtes de Provence Rosé](france-cotes-de-provence-rose.md) — The rosé of the Côtes de Provence appellation in the Provence region of France, the best-known and largest-volume wine of the region; a blend of Grenache, Cinsault, Syrah and Mourvèdre with short skin contact and pale colour, showing red fruit, citrus, white flowers and saline notes, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Côtes du Jura](france-cotes-du-jura.md) — The broadest appellation in the Jura region of France, recognised on 31 July 1937 and spanning 105 communes while covering only about 551 hectares; whites and crémant dominate, with red and white wines plus vin jaune and vin de paille, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–14.5% vol.
- [Crémant du Jura](france-cremant-du-jura.md) — The traditional-method sparkling wine appellation of the Jura region in France, made from Chardonnay, Savagnin, Pinot Noir, Poulsard and Trousseau, with the second fermentation in bottle and at least 9 months on the lees; the style is fresh with high acidity and fine bubbles, in white and rosé versions, commonly 750 mL at about 12.0%–12.5% vol.
- [Crozes-Hermitage](france-crozes-hermitage.md) — A Syrah red wine appellation on the east bank of the Rhône in the Northern Rhône Valley, France, spread around Tain-l'Hermitage and the Hermitage hill and the largest appellation in the northern Rhône; reds are made from Syrah and a small amount of white wine from Marsanne and Roussanne, with blackberry, black pepper and violet notes, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–14% vol.
- [Dom Pérignon](france-domperignon.md) — A vintage-only Champagne brand owned by Moët & Chandon; the first commercial vintage was 1921, released in 1936, usually bottled at 12.5% ABV.
- [Fleurie](france-fleurie.md) — A cru-level red wine from the village of Fleurie in northern Beaujolais, France, made from Gamay. Its vineyards sit on pink weathered granite, and named sites such as La Madone and Grille-Midi are often labelled on their own; the style is known for floral aroma, a light body and a supple texture, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Gevrey-Chambertin](france-gevrey-chambertin.md) — A red wine from the Gevrey-Chambertin commune in Burgundy's Côte de Nuits, France, made entirely from Pinot Noir. The commune holds nine grands crus including Chambertin and Chambertin-Clos de Bèze, and the style is known for structure, dark fruit and ageing potential; commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Gigondas](france-gigondas.md) — A red wine appellation in the Southern Rhône Valley, France, whose AOC covers the single village of Gigondas at the foot of the Dentelles de Montmirail; based on Grenache with Syrah, Mourvèdre and Cinsault, with vines from about 100 to 500 metres, a limestone-driven style, and white wine permitted from the 2023 vintage, commonly 750 mL at about 13%–15% vol.
- [Hautes-Côtes de Nuits](france-hautes-cotes-de-nuits.md) — A Burgundy appellation in the hills behind the Côte de Nuits, France, with vineyards on the slopes above the Côte de Nuits villages; red wine is made from Pinot Noir and white wine from Chardonnay, and the higher altitude and later ripening give a style known for fresh acidity and earlier drinking, commonly 750 mL at about 11.5%–13% vol.
- [Hennessy X.O](france-hennessyxo.md) — A Cognac brandy from France, blended from more than a hundred eaux-de-vie; since 2018 the X.O grade has required at least 10 years in oak, with 40% ABV in the standard bottling.
- [Juliénas](france-julienas.md) — A village cru from northern Beaujolais whose name is tied to a Julius Caesar legend; granite and schist slopes give it firmer structure than Chiroubles and earlier approachability than Moulin-à-Vent, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–14% vol.
- [Krug](france-krug.md) — A historic Champagne house in Reims, France, founded in 1843 by Joseph Krug and part of LVMH since 1999; known for the multi-vintage Grande Cuvée, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5% ABV, with batch and disgorgement date to be checked on the label.
- [L'Étoile](france-letoile.md) — A small white-wine appellation in northern Jura, France, making only white wine as well as vin jaune and vin de paille; the varieties are mainly Chardonnay and Savagnin, the soils mainly limestone and marl, and the style is fresh with high acidity and clear minerality, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–14.5% vol.
- [Margaux](france-margaux.md) — A communal red wine appellation in southern Médoc, Bordeaux, France, recognised in 1954 and spread across Arsac, Labarde, Margaux-Cantenac and Soussans; about 1,500 hectares and 62 estates, with 21 of the 1855 classified growths; Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot on gravel terraces, a 57 hl/ha base yield and 11% minimum natural alcohol.
- [Martell Cordon Bleu](france-martell-cordon-bleu.md) — A Cognac created by Martell in 1912, known for eaux-de-vie from the Borderies area and blended from several crus and vintages; commonly 40% ABV in 700 mL bottles, with the exact proportions undisclosed.
- [Mercurey](france-mercurey.md) — A village-level appellation in the Côte Chalonnaise, generally regarded as the largest village appellation of that sub-region. Reds come from Pinot Noir and whites from Chardonnay; there is no grand cru but many premiers crus, and the reds are direct with more obvious tannin, often found in everyday drinking and restaurant listings; commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Meursault](france-meursault.md) — A dry white wine from the Meursault commune on Burgundy's Côte de Beaune in France, made from Chardonnay and known for premiers crus such as Les Perrières, Les Genevrières and Les Charmes. Fuller than Chablis, with hazelnut, butter, citrus and minerality; commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Moët & Chandon](france-moet.md) — A Champagne house founded in Épernay in 1743, known for the non-vintage Brut Impérial, about 1,300 hectares of vineyards and more than 28 kilometres of cellars; this page explains why its style is built for consistency, easy drinking and large-scale hospitality.
- [Morey-Saint-Denis](france-morey-saint-denis.md) — A village in the middle Côte de Nuits whose name joins "Morey" to its Clos Saint-Denis grand cru. It holds four complete grands crus, Clos de la Roche, Clos Saint-Denis, Clos des Lambrays and Clos de Tart, and shares parts of Bonnes-Mares and Charmes-Chambertin, with a style between the power of Gevrey and the finesse of Chambolle; commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Morgon](france-morgon.md) — A village-level cru red wine from northern Beaujolais in France, made from Gamay, with vineyards on granite slopes around Villié-Morgon and Côte du Py as the signature slope. It is fuller in body with more noticeable tannin and can age, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–14% vol.
- [Moulin-à-Vent](france-moulin-a-vent.md) — A village-level cru red wine from northern Beaujolais in France, made from Gamay, with vineyards on granite slopes around Romanèche-Thorins and named after an old windmill on the slope. Among the Beaujolais crus it is fuller with more noticeable tannin and stronger ageing potential, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–14% vol.
- [Muscadet](france-muscadet.md) — A group of white wine appellations in the Nantes area of the Loire Valley, France, made from Melon de Bourgogne; high in acidity and neutral in aroma, often lees-aged, with green apple, lemon, white flower and saline notes, commonly 750 mL at about 11%–12.5% vol.
- [Nuits-Saint-Georges](france-nuits-saint-georges.md) — A village-level Pinot Noir red wine appellation in the Côte de Nuits of Burgundy, France, south of Dijon and north of Beaune; known for black cherry, liquorice and earthy notes with a firmer body, with several premiers crus but no grand cru in the village, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Pauillac](france-pauillac.md) — A communal red wine appellation in northern Médoc, Bordeaux, France, recognised on 14 November 1936, with about 1,200 hectares of vines and 18 classified growths in the 1855 classification, including the first growths Lafite, Latour and Mouton; Cabernet Sauvignon dominates on deep gravel croupes, with a 57 hl/ha base yield and 11% minimum natural alcohol.
- [Pernand-Vergelesses](france-pernand-vergelesses.md) — A village appellation in the valley on the western side of the hill of Corton in the Côte de Beaune, Burgundy, France, with 69.7 hectares of white and 76.6 hectares of red in 2022, so white makes up nearly half; it has eight premiers crus, including Île des Vergelesses, and contains the En Charlemagne climat of Corton-Charlemagne. Reds are lighter and more floral, whites mineral and fresh, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Pessac-Léognan](france-pessac-leognan.md) — A red and white wine appellation in northern Graves, south of Bordeaux city, France, separated from Graves in 1987; reds blend Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, whites use Sauvignon Blanc and Sémillon, and both show smoky, mineral and structured notes, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Pommard](france-pommard.md) — A village-level Pinot Noir red wine appellation in the Côte de Beaune village of Pommard, Burgundy, France, south of Beaune; the village has several Premier Cru sites but no Grand Cru, and the style is known for red fruit, earth, firmer tannin and stronger structure, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Pouilly-Fumé](france-pouilly-fume.md) — A dry Sauvignon Blanc appellation in the Centre-Loire area of the Loire Valley, France, awarded AOC status in 1937 and covering seven villages on the right bank of the Loire across about 1,424 hectares; silex flint and Kimmeridgian marl give citrus, grass and gunflint notes, commonly 750 mL at about 12%–13.5% vol.
- [Régnié](france-regnie.md) — A Beaujolais village cru approved only in 1988 and the youngest of the ten; granite slopes around Régnié-Durette give red fruit and fine tannin, usually at a more affordable level than Morgon, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Romanée-Conti](france-romaneeconti.md) — A grand cru monopole in Vosne-Romanée, Burgundy, owned by Domaine de la Romanée-Conti; 1.8140 hectares planted entirely to Pinot Noir.
- [Roussette de Savoie](france-roussette-de-savoie.md) — A white-wine appellation in the Savoie region of France made from Altesse, known locally as Roussette, with geographical names including Marestel, Monthoux, Monterminod and Frangy; the style shows white flowers, honey, hazelnut and minerality with a fuller body than Jacquère, commonly 750 mL at about 12.0%–13.5% vol.
- [Saint-Amour](france-saint-amour.md) — The northernmost village cru of Beaujolais, its appellation spanning the Saône-et-Loire and Rhône departments; granite and schist slopes make it firmer and spicier than the southern crus, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Saint-Aubin](france-saint-aubin.md) — A village-level appellation on the high ground of a Côte de Beaune valley, often hidden behind the ridge from Meursault and Chassagne-Montrachet. White wine dominates and red is also made; there is no grand cru, about thirty premiers crus, and the whites show high acidity and strong minerality, usually a more restrained Côte de Beaune white; commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Saint-Émilion](france-saint-emilion.md) — A red wine appellation around the town of Saint-Émilion and neighbouring communes on the right bank of the Dordogne in Bordeaux, France, northeast of Libourne; based on Merlot and often blended with Cabernet Franc, rounder in style, with plum, black cherry, chocolate and earth notes, and its own classification system, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Saint-Joseph](france-saint-joseph.md) — An appellation in the northern Rhône Valley of France, stretching tens of kilometres along both banks of the Rhône; mainly Syrah red wine, lighter in style than Côte-Rôtie and Hermitage, with a small amount of white wine, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–14% vol.
- [Saint-Julien](france-saint-julien.md) — A communal red wine appellation in central Médoc, Bordeaux, France, recognised on 14 November 1936, with about 910 hectares of vines and 11 classified growths in the 1855 classification (five second, two third and four fourth growths, no first growth); its gravel is the most uniform in the Médoc, with a 57 hl/ha base yield and 11% minimum natural alcohol.
