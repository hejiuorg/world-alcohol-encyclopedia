# Langjiu

Langjiu is produced in Erlang, Gulin, Sichuan, on the Chishui River. Its products span both sauce-aroma and strong-aroma styles, and sauce-aroma lines such as Qinghua Lang are widely known. A common sauce-aroma strength is 53% ABV.

## Definition and position

Langjiu is a brand within [baijiu](ref:baijiu) whose product range crosses sauce aroma and strong aroma. Brand, aroma type and origin must be read separately: Erlang is the producing area, Langjiu is the brand, and sauce or strong aroma is a process and flavour category. Sharing the Chishui valley does not make every product the same aroma type.

Sauce-aroma products are distilled, with strength stated as [alcohol by volume](ref:abv), commonly 53% ABV. Different lines have different strengths, bodies and blending aims, so confirm the aroma type before comparing styles.

## Origin and environment

Erlang sits in the middle Chishui valley, where the river cuts a deep gorge and humidity is high, with hot summers and relatively mild winters. This climate favours open qu making, stacked fermentation and microbial activity during long storage. Local water and valley air are treated as part of the environment for sauce aroma.

A distinctive feature of Langjiu is the use of natural caves for storing base spirit. Temperature and humidity in the caves are relatively stable, so the spirit in clay jars changes slowly in a more constant environment. Cave storage is not a mystical concept: it is environmental management of storage, and the result still depends on base quality, vessel and time.

The water quality and purple sandstone geology of the Chishui valley are often cited by local producers as brewing conditions. Such claims have a basis in regional experience, but should not be the only explanation: raw materials, qu, pits and blending also decide the finished style.

## Raw materials and qu

Sauce-aroma products use sorghum with a high-temperature wheat qu. The sorghum must survive repeated steaming, and glutinous varieties with a high proportion of branched starch are better suited. After culturing, the wheat qu is stacked and turned, forming a community of bacteria, moulds and yeasts that supplies enzymes and aroma precursors for later fermentation.

[Qu making](ref:qu-making) is the foundation of sauce aroma. The toasted, baked and qu notes of high-temperature qu convert during fermentation and distillation into the composite aroma of the finished product. Strong-aroma products rely more on mud pits and continued mash recycling, with different qu and pit management, so the flavour direction differs.

## Production

The public process for sauce-aroma products is close to practices around Maotai town: one annual production cycle, two grain additions, and multiple rounds of steaming, fermentation and spirit drawing. Each round yields a different base style, which must be stored separately and then blended. Maturation after distillation is an important stage in forming the style.

Strong-aroma products use mud-pit solid-state fermentation, continued mash recycling and mixed steaming and boiling. The two processes place different demands on equipment, pits and time, so sauce-aroma and strong-aroma products under the same brand cannot be covered by a single "Langjiu style". The aroma type on the label is the first-hand information about the process route.

## Flavour and tasting

Sauce-aroma products typically show soy-like, toasted and floral notes, a full body and a long finish; strong-aroma products emphasise pit and grain aroma with a sweeter entry. The valley climate and water are treated as brewing conditions, but different plants and product lines are not identical.

For sauce aroma, use a small cup, let it rest and smell, then sip and follow the order of sauce, toasted and floral notes and whether the body carries the alcohol. For strong aroma, focus on whether the pit aroma is clean and the grain note is clear. The two need different evaluation frameworks rather than one ruler.

## Product lines and labels

Langjiu has sauce-aroma lines such as Qinghua Lang and Honghua Lang, as well as strong-aroma and composite-aroma products. Series differ in positioning, price and body design, and the aroma type, strength, producer and standard on the label are key to identity. Similar names and counterfeits exist, so buy through official channels.

Terms such as "cave storage" and "vintage" must be read with company statements and labels. Cave storage describes a storage environment, not the exact age of the liquid; the rules for vintage products should also be explained by company standards rather than inferred from packaging.

## Drinking and pairing

Sauce-aroma products suit neat sipping in small cups at room temperature; strong-aroma products can be served neat or with food. At the table, sauce aroma suits sauce-flavoured dishes, braised snacks and barbecue, while strong aroma suits Sichuan and home-style cooking. High-strength spirit should not be drunk quickly; small amounts, sipped slowly, show the layers better.

Langjiu can be used in mixed drinks, but 53% ABV significantly changes the balance. Ice or a large amount of water is not traditional and dilutes the aroma and structure. After opening, store away from light in a cool place and finish within a reasonable time.

## Common misconceptions

First, treating all Langjiu as sauce aroma. The brand also makes strong-aroma and composite-aroma products, so read the label.
Second, assuming everything from the Chishui River is the same. The region shares an environment, but processes and product design differ.
Third, equating cave storage with a guarantee of age. Cave storage is a storage condition, not a specific age.
Fourth, that higher strength is better. Strength serves body design, and lower-strength versions also have complete aims.
Fifth, judging authenticity by packaging or price. Anti-counterfeit checks, channels and labels are reliable.
