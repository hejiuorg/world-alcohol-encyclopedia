# GB/T 10781.2-2022 Product quality requirements for baijiu — Part 2: Light-aroma baijiu

GB/T 10781.2-2022 is the current quality requirement for light-aroma baijiu. It was published on 2022-07-11, took effect on 2023-02-01 and replaces GB/T 10781.2-2006. The new edition removes the high-strength and low-strength product classification, sets alcohol at 21.0%vol to 69.0%vol, and introduces special, premium and first grades. It also applies different acid and ester indices within one year and after one year of production, responding to the rise in acids and fall in esters during storage. [Fenjiu](ref:fenjiu) in Shanxi is the representative product.

## Standard number and administration

The number is GB/T 10781.2-2022. The GB/T prefix marks a recommended national standard, not a mandatory one, and it is not a food safety standard. It was issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation and the Standardization Administration of China, proposed by the China National Light Industry Council and administered by the National Technical Committee for Baijiu Standardization (SAC/TC 358). It is Part 2 of the GB/T 10781 series.

## Version history and current status

The light-aroma standard was first published in 1989 as GB/T 10781.2-1989. The 2006 revision merged the content of GB/T 11859.2-1989 for low-strength light-aroma baijiu. The 2022 edition is the second revision. It changes the definition, deletes classification and hygiene requirements, and revises sensory and physicochemical requirements. It took effect on 2023-02-01, when the 2006 edition ceased to apply. The National Standards Information Public Service Platform lists it as current; until a new edition appears, the 2022 edition remains the sole basis.

## Scope and exclusions

The standard applies to the production, inspection and sale of light-aroma baijiu. The definition requires grain raw materials, qu made from daqu, xiaoqu or fuqu together with yeast as saccharifying and fermenting agents, solid-state fermentation in jars, pits or similar vessels, solid-state distillation, ageing and blending, with no direct or indirect addition of edible alcohol or colour, aroma and taste substances not produced by fermentation. It covers daqu, xiaoqu and fuqu routes, but not strong, sauce, Feng or other aromas, nor liquid-fermentation baijiu, solid-liquid baijiu or compounded liquor. Food safety is governed by GB 2757, GB 2762 and GB 2760.

## Key indices and limits

Sensory requirements are set for special, premium and first grades. Colour and appearance must be colourless or slightly yellow, clear and transparent, with no suspended matter, sediment or impurities. Aroma ranges from the special grade's pure light aroma with aged, grain, qu, fruit, floral, herb and toasted notes forming an elegant, harmonious compound aroma with long empty-cup persistence, through the premium grade's clear and elegant version, to the first grade's correct light aroma with some empty-cup aroma. Taste ranges from mellow, sweet, full, delicate, harmonious and very long-finishing to mellow, soft, sweet and long-finishing. Below 10°C a white sediment or slight cloudiness is permitted, and it should clear above 10°C.

Physicochemical limits are: alcohol 21.0%vol to 69.0%vol; solids no more than 0.50 g/L. Within one year of production, total acid as acetic acid is at least 0.50 g/L for special, 0.40 g/L for premium and 0.30 g/L for first grade; total esters as ethyl acetate at least 1.10, 0.80 and 0.50 g/L; ethyl acetate at least 0.65, 0.40 and 0.20 g/L. After one year, the index changes to total acid plus ethyl acetate plus ethyl lactate: at least 1.60 g/L for special, 0.60 g/L for premium and 0.40 g/L for first grade, calculated on a 45.0%vol basis. The permitted difference between measured and labelled alcohol is ±1.0%vol.

The design is deliberate. Within the first year, total acid, total esters and ethyl acetate show whether aroma compounds are sufficient. After a year, ester hydrolysis raises acids and lowers esters, so continuing to use total esters would penalise normal ageing. The combined index accepts the rise in acid while retaining a requirement for the main esters.

## Test methods

Sensory requirements follow GB/T 10345. Alcohol follows GB 5009.225. Solids, total esters and ethyl acetate follow GB/T 10345. Total acid follows GB 12456, expressed in g/L. For the combined index, total acid, ethyl acetate and ethyl lactate are measured separately and converted to 45.0%vol using the formula in the standard; duplicate results must agree within 5% of their mean. Net content follows JJF 1070. Inspection rules and labelling, packaging, transport and storage follow GB/T 10346.

## Relationship with neighbouring standards

Compared with GB/T 10781.2-2006, the new edition deletes the strength-based classification and hygiene requirements, adds a special grade, uses two sets of acid-ester indices for the first year and later, and fixes conversion at 45.0%vol. Compared with strong aroma, light aroma is led by ethyl acetate with a lower ethyl lactate share and stresses purity rather than rich pit aroma. Compared with sauce aroma, it has no high-temperature qu, high-temperature stacking or multi-round collection. GB/T 15109-2021 defines terms, GB/T 17204-2021 assigns broad categories, and GB 2757-2012 is the mandatory safety floor.

## Compliance and labelling

Labels should follow GB/T 10346 and prepackaged-food rules, stating the product name, aroma type, alcohol content, net content, production date, producer and standard number. Alcohol is expressed in %vol, with a tolerance of ±1.0%vol. The standard expressly requires prepackaged products to identify the product type as solid-state baijiu, a new labelling duty in the 2022 edition. Products may carry special, premium or first grade, but the grade must match the test results. Food safety requirements remain those of the standards in force.

## Common misconceptions

First, light aroma does not mean low alcohol; the new edition removes the strength-based classification and sets 21.0%vol to 69.0%vol. Second, special grade is a level within the standard, not an official certification. Third, a fall in total esters after one year does not mean failure, because the new edition switches to a combined acid-ester index. Fourth, light aroma does not mean no aroma; it stresses a clear, pure and complex profile. Fifth, not all light-aroma baijiu uses daqu; the standard also allows xiaoqu, fuqu and yeast.
