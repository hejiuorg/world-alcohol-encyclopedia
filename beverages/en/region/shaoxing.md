# Shaoxing

Shaoxing is in northern Zhejiang, on the south shore of Hangzhou Bay at the northern foot of the Kuaiji Mountains, and is one of the most concentrated huangjiu regions in [China](ref:china). Shaoxing wine combines glutinous rice, wheat qu and Jian Lake water, and forms its style through the tanfan method, open-mash stirring and jar ageing. The general definition and classification of [huangjiu](ref:yellow-wine) are covered on the style page; this region focuses on the raw materials, process and product structure of the Shaoxing region.

## Geography and water

Shaoxing's landscape runs from the Kuaiji Mountains in the south to plains and waterways in the north, with many rivers and lakes. Jian Lake lies southwest of the city and is treated by the local industry as a key water source. Water hardness, mineral composition and microbial condition affect saccharification and fermentation, so protecting the water source has long been part of regional management.

It should be said that good Jian Lake water is a point repeated in regional storytelling, but it is not the only variable. Rice, qu, temperature, fermentation management and ageing conditions all shape the result. Treating water as the sole guarantee of quality ignores differences in process.

## Raw materials and qu

Shaoxing wine uses glutinous rice as its main material. Glutinous rice has a high proportion of amylopectin and becomes sticky after cooking, which helps saccharification and gives a full-bodied ferment. Different products adjust rice quantity, water ratio and ingredients to form types ranging from dry to sweet.

Wheat qu is the main saccharifying agent, made by culturing moulds on wheat. Jiuyao contains Rhizopus and yeasts and is used to prepare lin-fan wine mother. Wheat qu supplies enzymes and qu aroma, while jiuyao starts fermentation. Temperature, humidity and culture management in [qu making](ref:qu-making) directly affect saccharification efficiency and the aromatic base.

## The tanfan method and open-mash stirring

Shaoxing wine traditionally uses the tanfan method: cooked rice is spread on bamboo mats to cool naturally, then mixed with qu and loaded into jars to ferment. The contrasting linfan method rinses rice with cold water to cool it quickly and is mostly used for wine mother. Tanfan cooling is slower and evens out moisture and temperature, which suits the long fermentation of Shaoxing wine.

The key action during fermentation is kaiba, or open-mash stirring. Stirring releases heat, exchanges air and remixes the ferment, controlling temperature and microbial condition. Timing and frequency depend on experience; too early or too late changes the direction of fermentation. Modern production uses temperature control and equipment, but kaiba remains one of the core steps of the traditional craft.

## Types and sugar levels

The Shaoxing product range is often represented by four types. Yuanhong is dry. Jiafan adds more rice to the recipe, is semi-dry and has a fuller body. Shanniang uses wine instead of water and is semi-sweet. Xiangxue uses zaoshao spirit instead of water and is sweeter. The differences come from ingredients and sugar levels, not from a simple hierarchy of quality.

Huadiao is often mistaken for a separate grade. It more often refers to a trade name for jar-packed, painted-jar and aged formats, while the sugar type still depends on the product label and the standard applied. Treating huadiao as the highest grade confuses packaging with the liquid.

## Ageing and products

Shaoxing wine is mostly aged in earthen jars. The slight porosity of the jar allows slow oxidation and esterification, creating aged aroma and a rounder palate. Ageing time, storage temperature and jar condition all affect the result, so vintage wording has to be read together with storage conditions.

[Guyuelongshan](ref:gu-yuelongshan) and [Huijishan](ref:huijishan) are representative product pages from the Shaoxing region. When comparing products, read sugar type, strength, ageing time and price band together rather than judging by brand or vintage alone.

## Standard and geographical indication

Shaoxing wine has a dedicated geographical indication standard, GB/T 17946-2008, Geographical indication product — Shaoxing wine, which sets requirements for the protected area, raw materials and process. The general huangjiu product standard is GB/T 13662-2018, Huangjiu, which covers classification, technical requirements and test methods.

Geographical indication protection means a product meets the area and process conditions, but it is not a quality ranking. Shaoxing wines that meet the standard still differ in style and price, and products outside the protection may also be good huangjiu, simply without the right to use the name.

## Common misconceptions

First, equating Shaoxing wine with huangjiu. Shaoxing wine is one regional type within the wider category.
Second, assuming darker colour means older wine. Colour is affected by qu type, ageing vessel and caramel colour, and cannot date a wine on its own.
Third, treating huadiao as a grade. Huadiao is more a trade name for jar-packed and aged formats.
Fourth, assuming all Shaoxing wine is sweet. Yuanhong and jiafan are dry or semi-dry, and sweetness varies widely.
Fifth, treating the geographical indication as a quality ranking. It confirms area and process compliance, not sensory quality or price.
