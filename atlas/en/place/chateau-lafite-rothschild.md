# Château Lafite Rothschild

Château Lafite Rothschild lies in Pauillac on the left bank of Bordeaux and is a first growth of the 1855 Médoc classification. The official history traces the name to the Gascon "La Hite", a small hill, and records that the ridge was planted with vines from the 17th century. In 1868 Baron James de Rothschild bought the estate, and it has remained in the Rothschild family since. The official account says it was ranked "First of the First" at the Universal Exhibition held under Napoleon III. It is now the core estate of Domaines Barons de Rothschild (Lafite), not a standalone brand.

## Setting and history

Lafite's history is often reduced to two dates, 1855 and 1868, but they are thirteen years apart and different in nature. The 1855 classification was a commercial ranking for the Paris Universal Exhibition, ordering the estates of the Médoc and Graves by price and reputation into five levels. Lafite was placed in the first, and the official phrase "First of the First" stresses its position within that group. The 1868 purchase marks the beginning of family ownership: after Baron James de Rothschild bought the estate, the family weathered phylloxera, war, economic crisis and repeated rebuilding while keeping control.

Those two moments explain Lafite's double identity: it is both a symbol of the Bordeaux classification and a family-run agricultural business. A visit that remembers only the word "first growth" misses the vineyard work, drainage, picking and barrel ageing that have sustained the reputation after 1855.

## Space and wine

The vineyard covers 112 hectares in three main blocks: the slopes around the château, the Carruades plateau to the west, and a 4.5-hectare parcel in Saint-Estèphe. The soils are deep fine gravel mixed with wind-blown sand over Tertiary limestone with good drainage. Cabernet Sauvignon dominates, with Merlot, Cabernet Franc and Petit Verdot. The grand vin uses only vines older than ten years, ferments for 15 days at 26–27°C and ages for 15 months in new oak. The second wine, Carruades de Lafite, comes from the Carruades plateau, has a higher proportion of Merlot and ages for about 16 months in French oak. There is no visitor "wine list" here; when visits resume, the focus is the vineyard, the cellar and barrel tasting.

In practice, the wines discussed are the grand vin and Carruades. The grand vin is Cabernet-led, fine-structured and built for long ageing; Carruades is more Merlot-forward and easier to approach young. They are not "good wine and inferior wine" but different parcels and different blending aims. The official site also notes that the estate became a B Corp in December 2023 and is moving towards organic farming; that says more about today's Lafite than memorising vintages.

## Visiting and tasting

Lafite does not receive visitors without an appointment. On 7 November 2026 the official visit page stated that the château is closed to visitors until January 2028 for renovation and invited visitors to Château Duhart-Milon in the same appellation, contacted through its booking form or visites@duhartmilon.com. The contact page still says renovation "until 2026", so the two official pages disagree and the position must be confirmed before travelling. In normal times visits are by appointment only and usually include the vineyard, cellar and a tasting; duration, language and price are set by the estate. Do not treat a stop at the gate for photographs as a visit, as the property is private.

Buying is a separate matter from visiting. The grand vin is sold mainly through en primeur and the merchant network, and the château gate is not a retail shop; Carruades and some older vintages can be found through merchants, restaurants or auctions. If you ask about buying during a visit, staff will usually refer you onward rather than sell a bottle on the spot. Keeping "visit" and "purchase" as separate plans avoids disappointment.

## Practical information

Address: Château Lafite Rothschild, 33250 Pauillac, France (coordinates 45.22500, -0.77306). The official address uses the estate name and postcode rather than a street number; navigate to Pauillac and follow the D2 north to the estate signs. Telephone +33 5 56 73 18 18.

Transport: Pauillac is about 50 km north-west of Bordeaux, so by car follow the D2 wine route north. Pauillac has a railway station with TER regional trains from Bordeaux Saint-Jean, subject to SNCF/TER timetables. There is no direct public transport from the station to the estate, so take a taxi or walk a considerable distance; the D2 has no continuous pavement, and cyclists should judge traffic and road conditions carefully.

Status and opening: on 2026-11-08 the official English visit page stated that the château is "closed to visitors until January 2028 due to renovations", while another notice on the site still says renovation to 2026; the two are inconsistent, so trust the English visit page and the reply to your enquiry. This record therefore gives the visitor status as `closed`. Production has not stopped: the vineyard, winemaking and sales continue.

Tickets and booking: no visits and no ticket sales during the renovation. The site suggests Château Duhart-Milon in the same appellation, booked through its official form or visites@duhartmilon.com, by appointment and subject to availability; dates, language and whether tasting is included are set by the estate. Post-reopening packages and prices have not been published and remain unverified, so check the official booking page once visits resume.

The surrounding roads are narrow, so do not park at vineyard entrances or on private drives; vineyard work involves machinery during the season, so do not block agricultural vehicles. Page checked on 2026-11-08; operating status, prices and bookings are subject to official channels.

## Etiquette and misconceptions

The first misconception is that Lafite belongs to LVMH. It belongs to Domaines Barons de Rothschild (Lafite), while Mouton Rothschild is a separate family company. The second is treating the 1855 classification as a current quality ranking; it is a historical classification, not an annual reassessment. The third is dismissing Carruades as an inferior wine; it comes from different parcels with a higher Merlot share and has its own style. The fourth is expecting to buy the grand vin at the gate, since it is sold through en primeur and allocations rather than a cellar-door shop.

Do not walk between vine rows, touch barrels or equipment, and use the spittoon at a tasting; anyone driving should not drink. Dress neatly and comfortably, as vineyard ground is uneven and heels are impractical. Ask before photographing, and do not enter private houses or production areas. If renovation hoardings are up, do not climb over them; a construction site is not a visitor area.

## Surroundings and itineraries

Pauillac is the core of the Médoc château route: Lafite, Mouton, Latour and Duhart-Milon to the north, Saint-Julien and Margaux to the south. Two châteaux in a day is realistic; three becomes a rush. While Lafite is closed, Duhart-Milon is the natural substitute, paired with an estate in Saint-Julien. From Bordeaux, drive the D2 or take the train to Pauillac and then a taxi or bicycle. A drive past the gates of the famous estates is possible, but interior visits require appointments.

With only one day, keep Pauillac and Saint-Julien separate: Lafite or Duhart-Milon in the morning, a Saint-Julien estate in the afternoon, and back to Bordeaux in the evening. Margaux is further south, and squeezing it in makes the driving exceed the visiting time. While renovation continues, do not list Lafite as a must-see; book the substitute first and then plan the route.
