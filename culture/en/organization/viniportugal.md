# ViniPortugal: an interprofessional promoter of Portuguese wine

Portugal's wine system has a particular shape: many regions, many varieties and protected appellations managed by different commissions. Forming a single image of Portuguese wine abroad requires an organisation able to bring growing, winemaking, trade and the appellations together. ViniPortugal fills that role, and the word “interprofessional” is the key to understanding it.![ViniPortugal: an interprofessional promoter of Portuguese wine thematic illustration](/media/photos/theme-wine-spirits.jpg)

## From 1996

ViniPortugal was founded on 15 October 1996 as a private, non-profit interprofessional body. Its timing matches the stage at which Portuguese wine was preparing to address overseas markets more systematically: a single estate or a single region could hardly build national recognition in an unfamiliar market.

It did not replace the appellation commissions. The rules and administration of protected appellations remained with their own bodies, while the association set out to organise these scattered actors inside one promotional framework. That division of labour set the boundary for all its later work.

## Private and non-profit, not a regulator

Its authority comes from its members, not from legislation. Wine law, appellation rules and export administration belong to government and the relevant bodies; the association works on promotion, market information and coordination.

On 1 October 2015 the Portuguese Ministry of Agriculture recognised ViniPortugal as the wine interprofessional organisation. That recognition made its coordinating and promotional role clearer, but it remains not a government enforcement body. Calling it a body that issues certificates is inaccurate: it can organise promotion, publish information and coordinate positions, but it cannot withdraw an export licence or decide on its own who may use an appellation name.

## Nine associations and the 2026 board

Members are industry associations, not individual estates. The published list includes ACIBEV, AEVP, ANCEVE, FENAVI, FEVIPOR, FENADEGAS, AND, CAP and ANDOVI, nine associations covering trade, production, cooperatives, distillation, agriculture and protected appellations.

In March 2026 the association elected its board for 2026/2028: Frederico Falcão as president, with João Gomes da Silva (trade) and Mário Joaquim Abreu Lima (production) as vice-presidents. The arrangement of one president and two vice-presidents is itself an expression of interprofessional balance: trade and production each have a representative, and major positions must be negotiated between them. The statutes were revised in December 2024, and the details of governance should be read there.

## Brand, markets and annual action

First, the national brand. The organisation runs the Wines of Portugal brand, created in 2010, gathering the diversity of regions and varieties under one recognisable national image. Second, market action: it works on four continents and in 15 strategic markets, spending more than EUR 8 million a year, running over 100 actions and reaching more than 500 trade contacts. Third, information and education, providing material on regions, varieties, terroir and wines for overseas buyers, media and consumers. Fourth, coordination, bringing different interests together to form a shared position for promotion abroad.

These actions are framed by planning documents: a five-year strategic plan for 2024/2030, a seven-year marketing plan and an annual operational plan. Planning and statutes turn “promotion” from a set of scattered events into checkable annual tasks and let members ask where the resources went.

## Budget and market figures that can be checked

The checkable figures include: foundation on 15 October 1996; creation of the Wines of Portugal brand in 2010; recognition by the Ministry of Agriculture on 1 October 2015; election of the 2026/2028 board in March 2026; four continents, 15 strategic markets, more than EUR 8 million a year, over 100 actions and more than 500 trade contacts; and the 2024/2030 strategic plan.

These measure different things: dates, market reach, annual spending and activity volume. Each should travel with its source and year rather than being compressed into “the association runs many events”.

## Where the influence comes from

Portugal's diversity is both an advantage and a communication problem: many regions, many varieties and difficult names make it hard for an overseas buyer to form an overall impression quickly. ViniPortugal's contribution is to gather that diversity into one brand, one set of materials and one series of events.

For small producers, collective promotion lowers the cost of entering an export market; for the appellation commissions, it provides a national window abroad. The influence is indirect: it does not change the quality of a bottle, but it changes whether a buyer is willing to give Portugal a first chance.

## Limits and controversies

Representation is the first limit. Members are associations rather than companies, the nine do not always want the same thing, and the balance built into the presidency and two vice-presidencies shows that disagreement is real. Second, allocation: how an annual budget is divided between regions, varieties and markets is rarely comfortable. Third, the tension between brand and region: a national brand stresses unity, while a single appellation or variety may feel that its character is diluted. Fourth, exposure to exchange rates, tariffs, drinking habits and climate. Fifth, measurement: the effect of promotional activity is hard to attribute directly to a given year's export figures.

## Common misreadings

Taking ViniPortugal for a government regulator; taking Wines of Portugal for some estate's brand; equating Portuguese wine with Port alone; taking the organisation's promotion for a quality guarantee. All four come from one habit: confusing national promotion with legal regulation. When buying, the legal information on the label still matters, as [how to start drinking wine](ref:first-wine) explains; Portugal is also an important source of [fortified wine](ref:fortified-wine) such as Port, but non-fortified [wine](ref:wine) matters just as much.
