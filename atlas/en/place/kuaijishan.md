# Kuaijishan: yellow-wine park and brewery visit

Kuaijishan is an old name in Shaoxing yellow wine attached to a relatively new visitor park. The company is registered at 2579 Yangshao Road in Hutang Subdistrict, Keqiao District, close to Jian Lake. The site brings together a yellow-wine museum, a guided factory walk and a tasting centre, so you can read the history on a panel and then see part of the production space on the same visit. It suits anyone who wants the craft made concrete, but it requires a booking and a willingness to accept that a working factory is not a free-roaming museum.

## The company and the region

Kuaijishan Shaoxing Rice Wine Co. presents itself as a company of the Jian Lake source. Its official site speaks of taking live water from Jian Lake and using Jiangnan glutinous rice and quality wheat, with the slogan that the company has done one thing for a hundred years. It is also a national production-based transmission base for Shaoxing yellow-wine brewing technique and one of the drafters of the national standards for yellow wine and Shaoxing wine. Those designations matter: this is not a visitor centre detached from production, but a working producer that opens part of its site to the public.

The Kuaijishan brand is often dated to 1743 and appears on products such as Lanting and 1743. Visitors do not need to memorise the corporate timeline. What matters is the bond with the place: the water system of Jian Lake, the brewing tradition around Hutang, and the combination of rice and wheat qu. Setting the company inside the longer history of Shaoxing wine prevents "1743" from being mistaken for the beginning of the whole style.

## The park and the factory

The heart of the park is a yellow-wine museum. The Jian Lake tourism resort describes it as standing beside the lake in the Shili Hutang area of Keqiao, on a site of more than 10,000 square metres, in a courtyard building with carved beams, upturned eaves and a central well. The ground floor covers the origins of yellow wine and local stories; the upper floor presents Kuaijishan's brand history; the basement holds a tasting centre. A 2025 itinerary from the Shaoxing culture and tourism bureau combines the museum with a factory visit and suggests about two and a half hours.

The names here are easy to confuse. The museum inside the park is usually called the Kuaijishan Yellow Wine Museum, and some tourism pages also label it the "China Yellow Wine Museum". It is not the China Yellow Wine Museum at 557 Xia Da Road in Yuecheng District, which was invested in by Guyuelongshan and tells the general history of Chinese yellow wine. Kuaijishan's site tells the story of the Jian Lake production area and one company's craft. The two are a taxi ride apart, and one ticket does not cover both.

## Craft: wheat qu, starter and winter brewing

Shaoxing yellow wine can be remembered through five elements: rice, qu, starter, water and time. Glutinous rice supplies starch, wheat qu supplies saccharifying enzymes and aroma, wine starter starts fermentation, Jian Lake water provides the base, and time determines the depth of fermentation and ageing. Kuaijishan summarises its own process with the sequence "15179": about 15% wheat qu, steeping the rice below 10°C, two mash-turnings adding up to more than 70°C, and a slow fermentation of 90 days. This is one company's quantified account rather than an industry-wide rule, but it helps visitors attach real conditions to words such as "low temperature", "slow fermentation" and "opening the mash".

Winter brewing is the part worth understanding in cold weather. Low temperatures suppress competing microbes and slow fermentation, giving a cleaner wine with finer aromas. Traditionally the weeks around the winter solstice are the key period for starting the mash; the winemaker turns the mash to control heat and oxygen, then presses, clarifies, heats the wine and stores it in clay jars. Kuaijishan also stresses the inheritance of its wine starter and the seasonal making of starter cultures in midsummer. These claims carry a corporate narrative, but the underlying point is shared across Shaoxing: starter has a season and fermentation depends on temperature.

## Visiting

The address is 2579 Yangshao Road, Hutang Subdistrict, Keqiao District, Shaoxing. A 2025 page from the Shaoxing culture and tourism bureau gives opening hours of 08:30–11:30 and 13:00–16:30, and says bookings are made through the "Kuaijishan Yellow Wine Museum" WeChat account; groups and study visits should contact the site earlier. Hours, sessions and ticket prices change, so confirm through the account or on-site notices before travelling.

Allow two to three hours. Start in the museum with the origins of yellow wine, the Shaoxing process and the Kuaijishan brand, then follow staff into the designated factory areas to see production spaces and equipment, and finish at the tasting centre or factory shop with whatever tasting, yellow-wine ice cream or gift items are available that day. The factory is not a free-walking park: do not leave the group, open production doors or touch equipment and jars. Follow instructions in the museum galleries and interactive areas as well.

By taxi, the site is a reasonable ride from the old city or Shaoxing North Station, though journey times depend on traffic. Drivers should use the site car park rather than the shoulder of Yangshao Road. In summer the temperature difference between the factory and the cellars is noticeable, so bring a light layer; floors can be wet, so wear shoes with grip. With half a day left, you could add the Jianhu distillery in Jianhu village, also in Hutang Subdistrict; it is a separate national industrial-heritage site, not part of the Kuaijishan park.

## Manners and common mistakes

The first mistake is to conflate the Kuaijishan park with the China Yellow Wine Museum. The former is in Keqiao District, the latter in Yuecheng District; both discuss yellow wine, but the collections, investors and routes differ. The second is to treat the site as Dongpu Yellow Wine Town, another yellow-wine district in Yuecheng. Do not navigate to one place using the other's name. The third is to read "factory visit" as permission to wander through workshops; production areas have safety and hygiene rules, and visitors must stay with staff.

Taste slowly and in small sips. Yellow wine is usually 14%–20% alcohol and can catch up with you, especially on an empty stomach. Do not drive after a tasting; use a taxi or public transport and check live maps for schedules. Minors must not drink. Families with children can focus on the museum, the courtyard architecture and yellow-wine ice cream. When buying bottles, check the vintage, type and volume, and do not treat "aged" as the only measure of quality. Store wine away from light in a cool place, upright, and drink opened bottles soon.
