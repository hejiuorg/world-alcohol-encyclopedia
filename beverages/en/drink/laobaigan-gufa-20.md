# Hengshui Laobaigan Gufa 20

"Hengshui Laobaigan Gufa 20" is a laobaigan-aroma product in the Gufa series of [Hengshui Laobaigan](ref:brand). Public product material gives the common specification as 67% ABV in 500 mL, made in Hengshui, Hebei. The 67% ABV strength is the most immediate feature of the bottle, and the "20" in the name is a series number, not the age of the liquid.

## Definition and position

The Gufa series occupies the mid-to-high and business-gift tier in Hengshui Laobaigan's portfolio, above the mainstream products. Understanding this bottle means looking at the [brand](ref:brand), the [producer](ref:producer), the exact specification and the fermentation method together, not only at the words "ancient method".

"Gufa 20" is a product name, not proof of age and not a quality grade. Company information shows that Gufa 20 has been updated to its sixth generation; generational changes involve packaging, blending and product positioning, so batches may differ, and the bottle label and batch code remain the final reference.

## Producer and brand

The product is made by [Hebei Hengshui Laobaigan Co., Ltd.](ref:producer) and the brand is [Hengshui Laobaigan](ref:brand). The company's direct predecessor was the state-run Hengshui distillery formed in 1946 when the government took eighteen traditional private workshops into state ownership. It listed on the Shanghai Stock Exchange on 29 October 2002.

The company also owns Bancheng Shaoguo, Wuling and Wenwang Gongjiu, which sit in different production areas and aroma types. A multi-brand structure means the taste of Hengshui Laobaigan cannot describe the whole company; the brand answers "what is it called and who is it for", while the listed company answers "who owns the trademark and who carries legal responsibility".

## Origin and raw materials

Hengshui lies on the North China Plain in a temperate monsoon climate with four distinct seasons, suited to grain growing and open qu making. Company material traces local brewing back more than one thousand nine hundred years and summarises the line as "rising in the Han, flourishing in the Tang, named in the Ming". This is regional and brand narrative, and it should not be converted directly into the founding year of the modern company.

Public product material emphasises pure-grain brewing, with [sorghum](ref:sorghum) and wheat qu as the common raw materials; [water](ref:water) is used in qu making, fermentation and strength reduction. Sorghum provides the frame and structure, while wheat products bring qu aroma and fermentation power. The exact ratio and ingredient list are governed by the bottle label.

Pure grain is a basic premise, not a quality grade. Qu, fermentation vessel, distillation cut and storage all shape the body; treating "pure grain" as the only standard ignores process differences.

## Process

Laobaigan aroma is characterised by earthen-jar fermentation: clay jars are sunk into the ground so that saccharification and fermentation take place inside the jar, avoiding direct contact between the fermenting grain and the soil. [Qu making](ref:qu-making) supplies microbes and enzymes, and [fermentation](ref:fermentation) in a relatively clean vessel favours a clean, crisp style.

After fermentation, [distillation](ref:distillation) separates the head, middle and tail of the run, with the middle used as the main base spirit. New spirit is stored by grade and then [blended](ref:blending) to unify aroma, body and finish. Laobaigan aroma has process links to light aroma, but it is a separate aroma category and should not be equated with it.

High-strength spirit places greater demands on cutting and blending: at high alcohol, the ratio and volatility of aroma compounds change the bite and the return of sweetness. Company material emphasises "comfort after drinking"; that is a company statement, and drinkers should judge it against their own tolerance and actual experience.

## Aroma and tasting

The nose is led by grain and qu aroma with a clean, elegant alcohol note. The entry has force, the body is clean, the middle opens and the finish is crisp. At 67% ABV the alcohol is clearly assertive and the aroma releases directly, so small glasses and slow drinking suit it. Drinkers unused to high-strength baijiu should try only a little first; adding water or ice changes the structure markedly.

For Hengshui Laobaigan Gufa 20, taste in a small glass: smell the resting spirit first, then swirl gently and smell again. Let a small amount stay in the mouth and follow the order of sweet, sour, bitter and umami, along with body weight. Adding water lowers the alcohol and opens the aroma, but it also changes the balance, so add drop by drop and compare.

## Buying and storage

For Hengshui Laobaigan Gufa 20, buy through regular channels and check the anti-counterfeit mark, producer and batch. Gift boxes and channel-specific editions should be checked against the label, so that a packaging difference is not mistaken for a difference in the liquid. For a full case, also inspect the seal and storage conditions.

Unopened high-strength baijiu should be kept upright, away from light, in a cool place without strong odours. Once opened, drink it reasonably soon; prolonged exposure to air costs aroma. Alcohol evaporates faster at high strength, so sealing after opening matters even more.

## Common misconceptions

First, treating "20" as age in the bottle. It is a series number.
Second, equating laobaigan aroma with light aroma. It is a separate aroma category.
Third, treating 1946 as the founding year of the listed company. 1946 is when the state-run distillery was formed.
Fourth, assuming higher strength is better. 67% ABV serves a specific product position and drinking style.
Fifth, judging authenticity by price or packaging. Anti-counterfeit marks, channels and labels are more reliable.
