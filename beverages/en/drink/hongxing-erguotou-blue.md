# Red Star Erguotou Blue Bottle

Red Star Erguotou Blue Bottle is a mass-market light-aroma baijiu under [Red Star](ref:brand), commonly bottled at 43% ABV in 500 mL and made in Beijing. Its blue bottle and open-bottle price band are the main identifiers, and its process follows the [er-guotou](ref:erguotou) tradition of cutting the head and tail and keeping the middle.

## Definition and position

The blue bottle is a packaging and product branch within the Red Star er-guotou series, not a separate aroma type. Within Red Star's portfolio it serves everyday drinking and catering, at a price band below gift and commemorative products.

Understanding this bottle means looking at the [brand](ref:brand), the [producer](ref:producer), the exact specification and the process together, not only at the bottle colour. "Blue bottle" is a market nickname, and the formal product name is the one on the label; the series also includes a green bottle and other strengths and volumes.

## Producer and brand

The product is made by [Beijing Red Star Co., Ltd.](ref:producer) and the brand is [Red Star](ref:brand). The company's predecessor, the Huabei Liquor Monopoly Experimental Factory, was founded in May 1949 and was the first state-run distillery in Beijing; after founding it absorbed twelve old distilleries including Yuanshenghao.

Company, brand and product are three layers: the company holds the trademark and carries legal responsibility, the brand faces the market, and the product answers the exact strength, volume and batch. Confusing the three makes it easy to read company history as the age of the liquid.

## Origin and raw materials

Beijing sits at the northern end of the North China Plain, and the alluvial plains of the Chaobai and Yongding rivers provide grain and water. Er-guotou craft took shape and continued in Beijing; the old urban site was around Jianguo Road, and the main production area is in Huairou district.

Public material gives the raw-material route as red [sorghum](ref:sorghum) as the main grain, a qu made from barley and peas as the saccharifying and fermenting agent, and [water](ref:water) used in qu making, fermentation and strength reduction. Light aroma emphasises cleanliness and crispness, and the choice of grain and auxiliary materials directly affects how clean the entry feels.

An ingredient list is a style route, not a grade table. Ratios change between product lines, and the ingredient order and standard on the label are more reliable than advertising language.

## Brewing process

The basic light-aroma path is clean steaming and clean firing, solid-state fermentation, distillation with cut points, and storage in ceramic jars. Red Star's public material describes mixed steaming and mixed firing with the five-pot process, low-temperature collection and reading the foam to cut the run.

[Qu making](ref:qu-making) relies mainly on low-temperature qu, whose microbial mix shapes the aroma direction. [Fermentation](ref:fermentation) takes place in pits or vats, and cycle and temperature determine yield and flavour. [Distillation](ref:distillation) separates the head, middle and tail of the run.

New spirit is stored in ceramic jars, then [blended](ref:blending) to unify aroma and body. Blending is a technical balance, not simple addition or dilution; for light-aroma products the focus is usually steady cleanliness and crispness.

## Er-guotou and the cut

The name "er-guotou" comes from the still and the order of collection. In traditional distillation, the spirit that flows after the first change of cooling water is the "head", the spirit that flows after the second change is "er-guotou", and what follows is the "tail". Head and tail are handled separately, and the middle cut becomes the base spirit.

This practice is essentially staged cutting, using selection to control impurities and palate. It is neither mysterious nor a promise that the second pot is always better; raw materials, qu and equipment all change the result, and the craft name only describes the logic of collection.

## Specification and label

Public material gives the common specification as 43% ABV in 500 mL, with the aroma type marked light-aroma, and the same series also comes in 750 mL, 250 mL and 150 mL. The industry standard T/CBJ 2109—2020 covers the production, testing and sale of er-guotou liquor and spans 21.0% to 69.0% ABV, while each product is governed by its own label and standard.

The producer, product name, aroma type, strength, net volume, raw materials, standard and anti-counterfeit information on the label are the basic identity checks. Buy through regular channels and verify the anti-counterfeit mark and batch.

## Aroma and tasting

The nose is led by grain aroma, qu aroma and fresh ester notes. The entry is crisp, the body relatively light, the sweetness clean and the finish short. At 43% ABV this sits at the moderate-to-low end for er-guotou, softer than the high-strength versions and suited to everyday drinking with food.

For Red Star Erguotou Blue Bottle, taste in a small glass: smell the resting spirit first, then swirl gently and smell again; let the liquid stay briefly in the mouth and follow the order of sweet, bitter and crisp. Adding water lowers the alcohol and opens the aroma, but it also changes the balance, so add drop by drop and compare.

## History and market

The brand narrative traces the craft to the 1680 reform at Yuanshenghao. After the factory was founded in 1949, the first Red Star Erguotou was presented as a gift for the founding of the People's Republic; in 2008 the traditional brewing craft of Beijing er-guotou liquor entered the national intangible cultural heritage list.

The blue bottle has long sat in the core price band of open-bottle spirits and mass catering, building scale through wide distribution and a stable flavour. Prices move with channel, region and supply and demand, and this entry gives no figures. Treating price as proof of quality, or "blue bottle" as a grade, are common misreadings.

## Buying and storage

Buy through regular channels and check the anti-counterfeit mark, producer and batch. The blue bottle comes in different volumes and strengths, so check the label rather than mistaking a packaging difference for a difference in the liquid. For a full case, also inspect the seal and storage conditions.

Unopened baijiu should be kept upright, away from light, in a cool place without strong odours. Once opened, drink it reasonably soon; prolonged exposure to air costs aroma. A home cellar cannot reproduce a distillery's storage conditions.

## Common misconceptions

First, treating "er-guotou" as a brand name. It is a craft and category name.
Second, treating "blue bottle" as a quality grade. It is a packaging branch nickname.
Third, treating 43% ABV as a low-alcohol drink. It is still a moderate-strength baijiu and should be measured as one.
Fourth, treating 1949 as the start of the craft. The craft traces to 1680.
Fifth, judging authenticity by price or packaging. Anti-counterfeit marks, channels and labels are more reliable.
