# Guizhou

Guizhou lies in south-west China, dominated by highlands, mountains and karst landscapes. Its drinks identity is almost inseparable from sauce-aroma baijiu: the Chishui River basin, Maotai town in Renhuai and the hot, humid river valley form the core production area. Guizhou also has other aroma types and local drinks, but sauce-aroma baijiu is its most nationally influential calling card.

## Geography and climate

Guizhou sits in the eastern part of the Yunnan-Guizhou Plateau, higher in the west and lower in the east, with mountains and hills covering most of the province. The Chishui River flows north through the northern part, cutting deep valleys at lower elevations and creating a relatively warm, humid microclimate. Guizhou has a subtropical humid climate overall, with mild winters, hot and rainy summers, high humidity and frequent cloud and fog.

This hot, humid environment strongly affects the microbial communities in qu making and fermentation. Heat and humidity in the river valley favour the open fermentation of sauce-aroma baijiu; higher elevations are cooler and ferment at a different pace. Geography is not mysticism: it acts on the process through temperature, humidity, water and microorganisms.

## History

Guizhou's brewing traditions are linked to ethnic communities, salt transport and river trade. The Chishui River basin developed market towns and brewing foundations in the Ming and Qing periods, and Maotai town in Renhuai gradually became a major drinks centre. In the twentieth century, nationalisation and industrialisation drove large-scale sauce-aroma production; after reform and opening, branding and marketisation brought Guizhou baijiu to the national market.

## The Chishui River and Maotai town

The Chishui River is the geographic axis of Guizhou baijiu. Purple soils, limestone and suitable water on both sides of the valley provide the basic conditions for brewing; Maotai town in Renhuai is the most representative origin of sauce-aroma baijiu. Ecological protection and industrial planning in the Chishui River basin directly affect water, air quality and the brewing environment.

The saying that "you cannot make Moutai outside Maotai town" emphasises the role of origin ecology and microbial environment rather than simple geographic determinism. Other sauce-aroma regions can also develop their own styles, but differences in raw materials, climate and process produce different results.

## Raw materials and process

Sauce-aroma baijiu uses [sorghum](ref:sorghum) as the main raw material, [wheat](ref:wheat) for qu making, and [water](ref:water) from the Chishui River basin. [Qu making](ref:qu-making) takes place at high temperature, and the microorganisms and enzymes in the qu provide the basis for later fermentation. Brewing usually involves two rounds of grain addition, multiple rounds of steaming, [fermentation](ref:fermentation) and spirit extraction, followed by [distillation](ref:distillation) to obtain base spirits from different rounds.

Base spirits need long storage and [blending](ref:blending) to form a stable style. Blending is not simply adding water; it combines spirits of different rounds, vintages and typical profiles in proportion. The many process steps and long cycle are an important source of both cost and flavour in sauce-aroma baijiu.

## Aroma types and styles

Guizhou baijiu is best known for the sauce-aroma type, with aromas of sauce, roast, floral and fruit notes and baked character, a full body and a long finish. The aroma classification of [baijiu](ref:baijiu) is the basis for understanding Guizhou spirits; compared with Sichuan's [strong aroma](ref:strong-aroma), sauce aroma uses higher qu-making temperatures, more fermentation rounds and longer storage.

Guizhou also produces other aroma types and local drinks, but sauce aroma has the largest industrial scale and brand influence. Aroma type is not a quality tier but a classification of process and flavour; clear differences remain within one aroma type.

## Representative drinks and producers

[Guizhou Moutai](ref:moutai) is a representative sauce-aroma baijiu from Maotai town in Renhuai, made from sorghum, wheat and water through qu making, fermentation, distillation, storage and blending. Its brand story is closely tied to origin, process and history.

Guizhou has other sauce-aroma and other-aroma producers. For specific wines, see the wine pages; region pages describe geography, raw materials and the regulatory framework and do not judge individual brands.

## Regulation and regional protection

Guizhou baijiu production and labelling are governed by national standards and food-safety regulations. GB/T 15109-2021 defines terminology for the baijiu industry, qu making, fermentation and distillation; GB/T 17204-2021 sets the classification and terminology of alcoholic beverages. Sauce-aroma baijiu also has industry and local standards, with current texts as the reference.

Ecological protection and regional management in the Chishui River basin involve water resources, land use and environmental governance. The use of region names and geographical indications must follow the relevant rules; when reading a label, distinguish origin, aroma type, alcohol content and producer.

## Market and drinking

Guizhou baijiu is mainly sold domestically, and sauce aroma holds an important place in business, banquet and gift settings. Drinking should be moderate; sauce-aroma baijiu suits small cups and slow sipping with strongly flavoured dishes. Storage after opening, temperature and glassware affect the experience. For a specific product, origin, aroma type, alcohol content and producer on the label are the most direct information.

## Common misconceptions

First, that Guizhou only has Moutai. Guizhou has other sauce-aroma and other-aroma producers.

Second, that sauce aroma equals high strength. Aroma describes process and flavour, not alcohol content directly.

Third, that older is always better. Storage conditions and base-spirit quality matter just as much.

Fourth, that the Chishui River is the only factor. Raw materials, qu making, fermentation and blending all shape the final style.

Fifth, that sauce-aroma baijiu has no sweetness. It can have a sweet finish and mellow body from fermentation and storage, not from added sugar.
