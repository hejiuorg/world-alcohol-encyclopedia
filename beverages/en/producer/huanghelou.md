# Huanghelou Distillery Co., Ltd.

The legal entity behind Huanghelou is Wuhan Tianlong Huanghelou Distillery Co., Ltd., although the company website also calls itself "Gujing Group Huanghelou Distillery Co., Ltd." It is a baijiu producer that is mainly light-aroma but also makes strong-aroma products, with sites in Wuhan, Xianning and Suizhou. In May 2016 Anhui Gujing Gongjiu Co., Ltd. acquired 51% for RMB 816 million and became controlling shareholder; that transaction is the starting point for understanding today's governance, channels and disclosure.

## Legal entity and name boundaries

The registered name, the website's self-description and the brand name are not identical, and this is the first distinction to make when reading Huanghelou material. The registered entity is "Wuhan Tianlong Huanghelou Distillery Co., Ltd."; in group language the website writes "Gujing Group Huanghelou Distillery Co., Ltd."; "[Huanghelou](ref:brand)" is the consumer-facing brand. All three point to the same production capacity, but legal liability, equity ownership and trademark use sit at different levels.

## Ownership and current control

In May 2016 Gujing Gongjiu acquired 51% of Wuhan Tianlong Huanghelou Distillery for RMB 816 million from Wuhan Tianlong Investment Group and the individual Yan Hongye, creating goodwill. Gujing Gongjiu is listed on the main board of the Shenzhen Stock Exchange under code 000596; Huanghelou Distillery is not separately listed, and its results are consolidated into Gujing Gongjiu's financial statements.

On the public record, Gujing Gongjiu's controlling position has not changed. The remaining equity is held in a dispersed way by the original shareholder side, and public disclosure is incomplete, so this page does not guess the names or ratios. On 29 April 2026 Gujing Gongjiu published an announcement on a goodwill impairment provision, stating that goodwill from the acquisition had entered impairment testing and that a provision had been made. Control stability and operating stability are two different things.

## Reliable points in the history

Dates that can be checked one by one include: 1952, the state-run Wuhan Distillery was established; 1979, output exceeded 10000 tonnes; 1984, the product was named a "Chinese Famous Liquor" at the fourth national evaluation; 1989, it retained the title at the fifth evaluation; 2006, it obtained the "pure grain solid-state fermentation" mark; 2011, the Ministry of Commerce recognised it as a "China Time-honoured Brand"; 2017, it was recognised as a "China Well-known Trademark"; 2024, it entered the Hubei provincial intangible cultural heritage list.

The company website also traces the brand to a story from 223 AD and to 1898, when the governor-general Zhang Zhidong presented the wine to the emperor and the name "Tianchengfang" was bestowed. These are corporate narratives that explain brand imagery; they should not be treated as independent archival evidence.

## Three sites and production organisation

The company website lists three sites — Wuhan, Xianning and Suizhou — with more than 2000 employees, including more than 30 technical specialists such as national-level baijiu judges, senior engineers and senior brewing technicians. Public material does not explain which site makes which product, so it is impossible to assign a bottle to a plant from the site count alone. What can be confirmed is a multi-site production organisation rather than a single distillery.

The three-site layout reflects Hubei's market structure: Wuhan is the core consumer market, while Xianning and Suizhou provide room for expansion and storage. For readers, the origin printed on the label remains the most reliable guide.

## Product structure and aroma mix

The main series published by the company include Southern School Great Light Aroma (Nanpai Da Qingxiang), Chenxiang, Ecological Original Pulp (Shengtai Yuanjiang) and the Lou series. Southern School Great Light Aroma is linked to the century-old Hanfen brewing tradition; Chenxiang emphasises elegant aroma and a soft entry; Ecological Original Pulp targets banquets; the Lou series carries the famous-liquor image. The company also makes strong-aroma products: "Gengshang Cenglou" is labelled strong aroma, with strengths of 52%vol and 38%vol.

Making both light and strong aroma is not a contradiction. Light aroma is a historical asset and a point of difference; strong aroma serves the much larger banquet and channel demand. The two lines share sales networks but belong to different process and flavour systems. To tell which line a bottle belongs to, read the aroma type and standard on the label, not the company name.

## Channels, market and capital market

Huanghelou's home market is Hubei, focused on Wuhan and its surroundings, with banquets, gifting and group purchases as the main occasions. After the acquisition the company was folded into Gujing Gongjiu's "dual famous-liquor matrix, dual-market resonance and dual-brand drive" framework, so channel coordination and spending follow the parent's rhythm.

On the capital market, Huanghelou Distillery has no separate ticker, so investors cannot buy its equity directly. What can be observed is Gujing Gongjiu's periodic reports, related-party transactions and goodwill impairment announcements. Treating Huanghelou as an independent listed company for valuation is a common factual error.

## Governance and the limits of public information

The company website provides sites, employees, history and products; exchange announcements provide equity and goodwill information. The two have different purposes. The website stresses "revival of a famous liquor", while the announcement discloses acquisition cost and impairment: the first is brand narrative, the second is accounting fact.

Public material does not disclose full capacity, output by plant, base-liquor inventory or the minority shareholder list. When such figures appear, ask where they come from: business registration, a listed-company announcement, or a promotional article. This page does not fill in unverifiable ratios or capacity.

Mixing the two layers leads to inferences such as "a change of shareholder changes the origin" or "a famous group means a premium product". Reading them separately puts capital relations and product relations back in their own places.

## Common misconceptions

First, treating Huanghelou as an independent listed company. The listed entity is Gujing Gongjiu; Huanghelou is its controlled subsidiary.
Second, reading the 51% stake as full ownership. A substantial minority remains with the original shareholder side, and public disclosure is incomplete.
Third, treating light aroma as the company's only product. It also makes strong-aroma products; the label is the guide.
Fourth, treating a historical famous-liquor title as a current quality guarantee. The title belongs to the 1984 and 1989 evaluations.
Fifth, treating the "three sites" as three separate companies. Sites are production units, not legal entities.
