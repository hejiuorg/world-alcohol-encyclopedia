# The China Alcohol Circulation Association: from commerce to circulation

The drinks business has a production side and a circulation side. Once a distillery has made the liquor, wholesalers, wholesale markets, supermarkets, restaurants and e-commerce have to move it to the consumer. The China Alcohol Circulation Association serves that side. It began as a "commerce" association and later renamed itself for "circulation", a change that reflects where the industry's centre of gravity has moved.

## April 1995 and the 2006 rename

The association was formally established in April 1995 under the name China Liquor Commerce Association, and was approved by the Ministry of Civil Affairs as a national first-tier association. In April 2006, with the ministry's approval, it took its present name, with the English title China National Association for Liquor and Spirits Circulation.

Its legal form is a social organisation, registered with the Ministry of Civil Affairs, and its charter states that its domicile is Beijing. The office address published on its website is the fourth floor of Tower C, Kaixuan Building, Xicheng District, Beijing. It is neither an administrative organ nor a liquor monopoly authority: it can write self-disciplinary rules, organise recognition and training, but it cannot enforce the law instead of the market regulator.

## Membership and the eighth council

Members include liquor producers, circulation enterprises, wholesale markets, supermarkets and research institutes, covering production, distribution, delivery and research.

In governance, it has a general assembly, a council, a standing council and a supervisory board. On 4 December 2025 the second meeting of the eighth council was held at the Beijing International Conference Center, approving new council and standing-council members, a press spokesperson, activity plans and the supervisory board report; President Qin Shuyao presented the 2025 council work report. The association has also held the third meeting of the eighth standing council. It also runs branch bodies.

## Self-discipline, information, training and recognition

The first function is to communicate the national rules on liquor circulation and industry policy, and to promote honest self-discipline among firms. It presents itself as a bridge: it explains policy to companies and condenses industry problems for the authorities.

The second is research and information. It studies liquor circulation, distributes production, sales and market information, and runs industry summits. The third is training, including marketing skills and talent development. The fourth is international exchange, connecting members with overseas counterparts and supporting the internationalisation of Chinese liquor.

The fifth is recognition and campaigns. In 2014 the association launched the national assured-liquor demonstration store programme; it also recognises "assured liquor project units", organised by a working committee with its office in the association secretariat. It also runs the "hundred cities, ten thousand stores" autumn consumption campaign, under the guidance of the circulation industry promotion centre of the Ministry of Commerce, working with local associations and retail outlets.

## Figures disclosed at the thirtieth anniversary

On 5 December 2025 the association held its thirtieth anniversary event in Beijing. A Ministry of Commerce report gives two sets of figures: since the demonstration store programme began in 2014, nearly 2000 stores have been recognised nationwide, covering 31 provincial-level regions; and the autumn consumption campaign covered 31 provinces, more than 200 key cities and drove more than RMB 1000 hundred million yuan, about RMB 100 billion, in consumption.

Both figures show where the association's leverage lies: demonstration stores are nodes in the retail network, and the campaign is a concentrated push at the point of sale. They are not a full census of the liquor market, only the reach of the association and its member network. Citations should keep that qualifier.

## Where circulation sits in Chinese drinks

The traditional story of Chinese liquor focuses on production: region, aroma type, craft, vintage. Yet a distillery's cash flow, inventory and price order are often decided on the circulation side. Distributors pay in advance, wholesale markets turn stock, supermarkets and restaurants decide shelf space, and e-commerce campaigns move volume. These steps decide whether the liquor leaves the warehouse for the table.

The association's membership and activities target exactly those steps. It brings producers, wholesale markets, supermarkets and research units into one body and tries to lower transaction costs through information and self-discipline. Anyone studying the drinks business of [China](ref:china) needs to look at channels as well as distilleries; a consumer can start with the basic categories of [baijiu](ref:baijiu) and then read the practical advice in [first tasting](ref:first-tasting).

## The limits of an association's recognition

A "national assured-liquor demonstration store" sounds like a government plaque, but it is an industry recognition led by the association, not an administrative licence. Consumers can read the plaque as a signal of channel self-discipline, but it is not a substitute for food-safety supervision. The association can set standards, organise reviews, publish lists and withdraw recognition, but it has no power to impose administrative penalties.

The second limit is data. The number of demonstration stores and the consumption figure come from the association and local associations, and are not the same as customs or statistics bureau data. The third is the industry cycle. The 2025 council report described the current phase as a "new round of adjustment", saying that consumer groups, consumer culture and digitalisation are changing production, marketing and thinking. The association can provide information and coordination, but it cannot solve inventory and price inversion for companies. The fourth is representativeness: members are mainly medium and large producers and distributors, and the concerns of corner tobacco-and-liquor shops, small restaurants and individual dealers may not be fully expressed.

## Common misreadings

- Taking the association for a government regulator. It is a social organisation; enforcement lies with the market regulator and other departments.
- Treating the demonstration store title as a permanent honour. Recognition has a term, a review and the possibility of withdrawal.
- Taking association figures as national statistics. They mostly come from association and local-association reporting.
- Taking it for a distributors' body only. Members also include producers, wholesale markets, supermarkets and research units.
- Treating the 2006 rename as a new organisation. It is the same association founded in 1995 under a new name.
