# Fu-aroma baijiu

Fu-aroma likes to introduce itself in six words: strong aroma first, light aroma mid-palate, sauce aroma last. That is not a random collage of three aroma types but a timeline in the mouth: the entry shows the fullness and pit aroma of strong aroma, the middle turns clean and crisp like light aroma, and the finish brings the scorch and age of sauce aroma. Achieving that timeline depends on using xiaoqu and daqu together, mud-pit fermentation and clean steaming with mixing.

## How to read the three phases

"Strong first" means the entry has the thickness of strong-aroma baijiu, with clear pit and grain aroma. "Light mid-palate" means the liquid opens into the cleanliness, coolness and lightness of light aroma. "Sauce last" means the aftertaste carries the scorch, age and slight sauce character of sauce aroma. These are sequential phases, not three bottles blended together.

The layering comes from the order of fermentation and distillation, not from adding spirits of different aroma types. Xiaoqu handles saccharification and early fermentation, daqu handles later aroma formation; the mud pit supplies pit aroma, and clean steaming with mixing preserves the cleanliness of the grain. Spirits from different rounds and layers are then aged and blended to produce the turn in the mouth. Reading fu-aroma as "lots of aromas" captures only half; structure is the point.

## Standard and definition

The current national standard is GB/T 10781.11-2021, Quality requirements for baijiu — Part 11: Fuyuxiangxing baijiu, published on 9 March 2021 and implemented on 1 April 2022. It defines the type as baijiu made from grain, using xiaoqu and daqu as saccharifying and fermenting agents, through mud-pit solid-state fermentation, clean steaming and mixing, ageing and blending, without direct or indirect addition of edible alcohol or colour, aroma and flavour substances not produced by its own fermentation, and having the distinctive strong-light-sauce profile.

One common misquotation needs clearing up: GB/T 22736-2008 is Geographic indication product — Jiugui liquor, not the aroma-type standard for fu-aroma. It sets the origin, materials and process for the protected Jiugui product and cannot serve as the general standard for the whole type. The general standard is GB/T 10781.11-2021.

## Xiangxi and Jiugui

The representative region is Xiangxi in Hunan, with Jiugui in Jishou as the core producer. Jiugui took part in and promoted the creation of the fu-aroma standard, lifting a local process into a national standard category. Xiangxi's humid climate and distinct seasons suit the culture of xiaoqu and daqu and provide a stable environment for mud-pit fermentation.

Region and brand must be kept apart. Jiugui is a geographic indication product protected by GB/T 22736-2008; fu-aroma is a wider aroma category, and other distilleries can in principle make it under GB/T 10781.11-2021. Equating fu-aroma with Jiugui confuses an aroma standard with a geographic indication product standard.

## The multi-grain recipe

Fu-aroma usually uses a multi-grain recipe, commonly sorghum, rice, glutinous rice, wheat and maize. Sorghum gives structure and tannin, rice gives cleanliness, glutinous rice adds softness, wheat supports aroma, and maize brings sweetness. More grains is not automatically better; the ratio sets body and aroma direction, and grinding, wetting temperature and steaming also shape later saccharification and fermentation.

Compared with single-grain light aroma, multi-grain fu-aroma is more composite in aroma; compared with strong aroma, its grain note is more forward and its pit aroma does not try to dominate. The variety of materials supplies the basis for the three phases: different starches, proteins and fats produce different acids, esters, alcohols and aldehydes, which become layers in the mouth.

## Xiaoqu saccharification, daqu fermentation

The key process is "xiaoqu saccharifies, daqu ferments". Xiaoqu is dominated by Rhizopus and yeast, with strong saccharifying power that quickly turns grain starch into sugar; daqu is cultured at high or medium-high temperature, enriching bacteria, moulds and yeasts that produce esters, acids and aroma precursors. The two act in sequence, safeguarding both yield and aromatic complexity.

This differs from strong aroma, which relies mainly on daqu, light aroma, which relies mainly on low-temperature daqu, and rice aroma, which relies mainly on xiaoqu. Using xiaoqu and daqu together is not simple mixing: xiaoqu first builds the sugar and fermentation base, then daqu drives aroma formation, and the ratio, inoculation order and temperature control decide the style. An imbalance can bring overly fast saccharification, violent fermentation or coarse aroma.

## Mud pits and clean steaming with mixing

The fermentation vessel is a mud pit, see [fermentation](ref:fermentation). Pit mud is rich in acetic-acid bacteria, butyric-acid bacteria and other microbes that produce ethyl hexanoate and other strong-aroma markers, providing the basis for "strong first". Pit age, mud maintenance, entry moisture and temperature all change pit aroma. New pits are thin, old pits are full, and old pits still need continuous care or the mud degrades.

"Clean steaming and mixing" is the other key. Grain is steamed clean first to remove off-notes and keep grain aroma; fermented mash is then mixed with the steamed grain in proportion before entering the pit or the still. This keeps the cleanliness of light aroma while giving strong-aroma pit character somewhere to land. Compared with the clean steaming and clean distillation of light aroma, fu-aroma adds a mixing step; compared with the mixed steaming and mixed distillation of strong aroma, it stresses cleaner grain.

## Ageing, blending and key parameters

New spirit is aged in clay jars or stainless steel tanks so sulphides escape, the body softens and aromas integrate. Blending selects spirits of different rounds, pits and years to hit the strong-light-sauce target; a little old spirit may lift aged aroma, while younger spirit keeps freshness. Blending is not concealment but the organisation of different strengths.

Key parameters: current standard GB/T 10781.11-2021; grain materials; xiaoqu and daqu as saccharifying and fermenting agents; mud-pit fermentation; solid-state fermentation, clean steaming and mixing, ageing and blending; 25.0%–68.0% ABV. The standard lists physical-chemical requirements separately for high-strength and low-strength spirit, with the exact figures in the standard text.

## Boundaries with strong, light, sauce and Jian

The difference from strong aroma lies in the combination of koji and pit. Strong aroma centres on medium-high-temperature daqu and old mud pits, with ethyl hexanoate prominent and pit aroma throughout; fu-aroma uses xiaoqu and daqu together, with pit aroma serving the "strong first" phase, the middle turning light and the end turning sauce-like. The difference from light aroma lies in koji and vessel. Light aroma uses low-temperature daqu, earthen jars and clean steaming and distillation, with ethyl acetate dominant and a direct, clean profile; fu-aroma uses mud pits and xiaoqu, giving a more composite aroma.

The difference from sauce aroma lies in process and rounds. Sauce aroma centres on high-temperature daqu, stone-walled pits with mud floors and multiple fermentation rounds, with sauce and scorch throughout; fu-aroma's sauce note appears only at the end, and its main body is the transition between strong and light. The difference from Jian aroma lies in structure. Jian aroma usually means a strong-sauce combination with a two-part strong start and sauce finish; fu-aroma is a three-part strong-light-sauce structure and explicitly requires xiaoqu plus daqu, mud-pit solid-state fermentation and clean steaming with mixing.

## Common misconceptions

First, that fu-aroma blends strong, light and sauce spirits. It is one process layered over time, not a mixture of three types. Second, that GB/T 22736-2008 is the fu-aroma standard. It is the geographic indication standard for Jiugui. Third, that more grains is always better. Ratio and process are what matter. Fourth, that xiaoqu can only make rice aroma. Xiaoqu can also drive saccharification in fu-aroma. Fifth, that the three-phase phrase is marketing. The standard definition states the style requirement. Sixth, that Jiugui equals all fu-aroma. Brand and aroma category are not the same thing.

For Fu-aroma baijiu, begin with the production rule, then the strength, and finish with the usual serving; that order keeps the account grounded.
