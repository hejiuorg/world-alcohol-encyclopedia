# Varieties

- [Aglianico](aglianico.md) — A late-ripening red grape of southern Italy with deep colour and high tannin and acidity, best known from Taurasi in Campania and the volcanic Vulture zone in Basilicata, and built for long ageing.
- [Albariño](albarino.md) — A white grape of Spain's Atlantic northwest, known as Alvarinho in Portugal, with thick skins, high acidity and aromas of citrus, white flowers and sea air, centred on Rías Baixas and Monção e Melgaço.
- [Assyrtiko](assyrtiko.md) — A white grape from Santorini in Greece that keeps high acidity in wind, drought and volcanic soil, giving dry wines of lemon, flint and sea salt, with Vinsanto as its sweet counterpart.
- [Barbera](barbera.md) — A red grape of Piedmont in Italy with high acidity, medium tannin and deep colour, heat-tolerant and steady in yield, often contrasted with Nebbiolo as high acid against high tannin. Barbera d'Asti became a DOCG in 2008 with a minimum of 90% Barbera.

- [Cabernet Franc](cabernet-franc.md) — A red grape variety from Bordeaux and the Loire Valley in France, with thinner skins than Cabernet Sauvignon and earlier ripening, moderate tannin and expressive aroma, known for raspberry, violet, graphite and green pepper notes. It is one of the parents of Cabernet Sauvignon and is used both as a varietal wine and in blends on Bordeaux's Right Bank and in the Loire.
- [Cabernet Sauvignon](cabernet-sauvignon.md) — A major red grape variety with small berries, thick skins and late ripening; it has high tannin and colour potential and originated in Bordeaux, now planted across the world's main wine regions.

- [Chardonnay](chardonnay.md) — A major white grape variety with a neutral aroma and high adaptability; used from Burgundy dry whites to Champagne sparkling wines, with style largely determined by region and winemaking.
- [Chenin Blanc](chenin-blanc.md) — A white grape native to the Loire Valley, thin-skinned, high in acidity and late-ripening, prone to botrytis and able to make dry, off-dry, sweet and sparkling wine. It shows quince, apple, honey and chamomile, and DNA work indicates it is a cross of Savagnin and an unknown variety; in South Africa it is called Steen.

Thin-skinned with moderate acidity, it shows green apple, pear and white flowers and is the core of Prosecco, called Serprino around the Euganean Hills.
