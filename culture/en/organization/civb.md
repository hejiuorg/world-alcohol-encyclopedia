# The Bordeaux wine trade council: growers and merchants at one table

Bordeaux is often told as a story of independent châteaux, yet the region runs on another set of institutions: growers and merchants sitting at one table to negotiate prices, promotion and rules. The CIVB is that table. What makes it unusual is not its size but the fact that it was created by statute while holding no enforcement power.

## From a law of 1948

The CIVB was founded on 18 August 1948 under a French law reorganising the wine trade after the war. Production had to be restored, prices stabilised and exports rebuilt, and neither growers nor merchants could manage that alone, so an interprofessional body was written into legislation. It is not a club of estates that chose to join; it has a legal foundation.

That legal identity has been adjusted since. Decree 2016-915 of 4 July 2016 changed its organisation and operation, and an order of 3 June 2014 recognised it as an interprofessional organisation. These dates matter because they show that the CIVB's shape is set by public law rather than by its own membership rules.

Its address can also be checked: 1 cours du XXX Juillet, 33075 Bordeaux Cedex. The address is not the point; the point is that it has long sat in central Bordeaux, close to the chamber of commerce, the regional bodies and the large merchants.

## Interprofessional is not the same as regulatory

“Interprofessional” means placing different stages of the chain inside one body. CIVB members come from both the grower and the merchant side, so it speaks not for one stage but for the shared conditions between them: purchase prices, quality requirements and how the cost of promotion is shared.

This differs from the role of the INAO, the French national institute for origin and quality. AOC rules are made and recognised by the INAO, and the legal system decides which parcel may carry which name. The CIVB can help the trade understand the rules and the market recognise the region, but it cannot grant or withdraw AOC status. Confusing the two is the commonest error in reading Bordeaux material.

## Members, a rotating presidency and decisions

Members are not individual estates but Bordeaux's grower and merchant organisations. That structure shapes how decisions are made: the CIVB is not a company and cannot be run by a majority shareholder, so major positions must be negotiated between the two camps.

The presidency alternates between growers and merchants, with a three-year term. This arrangement is not ceremonial but a balancing device: if one side held the chair for long, the other would suspect the body of speaking only for its rival. Rotation lets each camp preside in turn and forces every team to answer the other side's core concerns.

Day-to-day work is carried out by a permanent staff, while major direction is set by the assembly and council. Public material describes the internal committees in different ways, so this page gives no fixed number; the current statutes and official notices are the place to look.

## Promotion, data and technical output

The first output is promotion. The CIVB communicates Bordeaux wines in different markets, running events, media work and consumer education. Much of the Bordeaux material a consumer meets may involve it.

The second is market data. The council gathers sales, export and consumption information so the trade can judge trends. Such data does not decide an estate's business, but it shapes the strategy of the whole region.

The third is technical and sustainability work. The council supports research on growing and winemaking and promotes reduced pesticide use, climate response and water management. Findings have to pass through trials and extension before they become a grower's habit.

The fourth is coordination, acting as a channel between growers and merchants and between the trade and government. Its voice carries weight, but that weight comes from member authorisation, not from administrative power.

## Three-year agreements and how they become rules

A key tool of an interprofessional body is the agreement. The agreements the CIVB promotes usually run in three-year cycles and cover producer–buyer relations, quality and promotion; when an agreement expires, a ministerial order can extend its scope. An order of 30 July 2024 is one such extension.

This step matters. The agreement itself is the product of negotiation; only after extension does it acquire wider binding force. In other words, CIVB “rules” must pass through a government procedure before they land. Keeping that chain in view is how a reader separates industry consensus from legal obligation.

## Dates and an address that can be checked

The checkable points are: creation by law on 18 August 1948; recognition as an interprofessional organisation on 3 June 2014; Decree 2016-915 of 4 July 2016 amending its organisation; the order of 30 July 2024 extending an interprofessional agreement; and a headquarters at 1 cours du XXX Juillet, 33075 Bordeaux Cedex. The presidency rotates every three years between growers and merchants.

These items are not the same kind of fact: some are legal dates, one is an administrative recognition, one is an address. Each should travel with its source rather than being compressed into “founded in 1948”. For membership totals and budget size, public material offers no stable figure, so none is given here.

## Limits and controversies

Representation is the first limit. Because members join through trade organisations, a small estate and a large merchant house may not carry equal weight. The second is the tension of interest: growers want higher purchase prices, merchants want lower costs, and the council's position can only be a compromise. The third is the boundary of function: promotion and coordination are easily mistaken for certification and enforcement, when AOC matters belong to the INAO. The fourth is exposure: exchange rates, tariffs, drinking habits and climate are beyond its control. The fifth is transparency: interprofessional negotiation is not fully public, so outsiders struggle to see how a position was reached.

These tensions do not disappear. They are a reminder that when “the Bordeaux trade body says” appears, the first questions should be whom it represents and through what procedure.

## Common misreadings

Taking the CIVB for an AOC enforcement agency; taking its promotion for a quality guarantee; reading “interprofessional” as “only the big merchants”; treating Bordeaux as a single style. All four share one habit: mistaking a region's organisational capacity for authority over every bottle. When buying, the legal information on the label still matters, as [how to read a label](ref:read-a-label) explains; to understand differences inside the region, start with [terroir](ref:terroir).
