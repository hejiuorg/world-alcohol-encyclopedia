# Feng Zikai

Feng Zikai (1898-1975) was a native of Chongde in Zhejiang, now Tongxiang. He was a painter, essayist and translator and one of the founders of modern Chinese cartoon art. Neither his drawings nor his prose aim at the grand; they find human warmth in a teacup, a plate of small dishes or a night drink. In his essays wine is rarely the lead, but it is what loosens people and makes conversation flow.

## Life

He was born on 9 November 1898 in Shimenwan, Chongde county. In 1914 he entered the Zhejiang Provincial First Normal School, studying painting, music and literature under Li Shutong, later Master Hongyi. In 1921 he went to Japan, encountering Japanese cartoons and European art. In 1925 he published his first cartoon collection, Zikai's Cartoons; in 1931 came Random Notes from Yuanyuan Hall. He was displaced during the war against Japan and died on 15 September 1975.

His work spans painting, prose and translation, and this breadth keeps his wine writing close to ordinary hospitality.

## Relationship with wine

Feng Zikai writes wine with the focus on people. A friend comes, a pot is warmed, conversation grows and the lamp stays lit. Wine slows talk down rather than knocking people out. He seldom writes about brands or vintages; he cares more about expressions, gestures and relationships.

This matches his drawing: simple lines and restrained feeling. Quarrels after drinking are passed over; he prefers to remember the goodwill around the table, a choice to make ordinary life worth looking at.

## The Fine Wine of Shaping: textual evidence

The Fine Wine of Shaping describes his life in Shapingba, Chongqing, during the war against Japan. Guangxi's "mountain flower" and Guizhou's Maotai both contain a large amount of alcohol, and he will not drink them. After moving from Zunyi to Shapingba, he resumed an evening drink, and what he drank was "Yu wine", a yellow wine imitation made by Chongqing people.

His explanation is plain: he prefers yellow wine because strong spirit makes him drunk easily. He adds that drinking in order to get drunk is like lending money in order to earn interest. Wine here is not a symbol of heroism but a little relaxation in the evening, a measure of self-control in wartime.

The essay ends with a sentence often quoted: among the fine wines of the world, none surpasses the Yu wine made by the people of Shapingba. Not a technical ranking; circumstances, companions and mood form part of the taste. The line cannot be read apart from wartime inflation and displaced life.

## Drinking and A Night Drink by the Lake

Drinking describes a shrimp fisherman he met by West Lake in Hangzhou: fishing for shrimp, warming wine, chatting. At the end he says that when writing the sketch he had just recovered from a long illness and had "opened the wine taboo" again, closing with an old poem: once, when ill, he disliked the wine cup; now he fears the cup will be too shallow.

A Night Drink by the Lake describes four friends visiting West Lake in spring and drinking in his lakeside cottage. After the guests leave, the moon is bright and the lake is like a mirror. The essay is about the reluctance to let the spring moon go; wine makes the gathering happen, moonlight gives it an aftertaste.

The three essays show an evening drink in wartime, reopening after illness and a night drink with friends; wine's function changes, but drunkenness is never the goal.

## The wine table in his drawings

His cartoons often show wine: one person facing a pot, two people across a table, a few dishes. The faces are not exaggerated, yet the relationship and mood are visible. Wine pots, cups, dishes and lamplight are worth drawing; once drawn, the day takes shape.

For him, [wine and painting](ref:wine-and-painting) is not the routine of the literati gathering but the entry of daily life into art.

## The era and its wine

In Republican-era Jiangnan, [yellow wine](ref:yellow-wine) was an everyday drink, warmed and served with small dishes; foreign and strong spirits appeared in cities, but ordinary households still drank local wine in [China](ref:china).

During the war against Japan, supplies were tight and the quality of wine unstable. The Fine Wine of Shaping mentions Yu wine, but also the food served with it and the prices, so "fine wine" becomes a record of living conditions rather than a judgement of taste.

## Reputation and controversy

Feng Zikai's essays have long been used in school textbooks, and readers easily reduce him to a painter of "peaceful days". Yet The Fine Wine of Shaping is about displacement and scarcity, Drinking about reopening wine after illness, and A Night Drink by the Lake about meeting and parting. There is bitterness in these texts, only expressed with restraint.

A limitation is that his essays are literary writing, with selection and stylisation, and cannot be read as a drinking diary. "Yuanyuan Hall" involves a time lag: the collection was published by Kaiming Book Company in 1931, while the house in Shimenwan was completed only in 1933. The name came before the building, so the essays should not all be assumed to have been written there.

## Common misreadings

First, treating Feng Zikai as a painter only of "peaceful days". He lived through war and upheaval, and his writing contains a restrained bitterness.

Second, taking the drinking in his essays as a record of his habits. Literary writing selects and stylises and cannot be read as a diary.

Third, assuming that Random Notes from Yuanyuan Hall was written after the house was built. The collection appeared in 1931 and the house was completed in 1933.

Fourth, equating the wine of Republican Jiangnan with today's strong spirits. The everyday drink was mainly low-alcohol fermented wine; strong spirit was a different market.

## How to read his wine writing

Establish when and where an essay was written; then ask what wine is doing in it, whether warming, hospitality, reopening after illness or farewell. Finally compare essay and cartoon to see what the text omits and the drawing supplies. Wine then stops being a label of literary elegance and becomes a detail of ordinary life.

**In brief:** A modern Chinese painter, essayist and translator, 1898 to 1975, from Chongde in Zhejiang and a student of Master Hongyi. Essays such as The Fine Wine of Shaping, Drinking and A Night Drink by the Lake turn warmed wine, small dishes and late conversation into evidence of ordinary life.
