# Carlsberg: Beer, a Laboratory and a Garden

In Valby, south-west of central Copenhagen, an old industrial district rises gently around the Carlsberg brewery. The address is Gamle Carlsberg Vej 11, and the site now opens as Home of Carlsberg; the former Visit Carlsberg name and old links lead here. It is not an empty shell: exhibition, cellars, stables, garden and bar are in use, although mass production moved elsewhere long ago. Beer and laboratory belong together: much of Carlsberg's influence came from the latter.

## The old brewery on Valby hill

The site lies about 3.5 kilometres west of the centre, on slightly raised ground, with old buildings stepping up the slope. A nineteenth-century brewery needed water, rail and space, and Valby offered all three; today it is twenty minutes by bike, or an S-train to Valby plus a short walk. Housing, schools and new development surround it, leaving an industrial island inside the city.

The entrance sits within the old buildings, past a courtyard and ticket area. Brick walls, pitched roofs and industrial windows survive, while parts of the interior have become galleries, restaurants and shops; the stables and old garage still hold brewery horses and vintage cars. Reading it as a brewery district explains its scale: it was once a production town, now a cultural campus you can walk through.

## 1847: a family and a city

J.C. Jacobsen founded Carlsberg in 1847. He built the brewery at Valby and named the beer after his son Carl, so "Carlsberg" means "Carl's hill". From the start the company emphasised technology and quality: Jacobsen adopted cool-fermented lager methods, insisted on good raw materials and pushed brewing from experience toward a repeatable process.

Later in the century, Carlsberg split into Old and New Carlsberg, two branches of the same family whose rivalry and cooperation eventually merged into the modern company. The exhibition covers these relationships without every detail; the point is why a brewery was built like an institution. It had laboratories, gardens, housing and public buildings because its owner treated beer as part science, part craft and part civic culture.

## How the laboratory changed beer

The Carlsberg Laboratory was founded in 1875, initially to serve brewing research. In 1883 Emil Christian Hansen developed pure yeast culture and propagation there, letting a brewery work with a single strain and reduce contamination and off-flavours; the technique spread worldwide. Without pure yeast, the cleanliness and consistency of modern lager would be hard to achieve.

In 1909 S.P.L. Sørensen introduced the pH scale at the same laboratory, describing acidity in measurable terms. pH matters throughout brewing: mashing, fermentation, yeast activity, proteins and hop bitterness all respond to acidity. Put pH and pure yeast together and Carlsberg's contribution looks less like a single beer than like making brewing measurable and reproducible. Interactive displays explain them without chemistry.

## Exhibition, cellars and stables

The main exhibition covers about 3000 square metres in several strands: the Jacobsen family, scientific discoveries, brand and advertising, and beer itself. Objects, film and screens are mixed at a quick pace; with limited time, see the laboratory and old brewing equipment first, then brand history. A historic tour can be added for the old brewery area, usually in English, with times and prices online.

The old cellars host tastings in low light and cool air, with several styles on the table. Sessions usually compare lager, specialty beers and alcohol-free options, focusing on aroma, bitterness and body rather than dates. The stables house brewery horses, with vintage cars nearby; the courtyard and sculpture garden are places to rest, and the bar serves draught beer and simple food. Families can treat it as a half-day attraction.

## How to drink Carlsberg lager

Carlsberg's core product is a Danish-style pilsner lager: pale gold, clear, bottom-fermented, matured cold, clean on entry, mildly bitter and dry at the finish. Its aroma comes from light malt sweetness and a little fermentation ester, while hops provide bitterness and refreshment rather than a powerful aromatic punch. Serve it cold with fine foam, alongside open sandwiches, fried fish or simple home cooking.

In style terms it belongs to the [lager](ref:lager) family, not to every pale beer. Malted [barley](ref:barley) supplies sugar and colour, [yeast](ref:yeast) determines whether fermentation is clean, [fermentation](ref:fermentation) shapes aroma, and [original gravity](ref:original-gravity) affects body and alcohol potential. For a wider framework, return to [beer](ref:beer). Denmark has far more than one big brand, but this entry concerns only what the old brewery can show.

## Visiting: route and practical notes

The address is Gamle Carlsberg Vej 11, 1799 Copenhagen V. Regular opening is from about 10:00 daily, with longer hours on Friday and Saturday; check the official ticket page. Exhibition, historic tour and tasting can be bought separately or combined. Book online at weekends and in high season; the exhibition takes one and a half to two hours, longer with a tour or tasting.

From Copenhagen Central, take the S-train to Valby and walk about ten minutes; cycling and buses also work. Wear comfortable shoes, and bring a light layer for the cold cellars. The legal drinking age in Denmark is 18, and tastings may check ID; families can visit under on-site rules. Home of Carlsberg is a brand-run visitor project, not a working mass-production line: it tells family, science and brand history well, but from a selected viewpoint. For production, visit a working brewery. Tickets follow official channels.
