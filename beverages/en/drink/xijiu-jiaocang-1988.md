# Xijiu Jiaocang 1988

Xijiu Jiaocang 1988 is a core sauce-aroma product from Guizhou Xijiu, commonly 53% ABV in 500 mL and made in Xijiu town, Xishui county, Guizhou. It belongs to the sauce-aroma route of [baijiu](ref:baijiu) and sits in the upper-mid price band. The "1988" in the name points to a company milestone; it is not an age statement and does not describe the vintage of the spirit in the bottle.

## Definition and position

"Xijiu Jiaocang 1988" is an official product name, not a description of spirit matured in a particular year. It is produced by [Guizhou Xijiu](ref:producer) and carried by the [Xijiu brand](ref:brand); the line also has different editions and packaging. When discussing price and quality, separate the brand, the product, the edition and the batch.

The product sits in the upper-mid price band and is one of Xijiu's widely distributed core products. It is neither an entry-level bottle nor a limited commemorative release. Different packaging editions may appear under the same name, and whether the spirit and specification are identical should be checked against the current label and company information rather than appearance.

## Producer and brand

The producer is Guizhou Xijiu Co., Ltd., based in Xijiu town, Xishui county, Zunyi, Guizhou. Public company history traces the site to a Yin family workshop of the Ming Wanli era; in 1952 it was assembled into a state enterprise through acquisition, in 1998 it joined the Moutai group, and in 2022 it was converted into a joint-stock company. The joint-stock company, the holding group, the brand and the product are different layers with different responsible parties and data scopes.

At brand level, "Xijiu" covers the Junpin, Jiaocang and Jinzuan series. Jiaocang 1988 is one product within that structure, and its performance should not be read as the position of the whole brand. When you see "Xijiu price" or "Xijiu vintage", first establish which layer and which product are meant.

## Origin and raw materials

Xijiu town lies in northern [Guizhou](ref:guizhou) on the middle reaches of the Chishui River, with the plant near the Erlangtan crossing. The valley is deeply cut, hot in summer and humid, conditions that suit open qu making and multi-round pile fermentation. Local water, purple-red sandstone strata and the valley's microbial environment are treated as external conditions for the sauce-aroma style.

The main raw material is sorghum, with wheat used to make high-temperature qu. [Sorghum](ref:sorghum) must survive repeated steaming, and glutinous sorghum with a high proportion of amylopectin is better suited; wheat supplies enzymes and aroma precursors during qu making. The ratio and the qu culture are company processes, and the ingredient list on the label is the consumer's first-hand information.

## Brewing process

The public route for sauce-aroma baijiu is usually summarised as one production cycle a year, two rounds of grain addition, and repeated steaming, fermentation and spirit drawing. Base spirit from each round differs in style, is stored separately by type and is then blended into the finished product. High-temperature qu, pile fermentation and multi-round drawing together build the base of the composite aroma.

[Qu making](ref:qu-making) sets the qu aroma and fermentation power; during [distillation](ref:distillation) the run is cut by fraction, with head and tail separated and the middle run used as the main base. New spirit is stored and then blended to unify aroma, body and finish. Blending is not simple addition, nor is it dilution; it depends on base-stock inventory and sensory judgement. Round ratios and storage periods are company information, and this page describes only the public framework.

## Specification and label

The common specification is 53% ABV in 500 mL, often six bottles to a case; some editions use four bottles or other volumes. Strength is shown as [alcohol by volume](ref:abv), where 53% ABV means 53 mL of ethanol in 100 mL of liquid at 20°C. The product standard for sauce-aroma baijiu is commonly GB/T 26760, and the bottle label governs.

The producer, product name, aroma type, strength, volume, standard and batch on the label are the basics for identity. The product name contains "1988", but the label does not normally present 1988 as an age; if the packaging uses "vintage", "cave-stored" or "aged", read those terms against the company's explanation instead of converting them into the age of the liquid.

## Flavour and tasting

Xijiu Jiaocang 1988 typically shows a pronounced sauce aroma with roasted, baked and floral-fruit notes, a soft entry, a relatively full body, an opening mid-palate and a sweet aftertaste. The 53% ABV strength is noticeable, but whether the body carries the alcohol matters more than how "hot" it feels.

Taste from a small cup: smell at rest, then gently swirl and smell again. Let the liquid stay in the mouth and note the order of sauce aroma, roasted notes and sweetness, and whether the finish is clean. Comparison with other Xijiu series is useful, but keep the same temperature, cup and approximate price band; otherwise the conclusion is unreliable. Ice or heavy dilution is not the traditional way to drink it and will flatten aroma and change structure.

## History and market

Public company milestones include the 1952 state enterprise, a national quality award silver medal in 1988, joining the Moutai group in 1998 and the joint-stock conversion in 2022. Company narratives often link the "1988" in the product name to that award, but this is naming background, not the age of the spirit.

Jiaocang 1988 has long sat in the upper-mid price band and is a common choice for banquets, business occasions and gifting. Prices change with year, channel and supply and demand, and this page gives no figures. Treating "1988" as an age, or a single year's transaction price as permanent proof of quality, are common misreadings.

## Buying and storage

Prefer official channels and check anti-counterfeit marks, producer, batch and specification. Different packaging editions should be checked against the label, and gift-box differences should not be mistaken for body differences. For full cases, check seals, production dates and storage conditions; a price far below the usual range should first raise questions about source and authenticity.

For Xijiu Jiaocang 1988, unopened baijiu should be stored upright, away from light, in a cool place without strong odours. Once opened, drink it soon; prolonged exposure loses aroma. Home storage cannot reproduce distillery conditions, so purchase year should not be equated with quality gain.

## Common misconceptions

First, treating "1988" as an age. It is part of the product name.
Second, treating Jiaocang 1988 as the whole Xijiu brand. The brand has several series.
Third, assuming higher strength is better. Strength serves product design.
Fourth, treating a packaging edition as a body upgrade. Edition differences need official explanation.
Fifth, judging authenticity from price. Anti-counterfeit checks, channels and labels are more reliable.
