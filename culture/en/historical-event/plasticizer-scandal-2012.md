# The 2012 baijiu plasticiser incident

In November 2012 a baijiu sample sent to a laboratory by journalists was found to contain 1.08 mg/kg of the plasticiser dibutyl phthalate (DBP). The company involved was Jiugui liquor of Hunan. Against the 0.3 mg/kg limit then used as a reference, the result was reported as 260% above the limit. The affair quickly became an industry-wide crisis of trust; regulators carried out checks, and firms altered production lines and packaging materials. The core problem was not one company but plastic contact materials in baijiu production and a gap in the standards.

## Background

Baijiu production uses plastic hoses, plastic buckets, seals and packaging liners. Phthalates can act as plasticisers and may migrate when they contact alcohol-containing liquids. Alcohol is an organic solvent and dissolves some plastic components more readily than water, raising migration risk. Before 2012, baijiu national standards mainly set physicochemical and hygiene indicators, with no specific limit for plasticisers.

The document most often cited was the Ministry of Health's June 2011 letter on maximum residues of phthalates in food and food additives, known as Document No. 551, which set a DBP maximum of 0.3 mg/kg. It was a notification rather than a mandatory baijiu-specific standard, and the gap made the boundary between "detected" and "above limit" hard to define.

## Course and timeline

On 19 November 2012 the media published the test result, saying that a 50-degree Jiugui sample contained 1.08 mg/kg of DBP. After the report the company's shares were suspended, and the firm issued statements. On 21 November the General Administration of Quality Supervision, Inspection and Quarantine reported on inspections, and the Hunan quality and technical supervision department required the company to find the cause and rectify it. A later report by the Hunan Provincial Institute of Product Quality Supervision and Inspection recorded a maximum DBP value of 1.04 mg/kg, different from the 1.08 mg/kg in the media test.

The timeline runs as follows: 19 November, media exposure and suspension; 21 November, regulatory notice; late November, baijiu plants nationwide begin self-checks of raw materials, pipes and packaging; December, the industry debates test methods and limits; 2013, premium baijiu enters an adjustment period; 2016, the National Food Safety Standard for the determination of phthalates in food is issued, giving testing a common basis.

## Key people and institutions

The company was Hunan Jiugui Liquor, whose product is a fuyu-aroma baijiu; although the affair began with one company, inspections covered every aroma type. A financial news outlet published the first test result. On the regulatory side were the former General Administration of Quality Supervision, Inspection and Quarantine, the Hunan quality and technical supervision bureau and the Hunan provincial testing institute.

Industry bodies were also involved. The China Alcoholic Drinks Association issued an explanation during the affair, acknowledging that plasticisers might be detected in baijiu and stressing that firms could solve the problem. On standards, the Ministry of Health, later the National Health Commission, and the standardisation administration were involved; after the 2018 institutional reform, food-production supervision passed to the State Administration for Market Regulation.

## Concrete changes for the industry

First, consumer trust. Combined with the 2013 restrictions on official consumption, the incident put pressure on both price and volume in premium baijiu, and the industry entered a period of destocking. Second, production was retrofitted. Plants replaced plastic hoses with stainless steel pipes, adopted food-grade seals and increased batch testing, raising short-term costs but reducing migration risk. Third, standards and testing. The industry pushed for test methods, risk monitoring and self-inspection, and food-contact material rules tightened. Fourth, regulation changed. Sampling, notices, recalls and rectification were combined. Fifth, perception changed. Consumers began to notice food-contact materials, an invisible risk.

## Disputes and later assessments

First, the above-limit judgment. Because there was no baijiu-specific limit, different bodies used different reference standards and reached different conclusions; Document No. 551 was a notification letter, and whether it could serve directly as an enforcement basis was debated. Second, the numerical difference. The media test gave 1.08 mg/kg and the Hunan official test 1.04 mg/kg; the gap may come from batch, sampling and method. Third, source attribution. Plasticisers may come from production lines, packaging, environmental background or transport, and attributing them to one firm or one process lacks sufficient evidence. Fourth, the media role. The test report drove regulation, but the sample was not necessarily representative and the method was not fully disclosed. Fifth, the long view. The affair is treated as a milestone in Chinese food-safety governance and also criticised as a case of "detected means harmful" amplification.

## Common misreadings

One: "Detecting a plasticiser means it is harmful." Trace detection and exceeding a safety limit are not the same; risk depends on concentration, exposure and long-term intake. Two: "All baijiu makers added plasticisers deliberately." Plasticisers mainly migrate from contact materials; they are not added to improve taste. Three: "Only Jiugui had a problem." Inspections covered the whole country, and samples from several companies contained plasticisers at different levels. Four: "The affair caused the collapse of the baijiu industry." The industry went through adjustment in 2013 to 2015, but output and consumption later recovered; what changed was concentration and the intensity of regulation. Five: "1.08 mg/kg is the official final finding." The official report recorded 1.04 mg/kg, and the two figures should be attributed separately.
