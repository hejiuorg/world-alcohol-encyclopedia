# Around the World in 50 Wine Regions: A Photo Exhibition at La Cité du Vin, 2026

La Cité du Vin in Bordeaux turned ten in 2026. As part of its anniversary programme, the museum is showing a free photography exhibition, “Around the World in 50 Wine Regions” (Le tour du monde en 50 régions viticoles), on its ground and first floors from 13 February to 31 December 2026. It is not a wine fair built around drinking, nor a salesroom for classified growths; it is an attempt to show, through photographs, how a wine region becomes a region.

## What the event is

The exhibition consists of photographs from 50 wine regions, each image chosen by one of the museum's partner regions or partner estates. The museum handed the selection to its partners for a practical reason: those regions supply the “ambassador” wines poured at the Belvédère, the Via Sensoria sensory tasting route, the workshops and the cultural events throughout the year, and they know which landscape, gesture, building or community scene represents their land. The result is not a single curator's global survey but a collection of self-portraits. Bordeaux, other French regions and wine-growing areas beyond Europe sit on the same visitor route without being ranked.

The exhibition is free and occupies public circulation space on the ground and first floors. It does not require a separate ticket for the permanent exhibition. The permanent display, roughly 3,000 square metres divided into six themes and 18 modules, asks how people grow, make, trade and drink wine; the photo exhibition narrows that question to one subject: the visual identity of a wine region.

## History and mechanics

La Cité du Vin opened on 1 June 2016. The building was designed by Anouk Legendre and Nicolas Desmazieres of the Paris studio XTU, and its form is often explained as a combination of vine tendrils, wine swirling in a glass and the eddies of the Garonne. From the start it was not a promotional office for one region but a civic cultural institution devoted to wine civilisations. That explains why the permanent display is not organised as a league table of famous estates, and why the tenth-anniversary exhibition turns its lens on “partner regions” rather than telling only the Bordeaux story.

The anniversary programme in 2026 went beyond this exhibition. The museum held an anniversary weekend from 4 to 7 June, with the official tenth birthday on 1 June. The photo exhibition runs from February to the end of the year, stretching the anniversary into a full twelve months: whenever visitors come, they can see the photographs in the public areas on the ground and first floors. The exhibition is organised by the Fondation pour la culture et les civilisations du vin, with partner regions supplying images and wines and the museum handling the display and visitor route.

The “partners choose the pictures” mechanism has advantages and costs. The advantage is that the photographs come from people who actually work the land, so the scenes tend to be specific: the aspect of a slope, a pair of harvesting hands, the corner of a fermentation hall, a village gathering. The cost is that the selection carries the warmth of a partnership and lacks an outside curator's counterpoint or challenge. Understanding this is what stops the exhibition from being read as an objective ranking of the world's wine regions.

## Taking part on site

The exhibition is free. Visitors can walk into the ground and first-floor public areas without buying a ticket for it or booking a slot. The permanent exhibition, the Belvédère and the tasting workshops still follow the museum's ticketing: in November 2026 the official price for an adult permanent-exhibition ticket was €23, with a reduced rate of €18.40, €9 for ages 6–17 and free entry under 6, including a glass of wine at the Belvédère. That price has nothing to do with this exhibition, but the two are easy to confuse, so check what you are buying.

Thirty to forty-five minutes is enough for one circuit; an hour allows time to read the captions and locate the regions on a map. Start on the ground floor to see the spread of the 50 regions, then go up to the first floor for the individual scenes. Do not look for famous estates: the organising logic is the region, not the classification of producers. Families can use the exhibition as a geography lesson, finding regions they have drunk or heard of before moving to unfamiliar ones. Other interactive displays inside the museum follow the permanent-exhibition rules.

Follow the venue's notices on site: do not touch the works or the display walls, respect the museum's photography rules, and do not bring tripods or large equipment into the circulation areas. The ground and first floors are public routes, so avoid blocking the stairs on busy weekends. The building is accessible, pushchairs can enter, and large luggage must be checked. If you only want the free exhibition, you do not need a permanent-exhibition ticket; if you want a glass at the Belvédère, you do.

## Wine and the city

La Cité du Vin stands on the Bacalan quay, part of the redevelopment of the old Bassins à Flot harbour. The area was once docks and warehouses; after urban renewal it became a mix of culture, restaurants and housing. Placing the museum here addressed both visitors and local residents. It turned wine from a subject for estates and merchants into a public cultural space a city could use every day. Ten years on, that judgement still holds: the museum does not sustain its narrative by selling famous bottles but by using exhibitions, tastings, workshops and food to put wine back into urban life.

The photo exhibition also puts Bordeaux back on a world map. The permanent display has a Bordeaux section, but it is only one of six themes; in the photo exhibition, Bordeaux is one of 50 regions. For local visitors, that can be uncomfortable; for someone new to wine, it is a useful coordinate system. You first learn that there are many differently shaped wine-growing places, then look back at why Bordeaux matters.

There is a city-level context too. Bordeaux Fête le Vin normally takes place on the Garonne quays at the end of June. But there was no 2026 edition. The official site already points to the next festival on 7–11 July 2027, coinciding with a tall-ships event. In other words, Bordeaux had no traditional riverside wine festival in 2026. This free photo exhibition fills a different need: no temporary wine stalls, no glass-ticket sales, but an indoor display that keeps the regional narrative in the city. The two are not substitutes, but both show that Bordeaux's public wine culture does not depend on a single festival.

## Disputes and limits

The first limit is the meaning of “free”. Free applies only to the ground and first-floor public spaces used by this photo exhibition. It does not cover the permanent exhibition, the Belvédère, tasting workshops or food and drink. Reading “the exhibition is free” as “La Cité du Vin is free” is the most common mistake.

The second limit is the nature of the show. The partner regions choose the images, so the exhibition has an explicit partnership and promotional dimension. It is not an independent art survey and not a quality ranking of regions. Photographs offer a visual entry point but cannot replace understanding of a region's rules, varieties, climate and market. After the exhibition, go back to the permanent display's modules on world vineyards, craft and history, or go to the region itself.

The third limit is the absence of Bordeaux Fête le Vin in 2026. This event originally corresponded to the candidate “Bordeaux Fête le Vin 2026”, but the official site has no 2026 edition and points directly to 7–11 July 2027. The page therefore uses a same-city, venue-verifiable wine event, the Cité du Vin photo exhibition, and states the substitution here. Do not read this event as a record of the riverside wine festival.

The fourth limit is time. The exhibition runs from February to 31 December, crossing the change to winter time and the holidays. Opening hours shift with the season, the venue is closed on 25 December, and there may be early closures at the end of the year. `event_status` is `upcoming` because, as of 8 November 2026, the exhibition has not yet ended, not because it has not begun.
