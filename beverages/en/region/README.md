# Regions

- [Aconcagua Valley](aconcagua.md) — The Valle del Aconcagua sub-region of Chile's Aconcagua region, covering the provinces of San Felipe de Aconcagua, Los Andes and Quillota, with legal areas including Panquehue, Quillota and Hijuelas. On Wines of Chile's variety figures the main varieties total about 1,500 hectares, led by Cabernet Sauvignon, Carmenère, Sauvignon Blanc and Syrah. The Aconcagua River runs from the Andes to the Pacific, with a warm inland valley and a large day-night range, and a cooler coastal fringe. Soils are mainly alluvial and colluvial. Reserva is not a quality grade.

- [Anhui](anhui.md) — A province in eastern China crossed by the Yangtze and Huai rivers; northern Anhui is known for strong-aroma and mixed-aroma baijiu, and Gujing Town in Bozhou is the home of Gujing Gongjiu.

- [Armagnac](armagnac.md) — A grape brandy region in Gascony, France, known for continuous distillation, single-estate bottlings and vintage releases; Bas-Armagnac, Ténarèze and Haut-Armagnac each show different styles.
- [Australia](australia.md) — A major wine-producing country in the southern hemisphere, centred on regions in the south-east and south-west; Shiraz, Riesling and Chardonnay show distinctive styles, and appellations use the GI system.
- [Baden](baden.md) — The appellation of south-west Germany, running about 400 kilometres from Tauberfranken along the Rhine plain to Lake Constance and covering about 15,100 hectares in 2025, the third largest wine region in Germany, in nine sub-zones; volcanic rock in the Kaiserstuhl, limestone in Breisgau and Tuniberg, gneiss in Markgräflerland and glacial gravel by Lake Constance create varied soils, Spätburgunder is the leading red variety and Gutedel is the local speciality of Markgräflerland.
- [Barbados](barbados.md) — A Caribbean island country and a major rum region, where molasses, blended pot and column distillation and tropical ageing produce a more balanced style; place names are not a grade.
- [Barolo](barolo.md) — The Barolo DOCG in the Langhe hills of Cuneo province, Piedmont, promoted to guaranteed designation of origin by presidential decree on 1 July 1980 and made exclusively from 100% Nebbiolo; the zone covers the entire municipalities of Barolo, Castiglione Falletto and Serralunga d'Alba plus parts of eight others, 11 in all, with vineyards between 170 and 540 metres, a minimum density of 3,500 vines per hectare, a maximum yield of 8 tonnes per hectare, a minimum natural alcohol of 12.5%, at least 18 months in wood and 38 months total ageing, 62 months for Riserva, and release on 1 January of the fourth year after harvest.
- [Barossa](barossa.md) — Wine region north-east of Adelaide, South Australia, and a GI zone containing the Barossa Valley and Eden Valley regions. Barossa Valley GI covers 578 sq km with 11,609 hectares at 112-596 metres, Shiraz about half the plantings; Eden Valley covers 591 sq km with 2,169 hectares at 219-632 metres, known for Riesling and Shiraz. Mediterranean climate, warm on the valley floor and cooler in the hills. Shiraz was planted by 1843, and the 2009 Barossa Old Vine Charter sets tiers at 35, 70, 100 and 125 years, a voluntary label not a quality grade.
- [Bavaria](bavaria.md) — A federal state in southern Germany and one of the world's most famous beer-law territories. The 1516 Reinheitsgebot limited bottom-fermented beer to water, malt, hops and yeast, but modern law leaves room for top-fermented and imported beer, so Bavarian brewing is far more varied than a single rule suggests.
- [Beaujolais](beaujolais.md) — A wine region in eastern France, west of the Saône and north of Lyon, south of Burgundy and the Mâconnais. It is mostly red wine from Gamay, divided from regional and Beaujolais-Villages up to ten village crus, with clear differences in soil and style between north and south.

- [Belgium](belgium.md) — A Western European country whose beer culture was inscribed on the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2016. Two traditions stand out — Trappist-style abbey ales, centred on monasteries in Wallonia and Flanders, and lambic, a spontaneous-fermentation tradition around Brussels and the Pajottenland.
- [Bierzo](bierzo.md) — Red-wine DO in western León, Spain, provisionally recognised on 3 June 1988 and formally confirmed on 11 November 1989. It has 2,300 hectares registered, 981 growers and 69 wineries. Mencía accounts for 70.5% of grape production and the vineyards average about 75 years old. Slate and quartzite slopes and a sheltered basin give wines with ripe red fruit, herbal notes and minerality. Bierzo Alto and Bierzo Bajo are common geographic terms, not legal sub-zones.
- [Bordeaux](bordeaux.md) — A wine region and port city in the Gironde department of south-west France; the Gironde estuary divides the left and right banks, and an oceanic climate with gravel and clay-limestone soils shapes a style centred on Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot.
- [Bozhou](bozhou.md) — A prefecture-level city in northwestern Anhui on the Guo River; Gujing Town in Qiaocheng District is the home of Gujing Gongjiu and the Gujing Wine Culture Museum.
- [Burgenland](burgenland.md) — The easternmost federal-state wine region of Austria, shaped by the hot continental Pannonian climate and covering about 11,538 hectares; known for Blaufränkisch reds, Pinot Blanc and Chardonnay from the Leitha Range, and botrytis sweet wines from the Seewinkel east of Lake Neusiedl; it holds six DACs, Mittelburgenland from 2005, Leithaberg, Eisenberg, Neusiedlersee, Rosalia from 2018 and Ruster Ausbruch from 2020.
- [Burgundy](burgundy.md) — A wine region in eastern France, west of the Saône, built on Pinot Noir and Chardonnay. From north to south it runs through Chablis, the Côte d'Or, the Côte Chalonnaise and the Mâconnais, with four tiers — regional, village, Premier Cru and Grand Cru.
- [Campbeltown](campbeltown.md) — The smallest of Scotland's five protected whisky names, legally the South Kintyre ward of Argyll and Bute; more than thirty distilleries operated in the Victorian era, while today only Springbank, Glen Scotia and Glengyle remain, with sea air, oil and light peat as common stylistic clues.

- [Casablanca Valley](casablanca-valley.md) — Legal sub-region of Chile's Aconcagua wine region, north-west of Santiago on the road to Valparaíso. It was first planted in 1982 by Concha y Toro winemaker Pablo Morandé and formally defined by Decree 464 in 1994. Pacific cold current and morning fog make it one of Chile's earliest cool-climate regions. Sauvignon Blanc, Chardonnay and Pinot Noir lead, with granite clay and frost-free higher sites as the main terroir differences. Origin, variety and vintage claims follow Chilean wine law.
- [Cava](cava.md) — The Spanish appellation for traditional-method sparkling wine, covering Penedès in Catalonia and several other authorised areas; the main varieties are Macabeo, Xarel·lo and Parellada, often with Chardonnay and Pinot Noir, and bottle ageing on the lees splits into Cava de Guarda and Cava de Guarda Superior at minimums of 9 and 18 months.
- [Central Otago](central-otago.md) — New Zealand's southernmost wine region, inland Otago around Queenstown, Cromwell and Alexandra. The 2026 vineyard report records 2,116 hectares planted and 2,045 producing, about 5% of the national total. Pinot Noir is about 80% of the vineyard and the flagship variety. A dry semi-continental climate with large summer-winter and day-night ranges, frost risk and site selection driven by mountains and lakes. Six sub-regions are Gibbston, Bannockburn, Cromwell/Lowburn/Pisa, Bendigo, Wanaka and Alexandra. The Central Otago GI was registered on 29 October 2018, and labels follow the 85% rule.
- [Chablis](chablis.md) — The northernmost white-wine region of Burgundy, in the Serein valley of the Yonne, with about 5800 ha of vines. Kimmeridgian limestone and marl rich in marine fossils, a cool climate and Chardonnay alone define it, across four tiers from Petit Chablis to Grand Cru, the latter with only 7 climats.
- [Champagne](champagne.md) — A sparkling wine appellation in northern France, centred on Reims and Épernay, with vineyards in the Montagne de Reims, the Vallée de la Marne, the Côte des Blancs and the Côte des Bar to the south. It is made from Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Pinot Meunier by the traditional method of in-bottle second fermentation, aged on the lees and labelled by sweetness level.
- [Châteauneuf-du-Pape](chateauneuf-du-pape.md) — An appellation in the Southern Rhône north of Avignon, granted AOC status in 1936. Galets roulés on the La Crau plateau, the mistral and Grenache-led blends form its backbone; the permitted list is commonly summarised as 13 varieties, but it is a permitted range rather than a fixed recipe.
- [Chianti Classico](chianti-classico.md) — A red-wine DOCG between Florence and Siena in Tuscany, with a production zone of about 71,800 hectares covering the entire municipalities of Castellina in Chianti, Gaiole in Chianti, Greve in Chianti and Radda in Chianti plus parts of Barberino Tavarnelle, Castelnuovo Berardenga, Poggibonsi and San Casciano Val di Pesa; a decree of Cosimo III fixed the boundary on 24 September 1716, 33 producers founded the protection consortium in Radda on 14 May 1924 and adopted the Black Rooster, and the appellation became an independent DOCG on 5 August 1996; Sangiovese must be at least 80%, Annata needs 12% alcohol and 12 months ageing, Riserva 12.5% and 24 months, and Gran Selezione at least 90% Sangiovese and 30 months, including 3 months in bottle.
- [Chile](chile.md) — A wine-producing country on the west coast of South America, with about 126,249 hectares of vineyards in the 2024 SAG count, of which 116,961 are wine varieties and 9,287 are for Pisco; reds are 72.5%; the origin system comes from Decree 464 of 1994, with four levels from Región Vitícola to Subregión, Zona and Área; Maipo, in the Metropolitan Region, has a Mediterranean climate, Andean alluvial soils and about 5,517 hectares of Cabernet Sauvignon; Casablanca, in Valparaíso, is cooled by Pacific fog, was first planted in 1982 and is known for Sauvignon Blanc and Chardonnay.
- [China](china.md) — The country-level node in the region hierarchy; Chinese drinks are led by baijiu and huangjiu, alongside large wine, beer and imported-spirits markets.

- [Cognac](cognac.md) — A grape brandy region in the Charente area of France, built on Ugni Blanc, double pot distillation and French oak ageing, with VS, VSOP and XO grades defined by minimum age.
- [Colchagua](colchagua.md) — Wine zone within the Rapel sub-region of Chile's Central Valley, covering Colchagua and Cardenal Caro provinces. On Wines of Chile's variety figures the main varieties total about 28,600 hectares, led by Cabernet Sauvignon at 11,928, Carmenère at 3,657 and Merlot at 3,132. A Mediterranean climate, warm and dry inland and cooler near the Pacific in the west. Soils are alluvial terraces, Andean foothill deposits and coastal granitic hills. Legal areas include San Fernando, Chimbarongo, Nancagua and Santa Cruz, and Reserva is not a quality grade.
- [Columbia Valley](columbia-valley.md) — AVA covering central and south-central Washington, south-eastern Washington and north-central Oregon, formally designated on 13 November 1984. It totals about 11 million acres (roughly 4.5 million hectares), with 60,079 acres (24,313 hectares) of vineyard on the Washington side and over 99% of the state's vinifera acreage. Annual rainfall is only 6–8 inches, so irrigation is essential. Loess over gravel and slackwater sediment sits on basalt bedrock. Cabernet Sauvignon, Chardonnay, Merlot, Riesling and Syrah lead. An AVA label requires at least 85% from the named area.
- [Constantia](constantia.md) — Ward of the Cape Town district in the Western Cape, near False Bay. Simon van der Stel was granted the land in 1685 and named it Constantia; it was divided in 1716 into Groot Constantia, Klein Constantia and Bergvliet. Rainfall is about 1,000 mm and the February mean is 20.6 C, so irrigation is rarely needed. Sauvignon Blanc and other whites are important, and Muscat de Frontignan makes the sweet Vin de Constance. Constantia is a ward, not a district; SAWIS reported the Cape Town statistical area at 2,511 hectares in 2024. Wine of Origin requires 100% origin and 85% for variety and vintage.

- [Côte de Beaune](cote-de-beaune.md) — The white-wine heart of Burgundy's southern Côte d'Or, running from Ladoix-Serrigny to Santenay, with about 5,000 hectares of vines together with the Hautes-Côtes de Beaune and 25 AOCs, including 16 village appellations, 8 grands crus and around 300 premiers crus; Chardonnay dominates the white wines, Corton is the only grand cru mainly producing red wine, and Beaune is the centre of the négociant trade and the region's auction.
- [Côte de Nuits](cote-de-nuits.md) — The red-wine heart of Burgundy's northern Côte d'Or, running from south of Dijon to Corgoloin, with about 3,000 hectares of vines together with the Hautes-Côtes de Nuits and 39 AOCs, including 14 village appellations, 24 grands crus and around 120 premiers crus; Pinot Noir accounts for about 95% of plantings, the vineyards follow a Jurassic limestone slope about 40 km long, and the grands crus include Clos de Vougeot.

- [Islay](islay.md) — A whisky island in the Inner Hebrides, about 600 square kilometres in area with a population of roughly 3200. Phenols from peat-dried malt, sea air and long maturation shape its image, yet the island also makes unpeated and lightly peated whisky, so Islay is not simply a synonym for heavy peat.
- [Italy](italy.md) — A major wine-producing country in southern Europe, with vineyards in almost all of its 20 regions; known for native grape varieties, the DOCG/DOC/IGT hierarchy and a range of styles from sparkling wine to bitter aperitifs.
- [Itata Valley](itata.md) — The Valle del Itata sub-region of Chile's Sur region, covering parts of Ñuble and Biobío, with legal areas including Chillán, Quillón, Portezuelo and Coelemu. A Mediterranean climate moderated by the Pacific, with wet winters and dry summers, and many old vines are dry-farmed. Soils are granite, volcanic deposits and schist, and the decree also defines the Secano Interior special D.O. Reserva is not a quality grade.

- [Jerez](jerez.md) — A fortified-wine region in southern Cádiz, Andalusia, Spain, comprising the Jerez-Xérès-Sherry and Manzanilla-Sanlúcar de Barrameda appellations; based on Palomino and aged in the solera and criaderas system, with biological ageing under flor and oxidative ageing producing Fino, Manzanilla, Amontillado, Oloroso and Palo Cortado.
- [Jiangsu Province](jiangsu.md) — An eastern coastal province and one of China's important yellow wine regions; Danyang sealed-jar wine uses glutinous rice and wheat qu with sealed ageing for a sweet style, and Wuxi has traditions such as Huiquan wine.

- [Jura](jura.md) — A wine region in eastern France between Burgundy and Switzerland, known for limestone and marl slopes and local varieties such as Savagnin, Poulsard and Trousseau; vin jaune and vin de paille are its signatures, and it also has appellations for red, white and sparkling wines.
- [Kagoshima](kagoshima.md) — The core honkaku shochu region of southern Kyushu, centred on Satsuma sweet-potato shochu made with white, black, or yellow koji through a two-stage mash and pot-still distillation; the prefecture has more than 80 breweries and over 2000 honkaku shochu labels, with atmospheric and vacuum distillation, kame storage, and a 25-degree product convention shaping its flavour and market.
- [Kentucky](kentucky.md) — A US state and the dominant production area for bourbon, with about 95% of global bourbon supply; known for corn-based mashbills, new charred oak and limestone water, though bourbon is a nationwide US category and Kentucky is not a legal synonym.
- [Kyoto](kyoto.md) — A prefecture-wide name for sake in Kyoto, divided into Rakuchu, Fushimi, Kyoto Tanba, Miyazu, Fukuchiyama, and Kyotango-Mineyama brewing associations; Rakuchu is represented by small breweries such as Sasaki Shuzo, founded in 1893, and Kyoto sake is closely tied to Kyoto cuisine, temple vegetarian food, and pickles.
- [Loire Valley](loire.md) — A wine region band along the Loire river in western France, stretching from Nantes to the Centre-Loire across different climates and soils, with Chenin Blanc, Sauvignon Blanc, Cabernet Franc and Gamay as core varieties and a range of dry whites, sweet wines, reds and sparkling wines.
- [Lowland](lowland.md) — One of the five protected Scotch whisky regions, lying south of a line between Dundee and Greenock; often summarised as grassy, citrusy and creamy, yet home both to old triple-distilling sites and to farm-scale newcomers, so lightness is a starting point rather than a rule.

- [Madeira](madeira.md) — A fortified-wine appellation from the Portuguese island of Madeira, known for high acidity, long ageing and a heated oxidative style; the noble varieties Sercial, Verdelho, Bual and Malvasia run from dry to sweet, Tinta Negra makes most volume wines, and ageing follows either estufagem heated tanks or canteiro natural lofts, graded at 3, 5, 10, 15 and 20 years.
- [Maipo](maipo.md) — Legal sub-region of Chile's Valle Central around Santiago, and the traditional centre of the Chilean wine industry. In 1851 Silvestre Ochagavía introduced French varieties, the accepted starting point of modern Chilean wine; Concha y Toro was founded in 1883. Peñín records 12,216 hectares, with Cabernet Sauvignon the leading red. The land runs from Andean foothills to coastal hills, with cooler Alto Maipo and ocean-influenced coastal sites. Origin and variety claims follow Decree 464 and Chilean wine law.
- [Margaret River](margaret-river.md) — Wine region at the south-west corner of Western Australia, bounded by ocean and highly isolated. The GI covers 2,137 sq km with 5,725 hectares of vineyard (5,840 hectares on the 2024 association basis), from sea level to 234 metres, and 202 mm of growing-season rain. A maritime Mediterranean climate with a small day-night range and even heat accumulation, compared by Wine Australia to Bordeaux in a dry year. Gravelly loam over granite and gneiss has low water-holding capacity. Cabernet Sauvignon, Chardonnay, Semillon and Sauvignon Blanc lead; the region makes about 2% of the national crush.
- [Margaux](margaux.md) — A red-wine AOC on the left bank of the Gironde in the southern Médoc, recognised on 10 August 1954 and covering five communes, Margaux, Cantenac, Labarde, Arsac and Soussans; the 1855 classification lists Château Margaux as a first growth and Rauzan-Ségla, Rauzan-Gassies, Durfort-Vivens, Lascombes and Brane-Cantenac as second growths, and the rules set a minimum natural alcohol of 11%, a yield of 57 hl/ha with a ceiling of 63 hl/ha.
- [Marlborough](marlborough.md) — Marlborough wine region at the north-east end of New Zealand's South Island, the country's largest. The 2026 report records 32,191 hectares planted, 73% of the national total, and 31,603 hectares producing across 1,134 vineyards. Cool but sunny, low rainfall, free-draining moderately fertile soils. Three sub-regions, Wairau Valley, Southern Valleys and Awatere Valley, differ in soil, temperature and wind. Sauvignon Blanc leads, and it opened New Zealand's export market in the 1980s. The Marlborough GI was registered in 2018, and single variety, vintage or region claims require 85%.
