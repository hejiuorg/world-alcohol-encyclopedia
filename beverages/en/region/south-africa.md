# South Africa

South Africa is the largest wine producer in Africa, with vineyards concentrated in the coastal and mountain valleys of the Western Cape, best known for Stellenbosch, Chenin Blanc and Pinotage.

Figure: South Africa. Photograph: Vineyards in the Cederberg - South Africa. Credit: Ltz Raptor, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons ([source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?curid=4647540)).

## Definition and scope

South Africa is a country-level wine region. In the 2024 SAWIS count the national wine grape vineyard covered about 86,544 hectares, 55% white and 45% red, with about 2,255 grape producers. Vineyards run from the Western Cape coast into the interior over a distance of about 800 kilometres.

Production is concentrated in the Western Cape geographical unit, which has five regions, Breede River Valley, Cape South Coast, Coastal Region, Klein Karoo and Olifants River. There are also geographical units such as Eastern Cape, Free State, KwaZulu-Natal, Limpopo, North West and Northern Cape, all smaller in volume. The country is divided into 32 districts and about 102 wards.

## History

Vines were planted at the Cape in 1655, and by the end of the seventeenth century Stellenbosch and Constantia had established wine production. In the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries wine was an important Cape export, then fluctuated with British market policy and vine disease.

In the twentieth century cooperatives and large-scale planting supplied domestic consumption, and in 1973 the Wine of Origin scheme brought origin, variety and vintage under certification. From the 1990s South Africa re-entered the international fine wine market, and old-vine Chenin, Pinotage and Bordeaux-style blends from Stellenbosch drew more attention.

Chenin Blanc comes from the Loire in France and reached the Cape with colonial settlement in the seventeenth century, where it was long called Steen. Pinotage is a local crossing, made in 1925 by Abraham Izak Perold in Stellenbosch from Pinot Noir and Cinsaut, with the offspring entering commercial planting in the 1940s.

## Geography and climate

The Western Cape has a Mediterranean climate with cool wet winters and warm dry summers, and rain falls mainly from May to August. The coast is moderated by the Benguela current and False Bay; Stellenbosch is about 20 kilometres from False Bay and has a growing-season average of about 20 degrees Celsius, close to Bordeaux.

The summer south-easterly wind is known as the Cape Doctor and brings cooling and dry air, reducing fungal pressure while sometimes causing wind damage and water stress. Annual rain is about 600–800 mm, up to 1,000 mm on coastal mountains, while the interior is clearly drier and needs irrigation.

There are more than 50 soil types, with decomposed granite on slopes, sandstone on ridges, and shale and alluvial loam in valleys. Decomposed granite drains well and holds moderate nutrients, suiting Cabernet Sauvignon and Chenin Blanc; clay and alluvium hold more water and suit varieties that need it. These differences form the basis of [terroir](ref:terroir).

## Wine of Origin

South Africa's origin system is Wine of Origin (WO), managed by SAWIS and the Wine and Spirit Board. Production areas are divided into four levels, Geographical Unit, Region, District and Ward, and only officially demarcated names may be used on a label.

A wine stating an area of origin must be made 100% from grapes harvested there, and when a variety or vintage is stated the relevant component must be at least 85%. Certified wines may carry the Wine of Origin seal. Estate Wine has stricter rules, requiring estate-grown grapes and bottling on the estate.

WO is South Africa's national origin and certification system, not the EU PDO or PGI. It emphasises traceable origin and truthful labelling and does not rank wines by body, price or sweetness.

## Stellenbosch

Stellenbosch is a District in the Coastal Region, about 45 kilometres east of Cape Town and the most concentrated district for fine wine in South Africa. In 2024 it had about 11,653 hectares of vineyards, 13.5% of the national wine grape area, and more than 200 estates and producers.

The district is shaped by the Simonsberg, Stellenboschberg, Helderberg and Jonkershoek mountains, and vineyards mostly sit on slopes at about 100–600 metres. Its eight official WO wards are Banghoek, Bottelary, Devon Valley, Jonkershoek Valley, Papegaaiberg, Polkadraai Hills, Simonsberg-Stellenbosch and Vlottenburg. Altitude, aspect and parent material differ clearly between wards, with decomposed granite around Simonsberg, a cooler Banghoek valley, and earlier ripening slopes in Bottelary and Devon Valley.

Stellenbosch is dominated by red varieties, mainly Cabernet Sauvignon, Syrah, Pinotage and Merlot, with Chenin Blanc, Sauvignon Blanc and Chardonnay the main whites. Cabernet Sauvignon usually shows blackcurrant, cedar and firm tannin and is suited to [oak ageing](ref:oak-ageing); Chenin often comes from older bush vines and ranges from fresh citrus to oak-fermented beeswax and dried fruit.

## Varieties and raw material

Chenin Blanc is the largest white variety nationally at about 15,913 hectares, 18.4% of the wine grape vineyard, followed by Sauvignon Blanc at about 10,184 hectares, 11.8%, and Colombar at about 8,960, 10.4%. Among reds, Cabernet Sauvignon covers about 8,749 hectares, 10.1%, Syrah about 8,340, 9.6%, and Pinotage about 6,605, 7.6%, followed by Chardonnay at about 6,540 and Merlot at about 5,029. The raw material is [grape](ref:grape).

Chenin Blanc is used in South Africa for dry white, sparkling and sweet wine; old vines give low yields and concentrated fruit, often with citrus, beeswax, dried grass and honey. Pinotage is deep in colour with medium tannin and typical blackberry, plum, smoke and earth notes; high yields can make it coarse, so wineries emphasise site selection and yield control. It is made as a single variety and often blended with Cabernet Sauvignon, Cinsaut or Shiraz.

## Winemaking

White grapes are gently pressed and [fermented](ref:fermentation) cool. Chenin covers a wide range, from fresh styles in stainless steel without malolactic fermentation to complex wines with oak, lees contact, partial whole-bunch pressing or amphora for texture. Sauvignon Blanc is mostly kept in stainless steel to preserve aroma.

Reds are macerated according to variety. Pinotage often gets a shorter maceration to keep fruit, while structured wines macerate longer and age in oak; Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah need longer maceration and barrel ageing. Some wineries use whole-cluster fermentation and old barrels for lighter extraction and a clearer sense of place.

## Labels and serving

The label should be checked for the WO place name, variety, [vintage](ref:vintage), [alcohol](ref:abv), volume and bottler. A wine labelled as a [single variety](ref:single-varietal) must reach the 85% variety share; blends must list their varieties under the rules. The Wine of Origin seal shows that origin, variety and vintage have been certified and is not a quality grade.

Fresh Chenin Blanc is served at about 8–10 degrees Celsius and oaked Chenin at about 10–12; Pinotage at about 15–17, and Cabernet Sauvignon and Syrah at about 16–18. Chenin suits seafood, white meat and spicy food; Pinotage suits barbecue, red meat and game. Refrigerate after opening and finish soon. Excessive drinking harms health and minors should not drink.

## Common misconceptions

First, thinking South Africa makes only Pinotage. Pinotage is about 7.6%, and Chenin Blanc, Cabernet Sauvignon, Syrah and Sauvignon Blanc cover more.
Second, taking Chenin Blanc for a native South African variety. It comes from the Loire in France and reached the Cape only in the seventeenth century.
Third, treating Stellenbosch as a ward. Stellenbosch is a District with eight wards inside it.
Fourth, reading South African labels with the EU PDO system. Wine of Origin is a national certification system that runs parallel to, but is not the same as, the EU framework.
Fifth, thinking the Wine of Origin seal means a quality grade. It certifies origin, variety and vintage and does not judge body or price.
