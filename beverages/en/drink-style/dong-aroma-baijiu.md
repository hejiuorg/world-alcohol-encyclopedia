# Dong aroma baijiu

Dong aroma baijiu, also called medicinal aroma baijiu, is one of the aroma styles in baijiu classification, represented by Dongjiu from Guizhou. Its identity comes from adding a range of herb materials to qu making, plus a process that uses both large qu and small qu and co-distils two mashes. Guizhou local standard DB52/T 550-2013, Dong aroma baijiu, sets the origin, raw materials, process and quality indices for this style.

## Definition and standard

Within [baijiu](ref:baijiu) aroma classification, Dong aroma sits alongside strong aroma, sauce aroma, light aroma and rice aroma. Terminology and classification can be checked against GB/T 15109-2021, Terminology for the baijiu industry, while product-level requirements rest on Guizhou local standard DB52/T 550-2013.

Aroma style is a classification, not a quality grade. The "medicinal" label describes the aroma type formed by qu making and fermentation. It does not mean the product has medicinal effects, nor that it is more premium or more rare than other styles.

In the standards system, DB52/T 550-2013 is a Guizhou provincial standard binding producers in the province, while GB/T 15109-2021 supplies nationally applicable terminology and classification. One governs products, the other concepts, and neither replaces the other. When buying, the standard and production-licence information on the bottle still decide.

## Regions and examples

The main producing area is Zunyi in [Guizhou](ref:guizhou), with Dongjiu as the representative product. Production is concentrated around Zunyi, where raw materials, qu types and local climate together form the style base.

A place name is not a quality guarantee. Under one aroma style, producers differ widely in material ratios, herb selection and qu-making temperature, and finished products can range from a pronounced medicinal note to something closer to strong aroma or sauce aroma. Treating Dong aroma as one fixed flavour ignores product tiers inside a company.

## Raw materials and qu

Dong aroma uses [sorghum](ref:sorghum) as its main material, uses both large qu and small qu, and adds a range of herb materials to the qu. The herbs take part in qu making and shape the microbial community and aroma precursors; they are not herbs steeped in the spirit.

See the general principles of [qu making](ref:qu-making). Using large and small qu together means two microbial and enzyme systems must work in step: small qu drives saccharification and the start of fermentation, while large qu supplies aroma precursors and heat-tolerant microbes. Wrong herb ratios or poor qu temperature control can unbalance the aroma or derail fermentation.

Origin detail matters too: Dongjiu production is concentrated around Donggongsi in Zunyi. Qu-making is divided by role: small qu uses rice and drives saccharification and alcohol production, while large qu uses wheat with herbs and builds aroma precursors. The two are cultured and used separately, and co-distillation then carries the aromatic mash's notes into the spirit; that is what using both qu types actually means.

## Process

The core process is co-distillation of two mashes: the fermented grain mash and a separately prepared aromatic mash are distilled together, so aroma passes through the grain mash and enters the spirit. This step transfers the compound aroma built in qu making and fermentation into the finished product, and it is the key difference from other aroma styles.

The process also involves [fermentation](ref:fermentation) vessel, temperature and round management, and graded collection during [distillation](ref:distillation). Co-distillation time, steam intensity and how the mash is loaded into the still all affect how much aroma is extracted. Process parameters belong to the producer, and public sources usually describe principles rather than complete settings.

## Flavour profile

Dong aroma typically shows a compound of herbal, roasted, ester and light pit aromas, entering full-bodied on a relatively thick body with a long finish. The medicinal note is closer to a combined impression of herbs and qu aroma than to the taste of any single herb.

The difficulty of a compound aroma is balance. Too much herbal character masks grain and ester notes, too little loses identity. A good Dong aroma should have clear layers and a clean finish, with the aroma families not clashing.

## Tasting and serving

Taste from a small cup, first still to judge the ratio of herbal, ester and roasted notes, then with a gentle swirl to check for clashes. On the palate, watch the balance of sweet, sour and bitter and the thickness of body, then whether the finish is coherent. Too warm amplifies alcohol and herbal notes, too cold suppresses the layers.

Dong aroma suits heavier dishes such as braised foods, grills and red-braised cooking. High-strength products should be sipped slowly from small cups, and low-strength products should be kept dark and chilled after opening and finished soon. Judge strength by the label, not by the aroma style.

## Common misconceptions

First, treating the medicinal note as a medicinal liquor. The aroma comes from qu making and fermentation, not from steeping herbs.
Second, believing Dong aroma has health benefits. It is a spirit, not a medicine, and health claims on labels should not be taken at face value.
Third, treating aroma style as a grade. Aroma style is classification, not a ranking.
Fourth, assuming more herbs is better. The herb ratio must match qu type and fermentation conditions.
Fifth, treating a local standard as a nationwide one. Product standards for Dong aroma currently rest on the Guizhou local standard.

Start with the production rule. From there, consider the strength, then the usual serving. That is how a practical judgment about Dong aroma baijiu is built.
