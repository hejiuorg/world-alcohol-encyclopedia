The Jian Lake water system is an important water source for Shaoxing wine. ## Handmade winter brewing

"Handmade winter brewing" is a product and craft statement: fermentation starts in vats at low winter temperatures, stirring is done by hand to regulate temperature and oxygen, and the wine is then pressed, pasteurised, filled into jars and aged. Slow fermentation at low temperature means a long cycle and high demands on temperature control and operator experience.

Glutinous rice supplies starch, [wheat qu](ref:qu-making) handles saccharification and part of the aroma formation, and wine starter is used to start fermentation. After [fermentation](ref:fermentation) the liquid is pressed off, pasteurised and stored in ceramic jars. Ceramic jars are slightly permeable, and the wine slowly oxidises and esterifies to form aged aroma.

"Handmade" describes a mode of operation, not a quality grade. Handmade winter brewing and mechanised production can coexist, and quality still depends on raw materials, process control and storage conditions.

Traditionally, Shaoxing wine is divided by recipe and sugar level into yuanhong, jiafan, huadiao, shanniang and xiangxue types, which differ in the water added, the proportion of extra rice and whether wine replaces water. Jiafan adds glutinous rice for a fuller body; huadiao usually means aged jiafan sold in jars; shanniang uses wine instead of water and is sweeter; xiangxue uses distilled spirit or strong liquor instead of water and is very sweet. GB/T 17946-2008 sets the geographical-indication area, raw materials and process for Shaoxing wine, with Jian Lake water, glutinous rice, wheat qu and wine starter as core elements. ## Industry position

The Shaoxing huangjiu industry is characterised by many small and medium producers and strong seasonality, and the pace of production during winter brewing differs from the rest of the year. Taipai sits within this cluster and must maintain traditional craft while facing structural issues such as regionally concentrated consumption and limited cultivation of younger drinkers.

## Common misconceptions

First, treating "handmade winter brewing" as a quality grade. It describes a mode of operation and a seasonal schedule.
Second, treating Taipai as an export-only brand. It also sells products in the domestic market.
Third, treating an age number as the age of the liquid. Age statements should be read with the company's product explanation.
Fourth, treating the geographical indication as a quality certificate. It fixes origin and process requirements.
Fifth, judging authenticity by price or packaging. Channels, anti-counterfeit marks and labels are more reliable.
