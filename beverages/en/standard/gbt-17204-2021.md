# GB/T 17204-2021 Terminology and classification of alcoholic beverages

GB/T 17204-2021 is the current Chinese national standard for terminology and classification of alcoholic beverages. It places the umbrella concept of “alcoholic beverages”, together with categories such as fermented beverages, distilled spirits and compounded beverages, inside one terminology framework for production, testing, labelling, trade and statistics. It answers how things are named and grouped; it does not directly set the quality level of a particular drink.![GB/T 17204-2021 Terminology and classification of alcoholic beverages thematic illustration](/media/photos/theme-wine-spirits.jpg)

## How to read the classification framework

Alcoholic beverages form an umbrella category for drinks that contain alcohol and are intended for consumption. The most common industry split is by production method: fermented beverages, distilled spirits and compounded beverages. Fermented beverages are made by microbial fermentation of grains, fruit or other raw materials. Distilled spirits add a distillation step after fermentation and usually have higher alcohol content. Compounded beverages use a fermented beverage or distilled spirit as a base and add other ingredients, flavourings or permitted additives.

Within this framework, [baijiu](ref:baijiu) is a distilled spirit and [wine](ref:wine) is a fermented beverage. Liqueurs and some aperitifs may fall under compounded beverages or special categories. The category of a specific product depends on its raw materials, process and label statements, not only on alcohol content or a trade name.

The value of a classification standard is that different agencies and companies use the same vocabulary. When statistics report output of baijiu, beer or wine, the boundaries must be clear. When labels use the terms fermented beverage, distilled spirit or compounded beverage, the wording should map onto the regulations. When editors write content, they should confirm the category before discussing style and quality.

## Division of labour with the baijiu terminology standard

GB/T 17204-2021 covers all alcoholic beverages and provides a horizontal terminology and classification framework. GB/T 15109-2021, Terminology of baijiu industry, is a vertical industry terminology standard for baijiu alone. They are complementary rather than substitutes. For baijiu content, one can first use GB/T 17204-2021 to confirm that baijiu is a distilled spirit, then use GB/T 15109-2021 to explain qu, fermentation pits, solid-state fermentation and other baijiu-specific concepts. For wine or other drinks, refer to the relevant product standards and regional regulations.

## Points to watch

First, the standard text is protected by copyright. This page records public metadata such as the code, title, dates and technical committee, and explains the classification logic in original words. It does not reproduce the standard. For exact clauses, consult the official full-text system or a formally published copy.

Second, classification is not a quality ranking. Fermented beverages, distilled spirits and compounded beverages differ by production method; the distinction does not imply that one category is superior. Within a single category, raw materials, region, process and ageing still create enormous variation.

Third, standards are revised. Definitions, category names and boundaries may change with regulations and markets. Give the edition year when writing or citing, and prefer the current edition.

From a content-organisation perspective, the classification framework lets different sections speak the same language. A drink-style page answers “what processes do these drinks share”; a specific-drink page answers “what is this bottle”; a region page answers “where does it come from”; a standard page answers “what document supports these names”. Each has a different job, and they link without duplicating one another. Without shared category boundaries, a style page may describe a compounded beverage as a fermented one, and a drink page may mistake a brand name for a category.

The framework also helps readers interpret label words. When a label says “fermented beverage”, “distilled spirit” or “compounded beverage”, the words usually refer to production method, not strength or taste; when it says “baijiu” or “wine”, it refers to a more specific drink and product standard. A reader who takes “compounded beverage” to mean “blended alcohol”, or “fermented beverage” to mean “low-alcohol drink”, will reach the wrong conclusion. The standard page puts these words back into their original context.

For bilingual content, category words also need stable English renderings. Fermented beverage, distilled spirit and compounded beverage are common equivalents, but different regulations may use different terms. Keep the Chinese category name and give an English explanation in brackets, rather than letting one English word cover several Chinese concepts. For a specific product's English name, follow the producer or the official label.

A common misuse is treating a classification standard as proof of product compliance. A bottle labelled “baijiu” does not automatically meet a particular product standard; a bottle labelled “dry wine” does not automatically meet the residual-sugar and strength rules of its region. Classification only answers “which category is this”; compliance still requires checking product standards, labelling rules and test reports item by item.

In practice, editors can build a “boundary card” for each category: category name, parent concept, typical raw materials, core process, common misnomer and corresponding standard. Check the card before writing style and history. This reduces classification errors and helps different authors produce consistent wording.
