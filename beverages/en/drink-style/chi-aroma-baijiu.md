# Chi aroma baijiu

Chi aroma baijiu is a traditional baijiu aroma style from the Pearl River Delta in Guangdong, made from rice through xiaoqu semi-solid fermentation, distillation and then immersion with pork fat. Its aroma is clean and sweet with a distinct fatty note and a faint savoury suggestion, giving it a highly recognisable place among baijiu styles.![Chi aroma baijiu thematic illustration](/media/photos/theme-asian.jpg)

## Definition and standard

The current quality requirement is GB/T 10781.5-2021, Baijiu quality requirements, Part 5, Chi aroma baijiu. The standard sets sensory, physicochemical and hygiene indices and requirements for raw materials, process and labelling. Chi aroma is an aroma classification, not a quality grade.

Chi aroma is not the taste of fermented black beans, nor is it created by adding bean flavour. It comes from the interaction of the spirit with pork fat during immersion, producing a particular fatty, floral and faintly savoury impression. Reading the name as bean-flavoured essence misreads the process.

In the standards system, GB/T 10781.5-2021 and GB/T 15109-2021 do different jobs: the first sets sensory, physicochemical and labelling requirements for chi-aroma products, while the second places chi aroma within baijiu terminology and classification. Neither is an origin certification, and neither replaces a company standard or production licence.

## Regions and examples

Chi aroma baijiu is found mainly around Foshan and Jiujiang in Nanhai, Guangdong, with Shiwan Yubingshao as a representative product. The warm local climate, a deep base of rice wine and xiaoqu craft, and food culture all connect to the fat immersion tradition.

A place name is not a quality guarantee. Distilleries differ in fat handling, immersion time, base spirit quality and blending, so products range from crisp to rich. Understanding a region means understanding the process, not just the geography.

## Rice and Xiaoqu

Chi aroma uses [rice](ref:rice) as its main material, with sufficient starch and moderate protein and fat. Steamed rice is mixed with xiaoqu for saccharification and fermentation. Xiaoqu usually contains Rhizopus and yeast and performs both saccharification and alcohol production, as described under [qu making](ref:qu-making).

Xiaoqu strain and dose affect saccharification speed, acidity and aroma. Rice precision and steaming consistency affect fermentation stability. Raw material and qu are the base, but the character of chi aroma comes more from the later immersion stage.

## Fermentation and distillation

Fermentation follows a semi-solid or liquid route, in which steamed rice is saccharified and fermented by xiaoqu. [Fermentation](ref:fermentation) temperature and time affect acidity, yield and the cleanliness of the base spirit. After fermentation the mash is [distilled](ref:distillation), with heads and tails cut away to keep the middle as rice spirit.

The strength, cleanliness and cuts of the base spirit directly affect the result after immersion. A coarse base brings off-notes, while an overly clean base may lack compounds that interact with fat. The producer's difficulty is balancing base spirit and fat immersion rather than relying on one step.

## Fat immersion

Fat immersion is the signature process of chi aroma: treated pork fat is soaked in the spirit for a long period so fat and liquid interact slowly. Traditional practice has experience-based requirements for fat selection, preparation, resting and replacement cycles, while modern production stresses hygiene and standardisation.

Immersion brings fatty aroma, softness and a special note, and also brings oxidation and microbial risk. Temperature, time and container material all need control. Fat is part of the process, not a gimmick; more fat is not automatically better, and excess can bring greasiness and instability.

In practice, pork fat is boiled, cut, dried and placed in earthen jars with rice spirit in proportion, left for several months and then separated. Over the long contact the fat absorbs some impurities, and fatty acids slowly esterify with alcohols in the spirit, giving the fatty note and softness typical of chi aroma. Traditional practice controlled temperature by season and feel; modern production uses temperature-controlled tanks and stricter hygiene, but fat still takes part in the ageing.

## Flavour profile

Chi aroma is clean and sweet, with fatty, floral and faint savoury notes, a soft entry and a light to medium body with a clean finish. Compared with the honeyed note of rice aroma, chi aroma adds a layer of fat and ageing complexity; compared with the pit aroma of strong aroma, it is lighter and less earthy.

A good chi aroma baijiu should have clear recognition while staying clean and balanced. Aroma that is too heavy may come from over-immersion or an unclean base, while too little can seem thin. Style intensity is not quality; balance is the core of judgment.

## Common misconceptions

First, assuming chi aroma comes from fermented black beans. It comes from fat immersion and interaction with the spirit.
Second, treating fat immersion as a gimmick. It is part of the region's traditional process.
Third, treating chi aroma as a low-strength rice wine. It is a distilled spirit, and the label is the authority on strength.
Fourth, assuming more fat is better. Excess brings greasiness and instability.
Fifth, treating aroma style as a quality grade. It is only a classification.

**Short definition:** A baijiu aroma style from the Pearl River Delta, made from rice with xiaoqu semi-solid fermentation and fat immersion, giving a clean sweet profile with a distinct fatty note; GB/T 10781.5-2021 applies.
