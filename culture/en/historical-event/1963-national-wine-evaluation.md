# The 1963 second national wine evaluation

In October 1963 the second national wine evaluation was held in Beijing, organised jointly by the Ministry of Light Industry and the Ministry of Commerce. Compared with the first meeting in 1952, the categories were broader and the procedure more detailed, and for the first time a separate national quality award was granted. It marked the shift from a one-off list of names to a graded system of quality assessment.![The 1963 second national wine evaluation category image](/media/photos/place-tsingtao-beer-museum-6f368fcc.jpg)

## Background

The 1952 meeting had selected only eight Chinese famous wines. Over the following decade the number of plants and products grew, and a short list could no longer reflect production. The industry needed a way to distinguish famous wines from quality wines and to compare baijiu, yellow wine, wine, beer and fruit wine within one framework.

A second factor was quality management under the planned economy. Alcohol production and sales were managed by state plans, and the evaluation was both a technical event and an administrative act. Provinces recommended samples within quotas, central ministries organised judging, and the result affected allocation, pricing and local prestige.

## Course and timeline

In October 1963, 27 provinces, autonomous regions and municipalities recommended samples within assigned quotas, covering five categories: baijiu, yellow wine, wine, beer and fruit wine. Samples were screened locally and then judged in Beijing. Judges relied mainly on sensory assessment while also consulting chemical analysis. Coded blind judging was not yet standard, and judges knew the origin of samples.

The meeting selected 18 national famous wines: 8 baijiu, 2 yellow wines, 7 wines and fruit wines, and 1 beer. It also selected 27 national quality wines. The eight baijiu were Wuliangye, Gujing Gongjiu, Luzhou Laojiao Tequ, Quanxing Daqu, Moutai, Xifeng, Fenjiu and Dongjiu. Compared with the four baijiu famous wines of 1952, Wuliangye, Gujing Gongjiu, Quanxing and Dongjiu entered for the first time, while Xifeng and Fenjiu remained.

The timeline runs as follows: 1952, the first meeting selects eight famous wines; October 1963, the second meeting selects 18 famous wines and 27 quality wines; 1979, the third meeting introduces coded samples and aroma groups; 1984, the fourth meeting expands to 26 famous wines; 1989, the fifth meeting selects seventeen famous wines and the series ends.

## Key people and institutions

The hosts were the Ministry of Light Industry and the Ministry of Commerce, focused on production technology and on distribution. Provincial light-industry departments and sugar-and-wine companies organised sample submission, and the expert group was made up of technicians recommended from different regions. Unlike the period after 1979, judges in 1963 had no unified national examination.

At company level, [Luzhou Laojiao](ref:luzhou) Tequ remained on the list. Wuliangye and Quanxing from Sichuan; Moutai and Dongjiu from Guizhou; Fenjiu from Shanxi; Xifeng from Shaanxi; and Gujing Gongjiu from Anhui formed the eight baijiu. Among yellow wines, Shaoxing Jiafan and Fujian Chenggang were selected; wines, fruit wines and beer were represented by products from Shandong, Beijing, Heilongjiang and Qingdao.

## Concrete changes for the industry

First, it established the two-tier structure of famous wine and quality wine. Second, it promoted aroma classification. To compare like with like, judging was grouped by aroma type, and concepts such as strong, sauce, light and rice aroma gradually settled. Third, it raised the importance enterprises attached to sensory quality. Inclusion directly affected sales and reputation, and plants began to train assessors systematically. Fourth, it affected prices and allocation. Famous wines gained more stable raw materials and sales channels within the plan. Fifth, it accumulated method for later meetings: sample management, score sheets and the organisation of judges were inherited and improved in 1979.

The limits were also clear: selection carried a planned allocation character, quotas and local recommendation shaped the outcome, sensory judging was subjective, and there was no independent sampling to check whether the sample matched the product on sale.

## Disputes and later assessments

First, the number of entries: 18 famous wines and 27 quality wines is the common account, but some sources mix lists from different years or count only baijiu. Second, wines entering and leaving the list: later commentary often over-explains the movements of individual famous wines, when sample batches, quality or classification rules may have been responsible. Third, the method: in 1963 coded blind judging was not yet standard, judges knew the origin of samples, and the procedure was less fair than after 1979. Fourth, the name of the host body: sources differ between "Ministry of Light Industry and Ministry of Commerce" and "Ministry of Food Industry". Fifth, historical position: the second meeting is often called the starting point of the "old eight", yet it selected more than eight famous wines.

## Common misreadings

One: "Only eight famous wines were selected in 1963." The eight were the baijiu part; there were 18 national famous wines in total. Two: "The old eight are the eight famous wines of 1952." The 1952 eight included yellow wine and wine; the 1963 eight were all baijiu. Three: "The list is a quality ranking." Judging was grouped by category, and there was no common ranking across categories. Four: "Dropping out means quality fell." Sample batch, local recommendation and the composition of the panel all affected results. Five: "Blind judging was already in use in 1963." Coded blind judging became standard at the national evaluation only in 1979.
