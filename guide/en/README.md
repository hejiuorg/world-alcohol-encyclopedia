## Pairings

- [Cheese and Wine: Match Texture, Salt and Fat](pairing/cheese-and-wine.md) — The texture, salt and fat of a cheese decide the direction of the wine; from fresh goat cheese to blue cheese, acidity, sweetness and body matter more than the old rule that red wine goes with every cheese.
- [Dessert and Port: Sweeter Than the Dessert](pairing/dessert-and-port.md) — The core of dessert and Port pairing is that the wine must not be less sweet than the dessert; choose Ruby, Tawny or White Port by the dessert's sugar, fat and intensity, with acidity and oxidative notes deciding whether the finish stays clean.
- [French cuisine with Burgundy: Pinot Noir and the dialogue of sauce](pairing/french-burgundy.md) — The butter, mushrooms, poultry and sauces of French cooking suit the light body, high perfume and fine tannin of red Burgundy; the match relies on aroma and acidity, not on body weight overwhelming the dish.
- [Grilled Meat and Beer: Balance in the Smoke](pairing/grilled-meat-beer.md) — Charcoal char, fat and salt meet the bubbles, bitterness and low temperature of beer. The key is choosing the right type and controlling temperature and pace, not treating beer as water that cuts grease.
- [Hot Pot and Baijiu: One Hot, One Strong](pairing/hotpot-baijiu.md) — Hot pot brings fat, chill heat and high temperature; baijiu brings high alcohol and concentrated aroma. The key is not "the hotter the dish, the stronger the spirit" but controlling entry strength, temperature and pace.
- [Italian cuisine with Sangiovese: tomato, olive oil and acidity](pairing/italian-sangiovese.md) — The tomato, olive oil, herbs and hard cheese of Italian cooking suit the high acidity, cherry fruit and dry tannin of Sangiovese; the match is made by aligning acidity, not by seeking the heaviest tannin.
- [Japanese cuisine with sake: umami and rice aroma in balance](pairing/japanese-sake.md) — The raw freshness, lightness and soy umami of Japanese food suit the rice aroma, amino-acid umami and soft acidity of sake; choose between junmai and ginjo according to fat and sauce.
- [Jiangzhe cuisine with Shaoxing yellow wine: one language of umami](pairing/jiangzhe-shaoxing.md) — The river fish, sauces and sweet-savoury balance of Jiangzhe cuisine share a language with the umami and mellow body of Shaoxing yellow wine; choose yuanhong, jiafan, shanniang or xiangxue according to the weight of the dish.
- [Korean cuisine with soju: grilled meat, kimchi and the small-cup rhythm](pairing/korean-soju.md) — The fermented heat, grilled fat and shared-table style of Korean food suit the lower strength, slight sweetness and small cups of diluted soju; the key is rhythm, temperature and the order of dishes, not drinking volume.
- [Mexican Food and Beer: Maize, Chilli and Lime in Balance](pairing/mexican-food-and-beer.md) — The maize, chilli, lime and fat of Mexican food meet the bubbles, bitterness and malt sweetness of beer in several layers; choosing lager, IPA or dark malt beer by cooking method and heat is more accurate than "ice-cold beer with everything".
- [Seafood and Champagne: Bubbles, Acidity and Salt](pairing/seafood-and-champagne.md) — The savoury freshness, salt and fat of seafood meet the bubbles, acidity and low tannin of Champagne; choosing the right sweetness, body and bubble intensity matters more than the idea that Champagne is only for celebrations.
- [Shandong cuisine with sauce-aroma baijiu: savoury weight and a long finish](pairing/shandong-sauce-aroma.md) — The savoury saltiness, scallion aroma and thick sauces of Shandong cooking meet the roast, floral and long finish of sauce-aroma baijiu; the match is made by equal palate weight, not by making the dish saltier.
- [Sichuan cuisine with strong-aroma baijiu: heat against cellar aroma](pairing/sichuan-strong-aroma.md) — Sichuan heat, fat and fermented seasonings meet the cellar and grain aroma of strong-aroma baijiu; the key is controlling intensity and rhythm, not using high-strength spirit to put out the fire.
- [Spanish cuisine with Rioja: ham, roast lamb and oak](pairing/spanish-rioja.md) — The ham, olives, roast lamb and paella of Spanish cooking suit Rioja reds based on Tempranillo; vanilla and spice from traditional oak carry the savoury weight and fat.
- [Thai cuisine with Riesling: high acid and aroma against heat](pairing/thai-riesling.md) — The sour heat, lemongrass, fish sauce and coconut milk of Thai food meet the high acidity and perfume of Riesling; choose dry to off-dry according to heat, sweetness and fat, not by "the sweeter the better". When ordering, check the dry or off-dry term, the alcohol level and the vintage, and serve at about 8 to 10 degrees Celsius.
- [Vegetarian Food and Wine: A Logic of Vegetables, Pulses and Mushrooms](pairing/vegetarian-and-wine.md) — Vegetarian food is not one flavour but a group of very different ingredients; choose wine by intensity, acidity, bitterness, umami and fat, which is more reliable than the idea that vegetables only go with white wine.

## Techniques

- [Blind Tasting](technique/blind-tasting.md) — Judging a drink without seeing its label, using aroma, palate, structure and finish to infer type, origin, age and quality. Method matters more than guessing right.
- [Decanting](technique/decanting.md) — A serving technique that separates wine from sediment and exposes it to air; whether to decant depends on the drink type, age and condition, and not every wine benefits from long decanting.
- [Double straining](technique/double-straining.md) — A technique that passes a drink through a primary strainer to block ice and large solids, then through a fine mesh to remove pulp, herb fragments and ice shards; it gives a cleaner texture and appearance at the cost of speed.
- [Dry shaking](technique/dry-shaking.md) — A two-stage technique of shaking without ice first and then with ice; it is used for recipes with egg white, cream or plant protein to build a stable foam before chilling and diluting.
- [Flaming zest](technique/flaming-zest.md) — A technique in which citrus peel oil is squeezed through a flame and falls into the glass; it adds toasted aroma and drama, but it is an open-flame operation where safety comes first.
- [Layering](technique/layering.md) — A technique that uses differences in liquid density to build visible layers in a glass; it depends on slow pouring and the order given by the recipe rather than on stirring.
- [Muddling](technique/muddling.md) — A bartending technique in which herbs, fruit or sugar are gently pressed in the glass with a muddler to release aroma and juice; the key is to press without shredding, since force brings bitterness and cloudiness.
- [Nosing](technique/nosing.md) — A tasting technique for judging condition, layers and intensity through aroma gathered at the rim of the glass; nose still first, then swirl, approach from a distance and record specific smells rather than "good" or "bad".
- [Observing colour](technique/observing-colour.md) — A tasting technique for judging clarity, hue and depth under consistent light and background; colour is a clue to age, variety and method, never a verdict on quality on its own.
- [Opening a cork-sealed bottle](technique/opening-cork.md) — A serving technique for removing a cork intact, keeping crumbs out of the liquid and checking the stopper; the keys are a centred auger, a steady pull and the right tool for older bottles.
- [Opening sparkling wine](technique/opening-sparkling-wine.md) — A serving technique for safely opening a pressurised sparkling wine; the essentials are thorough chilling, a steady bottle and hand control over the cork so gas escapes with a whisper rather than firing the cork across the room.
- [Pouring](technique/pouring.md) — A serving technique for measuring wine from bottle to glass; the aims are controlling volume, limiting disturbance of sediment, preserving bubbles and keeping the rim clean, not filling the glass to the top.
- [Rolling](technique/rolling.md) — A mixing technique that pours a drink back and forth between two vessels; it is gentler than shaking and aerates more than stirring, and suits recipes that specifically call for rolling.
- [Sabrage](technique/sabrage.md) — Opening a Champagne bottle by sliding a sabre along the bottle's seam so the neck ring and cork fly off under pressure. It needs a cold bottle, a steady hold and a safe direction.
- [Shaking](technique/shaking.md) — A bartending technique in which ingredients and ice are shaken hard in a shaker to chill, dilute and mix the drink; compared with stirring, shaking adds more aeration and dilution.
- [Sipping](technique/sipping.md) — A tasting technique in which a small amount of liquid covers the mouth for a few seconds to assess sweetness, acidity, body, tannin, alcohol and finish; small sips, short contact and fixed conditions make comparison possible.
- [Spitting](technique/spitting.md) — A professional tasting technique in which liquid is held in the mouth and then spat out; the aim is to stay clear-headed, limit alcohol intake and taste many drinks in sequence, not to express dislike or waste the product.
- [Stirring](technique/stirring.md) — A bartending technique in which ingredients and ice go into a mixing glass and are stirred steadily with a bar spoon, chilling, lightly diluting and mixing the drink; compared with shaking, stirring adds less air and keeps the drink clearer.
- [Temperature Control](technique/temperature-control.md) — Using refrigeration, ice buckets, room warming and storage management to bring a drink to its serving temperature. Temperature shapes aroma, bubbles, texture and fermentation, and stability matters more than a single number.

## Tools

- [Bar spoon](tool/bar-spoon.md) — A long-handled bar tool for stirring cocktails, guiding pours and measuring small volumes; length, bowl capacity and spiral shaft affect stirring efficiency and handling.
- [Bottle Opener](tool/bottle-opener.md) — A simple tool for crown-cap beer bottles, common as a bar blade, wall-mounted opener or key shape. It grips the cap's lower edge and lifts steadily.
- [Channel knife](tool/channel-knife.md) — A bar knife with a small V- or U-shaped notch that pulls long, even strips of citrus peel; notch width, sharpness and cutting depth determine aroma release and whether the garnish holds its shape.
- [Citrus press](tool/citrus-press.md) — A manual tool that uses a lever or screw mechanism to press half a citrus fruit; cup size, stability and acid-resistant materials determine juice yield, cleaning and service life.
- [Cocktail pick](tool/cocktail-pick.md) — A thin pin used to hold olives, cherries, citrus peel or onions; common lengths run 10–15 cm, and material, length, point treatment and corrosion resistance decide whether it holds the garnish without getting in the way.
- [Cocktail shaker](tool/cocktail-shaker.md) — A bar tool for shaking cocktails, commonly in three-piece and two-piece forms; body, seal and straining method affect chilling, dilution and working efficiency.
- [Corkscrew](tool/corkscrew.md) — A tool that uses a helix and a lever to pull a cork. The screw must be centred, the pull vertical and slow, and a broken cork needs care.
- [Fine strainer](tool/fine-strainer.md) — The second strainer in double straining, used to catch fruit fibre, herb fragments and ice shards; mesh fineness, frame stability and cleaning affect clarity and speed.
- [Hydrometer](tool/hydrometer.md) — A glass instrument that reads liquid density by buoyancy, with density, specific gravity, alcohol and sugar scales; it needs temperature correction and measures density, not alcohol directly.
- [Ice Bucket](tool/ice-bucket.md) — A bucket-shaped vessel for chilling bottles, usually metal or glass. An ice-water mix chills faster than ice alone, the water should cover the wine and the time depends on the drink.
- [Jigger](tool/jigger.md) — A small bar measure for fast, repeated doses of liquid; dual-ended capacity, clear markings and a controlled lip affect consistency and speed in service.
- [Mixing glass](tool/mixing-glass.md) — A vessel used with a bar spoon for stirred cocktails; wall thickness, capacity and rim shape affect stirring efficiency, dilution and the final temperature.
- [Muddler](tool/muddler.md) — A rod-shaped bar tool for gently pressing herbs, fruit and sugar in the glass; length, head shape and material affect force control, cleaning and glass safety.
- [Speed pourer](tool/speed-pourer.md) — A spout fitted to a spirit bottle that restricts flow and allows one-handed, continuous pouring; it buys speed and consistency, not accuracy, which still belongs to the jigger.
- [Strainer](tool/strainer.md) — A bar tool that keeps ice, crushed ice and solid residue out of the glass; Hawthorne and julep strainers are the common types, with a fine-mesh strainer used for a second pass.
- [Thermometer](tool/thermometer.md) — A tool for measuring liquid, bottle or cellar temperature, common as a probe, infrared or stick-on model. Readings must settle, calibration must be checked and the probe placed where it represents the whole.
- [Y-peeler](tool/y-peeler.md) — A two-handed peeler with a Y- or T-shaped handle that cuts wide, thin ribbons of citrus peel or vegetable skin; blade type, cutting gap and pressure decide thickness and how much pith comes along.

- [Champagne flute](vessel/champagne-flute.md) — A tall, narrow, slightly tapered stemmed glass used for Champagne and sparkling wine; the narrow body helps retain bubbles but opens aroma less than wider shapes.

- [Coupe glass](vessel/coupe-glass.md) — A stemmed, shallow bowl-shaped glass used for Champagne, sparkling wine and short drinks served without ice; the wide rim opens the aroma but lets bubbles fade quickly.
- [Decanter](vessel/decanter.md) — A glass vessel for separating sediment and letting wine meet air, usually with a wide base, a long neck and a stopper. Decanting time depends on age, body and aroma.
- [Goblet](vessel/goblet.md) — A stemmed glass with a wide bowl and an open rim, used for Belgian abbey and Trappist ales, some wines and table water; capacities commonly run from 250 to 500 ml.
- [Highball glass](vessel/highball.md) — A straight-sided, tall, fairly large long-drink glass used for drinks over ice and sparkling cocktails; capacity has no single standard, so adjust ice and soda to your own glass.
- [Hurricane Glass](vessel/hurricane-glass.md) — A tall glass with a slender body, a bulging middle and a flared rim, shaped like an old hurricane lamp. It is used for tropical cocktails and crushed-ice drinks.
- [Julep Cup](vessel/julep-cup.md) — A small metal cup, usually silver or pewter, used for the mint julep. Metal conducts cold quickly, frosts the wall and keeps the drink icy with crushed ice and mint.
- [Martini glass](vessel/martini-glass.md) — A conical, stemmed short-drink glass with a slightly narrowed rim, also called a cocktail glass, used for the Dry Martini, Margarita and Daiquiri; capacity has no single standard and pre-chilling matters more than shape.
- [Nick and Nora glass](vessel/nick-and-nora.md) — Named after the detective couple in the 1934 film The Thin Man; a small stemmed glass with a shallow bowl and wide rim, typically holding about 150–200 ml and suited to short drinks served without ice.
- [Pint glass](vessel/pint-glass.md) — A straight-sided beer glass holding 568 ml in the UK and 473 ml in the US; conical, nonic and handled jug versions are common, and capacity varies by market and product.
- [Red Wine Glass](vessel/red-wine-glass.md) — A stemmed glass with a wide bowl and a slightly narrowed rim, used for red wine. The wide bowl opens the aroma and the narrowed rim gathers it at the nose.
- [Rocks glass](vessel/rocks-glass.md) — A short, wide-mouthed short-drink glass used for spirits over ice and stirred cocktails; capacity has no single standard, so adjust ice to your own glass.
- [Shot Glass](vessel/shot-glass.md) — A small, thick-walled glass used for one-swallow servings of spirits, for measuring and for layered cocktails. It is built for convenience and intensity, not aroma.
- [Snifter](vessel/snifter.md) — A short-stemmed glass with a large bowl and a narrowed rim, traditionally used for Cognac and brandy; typical capacity is 300–800 ml, while the actual pour is far smaller.
- [Tasting glass](vessel/tasting-glass.md) — The small tulip-shaped glass defined by ISO 3591, commonly called the INAO glass, with a nominal capacity of 215 ml; used for comparative tasting of wine, spirits and sake.
- [Tiki Mug](vessel/tiki-mug.md) — A shaped ceramic or glass mug carved as a tiki god, mask or totem. It belongs to tropical cocktail culture, and its capacity and form are more theatrical than an ordinary glass.
- [Tulip glass](vessel/tulip-glass.md) — A stemmed glass with a wide bowl that narrows towards the rim, used for Champagne, beer and some short drinks; it balances aroma concentration and bubble retention.
- [Whisky Nosing Glass](vessel/whisky-nosing-glass.md) — A small tasting glass with a tulip-shaped bowl and a narrowed rim, used for nosing and sipping whisky. The rim gathers aroma and the short base fits the palm.
- [White Wine Glass](vessel/white-wine-glass.md) — A stemmed glass with a smaller bowl and a narrower rim, used for white wine. The smaller bowl limits oxidation and the narrowed rim holds the floral and acidic freshness.
