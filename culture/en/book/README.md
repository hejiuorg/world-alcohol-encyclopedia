# Books

[返回English目录](../README.md)

- [Bencao Gangmu (wine sections)](bencao-gangmu.md) — Volume 25 of Li Shizhen’s Ming pharmacopoeia, in the fermented-and-brewed class of the grain section, collects entries on wine, shaojiu and grape wine with methods, properties and medicinal claims. It is a key text for Ming wine knowledge and for the “shaojiu began in the Yuan” account, but its pharmacology is not modern medicine.
- [Dongjing Menghua Lu: A Written Map of Bianjing, with Wine Houses as Coordinates](dongjing-menghua-lu.md) — Meng Yuanlao’s Dongjing Menghua Lu, completed in 1147, is a ten-juan memoir of Northern Song Bianjing. Wine houses, licensed breweries, petty wine shops, night markets and festival drinking customs are among its most important material clues. This article covers editions, author, wine content, historical value and common misreadings.
- [Dream of the Red Chamber](dream-of-the-red-chamber.md) — Cao Xueqin's novel, written in the mid-18th century and printed in a 120-chapter edition in 1791; wine runs through banquets, festivals and everyday hospitality, with yellow wine, Huiquan wine, shaojiu and acacia-flower infusion each belonging to a different occasion. Read the wine passages together with character, ritual and edition.
- [The Drunken Botanist](drunken-botanist.md) — A book that starts with plants and ends with drinks: wheat, corn, rice, sugarcane, agave, grapes, juniper and spices become spirits and wines through fermentation, distillation or infusion. It combines botany, craft and food history, and works best as an index and an introduction rather than a commercial distilling manual.
- [A History of Chinese Alcohol (Wang Saishi): From Prehistoric Remains to the Late Qing](history-of-chinese-alcohol-book.md) — Wang Saishi’s A History of Chinese Alcohol, published by Shandong University Press in 2010, is a 430,000-character survey from prehistoric remains to the late Qing and is presented as the first systematic history of alcohol in China. This article covers its editions, author, structure, arguments, limits and common misreadings.
- [Imbibe!: Turning Jerry Thomas from Legend into Bar History](imbibe-book.md) — David Wondrich’s Imbibe!, first published in 2007 and revised in 2015, uses Jerry Thomas’s 1862 How to Mix Drinks to reconstruct nineteenth-century American bar culture and the making of the classic cocktail. This article covers its editions, author, structure, arguments, limits and common misreadings.
- [Jin Ping Mei: Wine Tables, Trade Routes and Ming Life in a Novel of Manners](jinpingmei.md) — Jin Ping Mei, a Ming novel attributed to the pen name Lanling Xiaoxiaosheng, uses the rise and fall of Ximen Qing’s household to portray commerce, family, desire and urban life. Wine is both daily banquet fare and a tool of transaction, seduction and power. This article reads the novel through alcohol and food history, with editions, evidence limits and common misreadings.
- [Beishan Jiujing (Classic of Wine)](jiujing.md) — A three-volume brewing treatise by Zhu Gong of the Northern Song, also known as Beishan Jiujing; the upper volume discusses wine and brewing principles, the middle records qu-making, and the lower sets out the brewing process, making it a key text for understanding Song-era yellow wine.
- [The Joy of Mixology: Families and Ratios Instead of Memorized Recipes](joy-of-mixology.md) — Gary Regan’s The Joy of Mixology, first published in 2003 and revised in 2018, teaches cocktail structure through drink families and proportions rather than rote memorisation. This article covers its editions, author, method, influence, limits and common misreadings.
- [The Oxford Companion to Wine](oxford-companion-to-wine.md) — An alphabetical encyclopedia of wine covering viticulture, winemaking, law, regions, history and people. It is built for reference and cross-checking rather than linear reading, and its entries change with each edition, so the edition consulted should be cited.
- [Proof: The Science of Booze: From Yeast to Hangover, a Laboratory in a Glass](proof-science-of-booze.md) — Adam Rogers’s Proof: The Science of Booze, published in 2014, explains alcohol through fermentation, distillation, maturation, sensory perception and hangover science. This article covers its edition, author, structure, arguments, limits and common misreadings.
- [Qimin Yaoshu](qimin-yaoshu.md) — Jia Sixie’s Northern Wei agricultural treatise, completed between 533 and 544 in ten volumes and ninety-two chapters, gives volume seven to qu-making and brewing. This article covers the author, the editions, chapters 63 to 67, the wine-cultural value of the text and its limits.
- [Sake: A Modern Guide](sake-modern-guide.md) — An English-language introduction to sake that begins with rice, polishing, koji and multiple parallel fermentation, then moves to types, temperature, pairing and buying. It is useful for building a framework, but its 2006 brands and market details need to be updated from current sources.
- [The Savoy Cocktail Book: A 1930 Bar Ledger That Became a Classic](savoy-cocktail-book.md) — Harry Craddock’s The Savoy Cocktail Book, first published by Constable in London in 1930, gathered 750 recipes and Gilbert Rumbold’s Art Deco illustrations into a working record of the Savoy Hotel’s American Bar. This article examines its editions, structure, arguments, limits and common misreadings.
- [Classic of Poetry (Shijing)](shijing.md) — The oldest surviving anthology of Chinese poetry, with 305 poems in the received text; wine appears in sacrifices, banquets and village drinking rites, and “spring wine” links harvest, season and a wish for long life. Read the wine lines with their ritual setting and literary form in view.
- [Suiyuan Shidan (tea and wine list)](suiyuan-shidan.md) — Yuan Mei’s Qing food classic, first printed in 1792, runs to fourteen lists and some three hundred dishes, including a “tea and wine list” devoted to wine and tea. It is key to literati food aesthetics and pairing ideas, but it is personal criticism rather than a systematic brewing manual.
- [Tasting Beer: Turning “I Like It” into a Trainable Sensory Language](tasting-beer.md) — Randy Mosher’s Tasting Beer, first published in 2009 and revised in 2017, teaches sensory evaluation, styles, ingredients, pairing and draft service, turning beer tasting from preference into describable and comparable skill. This article covers its editions, structure, arguments, limits and common misreadings.
- [Tian Gong Kai Wu (chapter on qu and malt)](tian-gong-kai-wu.md) — Song Yingxing’s Ming technological compendium, first printed in 1637 in three volumes and eighteen chapters, devotes its seventeenth chapter to the making of wine qu and red qu. This article covers the author, editions, the qu-making process, its value for wine culture and its limits.
- [Water Margin: Wine as a Passport to the Jianghu and a Narrative Device](water-margin.md) — Water Margin, a Ming novel attributed to Shi Nai’an, turns wine into a ritual of brotherhood, a tool of trickery and a hinge of fate. This article reads “Three Bowls and You Cannot Cross the Ridge,” “Drunk Beating Jiang the Gate Guard” and “Cleverly Taking the Birthday Gifts” through alcohol history and narrative, with editions, limits and common misreadings.
- [Wine and War](wine-and-war.md) — A narrative history of how French winegrowers, merchants and estates tried to preserve their vineyards, stocks and trade during the Nazi occupation of 1940–1944. Wine appears here as both a commodity and a symbol of national identity, and therefore as an object contested by occupiers, the Vichy regime, resisters and ordinary families.
- [The Wine Bible: An American Wine Book Between Exam Syllabus and Dinner Table](wine-bible.md) — Karen MacNeil’s The Wine Bible, first published in 2001 and revised in 2015 and 2022, connects grape, place, tasting, food and storage in one accessible reference. This article covers its editions, author, structure, arguments, limits and common misreadings.
- [The World Atlas of Beer: A Map of Fermentation from Abbeys to Craft Breweries](world-atlas-of-beer.md) — Tim Webb and Stephen Beaumont’s The World Atlas of Beer, first published in 2012 and revised to a third edition in 2021, explains global beer through maps, styles, regions and food pairing. This article covers its editions, authors, structure, arguments, limits and common misreadings.
- [The World Atlas of Whisky: Distilleries, Places and Flavour on One Map](world-atlas-of-whisky.md) — Dave Broom’s The World Atlas of Whisky, first published in 2010 and fully revised in 2014, uses maps, more than 200 distilleries and 750 expressions to build a framework of place, process and flavour. This article covers its editions, author, structure, arguments, limits and common misreadings.
- [The World Atlas of Wine](world-atlas-of-wine.md) — Hugh Johnson and Jancis Robinson’s geographical reference on wine, first published in 1971 and revised to an eighth edition in 2019, explains climate, soil, regulation and style through maps and regional entries. This article covers its authors, editions, arguments, value and limits.
