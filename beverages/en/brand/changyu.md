# Changyu

Changyu is the wine brand of Yantai Changyu Pioneer Wine Co., Ltd., held by [Yantai Changyu Pioneer Wine Co., Ltd.](ref:producer). The company was founded by Zhang Bishi in Yantai in 1892, and the brand spans Jiebaina, chateau wines, ice wine and brandy.![Changyu category image](/media/photos/drink-style-wine-bbcaddfe.jpg)

## Brand definition

A brand and a producer are two different entities. The brand answers "what is this bottle called and whom is it for"; the producer answers "who owns the trademark, who organises production and who bears legal responsibility". This page records the brand layer: name, product line, positioning and marks; see the producer entry for corporate history and listing details.

Changyu's brand structure is unusual: the company and the main brand share a name, and the main brand sits above several sub-brands and chateau series. A question such as "how good is Changyu wine" needs a specific product first, because a volume item such as Jiebaina and a chateau series such as AFIP or Castel differ greatly in positioning and price band.

## Owning producer

The brand's producer is [Yantai Changyu Pioneer Wine Co., Ltd.](ref:producer). The company was incorporated in 1997, listed B shares on the Shenzhen Stock Exchange in the same year, and issued A shares in 2000. The listed company holds the brand and main production assets, while individual products are made by the company and its chateaux.

The producer entry records the founding date, the incorporation and the listing milestones. This page keeps only what relates directly to the brand, so corporate history and product history are not conflated.

## Product line

Public product categories list Changyu Jiebaina, AFIP, Changyu Castel, Keya, Golden Valley, Domaine, Longyu, Riena, Kilikanoon, Dielote, Liquan brandy, Riesling, vermouth and Xiaopu. Jiebaina is the most widely distributed core wine item, AFIP and Castel are chateau series, Golden Valley focuses on ice wine, Keya and Liquan brandy belong to the distilled category, and Domaine and Xiaopu address more everyday consumption.

The vintage, varieties, volume and packaging of a series change by batch, and one series can include several grades and channel versions. Buyers should check the producer, vintage, varieties, strength, net content and standard on the label rather than the series name alone.

## Positioning and price band

The brand covers a band from everyday wine to high-end chateau wine: Domaine and Xiaopu serve everyday drinking, Jiebaina carries mass banquets and business, AFIP, Castel and Longyu reach higher, and Keya brandy forms a separate distilled line. Prices change with year, channel and promotions, so this page gives no specific retail price and does not treat one promotion as long-term positioning.

A price band is market positioning, not a quality grade. Judging a wine means looking at vintage, varieties, origin, winemaking and the label standard, not price or packaging alone.

## History

Changyu's modern history has a clear starting point: in 1892 Zhang Bishi founded the Changyu winemaking company in Yantai, opening an industrial path for winemaking in China. In 1931 Changyu Jiebaina was born in Yantai and later became one of the brand's most widely distributed products.

At company level, the incorporation came in 1997 with a B-share listing on the Shenzhen Stock Exchange, followed by an A-share issue in 2000. After listing, the product structure expanded from wine alone to chateau wines, ice wine and brandy, forming a multi-brand structure. The "hundred years" in the brand narrative refers to the history since the company's founding, not the age of any one bottle.

## Name and marks

"Changyu" is both the company name and the main brand name, and this shared naming easily leads readers to conflate company, brand and product. Sub-brands and chateau series carry their own names and marks, so buyers should read the producer and series name on the bottle.

Buyers should check the trademark, producer, vintage marking and batch information. Similar names and counterfeit packaging exist, and bottle shape or the characters "Changyu" alone are not reliable.

## Common misconceptions

First, treating Changyu as a single product. It is a main brand with several series and chateaux.
Second, treating "a century of Changyu" as the age of the liquid. Company history and wine vintage are different things.
Third, treating Jiebaina as a grape variety. It is a product series name under the brand.
Fourth, conflating brandy with wine. Keya and similar products are distilled spirits, not wine.
Fifth, treating price as a quality grade. Price is market positioning, not a process standard.

Changyu is best read as the starting point of modern industrial winemaking in China and as a multi-layered brand structure, not as one product. Zhang Bishi founded the company in Yantai in 1892; the listed company, the estates, the sub-brands and the individual wines are separate layers. “Jiebaina” is a product-series name, not a grape variety, and Keya is a brandy rather than a wine. Read the producer, estate, series and label standard before repeating a “century-old” or “premium” claim, so that corporate history, brand positioning and bottle age are not conflated.
