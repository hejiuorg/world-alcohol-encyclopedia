# Real Minero: Clay Pots and Seeds in Santa Catarina Minas

Real Minero's palenque stands on Iturbide street, with no house number, in Santa Catarina Minas at the southern edge of Oaxaca's central valleys. It is not a demonstration plant but a working family site: earthen-pit roasting, hand crushing with a wooden mallet and double distillation in clay pots. It is also a concrete entry to [mezcal](ref:mezcal) and the [Oaxaca](ref:oaxaca) region.

## Santa Catarina Minas, at the southern edge of the central valleys

Santa Catarina Minas lies about thirty kilometres south of Oaxaca city on Highway 131, thirty to forty minutes by car. The village hangs on a slope, the palenque sits to one side of Iturbide street behind an unassuming entrance. Use official channels for exact coordinates.

Many families run small plants, and at the entrance you see low walls, adobe and maize drying.

## From "Papá Chico" to La Concepción in 1978

The family traces its line to the late nineteenth century and Francisco Ángeles, "Papá Chico", who took descendants into the hills to distil "Mezcal Minero" when the trade was semi-clandestine. The line passed to Lorenzo Ángeles Mendoza. In 1978 he and Nicolás Arellanes bought the family's first palenque, today's La Concepción.

Between 1978 and 2002 there was no brand: spirit went out in drums or recycled bottles with hand-written paper labels. The first proper label came in 2004; in 2005 the house adopted the regulated category Mezcal Joven. It exported to Germany in 2009 and the United States in 2015.

## Earth pit, wooden mallet and clay pots

The process starts underground. Agave is trimmed to the piña, laid in a conical earth pit, heated with wood and volcanic stone, and covered with earth and fibre to roast for days. The softened piña goes into a wooden canoa and is beaten by hand with a mallet, then fermented with ambient yeast and distilled twice in clay pots, where slow heat and small capacity force the distiller to judge cuts by smell and fire.

Output is far below a copper-still and steel-tank setup, but the flavour is thin and long: smoke does not bury fruit, and mineral and agave bitters stay clear. The scorched pit edge, grooves in the canoa and clay repairs explain [distillation](ref:distillation) and the [clay pot still](ref:potstill) better than the word "traditional".

## The agave garden, seed bank and milpa

Real Minero runs an ethnobotanical garden under Proyecto LAM. It holds seventeen agave species, fourteen propagated from seed, and a seed bank for scarce varieties such as lumbre, blanca, canastuda and becuela. Agave is planted with maize, beans and squash in milpa polyculture, which helps soil and pollinators.

This matters to the supply of [agave](ref:agave): the faster wild plants are taken, the higher the price, and a distillery that only buys in becomes less secure. Keeping seed and recording mother plants costs money but keeps varieties alive. Visits were folded into the project from 2006, turning the tour from equipment-watching into a life-cycle walk.

## NOM-070 and the edge of "ancestral"

Mezcal won its appellation in 1994, and the current basis is NOM-070-SCFI-2016: the raw material must be 100% agave, processed inside the protected zone. Age categories are Joven, Reposado and Añejo; production is classed as artesanal or ancestral. The ancestral path stresses earth-pit roasting, hand crushing, natural fermentation and clay or wooden stills, which is where Real Minero sits, though its labels use Mezcal Joven because the spirit sees no oak.

The appellation covers Oaxaca municipalities and other states, with Santa Catarina Minas in the Oaxacan core. Regulation guarantees agave distillate, not fair prices or wild varieties. On a label, the NOM number and "100% agave" are the floor; asking about variety, village, still and batch is where small houses live.

## The three-hour tour and booking

Visits are booked through the official site or WhatsApp, not taken on the day. The standard tour lasts about three hours: the Proyecto LAM garden, the palenque with its pit, canoa, fermentation and clay pots, then four spirits. Adults pay 750 Mexican pesos and ages 5 to 17 pay 500; tasting only costs 500.

Times are 10:00 and 15:00 Monday to Friday, and 10:00 Saturday with 48 hours' notice; Sundays are closed. Groups run from 1 to 25 and guiding is Spanish only. Lunch for adults costs 1,500 pesos and needs six people. Reservations are held fifteen minutes.

## Visitor notes: give the village time

**Getting there**: thirty to forty minutes by car from Oaxaca city on Highway 131; village roads are narrow, so park without blocking them.

**On site**: the pit and still area are hot and dusty, so wear closed, washable shoes and bring water. Clay pots and mallets are tools, not props; ask before photographing people or family space.

**Tasting**: four pours usually run from a lighter Espadín to Tobalá and Arroqueño; small pours and spitting are normal. Do not taste on an empty stomach.

**The boundary**: do not drive after the visit; Oaxaca has clear drink-drive limits, so use a non-drinking driver or stay overnight.

To connect the site with a city tasting, read [Mezcaloteca](ref:mezcaloteca), where bottles from different villages sit on one table.
