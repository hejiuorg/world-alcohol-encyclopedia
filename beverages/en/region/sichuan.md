# Sichuan

Sichuan lies in south-west China, where the basin terrain and humid climate create distinctive conditions for brewing. It is the core region for strong-aroma baijiu, with Yibin, Luzhou, the Chengdu Plain and Qionglai forming an industrial belt and with Wuliangye and Luzhou Laojiao among the leading producers. Sichuan baijiu is built on multi-grain recipes, continuous-culture fermentation and mud-pit aroma, forming a sharp contrast with Guizhou sauce-aroma baijiu.

## Geography and climate

Eastern Sichuan is the Sichuan Basin, ringed by mountains, with the Yangtze River and its tributaries crossing it. The basin is humid and cloudy, with mild winters, hot summers and a relatively small diurnal range. Western Sichuan is the high plateau, cold in climate and not a main baijiu region.

Microclimates and water sources within the basin affect distillery locations. Yibin sits on the upper Yangtze, where water and river-valley conditions suit brewing; Luzhou lies at the confluence of the Yangtze and Tuo rivers, warm and humid; the Chengdu Plain and Qionglai form a transition between plain and hills. Humidity and temperature affect qu making, fermentation and pit microbiology, which are important environmental conditions for strong-aroma baijiu.

## History

Sichuan's brewing tradition is linked to the Dujiangyan irrigation system, agriculture and the salt trade. In the Ming and Qing periods, Yibin, Luzhou and other towns developed brewing centres, and mud pits and continuous-culture techniques matured. In the twentieth century, state-owned distilleries and national wine competitions drove large-scale production; after reform and opening, Sichuan baijiu entered the national market through brand and capacity advantages.

## Production areas

- Yibin: home of Wuliangye, an important brewing centre on the upper Yangtze.
- Luzhou: home of Luzhou Laojiao, at the confluence of the Yangtze and Tuo, with mud-pit cellars as a core asset.
- Chengdu Plain and Qionglai: concentrated baijiu and base-spirit production, with many distilleries.
- Deyang Mianzhu: home of Jiannanchun, an important strong-aroma region.
- Suining Shehong: home of Tuopai Shede, with strong-aroma and other aroma types.

These areas share the basin climate, but water, soil and pit conditions differ. A region name and a specific distillery are not the same thing.

## Raw materials and process

Sichuan strong-aroma baijiu often uses a multi-grain recipe: [sorghum](ref:sorghum) as the main grain, with [wheat](ref:wheat), [rice](ref:rice), [glutinous rice](ref:glutinous-rice) and [maize](ref:maize), using local [water](ref:water). The multi-grain recipe gives more layers of aroma, sweetness and body.

[Qu making](ref:qu-making) is mainly based on wheat, and the type and temperature of the qu affect microorganisms and enzymes. Fermentation takes place in mud pits, using continuous-culture grain addition, layered removal and long fermentation; the continuous use of pits and their microbial community are an important source of strong-aroma style. After fermentation, spirit is obtained by [distillation](ref:distillation) and then formed into a finished product through storage and [blending](ref:blending).

## Aroma types and styles

In the aroma classification of [baijiu](ref:baijiu), [strong aroma](ref:strong-aroma) is dominated by ethyl hexanoate, often with pit aroma, grain aroma and fruity notes, a mellow sweet body and a long finish. Compared with Guizhou sauce aroma, strong aroma uses lower qu-making temperatures, shorter fermentation cycles and relies on mud pits and continuous-culture grain addition. Aroma type is not a quality tier but a classification of process and flavour.

Sichuan also produces sauce-aroma, light-aroma and other types, but strong aroma has the largest industrial scale. Within one aroma type, differences in grain recipe, pit age and blending produce clearly different styles.

## Representative drinks and producers

[Wuliangye Group](ref:wuliangye) is a representative Yibin producer, and [Wuliangye](ref:wuliangye-drink) is known for its multi-grain recipe and strong-aroma style; [Luzhou Laojiao Group](ref:luzhou) is a representative Luzhou producer, and [Guojiao 1573](ref:guojiao) builds a premium line on its pit group and traditional process. The history, pits and product structures of these two companies are important samples for understanding Sichuan strong-aroma baijiu.

Sichuan has other famous and local distilleries. For specific wines, see the wine pages; region pages describe geography, raw materials and the regulatory framework and do not judge individual brands.

## Regulation and regional protection

Sichuan baijiu production and labelling are governed by national standards and food-safety regulations. GB/T 15109-2021 defines terminology for the baijiu industry, qu making, fermentation and distillation; GB/T 17204-2021 sets the classification and terminology of alcoholic beverages. Strong-aroma baijiu also has industry and local standards, with current texts as the reference.

The use of region names and geographical indications must follow the relevant rules. Ecological and water-source protection in Yibin, Luzhou and other areas also affects the stability of the brewing environment. When reading a label, distinguish origin, aroma type, alcohol content and producer.

This table is a macro comparison; pits, water and qu rooms at a specific distillery still need separate verification. Treating basin climate as a guarantee of quality ignores the role of process and raw materials.

## Market and drinking

Sichuan baijiu is mainly sold domestically, and strong aroma holds an important place in banquets, business and everyday consumption. Drinking should be moderate; strong-aroma baijiu suits small cups and slow sipping with strongly flavoured Sichuan dishes. Storage after opening, temperature and glassware affect the experience. For a specific product, origin, aroma type, alcohol content and producer on the label are the most direct information.

## Common misconceptions

First, that Sichuan only makes strong aroma. It also produces sauce-aroma, light-aroma and other types.

Second, that strong aroma means added flavourings. The aroma comes mainly from qu making, mud-pit fermentation and storage; process is central.

Third, that older pits are always better. Pit age affects microorganisms, but raw materials, process and management matter just as much.

Fourth, that Sichuan baijiu is always high strength. Alcohol content spans a wide range, including lower-strength products.

Fifth, that multi-grain is always better than single-grain. Recipe and process must match; the number of grains alone is not the key.

Sichuan is best read by starting with the appellation and its permitted varieties, moving to the vintage and the producer, and ending with how the wine is labelled.
