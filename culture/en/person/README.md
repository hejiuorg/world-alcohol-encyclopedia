# People

[返回English目录](../README.md)

- [Angelo Gaja](angelo-gaja.md) — Italian winemaker and estate owner (born 1940) who took over the family's Barbaresco estate in 1961, used low yields, French barriques, single vineyards and cross-regional investments to transform Piedmontese wine, and in 1996 declassified three single-vineyard wines from Barbaresco DOCG to Langhe DOC, sparking a continuing traditionalist-modernist debate.
- [Arthur Guinness](arthur-guinness.md) — Irish brewer, entrepreneur and philanthropist (1725–1803) who signed the 9,000-year lease at St James's Gate in Dublin in 1759, scaled a small Leixlip brewery into Ireland's largest beer business and made porter and Guinness stout central to Dublin's brewing identity.
- [Aubert de Villaine](aubert-de-villaine.md) — French winemaker and co-director of Domaine de la Romanée-Conti (born 1939); returned to Burgundy in 1965, managed DRC with the Leroy family from 1974 to 2021, championed organic and biodynamic farming, founded Domaine de Villaine in Bouzeron with his wife Pamela and led the campaign that made the Climats of Burgundy a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2015.
- [Audrey Saunders](audrey-saunders.md) — American bartender and bar owner with no reliable public source for her birth year; she opened Pegu Club in SoHo in 2005 and made fresh juice, measured recipes and repeated training the working standard of the craft cocktail, with the Gin-Gin Mule and Old Cuban as her signature drinks.
- [Bai Juyi](bai-juyi.md) — Tang poet, 772–846; he called himself the Drunken Chanting Gentleman and wrote everyday wine poems about inviting a friend, saying farewell and retiring to the hills.
- [Cao Cao](cao-cao.md) — Late Han statesman, general and poet, 155–220; “Short Song” makes wine the entry to time and worry, while his prohibition order and Kong Rong's objection turned drinking into a political question.
- [Dale DeGroff](dale-degroff.md) — An American bartender born in 1948; from 1987 he ran the bar at the Rainbow Room in New York, using fresh juice, properly handled ice and restored classic recipes to drive the cocktail revival, and became known as King Cocktail.
- [David Wondrich](david-wondrich.md) — An American cocktail historian and writer, born in 1961; author of Imbibe! and Punch and editor-in-chief of The Oxford Companion to Spirits and Cocktails, he used archival research to rewrite the origins of the cocktail.
- [Dom Pérignon](dom-perignon.md) — A French Benedictine monk, 1638 to 1714, who long managed the cellars of the abbey of Hautvillers in Champagne. Later ages called him the father of champagne, but the claim that he invented it is largely a nineteenth-century commercial construction; his more defensible influence lies in blending, clarification and cellar management.
- [Du Fu](du-fu.md) — Tang poet, 712–770; his drinking poems rarely celebrate excess, dwelling instead on hosting guests, giving up wine in illness and the instability of a wandering life.
- [Du Mu](du-mu.md) — Late Tang poet and prose writer, 803–852; he turns wineshops, wine banners and Apricot Blossom Village into landmarks of travel and city nights, while the widely anthologised "Qingming" is absent from his collected works.
- [Émile Peynaud](emile-peynaud.md) — A Bordeaux oenologist, 1912 to 2004, born in Madiran and died in Talence. As professor of oenology at the University of Bordeaux he promoted temperature-controlled fermentation, managed malolactic fermentation, cellar hygiene and systematic tasting, and is regarded as a founder of modern oenology.
- [Feng Zikai](feng-zikai.md) — A modern Chinese painter, essayist and translator, 1898 to 1975, from Chongde in Zhejiang and a student of Master Hongyi. Essays such as The Fine Wine of Shaping, Drinking and A Night Drink by the Lake turn warmed wine, small dishes and late conversation into evidence of ordinary life.
- [Frank Schoonmaker](frank-schoonmaker.md) — An American wine writer and critic active in the mid-twentieth century, known for urging American wines to be labelled by grape variety and place rather than by borrowed European place names; no reliable public source gives his birth or death year, so both are left empty here.
- [Gary Regan](gary-regan.md) — A British-born American bartender and author, 1951–2019; in The Joy of Mixology he grouped cocktails into families, replacing scattered recipes with a teachable structure, and he created Regan's Orange Bitters No. 6.
- [George Garvin Brown](george-garvin-brown.md) — An American whisky entrepreneur, 1846 to 1917; in 1870 in Louisville he founded J.T.S. Brown & Brother and sold Old Forester bourbon in sealed glass bottles, and in 1890 a partnership with the accountant George Forman produced the name Brown-Forman.
- [Harry Craddock](harry-craddock.md) — An Anglo-American bartender, 1876-1963; head bartender at the American Bar of the Savoy Hotel in London from 1925 and author of The Savoy Cocktail Book (1930), which preserved Prohibition-era recipes as a classic text.
- [Harry Johnson](harry-johnson.md) — A nineteenth-century American bartender and author on bar management, usually dated to 1845; in 1882 he self-published the New and Improved Bartender's Manual, a systematic treatment of bar operation, equipment, staffing and standards that moved bartending from performance toward professional management.
- [Henri Jayer](henri-jayer.md) — Burgundy winemaker, 1922–2006; farming Vosne-Romanée parcels as a sharecropper, he used low yields, full destemming, cool maceration and restrained new oak to make precise Pinot Noir, and turned the abandoned Cros Parantoux into a premier cru.
- [Hugh Johnson](hugh-johnson.md) — A British wine writer born in 1939; he published The World Atlas of Wine in 1971 and has produced Hugh Johnson's Pocket Wine Book since 1977, shaping several generations of readers with maps, vintage tables and regional narrative.
- [Jack Daniel](jack-daniel.md) — American distiller (c. 1850–1911) who registered Distillery No. 1 in Lynchburg, Tennessee, in 1866 and standardised the sugar-maple charcoal filtration known as the Lincoln County Process, defining Tennessee whiskey; his brand won gold in St Louis in 1904 and became one of America's most recognisable whiskies.
- [James Buchanan](james-buchanan.md) — Scotch whisky blender and brand builder, 1849–1935; born in Canada and raised in Ireland, he set up on his own in London in 1884, built Buchanan Blend, later Black & White, and joined the Distillers Company in 1925.
- [Jancis Robinson](jancis-robinson.md) — A British wine writer and editor, born in 1950; she passed the Master of Wine examination in 1984, has written a weekly Financial Times column since 1989, edits The Oxford Companion to Wine and has co-authored The World Atlas of Wine with Hugh Johnson since 2001.
- [Jerry Thomas](jerry-thomas.md) — An American bartender, commonly dated 1825 to 1885 (some sources give 1830). In 1862 he published How to Mix Drinks in New York, turning word-of-mouth recipes into a book, and was known as the Professor. The Blue Blazer gave bartending a theatrical side, though he did not invent the classic cocktails.
- [Josef Groll](josef-groll.md) — Bavarian brewer (1813–1887) who was hired by the citizens of Plzeň in 1842 and brewed the first golden, clear Pilsner lager using soft local water, Saaz hops, Moravian barley and bottom-fermenting yeast, establishing the template for the modern pale lager.
- [Lao She](lao-she.md) — A modern Chinese novelist and playwright, 1899 to 1966, a Beijinger of Manchu descent. His fiction and drama turn teahouses, wine shops and courtyard compounds into slices of history, and Camel Xiangzi and Teahouse contain verifiable drinking scenes; reliable records of his own drinking are limited and should not be exaggerated.
- [Li Bai](li-bai.md) — A poet of the high Tang, 701 to 762; wine in his poetry is both a setting for banquets and farewells and a metaphor for solitude, time and ambition. Reading his wine poems means separating the literary figure from the drink actually consumed in Tang times.
- [Li Qingzhao](li-qingzhao.md) — Song poet, born 1084 and usually said to have died around 1155; her wine lyrics run from "so drunk I did not know the way home" to "three cups, two cups of thin wine", cooling as her life moves from Shandong to the south.
- [Louis Pasteur](louis-pasteur.md) — A French chemist and microbiologist, 1822 to 1895; he showed that fermentation is caused by microorganisms and proposed a method of heat treatment that kills them, with a lasting influence on wine, beer and food safety.
- [Lu Xun](lu-xun.md) — A modern Chinese writer, 1881 to 1936, from Shaoxing in Zhejiang. Kong Yiji and Upstairs in the Wine Shop put Shaoxing yellow wine, warming and tavern hierarchy into fiction, while his 1927 lecture explained Wei-Jin drinking through political pressure.
- [Lu You](lu-you.md) — Southern Song poet, 1125–1210; his many wine poems span army life, village retirement and old love, with named drinks and precise settings in “Touring West Mountain Village”, “The Phoenix Hairpin” and “Rain at Sword Gate”.
- [Max Schubert](max-schubert.md) — Australian winemaker (1915–1994) who joined Penfolds as a messenger in 1931, became its first chief winemaker in 1948 and created the experimental Grange in 1951; after the board ordered him to stop in 1957, he continued the 1957–1959 vintages in secret, and Grange was officially reinstated for 1960.
- [Michael Jackson (beer writer)](michael-jackson.md) — British beer and whisky writer (1942–2007) whose 1977 World Guide to Beer turned style, sensory description and cultural context into a shared language, and whose books and TV series helped Belgian beer, British ale and American craft brewing reach a global audience.
- [Michael Jackson (beer writer)](michael-jackson.md) — British beer and whisky writer (1942–2007) whose 1977 World Guide to Beer turned style, sensory description and cultural context into a shared language, and whose books and TV series helped Belgian beer, British ale and American craft brewing reach a global audience.
- [Michel Rolland](michel-rolland.md) — A Bordeaux wine consultant, 1947–2026; he combined ripeness assessment, fermentation and maceration control, oak and micro-oxygenation into a consulting process, with clients across many regions, and became a focus of the argument over internationalised style.
- [Miguel A. Torres](miguel-torres.md) — Spanish winemaker and family-business president (born 1941) who joined Familia Torres in 1962, made Mas La Plana in 1970, won the 1979 Paris Wine Olympics, expanded to Chile in 1979 and California in 1982, launched Torres & Earth in 2008 and co-founded the International Wineries for Climate Action in 2019.
- [Ouyang Xiu](ouyang-xiu.md) — Northern Song writer and statesman, 1007–1072; he called himself the Old Drunkard and made wine the vehicle of landscape pleasure, later listing it among the five objects of his retirement; Chuzhou and Yingzhou are the two places where his relationship with wine is deepest.
- [Baron Philippe de Rothschild](philippe-de-rothschild.md) — French estate owner, poet and entrepreneur (1902–1988) who took over Château Mouton Rothschild in 1922, pioneered château bottling, artist labels, Mouton Cadet and the 1973 promotion to First Growth, and co-founded Opus One with Robert Mondavi in 1979.
- [Qin Hanzhang](qin-hanzhang.md) — A Chinese food-industry and fermentation engineer, 1908 to 2019; he led the Fenjiu technical trial, took part in research on Maotai and taught at Jiangnan University, and is regarded as one of the founders of scientific winemaking in China.
- [Robert Mondavi](robert-mondavi.md) — American winemaker and winery owner (1913–2008) who founded Robert Mondavi Winery in Oakville, Napa Valley, in 1966; championed varietal labelling, French oak and wine tourism, and in 1979 co-founded Opus One with Baron Philippe de Rothschild.
- [Robert Parker](robert-parker.md) — An American wine critic, born in 1947; in 1978 he founded the independent newsletter The Wine Advocate, and his 100-point scale shaped the global wine market, while the “Parkerization” debate over homogenised taste made him a focus of the industry. He retired in 2019.
- [Steven Spurrier](steven-spurrier.md) — A British wine merchant, educator and writer, 1941–2021; he founded the Académie du Vin in Paris in 1973, organised the 1976 Judgment of Paris with Patricia Gallagher, and later served as a Decanter consultant editor, shaping international perceptions of California wine and the old hierarchy.
- [Masataka Taketsuru](taketsuru-masataka.md) — A pioneer of Japanese whisky, 1894–1979; he studied distilling and blending in Scotland from 1918 to 1920, helped build the Yamazaki distillery, and founded Dai Nippon Kaju, the predecessor of Nikka, in Yoichi, Hokkaido, in 1934 after parting ways with Shinjiro Torii.
- [Tang Yin](tang-yin.md) — A Ming dynasty painter and poet, 1470 to 1524, from Wuxian in Suzhou. After an examination scandal he gave up official ambition and sold paintings. The Peach Blossom Cottage Song and Drinking Wine Facing the Moon make flowers, wine and reclusion a position on life, and the official history also records him feigning madness through drink.
- [Tao Yuanming](tao-yuanming.md) — Eastern Jin poet, 365–427; he described himself as “addicted to wine” and made drinking part of the same choice as leaving office and returning to the fields.
- [Shinjiro Torii](torii-shinjiro.md) — Japanese entrepreneur (1879–1962) who founded Torii Shoten in Osaka in 1899, launched Akadama Port Wine in 1907, built the Yamazaki distillery in 1923, hired Masataka Taketsuru and released Japan's first commercial malt whisky, Shirofuda, in 1929.
- [Wang Wei](wang-wei.md) — Tang poet and painter, 701–761; “A Farewell to Yuan Er on His Mission to Anxi” turns one cup of wine into the definitive Chinese image of farewell, later set to music as the “Three Refrains of Yangguan”.
- [Wang Zengqi](wang-zengqi.md) — A contemporary Chinese writer, 1920 to 1997, from Gaoyou in Jiangsu and a student of Shen Congwen. His food essays treat wine as part of the table, valuing dishes, people and place over prices and display; “a cup of cloudy wine past midday” in “The Rain of Kunming” is his most concentrated passage on drinking.
- [William Grant](william-grant.md) — A Scottish whisky entrepreneur, 1839 to 1923; he worked his way up at Mortlach, built Glenfiddich with his family from 1886, ran the first spirit on Christmas Day 1887 and added Balvenie in 1892; the company remains family-owned.
- [Xin Qiji](xin-qiji.md) — Southern Song poet and soldier, 1140–1207; after crossing south from the Jin-occupied north in his twenties, he spent two long retirements writing ci in which wine opens memory, questions old age and mocks its own consolation.
- [Yu Dafu](yu-dafu.md) — A modern Chinese writer, 1896 to 1945, from Fuyang in Zhejiang and a founding member of the Creation Society. His classical-style poems and prose often mention wine, and in 1942 he ran the Zhao Yuji distillery in Sumatra under the alias Zhao Lian; he was killed in 1945.
- [Zhang Bishi](zhang-bishi.md) — A Chinese industrialist and overseas merchant, 1841 to 1916; in 1892 he invested in the Changyu wine company in Yantai, Shandong, bringing European grape varieties, equipment and winemakers to China and helping to establish a modern wine industry there.
- [Zheng Banqiao](zheng-banqiao.md) — A Qing dynasty painter, calligrapher and poet, 1693 to 1766; he served as magistrate of Fan and Wei counties, then sold paintings in Yangzhou as one of the Eight Eccentrics. His inscriptions and his published price list place wine, painting prices and livelihood on the same line.
- [Zhou Henggang](zhou-henggang.md) — A Chinese baijiu technologist, 1918 to 2004; he led the 1955 and 1962 Yantai trials and the 1964-1966 Maotai trial, summarised the Yantai method, and advanced research on fuqu, caproic-acid bacteria and pit mud.

- [André Simon](andre-simon.md) — A French-born British wine merchant, writer and bibliophile, 1877–1970; he moved to London in 1902 as Pommery's agent, co-founded the Wine & Food Society in 1933, and used trade records and bibliography to make wine history verifiable.
- [André Tchelistcheff](andre-tchelistcheff.md) — A Russian-born American winemaker, 1901–1994; chief winemaker at Beaulieu Vineyard from 1938, he brought cold fermentation, malolactic control and small French oak to Napa, and mentored generations of California winemakers.
- [Bruno Giacosa](bruno-giacosa.md) — A Piedmont winemaker, 1929–2018; in 1964 he labelled a single Barbaresco vineyard, Santo Stefano, later bought Falletto, and held to long cask ageing and strict vintage selection, refusing to bottle Barolo 2010.
- [Giacomo Conterno](giacomo-conterno.md) — A traditionalist Barolo producer in Monforte d'Alba; his long maceration, large Slavonian oak and Monfortino riserva made Barolo built for decades, and his family bought Cascina Francia in 1974.
- [Jim Barrett](jim-barrett.md) — An American winery owner and lawyer, 1926–2013; with Ernie Hahn he bought and revived Chateau Montelena in 1972, whose 1973 Chardonnay, made by Mike Grgich, won the white-wine flight of the 1976 Judgment of Paris.
- [Maynard Amerine](maynard-amerine.md) — An American viticulturist and UC Davis professor, 1911–1998; with Albert Winkler he built the growing-degree-day heat index, and he standardised wine sensory evaluation through trained panels, blind tests and statistics.
- [Michael Broadbent](michael-broadbent.md) — A British wine critic, auctioneer and writer, 1927–2020; he rebuilt Christie's wine department in 1966, recorded tens of thousands of wines in red notebooks from 1952, and wrote The Pocket Guide to Wine Tasting and Vintage Wine.
- [Piero Antinori](piero-antinori.md) — An Italian producer, born 1938 and the 26th generation of Marchesi Antinori; he took over in 1966, released Tignanello in 1971 with Sangiovese, Cabernet and small oak, and expanded the family estates internationally.
- [Warren Winiarski](warren-winiarski.md) — An American Napa winemaker, 1928–2024; he founded Stag's Leap Wine Cellars in 1973, won the red-wine flight of the 1976 Judgment of Paris with his 1973 Cabernet, and later worked on Napa's conjunctive labelling law.

- [Peter Lehmann](peter-lehmann.md) — An Australian Barossa Valley winemaker, 1930–2013; after Yalumba and Saltram he founded Masterson in 1979 to buy grapes from growers facing a vine-pull scheme, renamed the company Peter Lehmann Wines in 1982, and shaped modern Barossa with Stonewell and Eight Songs Shiraz.
- [Jimmy Russell](jimmy-russell.md) — An American bourbon master distiller at Wild Turkey; he joined in 1954 at eighteen, worked from the floor up, became master distiller in the 1960s, kept the 101 formula, and shaped Rare Breed, Kentucky Spirit and Russell's Reserve before forming a father-son master-distiller pair with Eddie in 2015.
- [Booker Noe](booker-noe.md) — An American sixth-generation master distiller of the Jim Beam family, 1929–2004; he took over the Boston, Kentucky plant in 1960, filled the millionth barrel in 1965, launched Booker's in 1987/1988 and built the Small Batch Bourbon Collection in 1992.
- [Fritz Maytag](fritz-maytag.md) — An American brewer and Anchor Brewing owner, born 1937; in 1965 he bought the failing Steam Beer brewery in San Francisco, rebuilt Anchor Steam with open fermentation and whole-cone hops, and became a founding figure of the post-Prohibition craft-beer movement.
- [Ken Grossman](ken-grossman.md) — An American founder of Sierra Nevada Brewing, born 1955; he opened a homebrew shop in Chico in 1976, built a brewery from scrap dairy equipment in 1980, and used Cascade hops and Pale Ale to drive the craft-beer revolution while keeping the company family-owned.
- [Jim Koch](jim-koch.md) — An American founder of Samuel Adams and The Boston Beer Company, born 1949; he brewed his great-great-grandfather's lager recipe at home in 1984, launched Boston Lager in 1985, listed the company in 1995 and created the Brewing the American Dream programme.
- [John Jameson](john-jameson.md) — A Scottish-born Irish merchant and distiller, 1740–1823; he opened the Bow Street distillery in Dublin in 1780, experimented with malted and unmalted barley under the malt tax, and laid the basis for Jameson's triple-distilled single-pot-still whiskey.
- [George Smith](george-smith.md) — A Speyside distiller in the Livet valley, died 1871; he obtained an Elgin licence in 1824, became the first legal distiller in the Glenlivet parish, built the plant amid threats from illicit producers, and laid the foundation for The Glenlivet single malt.
- [Lalou Bize-Leroy](lalou-bize-leroy.md) — A Burgundy merchant and estate owner, born 1932; she took over Maison Leroy in 1955, co-managed DRC from 1974 to 1992, founded Domaine Leroy in 1988 and converted it to biodynamics, influencing modern Burgundy through very low yields and massal selection.
