# Techniques

[返回English目录](../README.md)

- [Blind Tasting](blind-tasting.md) — Judging a drink without seeing its label, using aroma, palate, structure and finish to infer type, origin, age and quality. Method matters more than guessing right.
- [Decanting](decanting.md) — A serving technique that separates wine from sediment and exposes it to air; whether to decant depends on the drink type, age and condition, and not every wine benefits from long decanting.
- [Double straining](double-straining.md) — A technique that passes a drink through a primary strainer to block ice and large solids, then through a fine mesh to remove pulp, herb fragments and ice shards; it gives a cleaner texture and appearance at the cost of speed.
- [Dry shaking](dry-shaking.md) — A two-stage technique of shaking without ice first and then with ice; it is used for recipes with egg white, cream or plant protein to build a stable foam before chilling and diluting.
- [Flaming zest](flaming-zest.md) — A technique in which citrus peel oil is squeezed through a flame and falls into the glass; it adds toasted aroma and drama, but it is an open-flame operation where safety comes first.
- [Layering](layering.md) — A technique that uses differences in liquid density to build visible layers in a glass; it depends on slow pouring and the order given by the recipe rather than on stirring.
- [Muddling](muddling.md) — A bartending technique in which herbs, fruit or sugar are gently pressed in the glass with a muddler to release aroma and juice; the key is to press without shredding, since force brings bitterness and cloudiness.
- [Nosing](nosing.md) — A tasting technique for judging condition, layers and intensity through aroma gathered at the rim of the glass; nose still first, then swirl, approach from a distance and record specific smells rather than "good" or "bad".
- [Observing colour](observing-colour.md) — A tasting technique for judging clarity, hue and depth under consistent light and background; colour is a clue to age, variety and method, never a verdict on quality on its own.
- [Opening a cork-sealed bottle](opening-cork.md) — A serving technique for removing a cork intact, keeping crumbs out of the liquid and checking the stopper; the keys are a centred auger, a steady pull and the right tool for older bottles.
- [Opening sparkling wine](opening-sparkling-wine.md) — A serving technique for safely opening a pressurised sparkling wine; the essentials are thorough chilling, a steady bottle and hand control over the cork so gas escapes with a whisper rather than firing the cork across the room.
- [Pouring](pouring.md) — A serving technique for measuring wine from bottle to glass; the aims are controlling volume, limiting disturbance of sediment, preserving bubbles and keeping the rim clean, not filling the glass to the top.
- [Rolling](rolling.md) — A mixing technique that pours a drink back and forth between two vessels; it is gentler than shaking and aerates more than stirring, and suits recipes that specifically call for rolling.
- [Sabrage](sabrage.md) — Opening a Champagne bottle by sliding a sabre along the bottle's seam so the neck ring and cork fly off under pressure. It needs a cold bottle, a steady hold and a safe direction.
- [Shaking](shaking.md) — A bartending technique in which ingredients and ice are shaken hard in a shaker to chill, dilute and mix the drink; compared with stirring, shaking adds more aeration and dilution.
- [Sipping](sipping.md) — A tasting technique in which a small amount of liquid covers the mouth for a few seconds to assess sweetness, acidity, body, tannin, alcohol and finish; small sips, short contact and fixed conditions make comparison possible.
- [Spitting](spitting.md) — A professional tasting technique in which liquid is held in the mouth and then spat out; the aim is to stay clear-headed, limit alcohol intake and taste many drinks in sequence, not to express dislike or waste the product.
- [Stirring](stirring.md) — A bartending technique in which ingredients and ice go into a mixing glass and are stirred steadily with a bar spoon, chilling, lightly diluting and mixing the drink; compared with shaking, stirring adds less air and keeps the drink clearer.
- [Temperature Control](temperature-control.md) — Using refrigeration, ice buckets, room warming and storage management to bring a drink to its serving temperature. Temperature shapes aroma, bubbles, texture and fermentation, and stability matters more than a single number.
