# Brown-Forman

Brown-Forman is a spirits company founded in Louisville, Kentucky in 1870. It began with Old Forester and [Jack Daniel's](ref:jackdaniels) and today covers American whiskey, tequila, rum, liqueurs and some wine. It is family-controlled and listed on the New York Stock Exchange, with voting Class A shares and non-voting Class B shares. In 2024 the company completed the Sonoma-Cutrer sale and exited its Duckhorn stake, a key change for understanding its current boundaries.

## Company identity and location

Brown-Forman is a producer and a listed company, not a single brand. Headquartered in Louisville, Kentucky, it handles distillation, ageing, bottling and global distribution; the brand layer consists of individual names, recipes and origins. Equating the company with one brand leads to misattributing products.

The company's place in American spirits history is linked to branded bottling: in the late nineteenth century, bourbon was often sold in bulk barrels, and Old Forester entered the market in sealed bottles with consistent quality labelling, an early example of branded bourbon.

## Dual-class shares and family control

The company has Class A and Class B shares: Class A carries voting rights, Class B does not, and both trade on the New York Stock Exchange under BFA and BFB. This structure lets the Brown family retain control while raising capital from the public market.

Family control is a long-term feature, but public material uses different definitions for the combined voting power of family members and trusts, so this page does not state a specific percentage. What can be confirmed is that dual-class shares make the company favour a steady pace in acquisitions, long-term ageing investment and dividends rather than short-term financial engineering.

## Key points since 1870

Key points include: 1870, George Garvin Brown and a partner founded the company and launched Old Forester; the 1930s, the business rebuilt after Prohibition; 1956, the acquisition of Jack Daniel's; later purchases of Woodford Reserve, Herradura and other brands built a multi-category portfolio; 2022, the acquisition of Diplomático rum; 2023, the announcement of the Sonoma-Cutrer sale; 2024, completion of the deal and exit from Duckhorn.

These points show a company with American whiskey as its cash engine and acquisitions filling in categories, not a "bourbon-only" business.

## Portfolio: whiskey at the core

Whiskey is the core: Jack Daniel's is one of the largest American whiskey brands, Old Forester preserves Kentucky bourbon tradition, Woodford Reserve focuses on small batches and flavour expression, and GlenDronach, Benriach and Glenglassaugh add Scotch single malts.

Origins and rules differ: Jack Daniel's is made in Lynchburg, Tennessee, and is Tennessee whiskey; Old Forester and Woodford Reserve are made in Kentucky and are [bourbon](ref:bourbon). Tennessee whiskey passes through Lincoln County process charcoal filtration, while bourbon does not. Calling Jack Daniel's a bourbon is the most common category error.

## Tequila, rum and liqueurs

In tequila the company owns Herradura and el Jimador, the first traditional and premium, the second broader and more everyday. In rum, Diplomático, acquired in 2022, comes from Venezuela and stresses sweet aroma and aged expression. In liqueurs, Chambord is a leading blackberry liqueur.

A multi-category portfolio spreads risk, but channels, regulations and occasions differ greatly; group scale does not automatically become brand strength.

## Wine: from Sonoma-Cutrer to Korbel

The company still owns Korbel California Champagne; the FY2025 results, explaining the US sales decline, still list Korbel among brands with lower volume, confirming it remains in the portfolio. The company previously owned Sonoma-Cutrer, but that business was sold in fiscal 2024.

Today, discussion of Brown-Forman's wine business should separate Korbel from the divested Sonoma-Cutrer; continuing to describe the latter as a company brand is outdated.

## The 2024 Sonoma-Cutrer transaction

In November 2023 the company announced the sale of Sonoma-Cutrer Vineyards and related trademarks to The Duckhorn Portfolio in exchange for Duckhorn equity. After the deal closed in fiscal 2024, the company held 21.4% of Duckhorn.

In October 2024 Duckhorn agreed to be acquired by private equity funds managed by Butterfly Equity, and the deal closed on 24 December 2024. After closing, Brown-Forman exchanged its 21.4% stake for USD 350 million in cash and exited, recognising a gain. The sequence shows a deliberate retreat from wine assets and a focus on spirits.

## Regions and raw materials

Central Kentucky's limestone water, corn and rye supply, and four-season climate form the bourbon region's conditions. Wooden warehouses let the liquid expand and contract through summer and winter, accelerating oak extraction; this "warehouse flavour" is a key Kentucky variable.

The mash bill shapes style: high corn brings sweetness, high rye brings spice, and wheat recipes are softer. Recipes are public or semi-public by brand, but exact ratios are not always fully disclosed. Tennessee whiskey's mash is close to bourbon; the difference lies mainly in charcoal filtration and origin labelling.

## Process and style

Bourbon requires new charred oak barrels, at least two years of ageing and specific grain rules; Tennessee whiskey adds charcoal filtration. Brown-Forman products are generally [distilled](ref:distillation), filled into new barrels, aged by year and barrel type, then blended.

[Oak barrels](ref:oak-ageing) are central to style: warehouse position, floor and barrel age determine oxidation and extraction. The range runs from the traditional spice of Old Forester to the roundness of Woodford Reserve and the vanilla and banana notes of Jack Daniel's.

## Channels and capital market

The United States is the base, with global distribution into Europe and Asia. Jack Daniel's is the most internationally recognised brand and a symbol of American whiskey exports; rising demand for premium bourbon has led the company to expand ageing inventory and capacity.

On the capital market, the company trades under BFA/BFB, giving investors different voting rights in the same business. Dividends, buybacks and family trust holdings affect the share price but do not change brand ownership at the product level.

## Recent performance and pressure

FY2025 results show US net sales down 7% and organic sales down 2%, driven by the Sonoma-Cutrer divestiture, lower Jack Daniel's and Korbel volume, and distributor inventory adjustments; Woodford Reserve growth partly offset the decline.

Broader pressure comes from tariffs, slower consumption and the inventory cycle; the company continued to adjust its portfolio and spending in 2025–2026. The percentages in the report are group-level and cannot be used directly to infer the quality or price of a single bottle.

## Governance and controversy

Controversy centres on three areas: slower US whiskey demand and inventory pressure; tariffs and trade friction affecting exports; and portfolio concentration after divestitures. Dual-class shares preserve family control, limiting outside shareholder influence, a long-running governance discussion.

Public material does not disclose the full family voting percentage or all related-party details, and this page does not fill them in. Specific equity and compensation matters should be checked against SEC filings and proxy statements.

## Division of labour with the brand page

This page covers the company: who owns it, what brands it holds and how it discloses. Brand positioning, product lines and flavour belong on brand pages; Brown-Forman has no separate brand page, so this page gives only a portfolio-level overview.

Treating company and brand as one leads to inferences such as "divesting wine makes every spirit cheaper" or "family control means shareholders do not matter". Reading them separately keeps capital actions and product choices in their own places.

## Common misconceptions

First, calling Jack Daniel's a bourbon. It is Tennessee whiskey, with different rules.
Second, thinking Tennessee whiskey is only a marketing name. Charcoal filtration and origin are substantive differences.
Third, treating family control as a small business. Brown-Forman is a listed multinational.
Fourth, still counting Sonoma-Cutrer as a company brand. The business was sold in fiscal 2024.
Fifth, assuming older bourbon is always better. Beyond a certain age, barrel notes can overwhelm grain flavour.
