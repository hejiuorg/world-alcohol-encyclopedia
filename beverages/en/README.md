## Brands

- [Absolut](brand/absolut.md) — A Swedish vodka brand now owned by Pernod Ricard, sold on single-origin production, winter wheat and continuous distillation, and known for its clear bottle, minimal label and long-running art collaborations.
- [Baiyunbian](brand/baiyunbian.md) — A mixed-aroma baijiu brand from Songzi, Hubei, named after a line by Li Bai; known for a strong-head, sauce-tail style, with vintage, era and star series, and core vintages at fifteen, twenty and thirty years.
- [Changyu](brand/changyu.md) — The wine brand of Yantai Changyu Pioneer Wine Co., Ltd.; the company was founded by Zhang Bishi in 1892, and the brand spans Jiebaina, chateau wines, ice wine and brandy.
- [Chivas Regal](brand/chivas-regal.md) — A Scotch blended whisky brand operated by Chivas Brothers under Pernod Ricard, known for its 12-year and 18-year age statements and a smooth blending style, and one of the representative names in premium blended Scotch.
- [Daohuaxiang](brand/daohuaxiang.md) — A strong-aroma baijiu brand from Yichang, Hubei, under Daohuaxiang Group; its name comes from a line by Xin Qiji, with Qingyang as the premium flagship, the Huoli family for banquets, Zhenpin for traditional channels and Shouchaojian as a soy-aroma experiment.
- [Dongjiu](brand/dongjiu.md) — A Dong-aroma (also called medicinal-aroma) baijiu brand from Donggongsi, Zunyi, Guizhou, held by Guizhou Dongjiu Co., Ltd. Its process uses herbs in the qu, separate large and small qu, and twin cellars, with key products including Guomi Dongjiu and Guomi Baicaoxiang.
- [Fenjiu](brand/fenjiu.md) — A light-aroma baijiu brand within the Shanxi Xinghuacun Fenjiu Group system, with series including Qinghua, Laobaifen and Bofen. This page covers brand structure, not the product page's flavour detail.
- [Greatwall](brand/greatwall.md) — A domestic Chinese wine brand owned within COFCO and operated by COFCO Greatwall Wine Co., Ltd., built around star-rated series, estate wine and regional wines and covering everyday table wine to premium gifting; the producer and origin of a specific bottle should be checked on its label.
- [Guinness](brand/guinness.md) — A beer brand originating in Dublin, Ireland and now owned by Diageo, known for its dark stout, roasted barley flavour and nitrogen-conditioned foam, and one of the defining names in the stout style.
- [Gujing Gongjiu](brand/gujing-gongjiu.md) — A strong-aroma baijiu brand from Bozhou, Anhui, known for its "Nianfen Yuanjiang" series and products across several strengths.
- [Hennessy](brand/hennessy.md) — A French Cognac brand operated by Moët Hennessy under LVMH, known for its VS, VSOP and XO grades and long-matured blended eaux-de-vie, and one of the defining names of the Cognac region.
- [Red Star](brand/hongxing.md) — A light-aroma baijiu brand under Beijing Red Star Co., Ltd., built around the er-guotou cut-the-head-and-tail craft; the blue-bottle mellow series and green-bottle traditional series form the everyday core, with Red Star Gaozhao for gifting and commemoration.
- [Huanghelou](brand/huanghelou.md) — A Wuhan baijiu brand held by Huanghelou Distillery, built around Southern School Great Light Aroma while also offering Chenxiang, Ecological Original Pulp, the Lou series and the strong-aroma Gengshang Cenglou; it covers banquets, gifting and upper-mid-market occasions.
- [Kuaijishan](brand/huijishan.md) — A Shaoxing huangjiu brand of Kuaijishan Shaoxing Rice Wine Co., Ltd., originating in the 1743 Yunji winery in Dongpu and brewed from glutinous rice, wheat qu and Jian Lake water; the range includes five-year aged wine, Lanting and Shuixiang Guose within the Shaoxing geographical-indication system.
- [Jiannanchun](brand/jiannanchun.md) — A strong-aroma baijiu brand from Mianzhu, Sichuan, held by Sichuan Jiannanchun (Group) Co., Ltd.; it is known for multi-grain strong aroma and the Tianyi Laohao pit complex, with key items including Crystal Jiannanchun, the Zhencang grade and Dongfanghong 1949.
- [Jiugui](brand/jiuguijiu.md) — A fuyu-aroma baijiu brand under Jiugui Liquor Co., Ltd., made in Jishou, Xiangxi; Huang Yongyu named it and designed the sack-shaped bottle in 1987, and the range includes Neican, Jiugui and Xiangquan, known for three aromas in one sip and a multi-grain process.
- [Johnnie Walker](brand/johnnie-walker.md) — A blended Scotch whisky brand originating in Scotland and now owned by Diageo, known for its square bottle and colour-coded Red, Black, Green, Gold and Blue Label range, from everyday mixing to high-end gifting.
- [Kouzijiao](brand/kouzijiao.md) — A Jianxiang-aroma baijiu brand from Suixi, Huaibei, Anhui, known for a one-step Jianxiang process using multiple qu types and grains; the range covers the Kouzijiao vintage series, Lao Kouzi, Kouzifang and Kouzijiu, and the company listed in 2015.
- [Langjiu](brand/langjiu.md) — A sauce-aroma baijiu brand from Erlang, Gulin, Sichuan, on the left bank of the Chishui River, held by Sichuan Langjiu Group Co., Ltd.; its main line includes Qinghua Lang, Hongyun Lang, Honghua Lang and Langpai Lang, with jianxiang products such as Langpai Tequ, Shunpin Lang and Xiaolangjiu.
- [Hengshui Laobaigan](brand/laobaigan.md) — A Laobaigan-aroma baijiu brand under Hebei Hengshui Laobaigan Liquor Co., Ltd.; the company originates from the state-run Hengshui distillery formed in 1946 and listed in 2002.
- [Luzhou Laojiao](brand/luzhou-laojiao.md) — A strong-aroma baijiu brand within the Luzhou Laojiao Group system, with core products including Guojiao 1573 and Luzhou Laojiao Tequ. The name relates to pit age, but the name is not the age of the liquid.
- [Niulanshan](brand/niulanshan.md) — A baijiu brand of Beijing Shunxin Agriculture's Niulanshan Distillery, centred on er-guotou craft and the mass price band, with lines spanning light-aroma er-guotou, strong-aroma Niulanshan Chenniang and Bainian Niulanshan.
- [Shede](brand/shed.md) — A high-end strong-aroma baijiu brand under Shede Spirits, launched in December 2001; positioned around the Shede cultural idea and aged-liquor reserves, with Pinwei Shede, Zhihui Shede, collectible Shede and crystal Shede in the range.
- [Suntory](brand/suntory.md) — A Japanese beverage and alcohol brand whose drinks portfolio covers Japanese whisky, beer and spirits; its global spirits business has operated under the Beam Suntory name.
- [Swellfun](brand/swellfun.md) — A high-end strong-aroma baijiu brand under Sichuan Swellfun Co., Ltd., launched in 2000. Built on the Shuijingjie distillery site, with products including Jingcui, Diancang, Jingtai and Zhenniang Bahao.
- [Taipai](brand/taipai.md) — A Shaoxing huangjiu brand operated by Zhejiang Taipai Shaoxing Rice Wine Co., Ltd., built around handmade winter brewing, aged series and export packaging; it draws on the Shaoxing geographical indication and intangible-heritage narrative and spans everyday drinking to gifting.
- [Tsingtao Beer](brand/tsingtao.md) — A beer brand originating in Qingdao in 1903 and held by Tsingtao Brewery Company Limited. Its main products are lagers, spanning the Classic 1903, pure draft, white beer and Augerta series.
- [Wuliangye](brand/wuliangye.md) — A strong-aroma baijiu brand under Sichuan Yibin Wuliangye Group, named for five grains such as sorghum, rice, glutinous rice, wheat and corn. This page covers brand structure, not the product page's flavour detail.
- [Xifengjiu](brand/xifeng.md) — A feng-aroma baijiu brand from Fengxiang, Shaanxi, held by Shaanxi Xifeng Liquor Group Co., Ltd.; its range covers Hong Xifeng, Zhencang, Jiuhai Chencang, the old green bottle, Huashan Lun Jian and Guohua Ci, from mass-market liquor to premium gifting.
- [Xijiu](brand/xijiu.md) — A sauce-aroma baijiu brand from Xishui, Guizhou, held by Guizhou Xijiu Co., Ltd. Its plant sits by the Erlangtan stretch of the middle Chishui River, and key products include Junpin Xijiu and Xijiu Jiaocang 1988.
- [Yanghe](brand/yanghe.md) — A strong-aroma baijiu brand within the Jiangsu Yanghe Group system, known for its "mellow-soft" style and Blue Classic series, including Hai Zhi Lan, Tian Zhi Lan and Meng Zhi Lan.
- [Yingjia Gongjiu](brand/yingjia-gongjiu.md) — A strong-aroma baijiu brand from Huoshan, Anhui, publicly positioned as the Chinese people's welcoming liquor, built around ecological brewing and cave storage; the range covers Master Edition, Yingjia Dongcang, Gold and Silver Star, Bainian Yingjia and Yingjia Gufang.

## Cocktails

- [Americano](cocktail/americano.md) — A low-alcohol aperitif of Campari, sweet vermouth and soda water, dating to 1860s Milan. The balance of bitter, sweet and bubbles makes it the direct predecessor of the negroni.
- [Aperol Spritz](cocktail/aperol-spritz.md) — A low-alcohol aperitif of Aperol, Prosecco and soda water in the classic three-two-one ratio, defined by orange peel, gentle bitterness, sweetness and persistent bubbles.
- [Aviation](cocktail/aviation.md) — Recorded in 1916 by Hugo Ensslin of New York's Hotel Wallick in Recipes for Mixed Drinks, the Aviation combines gin, maraschino, lemon juice and crème de violette; it is pale violet to grey-blue and usually garnished with a cherry.
- [Bee's Knees](cocktail/bees-knees.md) — A Prohibition-era gin sour in which honey syrup replaces sugar, with sweetness and texture set by the honey-to-water ratio.
- [Bellini](cocktail/bellini.md) — A Venetian classic of Prosecco and white peach purée, usually dated to Harry's Bar in 1948 and credited to Giuseppe Cipriani; the ratio is commonly two parts Prosecco to one part purée, with peach season and temperature deciding the flavour.
- [Between the Sheets](cocktail/between-the-sheets.md) — A classic of white rum, cognac, orange liqueur and lemon juice, sitting between the Sidecar and the margarita. The name belongs to Paris bar culture and carries a deliberate ambiguity.
- [Blood and Sand](cocktail/blood-and-sand.md) — A classic of equal parts Scotch, cherry liqueur, sweet vermouth and orange juice, shaken and deep red. Named after the 1922 bullfighting film, it is rich and balanced.
- [Bloody Mary](cocktail/bloody-mary.md) — A long drink of vodka, tomato juice, lemon, Worcestershire sauce, hot sauce, salt and pepper, garnished with celery; its origin is often traced to Harry's New York Bar in Paris, though the people and dates are disputed.
- [Boulevardier](cocktail/boulevardier.md) — A Paris classic of whiskey, Campari and sweet vermouth, first printed in 1927, with equal-parts and 1.5:1:1 versions both in use.
- [Brandy Alexander](cocktail/brandy-alexander.md) — A dessert cocktail of equal parts brandy, white crème de cacao and cream, shaken and topped with grated nutmeg. Its name and origin are disputed, but it is a fixture of early-twentieth-century American bar culture.
- [Caipirinha](cocktail/caipirinha.md) — Brazil's national cocktail, made by muddling cachaça, lime and sugar over ice; the IBA recipe uses 60 ml cachaça, one lime and two teaspoons of sugar, and muddling and ice decide the result.
- [Champagne Cocktail](cocktail/champagne-cocktail.md) — A classical cocktail of a sugar cube, Angostura bitters and Champagne, with optional cognac. Bubbles rise from the cube, and the bitters' spice meets the wine's acidity.
- [Clover Club](cocktail/clover-club.md) — A classic cocktail of gin, lemon juice, raspberry syrup and egg white, shaken to a fine foam; balanced sour-sweet and usually garnished with a raspberry; the name comes from an American club of the same name in the early 20th century.
- [Corpse Reviver #2](cocktail/corpse-reviver-2.md) — A classic sour of equal parts gin, orange liqueur, Lillet Blanc and lemon juice, with a rinse of absinthe and an anise-citrus character.
- [Cosmopolitan](cocktail/cosmopolitan.md) — A classic cocktail of citron vodka, Cointreau, lime juice and cranberry juice, shaken and strained into a glass; tart and refreshing, pink in colour, commonly garnished with an orange twist or lime slice.
- [Daiquiri](cocktail/daiquiri.md) — A classic sour of white rum, lime juice and fine sugar, shaken and strained. Its structure is so simple that any imbalance in acidity, dilution or temperature shows at once.
- [Dark 'n' Stormy](cocktail/dark-n-stormy.md) — A Bermuda classic of dark rum, ginger beer and lime, with a trademark held by Gosling's. Ginger's heat against the rum's caramel makes a strong contrast, and the simple build is very sensitive to the quality of the ginger beer.
- [Dry Martini](cocktail/dry-martini.md) — A classic cocktail of gin and dry vermouth, stirred and strained into a chilled glass, garnished with lemon peel or olives. Dryness is a matter of vermouth ratio, not the name.
- [Espresso Martini](cocktail/espresso-martini.md) — A modern classic of vodka, coffee liqueur, espresso and syrup, hard-shaken and served with a dense coffee foam and three coffee beans.
- [French 75](cocktail/french-75.md) — A sparkling short drink of gin, lemon, sugar and Champagne, commonly traced to Harry's New York Bar in Paris during the First World War and named after a French 75 mm field gun.
- [Gimlet](cocktail/gimlet.md) — A short drink of gin and lime cordial, or gin with fresh lime and syrup, whose acidity, sweetness and texture depend on which lime you use.
- [Gin Fizz](cocktail/gin-fizz.md) — A short drink of gin, lemon juice, syrup and soda water; the IBA version uses 45 ml gin, 30 ml lemon juice and 10 ml syrup, shaken and strained into a slim glass, topped with a splash of soda and served without ice; egg white makes a Silver Fizz, cream and orange flower water a Ramos Gin Fizz.
- [Grasshopper](cocktail/grasshopper.md) — A dessert cocktail of green crème de menthe, white crème de cacao and cream, shaken into a pale green drink that tastes of mint chocolate. The New Orleans origin is the best-known account.
- [Hemingway Daiquiri](cocktail/hemingway-daiquiri.md) — White rum, grapefruit, lime and maraschino with no sugar, dry and strong, tied to Havana's El Floridita and the "Papa Doble".
- [Irish Coffee](cocktail/irish-coffee.md) — A hot drink of coffee, Irish whiskey, brown sugar and lightly whipped cream, layered so the cream floats. It began at Foynes airport in Ireland in the 1940s; temperature, sugar and cream texture decide the result.
- [Kir Royale](cocktail/kir-royale.md) — A French classic of crème de cassis and Champagne, descended from the Kir of Dijon. Deep red, finely bubbled and sharply balanced between sweet and sour, it is one of the simplest Champagne cocktails.
- [The Last Word](cocktail/last-word.md) — An equal-parts cocktail of gin, green Chartreuse, maraschino liqueur and lime juice, defined by herbal intensity, cherry-pit aroma and acidity, and a symbol of the 2000s modern-classic revival.
- [Long Island Iced Tea](cocktail/long-island-iced-tea.md) — A highball of five spirits, lemon juice, syrup and cola that looks like iced tea and is far stronger than it appears. The origin story in 1970s Long Island is disputed.
- [Mai Tai](cocktail/mai-tai.md) — The tiki classic created by Trader Vic in Oakland in 1944; the IBA version pairs 30 ml Jamaican rum and 30 ml Martinique molasses rum with orange liqueur, orgeat and lime, shaken and poured unstrained into a double rocks glass with pineapple, mint and lime peel.
- [Manhattan](cocktail/manhattan.md) — A classic cocktail of rye whiskey, sweet vermouth and bitters, stirred and strained into a glass, garnished with a cherry. The structure is simple, with sweetness and spice unfolding in layers.
- [Margarita](cocktail/margarita.md) — A classic cocktail of tequila, orange liqueur and fresh lime juice, shaken and served with an optional salt rim. Sweetness, sourness and salt are part of the structure.
- [Martinez](cocktail/martinez.md) — A classic of gin, sweet vermouth, maraschino and orange bitters, dating to the 1880s in the United States. It bridges the Manhattan and the dry martini, with a delicate bitter-sweet balance.
- [Mimosa](cocktail/mimosa.md) — A brunch cocktail of equal parts sparkling wine and orange juice, low in alcohol and light on bubbles; its name comes from a yellow flower, and its simple formula is sensitive to temperature and ratio.
- [Mint Julep](cocktail/mint-julep.md) — A classic cocktail of bourbon, mint and sugar served over crushed ice in a filled glass, usually garnished with a mint sprig; cool and sweet, and a signature drink of American Southern horse-racing culture.
- [Mojito](cocktail/mojito.md) — A long drink of white rum, lime, mint, cane sugar and soda. Press the mint, use plenty of ice, and let acidity, sweetness and bubbles decide whether it refreshes.
- [Moscow Mule](cocktail/moscow-mule.md) — A long drink of vodka, ginger beer and lime, balanced by ginger heat, carbonation and fresh lime acidity, usually served over ice in a copper mug or highball glass.
- [Negroni](cocktail/negroni.md) — A classic cocktail of equal parts gin, bitter aperitif and sweet vermouth. Cocktail identity and recipe versions are recorded separately.
- [Old Fashioned](cocktail/old-fashioned.md) — A classic cocktail of whiskey, sugar, bitters and water. A little sugar and water open up the spirit, and the simple structure lets the whiskey speak for itself.
- [Old Pal](cocktail/old-pal.md) — A classic cocktail of equal parts rye whiskey, Campari and dry vermouth, stirred and strained into a chilled glass, usually garnished with lemon peel; bitter and dry in style.
- [Paloma](cocktail/paloma.md) — A refreshing long drink of tequila, grapefruit and lime, defined by grapefruit bittersweetness, an optional salt rim and lively carbonation.
- [Paper Plane](cocktail/paper-plane.md) — A modern classic of equal parts bourbon, Aperol, amaro and lemon juice, balancing bittersweet herbs with bright citrus.
- [Penicillin](cocktail/penicillin.md) — A modern classic of blended Scotch, lemon, honey-ginger syrup and a float of Islay whisky, recognised by its smoke and ginger heat.
- [Piña Colada](cocktail/pina-colada.md) — A tropical cocktail of white rum, coconut cream and pineapple juice. A version existed in the 1930s, the modern drink was fixed at the Caribe Hilton in Puerto Rico in 1954, and sweetness and ice decide whether it works.
- [Pink Lady](cocktail/pink-lady.md) — A classic sour of gin, applejack, lemon juice, grenadine and egg white; the pink comes from grenadine and the egg white gives a fine foam. The name is usually linked to the 1911 Broadway musical The Pink Lady.
- [Pisco Sour](cocktail/pisco-sour.md) — A sour of pisco, lime juice, syrup and egg white, shaken into a silky foam and finished with a few dashes of Angostura bitters.
- [Ramos Gin Fizz](cocktail/ramos-gin-fizz.md) — A New Orleans classic of gin, citrus, cream, egg white and orange flower water, shaken long and topped with soda, with a cloud of foam. Henry C. Ramos fixed it at Meyer's Restaurant in 1888.
- [Sazerac](cocktail/sazerac.md) — A classic cocktail originating in New Orleans, made with cognac, a sugar cube and Peychaud's bitters in the IBA version, with an absinthe rinse; the rye whiskey version is the post-Civil War substitution tradition.
- [Sidecar](cocktail/sidecar.md) — A classic sour of cognac, orange liqueur and fresh lemon juice, usually built at 50:20:20, with an optional sugar rim.
- [Singapore Sling](cocktail/singapore-sling.md) — A classic cocktail of gin, cherry liqueur, citrus juice, pineapple juice and a little bitters, shaken and strained into a hurricane glass; the IBA version contains no soda and is garnished with pineapple and a maraschino cherry.
- [Tequila Sunrise](cocktail/tequila-sunrise.md) — A layered highball of tequila, orange juice and grenadine, shading from orange to red. It became popular in 1970s California and reached a wide audience through a rock band's tour.
- [Tom Collins](cocktail/tom-collins.md) — A long drink of Old Tom gin, lemon juice, syrup and soda water; the IBA lists it under John Collins (45 ml gin, 30 ml lemon juice, 15 ml syrup, 60 ml soda), notes Old Tom for a true Tom Collins, and serves it over ice in a Collins glass with lemon and cherry.
- [Vieux Carré](cocktail/vieux-carre.md) — A New Orleans classic of rye whiskey, Cognac, sweet vermouth, Bénédictine and two bitters, defined by its double base and herbal sweetness.
- [Whiskey Sour](cocktail/whiskey-sour.md) — A classic cocktail of whiskey, lemon juice and sugar syrup, shaken and served, optionally with an egg-white foam; commonly garnished with a lemon slice or cherry.
- [Zombie](cocktail/zombie.md) — A tiki classic created by Donn Beach in Hollywood in 1934, built on Jamaican, Puerto Rican and Demerara rums with falernum, Donn's Mix and grenadine; strong and usually garnished with a mint sprig.

## Drinks

- [Penfolds Grange](drink/australia-penfolds-grange.md) — The flagship wine of Penfolds in Australia, first made by Max Schubert in 1951 from mostly Shiraz with a little Cabernet Sauvignon, aged in American oak.
- [Erguotou](drink/beijing-erguotou.md) — A traditional Beijing baijiu process and product category, named for the spirit taken from the second distillation pot; light-aroma and direct, commonly 43% to 56% ABV.
- [Chimay Blue](drink/belgium-chimay-blue.md) — A dark Trappist ale from Scourmont Abbey in Belgium, 9% ABV and refermented in the bottle for about 21 days; known for dried fruit, caramel, cocoa and spice, with the 75 cl bottle also called Grande Réserve.
- [Lindemans Framboise](drink/belgium-lindemans-framboise.md) — A raspberry lambic from the Lindemans family brewery in the Pajottenland, Belgium, made on a spontaneously fermented lambic base with raspberries added. Commonly 2.5%–4% ABV in 250 mL or 750 mL bottles; the style is sweet and differs from traditional dry lambic.
- [Orval](drink/belgium-orval.md) — The single Trappist ale made at Orval Abbey in Belgium, commonly 5.2% ABV in 330 mL bottles, dry-hopped and fermented again in the bottle. Known for floral, citrus and dry notes with a light acidity, its character changes with bottle age.
- [Rochefort 10](drink/belgium-rochefort-10.md) — A dark Trappist ale from Abbaye Notre-Dame de Saint-Rémy in Belgium, 11.3% ABV in 33 cl bottles; the number 10 refers to an old Belgian wort-density unit, not alcohol, and the beer is known for roasted, coffee, chocolate and spice notes.
- [Westmalle Tripel](drink/belgium-westmalle-tripel.md) — A pale Trappist tripel from Westmalle Abbey in Belgium, commonly 9.5% ABV in 330 mL bottles and fermented again in the bottle. Known for banana, citrus, spice and a dry bitterness, it is a benchmark of the tripel style.
- [Westvleteren 12](drink/belgium-westvleteren-12.md) — A dark quadrupel brewed by the Sint-Sixtus Abbey in Vleteren, West Flanders, Belgium, usually 10.2% vol in 330 mL and bottle-conditioned; sold only in limited quantities at the abbey, it shows dark fruit, caramel and spice and is one of the scarcest Trappist beers.
- [Changyu Jiebaina](drink/changyu-jiebaina.md) — A dry red wine series from Changyu, first made in Yantai in 1931, commonly 750 mL with about 12%-13% ABV; Jiebaina is a product-series name, not a grape variety.
- [Almaviva](drink/chile-alma-viva.md) — A Bordeaux-style red from Puente Alto in Chile's Maipo Valley, founded in 1997 by Viña Concha y Toro and Baron Philippe de Rothschild SA; sixty-five hectares of Andean alluvium, a Cabernet Sauvignon-led blend and the EPU second wine explain its difference from Opus One.
- [Crystal Jiannanchun](drink/crystal-jiannanchun.md) — The core strong-aroma product of Jiannanchun, commonly called Crystal Sword in the market; usually 52% ABV in 500 mL and made in Mianzhu, Sichuan. This page covers raw materials, process, specification and buying at product level.
- [Bacardí Carta Blanca](drink/cuba-bacardi-carta-blanca.md) — A white rum made from molasses, fermented, distilled and charcoal-filtered, commonly 40% ABV, and a base for Mojitos and Daiquiris.
- [Havana Club 7](drink/cuba-havana-club-7-year-old.md) — A Cuban rum brand; molasses-based rums are blended and aged, commonly 40% ABV, with vanilla, caramel and tropical-fruit notes.
- [Pilsner Urquell](drink/czech-pilsnerurquell.md) — A pale lager from Plzeň, Czechia, first brewed in 1842 with soft water, Saaz hops and triple decoction, about 4.4% ABV.
- [Dongjiu Baicaoxiang](drink/dongjiu-baicaoxiang.md) — A high-end Dongjiu product in the Dong aroma type, commonly 54% ABV in 500 mL, made with herbs in qu, dual small and large qu fermentation and double-mash steam distillation; the Dong aroma type was formalised by a Guizhou local standard in 2008.
- [Aloxe-Corton](drink/france-aloxe-corton.md) — A village appellation at the northern end of the Côte de Beaune, Burgundy, France, with about 118.5 hectares of village-level red and only 1.4 hectares of village-level white; the commune also contains part of Corton, the Côte de Beaune's only red-led grand cru, and of the white Corton-Charlemagne. Reds are structured and earthy, whites are scarce and come mostly from grand cru parcels, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Alsace Gewürztraminer](drink/france-alsace-gewurztraminer.md) — A Gewürztraminer white wine from the Alsace region of France, labelled by variety and available as dry, late-harvest (VT) and botrytised selection (SGN) styles. Known for lychee, rose, ginger and cinnamon aromas, with low acidity and bold aroma; commonly 750 mL, dry styles about 12.5%–14% vol.
- [Alsace Riesling](drink/france-alsace-riesling.md) — A Riesling white wine from Alsace, France, mostly dry, with high acidity and clear aromatics of citrus, white flowers and flint, developing a petrol note with age. Alsace labels wines by variety and has Grand Cru, late-harvest and botrytised designations; commonly 750 mL at about 12%–13.5% vol.
- [Arbois](drink/france-arbois.md) — One of the Jura's earliest appellations, recognised on 15 May 1936 and covering 12 villages and about 766 hectares; all five Jura varieties are permitted, reds account for roughly 70% of Jura red output, and the area is known for the coexistence of vin jaune, vin de paille and ouillé whites.
- [Bandol](drink/france-bandol.md) — An appellation on the Mediterranean coast of Provence in France, best known for red wines based on Mourvèdre, with a legal minimum of 50% Mourvèdre and at least 18 months in oak; the reds are structured with dark fruit and spice and also age well, and the appellation makes rosé and a little white wine, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–14.5% vol.
- [Bourgueil](drink/france-bourgueil.md) — A Cabernet Franc red wine appellation in the Touraine area of the Loire Valley, France, on the right bank of the Loire west of Tours; recognised on 31 July 1937, with roughly 1,200–1,400 hectares divided between gravel terraces and clay-limestone slopes, the former supple and early-drinking, the latter firm and long-lived, plus about 4% rosé, commonly 750 mL at about 12%–13.5% vol.
- [Brouilly](drink/france-brouilly.md) — A cru-level red wine from southern Beaujolais, France, made from Gamay, with vineyards circling Mont Brouilly across six villages. It is the largest of the ten crus, known for light fruit and relatively early drinking, commonly 750 mL at about 12.5%–13.5% vol.
- [Chablis](drink/france-chablis.md) — A dry white wine from the Chablis appellation in northern Burgundy, France, made only from Chardonnay and known for Kimmeridgian limestone soils and a lean, mineral style. Commonly 750 mL at about 12%–13% vol, with Petit Chablis, Chablis, Premier Cru and Grand Cru tiers.

- [Hops](ingredient/hop.md) — Hops are the female flower cones of a perennial climbing plant in the Cannabaceae family; alpha acids isomerise during the boil to give bitterness, essential oils give aroma, and polyphenols and antibacterial compounds affect foam, clarity and shelf life.

- [Sorghum](ingredient/sorghum.md) — An annual cereal of the genus Sorghum and one of the main grain ingredients in Chinese baijiu; the balance of starch, protein, fat and tannin in the grain shapes fermentation and flavour.
- [Sugarcane](ingredient/sugarcane.md) — Sugarcane is a tall grass of the genus Saccharum whose stems store sucrose; pressing the juice and fermenting it directly yields cane-juice rum and Brazilian cane spirit, while refining sugar first and fermenting the molasses gives the other major rum route.
- [Water](ingredient/water.md) — The most easily overlooked yet ubiquitous ingredient in brewing; its minerals, hardness and pH affect saccharification, fermentation, microbial activity and final flavour, and have long shaped where distilleries and breweries are sited.
- [Wheat](ingredient/wheat.md) — An annual cereal of the genus Triticum, used as a staple food and as a key ingredient in qu making, multi-grain baijiu, beer and whisky; the balance of protein and starch shapes both qu quality and the body of the drink.
- [Yeast](ingredient/yeast.md) — Yeast is a single-celled fungus that turns sugar into ethanol and carbon dioxide and produces esters, higher alcohols and other flavour compounds; strain, pitching method and temperature steer fermentation, making yeast the most important and most overlooked variable in brewing.

## Processes

- [Blending](process/blending.md) — Combining liquids from different batches, vintages, varieties or cask types in set proportions to stabilise quality, balance structure or build a fixed style; wine, Champagne, whisky, brandy and baijiu follow different rules and logics.
- [Distillation](process/distillation.md) — A process that separates and concentrates ethanol from a fermented liquid or mash by exploiting differences in boiling points; equipment, reflux and cut points shape the spirit's flavour.
- [Fermentation](process/fermentation.md) — The metabolic process in which yeast or bacteria convert sugar into ethanol, carbon dioxide and flavour compounds under low oxygen; strain, temperature, sugar concentration, pH and oxygen set speed, strength and aroma.
- [Fortification](process/fortification.md) — The addition of grape spirit to wine to raise alcohol and stabilise the wine; fortifying mid-fermentation retains residual sugar, while fortifying after fermentation gives a dry fortified style, so timing and proportion decide the result.
- [Kimoto and yamahai](process/kimoto-yamahai.md) — Two traditional sake-mother routes that rely on natural lactic acid bacteria; kimoto keeps the yamafuri mashing step while yamahai omits it, and both differ from the sokujo route that adds lactic acid, shaping acidity, umami and production time.
- [Lagering](process/lagering.md) — The cold-storage stage after primary fermentation, in which yeast takes up diacetyl, suspended solids settle, sulfur compounds disperse and natural carbonation settles; the temperature curve, time and dissolved oxygen decide the cleanliness and shelf life of lager.
- [Lees ageing](process/lees-ageing.md) — An ageing method that keeps wine in contact with the fine lees left after fermentation; stirring frequency, temperature and time bring roundness, creamy and bready notes and a reductive barrier, but poor handling produces sulfides and bitterness.
- [Malolactic fermentation](process/malolactic-fermentation.md) — A secondary fermentation in which lactic acid bacteria convert malic acid into lactic acid and carbon dioxide; it lowers titratable acidity, raises pH and softens texture, adding creamy notes in moderation but volatile acidity, biogenic amines and haze when uncontrolled.
- [Malting](process/malting.md) — The pre-treatment that steeps, germinates, kilns and de-roots barley and other grains; the degree of germination and the kilning curve set amylase power, modification and colour, and therefore saccharification efficiency and finished flavour.
- [Mixed steaming and distillation](process/mixed-steaming.md) — A solid-state baijiu arrangement in which fresh grain and fermented mash are steamed in the same vessel and spirit is collected in one pass, as opposed to separate steaming; it usually works with continuous mash replenishment and shapes grain aroma, body and pit cycling.
- [Oak ageing](process/oak-ageing.md) — Storing wine or spirit in oak casks so that wood, trace oxygen and time change aroma, tannin, colour and texture; species, cask size, age and toast level are adjustable variables.
- [Pressing](process/pressing.md) — Pressing separates juice or wine from skins, seeds and stems. White wines are usually pressed before fermentation, red wines after maceration or fermentation, and the pressure curve and fraction cuts decide yield, tannin and body.
- [Qu making](process/qu-making.md) — A traditional process that cultures moulds, yeasts and bacteria on grain to make a saccharifying and fermenting starter; qu quality and microbial community directly affect yield and flavour in baijiu and yellow wine.
- [Saccharification](process/saccharification.md) — The process that breaks starch in grains and other raw materials into fermentable sugars; malt enzymes, mould enzymes from qu, temperature, pH and the water-to-grain ratio set the sugar profile and shape body, yield and fermentation.
- [Solid-state fermentation](process/solid-state-fermentation.md) — Fermentation carried out on a low-moisture solid grain mash, the basis of solid-state baijiu; unlike semi-solid and liquid routes, vessels, qu and temperature management shape the flavour.
- [Stacked fermentation](process/stacked-fermentation.md) — An open process step in baijiu production in which qu-inoculated mash is piled for a period before pit loading, allowing natural warming and microbial multiplication. Common in sauce-aroma baijiu.

## Standards

- [Bordeaux AOC Decree](standard/bordeaux-aoc.md) — Bordeaux is a regional appellation, recognised in 1936, with a specification approved in September 2025. It sets grape varieties, yield ceilings, pruning and labelling for red, white and rosé wines, and sits above dozens of more specific village-level AOCs.
- [Burgundy AOC Decree](standard/burgundy-aoc.md) — Burgundy has 84 appellations in three tiers — regional, village and grand cru — with parcels traced down to historical units called climats. In 2015 the climats were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage site, giving a set of vineyard names a cultural identity.
- [Champagne AOC: The Appellation Rules](standard/champagne-aoc-decree.md) — Champagne was recognised as an appellation d'origine contrôlée on 29 June 1936, with its area delimited by the law of 22 July 1927 and supervised today by INAO. The current specification requires seven main varieties, in-bottle second fermentation and at least 15 months' ageing for non-vintage and 36 months for vintage Champagne.
- [Cognac AOC Decree](standard/cognac-aoc.md) — Cognac was among the first French wine regions to be delimited, and its current product specification was approved and published in October 2022. It fixes six growing crus, permitted varieties, double copper-still distillation and oak ageing, and writes VS, VSOP and XO into law.
- [Regulation (EU) 2019/787 on Spirit Drinks](standard/eu-spirits-regulation-2019-787.md) — Adopted on 17 April 2019 and applicable from 25 May 2021, this framework regulation replaced Regulation (EC) No 110/2008. It defines spirit drinks, sets a 15% vol floor, lists 44 categories in Annex I, and governs legal names, labelling, age statements and geographical indications.
- [EU Wine Regulation 1308/2013](standard/eu-wine-regulation-1308-2013.md) — The European Union's framework for the common organisation of the wine market. Adopted in December 2013 and applicable from January 2014, it harmonises planting authorisations, geographical indications, labelling and trade. It does not tell a winemaker how to ferment; it defines when a wine may use a name, and what the bottle must disclose.
- [GB 15037-2006 Wines](standard/gb-15037-2006.md) — Published on 2006-12-11 and effective from 2008-01-01, this wine standard replaced GB/T 15037-1994; it classifies wine by colour, sugar and carbon dioxide, and sets a minimum alcohol of 7.0%vol plus dry extract, volatile acid, citric acid, methanol and preservative limits. It was issued as a partly mandatory GB 15037, but the 2017 No. 7 announcement converted it in full to recommended GB/T 15037 with the number and year unchanged.
- [GB 2757-2012 National food safety standard — Distilled spirits and compounded spirits](standard/gb-2757-2012.md) — A mandatory national food safety standard published on 2012-08-06 and effective from 2013-02-01, replacing GB 2757-1981; it covers distilled spirits and compounded spirits and limits methanol to 0.6 g/L for grain-based products and 2.0 g/L for other raw materials, and cyanide as HCN to 8.0 mg/L, both calculated on a 100% alcohol basis. It also requires alcohol content and the warning about excessive drinking on labels. Lead is not set here; it falls under GB 2762.
- [GB/T 10781.1-2021 Quality requirements for baijiu — Part 1: Nongxiangxing baijiu](standard/gbt-10781-1-2021.md) — The current Chinese national standard for Nongxiangxing (strong-aroma) baijiu, published on 2021-03-09 and effective from 2022-04-01; it fully replaces GB/T 10781.1-2006 and forms Part 1 of the "Quality requirements for baijiu" series.
- [GB/T 10781.2-2022 Product quality requirements for baijiu — Part 2: Light-aroma baijiu](standard/gbt-10781-2-2022.md) — Published on 2022-07-11 and effective from 2023-02-01, this quality requirement replaces GB/T 10781.2-2006; it removes the high-strength and low-strength product classification, introduces special, premium and first grades, sets alcohol at 21.0%vol to 69.0%vol, and applies different acid and ester indices within one year and after one year of production.
- [GB/T 10781.4-2024 Product quality requirements for baijiu — Part 4: Sauce-aroma baijiu](standard/gbt-10781-4-2024.md) — Published on 2024-05-28 and effective from 2025-06-01, this quality requirement fully replaces GB/T 26760-2011; it writes high-temperature qu production, two rounds of grain feeding and multi-round stacking and pit fermentation into the standard, converts acidity and ester indices to a 53.0%vol basis, and sets separate limits for the first year after production and for later storage.
- [GB/T 13662-2018 Huangjiu](standard/gbt-13662-2018.md) — The current Chinese national standard for Huangjiu (yellow wine), published on 2018-09-17 and effective from 2019-04-01; it fully replaces GB/T 13662-2008.
- [GB/T 14867-2007 Feng-flavour baijiu](standard/gbt-14867-2007.md) — A 2007 national standard for Feng-flavour baijiu replacing GB/T 14867-1994; its compound aroma is led by ethyl acetate with ethyl hexanoate as support, earthen pit fermentation and jiu-hai storage are process markers, and Shaanxi Xifeng is the representative product. The standard was replaced in full by GB/T 10781.6-2025 with effect from 2026-08-01.
- [GB/T 15109-2021 Terminology of baijiu industry](standard/gbt-15109-2021.md) — The current Chinese national standard for baijiu industry terminology, published on 2021-05-21 and effective from 2022-06-01; it fully replaces GB/T 15109-2008.
- [GB/T 17204-2021 Terminology and classification of alcoholic beverages](standard/gbt-17204-2021.md) — The current Chinese national standard for alcoholic-beverage terminology and classification, published on 2021-05-21 and effective from 2022-06-01; it fully replaces GB/T 17204-2008.
- [GB/T 23547-2009 Strong-sauce mixed-aroma baijiu](standard/gbt-23547-2009.md) — Published on 2009-04-14 and effective from 2009-12-01, this standard defines strong-sauce mixed-aroma baijiu by a harmonious combination of strong and sauce character, classifies it by alcohol content into high-strength and low-strength liquor with premium and first grades, and lists Hubei Baiyunbian and Anhui Kouzijiao among representative products. It was fully replaced by GB/T 10781.8-2021 with effect from 2022-06-01.
- [GB/T 26760-2011 Jiang-flavour Chinese spirits](standard/gbt-26760-2011.md) — The 2011 Chinese national standard for Jiang-flavour (sauce-aroma) baijiu, published on 2011-07-20 and effective from 2011-12-01; it was withdrawn on 2025-06-01 and fully replaced by GB/T 10781.4-2024.
- [GB/T 4927-2008 Beer](standard/gbt-4927-2008.md) — The Chinese beer product standard, published on 2008-12-29 and effective from 2009-10-01, replacing GB 4927-2001; it defines beer as a low-alcohol carbonated fermented drink made mainly from malt and water with hops and yeast, classifies it by colour and special styles, and sets alcohol, original wort extract, total acid, carbon dioxide and diacetyl limits. Only clauses 3.1 and 8.1 were originally mandatory, and the whole standard became recommended GB/T in 2017.
- [China GI protection](standard/china-geographical-indication.md) — The Measures for the Protection of GI Products, in force from 2024-02-01, set product protection, the special mark and four characteristics; it is a different track from GI marks and agricultural GIs.
- [German Wine Law](standard/german-wine-law.md) — Germany's wine law was last overhauled in 2021, with a new origin hierarchy applying from the 2026 harvest. It keeps four quality tiers and six Prädikat levels while shifting emphasis from ripeness to place, organised around 13 Anbaugebiete.
- [Italian DOCG System](standard/italian-docg.md) — DOCG is the top tier of the Italian wine pyramid. Created with the DOC system in 1963 and first awarded in 1980, it requires at least 7 years as a DOC, bottles of no more than 6 litres, batch-by-batch chemical and sensory testing, and a state seal.
- [Japan Liquor Tax Law](standard/japan-liquor-tax-law.md) — A 1953 statute defining liquor, classification, manufacturing licences and specific duty; a new rate table applies from 2026-10-01, and it is not a sake grading law.
- [Japanese Sake Labelling](standard/japan-sake-labelling.md) — Japan's National Tax Agency set the standards for sake quality labelling in 1989 by Notification No. 8. Eight specific names separate junmai, ginjo and honjozo styles, tied to polishing ratios, koji share and a cap on added brewing alcohol. The names are process claims, not a ladder of quality.
- [Tequila NOM-006](standard/nom-006-scfi-2012.md) — Mexican Official Standard NOM-006-SCFI-2012 sets the conditions for using the name tequila: one agave species, a protected territory of 181 municipalities, and certification by the Tequila Regulatory Council. Botany, geography and labelling are welded onto a single word.
- [Mezcal NOM-070](standard/nom-070-scfi-2016.md) — Mexican Official Standard NOM-070-SCFI-2016 defines mezcal as a 100% agave distillate and splits it into Mezcal, Artesanal and Ancestral according to the equipment used for cooking, crushing, fermentation and distillation. Craft stops being an adjective and becomes a checklist.
- [German Beer Purity Law](standard/reinheitsgebot.md) — On 23 April 1516 the Dukes of Bavaria issued an edict at Ingolstadt limiting beer to barley, hops and water. Its principle now sits in section 9 of the German Provisional Beer Law, making it one of the few early-modern ingredient rules still enforced.
- [The Scotch Whisky Regulations 2009 (SI 2009/2890)](standard/scotch-whisky-regulations-2009.md) — UK statutory instrument in force from 23 November 2009. It defines Scotch Whisky as distilled in Scotland, matured in oak casks for at least three years, distilled below 94.8% vol and bottled at no less than 40% vol, and lays down five categories and five protected area names.
- [Spanish DO System](standard/spanish-do.md) — Spain's Denominación de Origen system was set by the 2003 vine and wine law, with DO, DOCa, VP and IGP tiers. A region must first exist for 5 years as a geographical-indication wine before applying for DO; DOCa requires 10 years as a DO and bottling inside the zone.
- [US Distilled Spirits Labeling: 27 CFR 5](standard/us-27-cfr-5.md) — Title 27, Part 5 of the US Code of Federal Regulations, enforced by the Alcohol and Tobacco Tax and Trade Bureau. It uses standards of identity to define what whisky, bourbon, gin and vodka must be made from, and then dictates what the label has to say. Read it and a bottle's name stops being a marketing choice.
- [US AVA System](standard/us-ava.md) — American Viticultural Areas are the US geographical label system for wine, created in 1978 with the first AVA approved in 1980. An AVA only draws a boundary; it does not regulate varieties, yields or winemaking. To name an AVA, at least 85% of the grapes must come from it.

## Terms

- [Acidity](term/acidity.md) — Acidity describes the amount and strength of acids in a drink, commonly through total acidity in g/L and pH, two different measures; it supports freshness, balance and ageing potential, and affects sulfur dioxide effectiveness.
- [Alcohol by volume (ABV)](term/alcohol-by-volume.md) — ABV is the volume fraction of ethanol in a drink measured at 20 °C, usually written as % vol; it is a basic labelling and formulation parameter, not a quality score.
- [Angel's share](term/angels-share.md) — The angel's share is the portion of a spirit lost through the cask wall during maturation; the Scotch whisky trade often cites about 2% a year, but the real rate depends on temperature, humidity, cask size and warehouse type, and is neither a precise constant nor a quality grade.
- [Appellation](term/appellation.md) — An appellation is a legal designation that binds a geographical area to production rules, such as French AOC/AOP, Italian DOC/DOCG, Spanish DO and EU PDO/PGI; it states origin and rules, not a quality ranking.
- [Aroma](term/aroma.md) — Aroma is the volatile smell perceived by the nose, layered by origin into varietal, fermentation and maturation notes; it is not the same as flavour, and aroma is not a synonym for bouquet.
- [Barrel finishing](term/barrel-finishing.md) — Barrel finishing moves a drink from its main maturation container into a different cask for a further period to add or adjust flavour; it is secondary maturation, not full-term use of that cask, and not cask strength.
- [Blend](term/blend.md) — A blend combines different varieties, vintages, sites or base wines into a final product to balance structure, stabilise style or meet regulations; it is a normal craft, not a synonym for low quality or adulteration.
- [Body](term/body.md) — Body is the sensory impression of a drink's weight and fullness in the mouth, often described as light, medium or full; alcohol, glycerol, residual sugar, tannin, acidity and temperature all shape it.
- [Brix](term/brix.md) — Degrees Brix (°Brix) express the mass fraction of sucrose in a solution at 20 °C, where 1 °Brix is 1 g of sucrose per 100 g of solution; they measure sugar and soluble solids, not alcohol or sweetness.
- [Cask strength](term/cask-strength.md) — Cask strength means whisky bottled with little or no added water, retaining its natural strength from the cask, commonly around 50–65% vol; it is a bottling choice, not single cask or a quality grade.
- [Chill filtration](term/chill-filtering.md) — Cooling whisky so long-chain fatty-acid esters and proteins precipitate before filtration; it prevents cold haze, is an optional process, not a legal grade, and does not strip all flavour.
- [Clos](term/clos.md) — A clos is a vineyard plot enclosed by a wall, fence or natural boundary, common in Burgundy, the Loire and Alsace; it identifies a bounded site, is not a legal classification, and does not imply single ownership.
- [Column still](term/column-still.md) — A column still is a continuous distillation device that uses multiple plates or packing to create repeated vapour-liquid exchange and produce high-purity alcohol; plate count, reflux ratio and feed position decide purity and flavour retention.
- [Cork taint](term/cork-taint.md) — Cork taint is caused mainly by TCA and related haloanisoles, showing as damp cardboard and mustiness that mask fruit; it is perceptible at a few nanograms per litre but is not a safety issue and is not the same as cork crumbs.
- [Craft beer](term/craft-beer.md) — A small-scale, independent and tradition-minded beer production and consumption movement that emerged in the United States in the late 20th century; an industry and market concept, not a legal grade or a guarantee of quality.
- [Cuvée](term/cuvee.md) — Cuvée can mean the earliest, gentlest juice from sparkling-wine pressing, or a batch blended from several base wines; in most regions it is not a legal grade and often serves as a product name.
- [Decanting](term/decanting.md) — Decanting moves wine from its bottle into another vessel for two purposes — separating sediment and exposing the wine to air; old reds and young tannic wines have different goals, not every wine benefits, and timing depends on age and condition.
- [Disgorgement](term/disgorgement.md) — Disgorgement removes the yeast sediment from the neck of a traditional-method sparkling wine after riddling, by hand or with a frozen neck; dosage usually follows before the final closure.
- [Dosage](term/dosage.md) — Dosage is the topping liquid added after disgorging traditional-method sparkling wine, usually a blend of base wine and sugar; it replaces the volume lost at disgorging and sets the finished sweetness class, from brut nature to doux under EU rules.
- [Dry](term/dry.md) — Dry is a residual-sugar category on wine labels, usually no more than 4 g/L under EU rules and up to 9 g/L when acidity is high; it says nothing about fruit intensity or quality.
- [Fermentation pit](term/fermentation-pit.md) — A solid-state fermentation vessel for baijiu, usually built from yellow mud, stone, brick, concrete or wood and often rectangular. Pit type and pit-mud condition shape the microbial environment.
- [Filtration](term/filtration.md) — Filtration passes wine through a medium that physically retains particles and microorganisms; depth and surface filtration differ in structure, and filtration affects clarity, stability and flavour but is not the same as fining.
- [Fining](term/fining.md) — Fining adds bentonite, egg white, gelatin or synthetic polymers to wine so that they adsorb suspended or dissolved targets and settle out, removing protein, tannin, colour or off-flavours; it is a clarification and stabilisation step, not filtration, and raises vegan and allergen questions.
- [Finish](term/finish.md) — Finish is the taste, aroma and mouthfeel that persist after a drink is swallowed or spat out; length, layers and cleanliness determine its quality, and a long finish is not a quality guarantee.
- [Flor](term/flor.md) — Flor is the yeast film that forms on the surface of Sherry during biological ageing, shielding the wine from oxygen and metabolising alcohol and glycerol; Fino and Manzanilla depend on flor, while Oloroso follows an oxidative route.
- [Heads, hearts and tails](term/heads-hearts-tails.md) — A distillation run is split by volatility into heads, hearts and tails; hearts are the balanced middle cut and the body of the spirit, while heads and tails carry more impurities and are usually recycled, with cut points shaping style.
- [Hongqu](term/hongqu.md) — A purple-red qu made by culturing Monascus on rice; it is a saccharifying starter for huangjiu, a colouring material and a base for red-qu wine and foods, with citrinin risk requiring process control.
- [IBU (International Bitterness Units)](term/ibu.md) — IBU is the unit for beer bitterness, with 1 IBU roughly equal to 1 milligram of iso-alpha acids per litre; it measures hop-derived bitterness, not the bitterness felt on the palate, and not hop aroma.
- [Late harvest](term/late-harvest.md) — Late harvest is the practice of leaving grapes on the vine beyond normal ripeness so that water loss concentrates sugar and flavour; it can make sweet wine, but it is not the same as noble rot and not always sweet.
- [Lees](term/lees.md) — Lees are the solids, mostly spent yeast plus pulp residue and tartrates, that settle after fermentation; keeping fine lees and stirring them changes texture, aroma and stability.
- [Maceration](term/maceration.md) — Maceration is the contact of juice with skins, pulp, seeds or stems to extract colour, tannin and aroma; time, temperature and alcohol level decide how much, and in which direction, extraction happens.
- [Malolactic fermentation](term/malolactic-fermentation.md) — A secondary fermentation in which lactic acid bacteria convert malic acid into lactic acid and carbon dioxide, softening acidity; it differs from alcoholic fermentation and is not needed in every wine.
- [Mash](term/mash.md) — A mash is the mixture of milled malt and hot water in a mash tun, where enzymes convert starch into fermentable sugar. It is the precursor of wort, not wort itself, and its water-to-grist ratio, temperature and pH shape the sugar profile and the extract yield.
- [Mousse](term/mousse.md) — Mousse is the formation, bubble size and persistence of the foam in a glass of sparkling wine; bottle pressure is typically about 5–6 bar, and temperature, glassware, wine composition and lees ageing together shape the bubbles, which are not a direct quality score.
- [Must](term/must.md) — Must is the juice, pulp, skins and solids obtained from crushed or pressed grapes and the starting point of wine fermentation; its sugar, acid, solid content and clarity shape fermentation and style.
- [Noble rot](term/noble-rot.md) — Noble rot is infection of ripe grapes by Botrytis cinerea under humid nights and dry, sunny days, which shrinks the fruit and concentrates sugar, acid and flavour; it makes Sauternes and Tokaji-style sweet wines but differs from grey rot in persistently wet conditions.
- [Original gravity](term/original-gravity.md) — Original gravity is the concentration of soluble solids, mainly sugars and other extract, in wort before fermentation, usually expressed in °P (degrees Plato); it reflects raw-material extract and is not alcohol content.
- [Oxidation](term/oxidation.md) — Oxidation is the reaction of compounds in wine with oxygen, altering aroma, colour and tannin structure; a small controlled amount builds styles such as Oloroso, Madeira and vin jaune, while excess brings loss of fruit, browning and acetaldehyde.
- [Peat](term/peat.md) — Peat is partially decomposed plant matter formed in oxygen-poor bogs, and its smoke deposits phenols on kilned malt; peatiness is an optional whisky style, not a required feature of all Scotch whisky.
- [Peat phenol value (PPM)](term/peat-phenol-ppm.md) — Usually the total phenol content of malt, where 1 ppm is 1 mg/kg; it describes a malt smoking specification, not the concentration in the bottle or a legal peat grade.
- [Polishing ratio](term/polishing-ratio.md) — The polishing ratio is the percentage of sake rice remaining after milling, so 50% means half has been removed and half remains; it is tied to daiginjo, ginjo and honjozo grades but is not a direct quality score.
- [Pomace](term/pomace.md) — Pomace is the solid residue of skins, pulp, seeds and stems left after pressing grapes, distinct from lees; it can be distilled into grappa or marc and used to recover tartaric acid, but must be handled quickly to avoid acetification.
- [Pot still](term/pot-still.md) — A pot still is a batch-distillation copper vessel made of a heated pot, a neck and a condenser; pot shape, heating method and cut points decide the style of the spirit, trading efficiency for flavour retention against continuous distillation.
- [Standard strength and proof](term/proof-and-abv.md) — Standard strength is usually the volume fraction at 20°C; US proof doubles ABV at 60°F, so 100 proof is 50% ABV, while the old British system differs.
- [Reduction](term/reduction.md) — Reduction covers both reductive winemaking that keeps oxygen out and the hydrogen sulfide, thiols and disulfides formed in oxygen-poor conditions; the first is a style choice, the second a fault only at certain levels.
- [Residual sugar](term/residual-sugar.md) — Residual sugar is the sugar left after fermentation, usually measured in g/L; it sets sweetness labels, while perceived sweetness also depends on acidity, alcohol, temperature and bubbles.
- [Riddling](term/riddling.md) — Riddling (French remuage) gathers the yeast sediment of traditional-method sparkling wine at the bottle neck; hand riddling takes about 26–30 days, while a gyropalette can finish in a few days, with different trade-offs in consistency, cost and bottle-by-bottle judgement.
- [Semi-dry](term/semi-dry.md) — Semi-dry sits between dry and semi-sweet or sweet; the exact residual sugar varies by category and regulation, and sweetness cannot be judged by taste alone.
- [Single malt](term/single-malt.md) — Single malt means whisky from one distillery, made from 100% malted barley and distilled in pot stills; it differs from single grain and blended malt in raw material and scope, and while Scotland defines it in law, other markets mostly use the term as trade language.
- [Single varietal](term/single-varietal.md) — Single varietal describes a wine made mainly from one grape variety; whether the variety may appear on the label depends on national rules, with the EU and Australia usually requiring 85% and the United States 75%, so it is not a synonym for 100% of that variety.
- [Solera](term/solera.md) — A solera is a stacked system of fractional blending and ageing in which the oldest wine sits at the bottom and younger wine replenishes each level above; the finished wine has no single vintage and keeps a stable style, and it is not "padding with cheap wine".
- [Sweet](term/sweet.md) — Sweet is the highest residual-sugar category on wine labels, at 45 g/L or more for still wine under EU rules; sweetness can come from late harvest, noble rot, ice wine, fortification or dosage, and balance depends on acidity and alcohol.
- [Tannin](term/tannin.md) — Tannins are a group of polyphenolic compounds that bind proteins and create astringency; in wine they come mainly from skins, seeds, stems and oak.
- [Terroir](term/terroir.md) — Terroir is the combined effect of a place's natural conditions and long-standing human practice on the character of its produce; it is not a single soil measurement.
- [Vintage](term/vintage.md) — Vintage is the year the grapes were harvested and reflects that year's weather and ripening conditions; it is not age or bottling date, and non-vintage (NV) is not a sign of low quality.
- [Whole bunch](term/whole-bunch.md) — Whole-bunch fermentation puts intact grape clusters, stems included, into the fermenter; stems bring structure, spice and freshness but also raise pH and can add herbal notes, so the proportion depends on variety, ripeness and vintage.
- [Wort](term/wort.md) — Wort is the sugar-rich liquid separated from spent grain after mashing. It is the starting point of beer fermentation, not beer itself, and its composition depends on the grain, the mash schedule, the lautering and whether it is boiled with hops.
