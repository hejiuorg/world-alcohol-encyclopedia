# Greatwall Five-Star Cabernet Sauvignon

Greatwall Five-Star Cabernet Sauvignon is a [Cabernet Sauvignon](ref:cabernet) dry red wine in the five-star series from [Greatwall](ref:brand), commonly 750 mL with about 12%-13% ABV. The stars belong to the company's grading system, not a legal grade; origin, producer and batch should be checked on the bottle label.

## Definition and position

The wine belongs to the [red wine](ref:red-wine) category within [wine](ref:wine) and is made by companies within the [COFCO Greatwall Wine Co., Ltd.](ref:producer) system. Within the brand structure it serves business banquets and gifting: priced above everyday table wine and below top estate wine.

Three levels need separating: wine is the broad category, red wine is the category, and five-star Cabernet Sauvignon is a specific product. "Five-star" in the name is a company grade, while "Cabernet Sauvignon" is the variety; the two cannot be swapped, and the star rating cannot be used to infer the variety.

## Producer and brand

The wine is made by companies within the Greatwall brand system, carried by the [Greatwall](ref:brand) brand, with the platform described in [COFCO Greatwall Wine Co., Ltd.](ref:producer). Greatwall has production and sourcing bases in Shacheng and Changli in Hebei, Penglai in Shandong and Ningxia.

Company, brand and product are three levels: the brand answers "what is it called and who is it for", the platform answers "who holds the trademark and who carries legal responsibility", and the product answers the specific origin, vintage and grade. The producer on the label may be a subsidiary in a particular region, and the word "Greatwall" cannot replace it.

## Origin and variety

Five-star Cabernet Sauvignon commonly declares regions such as Shacheng (Huailai) in Hebei, to be confirmed on the label. Shacheng lies in north-western Hebei, with higher altitude, large day-night temperature swings and a relatively dry ripening season, which helps retain acidity and aroma.

Cabernet Sauvignon is a late-ripening variety with small berries and thick skins, rich in tannin and colour compounds, suited to skin fermentation and ageing. Ripeness in a given year affects concentration and acidity, and vintage differences cannot be hidden behind the brand name.

## Star system

Stars are Greatwall's product-grading method, using the number of stars to distinguish grades and price bands within a series. Five stars usually sit at the top of the star system, with packaging and channels oriented towards banquets and gifting.

The star rating belongs to the company system, not to national standards. Five-star products from different vintages and channels may differ in label and packaging, and batch and anti-counterfeit information should be checked.

## Production

The basic red-wine flow is: harvest, sorting, crushing, [fermentation](ref:fermentation) on the skins, pressing, clarification, ageing and [blending](ref:blending). Skin contact extracts colour, tannin and aroma, and fermentation temperature and maceration time affect the weight of the structure.

Some batches use [oak ageing](ref:oak-ageing) to add aroma layers and soften tannin; whether and for how long is a company process decision that labels may not fully disclose. Process descriptions only indicate a style route and cannot replace a judgment about a specific product.

## Specification and label

The common format is a 750 mL bottle, about 12%-13% ABV, as a dry red wine. Producer, origin, vintage, varieties, strength, net volume and standard on the label are the basics for identifying a product.

Star ratings, gift boxes and channel versions may use different packaging, and packaging differences do not equal differences in the liquid. Buyers should check label information and anti-counterfeit marks and avoid treating a promotional pack as a separate grade.

## Flavour and tasting

The aroma is led by blackcurrant, black cherry and green pepper, with vanilla, toast and cedar appearing after ageing. Tannin is evident on entry, acidity supports the structure, and the finish varies with vintage and grade.

Serve at about 16-18°C. Too warm accentuates alcohol, too cold suppresses aroma. After pouring, wait a moment for the wine to meet the air, then judge whether the aroma is open and whether the tannin is coarse.

## Drinking and pairing

Five-star Cabernet Sauvignon suits steak, roasted lamb, braised pork and hard cheese. Tannin binds with protein to soften astringency, while acidity cuts through fat. With spicy dishes, choose a batch with more obvious fruit and softer tannin.

After opening, refrigerate and finish within a few days. Red wine oxidises with prolonged air contact, and fruit aroma turns to sour and nutty notes; a vacuum stopper or decanting into a smaller bottle reduces oxidation.

## Buying and storage

Buy through regular channels and check anti-counterfeit marks, producer, origin, vintage and batch. Prices differ markedly by vintage and channel, and a promotional price does not represent long-term positioning.

For Greatwall Five-Star Cabernet Sauvignon, unopened red wine should be stored on its side, away from light, at a steady temperature, ideally 12-16°C, avoiding sharp temperature swings and strong odours. Home storage cannot replicate cellar conditions, so the year of purchase should not be equated simply with an improvement in quality.

## Common misconceptions

First, treating "five-star" as a legal grade. It belongs to the company's star system.
Second, treating five-star Cabernet Sauvignon as a single-variety estate wine. It may be a blend.
Third, taking the brand name as the origin. Origin is on the label.
Fourth, treating the star rating as a guarantee of vintage quality. Vintage and batch matter equally.
Fifth, judging quality by price or packaging. Labels and channels are more reliable.

Read Greatwall Five-Star Cabernet Sauvignon through the base and the stated strength, then the sweetness or bitterness, and finally how it is served. The sequence matters.
