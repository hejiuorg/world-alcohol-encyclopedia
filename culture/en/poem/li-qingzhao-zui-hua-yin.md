# Zui Hua Yin: Li Qingzhao and a Cup of Wine on the Double Ninth

This song is not about a banquet but about a whole day. From the thin mist and dense clouds of daylight to the chill of midnight, and then to a cup of wine at dusk, time is stretched long while the emotion hardly rises or falls. Li Qingzhao does not say whom she is waiting for, nor why she is sad; she sets out the incense burner indoors, the jade pillow on the bed, the wine by the fence and the chrysanthemum scent in her sleeve, and closes everything with one line: the person is thinner than the yellow flower.

## The song and a single day

Zui Hua Yin is the name of a ci tune, in two halves of five lines each. The whole song describes one Double Ninth festival. The Double Ninth falls on the ninth day of the ninth lunar month, and the old customs included climbing high, admiring chrysanthemums and drinking wine; the song has no climb, but it has chrysanthemums and wine, so the festival is clearly present.

Li Qingzhao was born in 1084 and her death is usually given as about 1155. Her early life was comfortable; later she lived through the fall of the Northern Song and the move south, and her style changed with those years. This song is generally taken to have been written after her marriage, on a Double Ninth when Zhao Mingcheng was away; but the exact date and whether it was occasioned by a particular journey are still discussed, and should not be stated too firmly. What is certain is that it belongs to her earlier work, delicate in tone, with the scene kept between room and courtyard.

## Text

> Thin mist and dense clouds, sorrow through the long day; borneol incense smoulders in the golden beast-shaped burner.
> The festival is again the Double Ninth; jade pillow and gauze curtain, at midnight the chill first pierces through.
>
> By the eastern fence I raise my cup after dusk; a hidden fragrance fills my sleeves.
> Do not say it does not wound the soul: the curtain rolls in the west wind, and the person is thinner than the yellow flower.

This is the received text. The golden beast smoulders also appears with a different character for smoulder, wound the soul also appears as melt the soul, and thinner than the yellow flower also appears as like the yellow flower. These variants are mostly substitutions of sound or near-synonym and do not change the overall image.

## The upper half: from daylight to midnight

The opening line, thin mist and dense clouds, sorrow through the long day, presses weather and mood together. The mist is thin, the clouds dense, the light unclear, and the day feels unusually long; sorrow here is not an abstract emotion but a sense of time: because there is nothing to do, or because the person is absent, the day becomes long.

“Borneol incense smoulders in the golden beast-shaped burner” gives the incense burner. Borneol is an aromatic resin, and the golden beast is a burner shaped like an animal; the incense slowly burns away in the burner, and this detail makes the long day concrete. Time is measured not by a clock but by a burner of incense going from full to empty.

“The festival is again the Double Ninth” marks the season. The word again is light but weighted: the Double Ninth comes once a year, so such a day has already passed once and again; a festival is meant to be lively, but for a person alone it feels empty instead.

“Jade pillow and gauze curtain, at midnight the chill first pierces through” moves the scene to the bed. The jade pillow is a ceramic pillow, the gauze curtain a thin bed curtain; the chill of midnight comes through, showing that autumn is deep and that the person has not slept soundly. The upper half has no wine, only daylight, incense, a festival and the chill of midnight, stretching out the waiting.

## The lower half: a cup of wine at dusk

The lower half begins with “By the eastern fence I raise my cup after dusk; a hidden fragrance fills my sleeves.” The eastern fence alludes to Tao Yuanming's line “picking chrysanthemums by the eastern fence”, so chrysanthemums and a fence belong together; raising the cup means holding wine, not drinking deeply; the phrase “after dusk” pushes the time to evening, just before the midnight chill of the upper half. A cup of wine here is not social but a private rite performed facing a bed of chrysanthemums.

“A hidden fragrance fills my sleeves” describes the scent of chrysanthemums. The fragrance does not rush at the face; it is hidden, and lingers lightly in the sleeve. The detail is small, but it gives the dusk a temperature: the wine is on the tongue, the fragrance is on the body, and neither makes a noise.

“Do not say it does not wound the soul: the curtain rolls in the west wind, and the person is thinner than the yellow flower” contains the three most famous lines of the song. Wounding the soul means extreme sorrow; the curtain rolls in the west wind brings wind indoors, the curtain lifted by the autumn wind; the person is thinner than the yellow flower compares a person to a chrysanthemum, whose petals are long and narrow, and the person is thinner still. Thinness is not a medical diagnosis but the shape left by longing and sleeplessness. Wine does not make a person warmer or fuller; it only holds this dusk up for a while.

## Wine, chrysanthemum and the person

First, wine is part of the festival. Drinking at the Double Ninth was an old custom, and the song need not explain it; but Li Qingzhao does not write a shared drink, she writes one person raising a cup, so the festival turns from a public custom into a private moment.

Second, wine is a measure of time. The upper half gives daylight and midnight, the lower half gives dusk; wine appears at dusk, in the middle of the day, and links what comes before and after.

Third, wine and chrysanthemum belong to the same cluster of images. Eastern fence, hidden fragrance, yellow flower are all chrysanthemum; wine places the person and the flower in one picture, and only then can the person be thinner than the yellow flower.

Fourth, wine solves nothing. It is not responsible for dispelling sorrow or for making anyone drunk; it simply gives this Double Ninth a movement at which to stop. For another way of writing one person with wine, compare Bai Juyi's [Asking Liu Shijiu](ref:asking-liu): that poem has a stove and an invitation, this song has only the west wind rolling the curtain.

It should be said that everyday drinking in the Song was a low-strength grain wine, and the craft lineage of [yellow wine](ref:yellow-wine) is close to it; the wine in the song is not today's strong baijiu, and neither its volume nor its strength can be converted directly. Drinking through a day without falling over is not unimaginable in a low-strength context, but the point of the song was never the quantity.

## Variants and the anecdote

The best-known story about this song is a note in a collection of anecdotes: Li Qingzhao sent Zui Hua Yin to Zhao Mingcheng, who was unwilling to be outdone, shut himself away for three days and wrote fifty ci songs, mixed this one among them, and asked a friend to judge them. The friend said only the three lines beginning do not say it does not wound the soul were supreme. The story comes from Langhuan ji, traditionally attributed to Yi Shizhen of the Yuan dynasty; it belongs to note-style fiction, vivid but of limited reliability, and should be read as part of the song's reception rather than as a record of the writing scene.

On the text, the common differences centre on smoulder and melt, and on thinner than versus like. The difference is small but affects the tone: thinner than is a comparison, like is a resemblance; the first is more direct, the second more muted. Different anthologies follow different editions, and it is enough for a reader to know that the variants exist.

## Common misreadings

First, taking borneol as a kind of wine. It is an aromatic incense burned in a burner.

Second, taking the eastern fence as a specific place name. It alludes to Tao Yuanming and means the fence where chrysanthemums are grown.

Third, taking the yellow flower as a flower in general. It means chrysanthemum, fitting the Double Ninth custom of admiring chrysanthemums.

Fourth, taking the person is thinner than the yellow flower as a description of dieting. It writes the thinness produced by longing, sleeplessness and autumn cold, not an aesthetic standard.

Fifth, taking the fifty songs in the anecdote as established history. The story shows how widely the song circulated and how highly it was praised, but it cannot be treated as a transcript of the writing process.
