# 1952 National Wine Evaluation: the eight famous wines and the start of a system

In September 1952 the first national wine evaluation was held in Beijing. From samples of baijiu, yellow wine, wine and fruit wine sent from around the country, it selected eight Chinese famous wines; Moutai, Luzhou Laojiao, Fenjiu and Xifeng later became known as the four famous baijiu. The significance of the meeting lies not only in the list but in the way it brought scattered local famous wines into a national system of quality and reputation.

## Background

After 1949, wine production and sales were gradually brought into the state-run and monopoly system. On 5 May 1951 the Ministry of Finance issued the Provisional Regulations on the Monopoly Business (draft) and the Organisational Rules for Monopoly Companies at All Levels, giving alcohol monopoly a national institutional basis; by 1952, except in a few areas, alcohol was placed under monopoly nationwide. Many traditional distilleries remained, and product quality, names, specifications and pricing were inconsistent. The state needed a comparable list of famous wines for quality management, market supply and external display. In autumn 1952 the China Monopoly Business Corporation held a national monopoly conference; local monopoly officials brought samples from their home regions, and an internal tasting was organised during the meeting. It was later recognised as the first national wine evaluation.

## Course and timeline

- May 1951: the Ministry of Finance issues the draft monopoly regulations and organisational rules, and the alcohol monopoly system begins to take shape.
- Autumn 1952: the China Monopoly Business Corporation holds the national monopoly conference in Beijing, with delegates bringing wine samples.
- September 1952: the first national wine evaluation is held in Beijing with 103 samples covering baijiu, yellow wine, wine and fruit wine.
- Selection: staff analysed the samples chemically and recorded sensory impressions; judges assessed them against criteria such as meeting quality standards, being safe for ordinary drinkers, enjoying high recognition and circulating widely; the analysis and opinions were submitted to the national monopoly conference for review.
- After approval: Moutai, Luzhou Daqu, Fenjiu, Xifeng, Shaoxing jiafan wine, Zhangyu Gold Medal Brandy, Red Rose Wine and Vermouth were named the eight famous wines.
- 1963, 1979, 1984 and 1989: the second to fifth national evaluations followed, with methods, categories and awards becoming more detailed.

## Key people and institutions

The host was the China Monopoly Business Corporation, which also managed alcohol production, sales and monopoly enforcement. The judging work was led by Zhu Mei, with judges including Wang Qiufang, Xin Haiting, Ma Zhanwen and Wang Ming'ai; most later became technical leaders in baijiu, yellow wine and wine. On the enterprise side, the Moutai distillery, [Luzhou Laojiao](ref:luzhou), [Fenjiu](ref:fenjiu), the Xifeng distillery, the Shaoxing winery and the Zhangyu company in Shandong entered the list, while local monopoly companies and industrial departments handled sample submission and preliminary selection. The list balanced categories and places: the four [baijiu](ref:baijiu) came from Guizhou, Sichuan, Shanxi and Shaanxi; the yellow wine came from Zhejiang; the three wines and aromatised wines came from Zhangyu in Shandong.

## Concrete changes for the industry

First, it established the institution of the national wine evaluation. Further meetings followed in 1963, 1979, 1984 and 1989, with methods moving from recommendation and discussion to coded blind tasting, and categories expanding to baijiu, yellow wine, wine, beer and fruit wine. Second, it shaped famous-wine brands. The list became an important resource for corporate storytelling, packaging and marketing, and it influenced consumer choice. Third, it pushed standardisation. To take part, producers had to align sensory vocabulary, testing indicators and sample management, work that later entered industry standards. Fourth, it strengthened awareness of aroma types. In 1952 aroma classification was not yet systematic, but regional craft differences were already visible, preparing the ground for group-by-aroma judging in 1979. Fifth, it influenced industrial layout. Famous-wine enterprises received more policy, raw-material and technical support, while local distilleries treated entry into the list as a goal.

## Disputes and later assessments

First, the organisation's name and exact date differ between sources. The common account is September 1952, hosted by the China Monopoly Business Corporation, but variants such as "monopoly industrial corporation" and "monopoly corporation" also appear; citations should name the source and avoid treating one version as fact. Second, the number of samples. The common figure is 103, while some memoirs say the eight were chosen from more than two hundred wines, possibly reflecting different counts for submitted and formally judged samples. Third, the eight famous wines and the four famous baijiu are not the same concept. The four refer only to baijiu, while the eight also include yellow wine and wine. Fourth, early evaluations differed from modern blind judging. They carried administrative review and industry recommendation, and cannot simply be measured against today's blind-tasting standards. Fifth, the list is a historical marker, not an eternal ranking; later evaluations changed places, and market standing rose and fell with management and consumption trends.

## Common misreadings

First, the 1952 evaluation was not a strict modern blind tasting but an internal assessment during a monopoly conference. Second, the eight famous wines are not eight baijiu; baijiu held only four places. Third, it was not an independent commercial competition but part of the state monopoly and quality-management system. Fourth, the list was not fixed; the second evaluation in 1963 brought major changes, and later aroma grouping and blind judging altered results further. Fifth, equating the 1952 eight famous wines with today's premium brand ranking ignores more than seventy years of ownership, craft and market change.
