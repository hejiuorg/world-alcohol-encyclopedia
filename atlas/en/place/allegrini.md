# Allegrini

Allegrini is in Fumane, inside the Valpolicella Classica zone. It is not far from Prosecco but follows a completely different wine logic: here the main characters are Corvina, dried grapes and Amarone, a wine that needs time and oxygen to open. The official account traces the family in Fumane to the sixteenth century; today Francesco, Giovanni, Matteo and Silvia run the estate, which farms more than 100 hectares and builds its range around La Poja, La Grola, Palazzo della Torre and Amarone.![Allegrini category image](/media/photos/q2-region-veneto-011f6328.jpg)

## The family and the sixteenth century in Fumane

Fumane is a village in the Valpolicella Classica, with south-facing slopes and soils mixing limestone, clay and volcanic rock. Allegrini's official introduction describes the family's relationship with this land as dating "since the sixteenth century", but the figure who changed the estate's fortunes was Giovanni Allegrini in the twentieth century: he drove the revival of Valpolicella and renewed attention to sites and drying. Today's hospitality page still centres on family and land rather than scale or equipment.

The practical meaning is that the estate's history is not a straight line but several turns. From selling bulk wine to bottling under its own name, from traditional large casks to more precise parcel management, each step left a mark. Not every building or cellar you see is an old relic; some were later adapted. Understanding which parts are history and which are contemporary choices matters more than memorising dates.

## Valpolicella's grapes and the drying process

Valpolicella's permitted red grapes are led by Corvina, with Corvinone, Rondinella and others. Amarone starts by picking healthy fruit and drying it for months in ventilated rooms, concentrating sugar before a long fermentation and ageing. Drying is not "making raisins" but managing humidity and airflow to prevent mould; the fruit must be turned, sorted and discarded when necessary, which is labour-intensive.

This process explains Amarone's concentration and alcohol, and why it is expensive. Recioto uses the same dried-grape system but stops fermentation early to keep sweetness; Valpolicella is the everyday red from fresh fruit; Ripasso re-soaks Valpolicella on Amarone pomace for extra structure and flavour. Four wines, one landscape, four different occasions.

## From La Poja to Amarone

La Poja is Allegrini's most distinctive single-vineyard wine: in 1979 Giovanni Allegrini planted only Corvina on the top of La Grola, and the site later proved capable of a wine of its own. It is not Amarone but a pure Corvina from fresh grapes, emphasising fruit, acidity and precise tannin. La Grola is another high site in the Valpolicella Classica, broader in body with clearer spice and mineral notes.

The Amarone range also includes higher-tier selections such as Fieramonte, from specific sites and stricter sorting. Palazzo della Torre takes a more approachable route, combining partly dried grapes with fresh fruit so that concentration does not become heaviness. Read the list by first building a fresh-versus-dried contrast, then separating single vineyards from village level; do not start with the most expensive Amarone, or you will have no reference point.

## Cellar experiences and booking

The hospitality page lists several experiences: a 45-minute classic tasting at €30 per person; a 1 hour 30 minute "Soul of Valpolicella" with the historic cellar and tasting at €45 per person; a 3-hour "from vineyard to glass" at €70 per person; a 2-hour "journey through time with classic Amarone" including the drying centre and a vertical tasting of five vintages at €150 per person; and a €190 per person La Grola hillside experience reserved for Wine Club members. Prices and contents are subject to the booking page.

When booking, confirm the language, meeting point, whether a vineyard walk is included, whether children are welcome and the cancellation policy. The cellar and drying centre are production areas, floors may be uneven, so wear non-slip shoes; the drying rooms have strong airflow and intense aromas, so tell the guide early if you are sensitive. Do not drive after the visit; Valpolicella's country roads are narrow and poorly lit at night.

## A contrast with Prosecco

Begin with the address, move on to the current opening status, and finish with what the place is known for. That order keeps the reading of Allegrini practical.

Putting Allegrini and [Bisol](ref:bisol) on the same Veneto itinerary makes the contrast obvious. Bisol grows [Glera](ref:glera) on the steep slopes of Valdobbiadene and uses tank fermentation for a fresh sparkling wine built on acidity, pressure and primary fruit; Allegrini grows Corvina in Valpolicella and uses drying and long [oak](ref:oak) ageing for a dense red built on concentration, tannin and oxidation management. Both belong to [Veneto](ref:veneto), yet they share almost no technique.

## Arriving, seasons and surroundings

The address is Via Giare 5, 37022 Fumane (VR), telephone +39 045 6832011. Verona is about half an hour by car and the southern shore of Lake Garda about forty minutes; public transport reaches Verona or Fumane, then a walk or taxi, but the estate is on a slope and services are infrequent. Spring and autumn are the most comfortable; September and October bring harvest and drying preparation, the busiest period, so book early.

Fumane has a church, a square and a few local restaurants, enough for lunch. Continue north to Lake Garda or east back to Verona. Do not schedule Amarone right after a heavy midday meal; alcohol and tannin are high, so nose before sipping and spit what you do not finish.
