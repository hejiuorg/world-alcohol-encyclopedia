# Joh. Jos. Prüm Wehlener Sonnenuhr Riesling

Joh. Jos. Prüm Wehlener Sonnenuhr Riesling comes from Wehlen in the Middle Mosel, Germany, and is made by the Prüm family estate, Weingut Joh. Jos. Prüm. The Sonnenuhr is one of the best-known sites on the Mosel, facing generally south on slate soils, and the wines are known for lime, white peach, wet slate and honey, with [alcohol by volume](ref:abv) usually low, mostly 7%–9.5% vol.

## Definition and position

Start with the levels: [white wine](ref:white-wine) is the category, [Riesling](ref:riesling) is the variety, the Mosel is the region, Prüm is the estate and brand, and Wehlener Sonnenuhr Riesling is a specific product line. Keeping the four apart separates variety character, site character and estate technique.

German Riesling is graded by ripeness at harvest, and Kabinett, Spätlese, Auslese, Beerenauslese and Trockenbeerenauslese may all come from the same estate and the same site. So "Prüm Sonnenuhr" is not one single style but a group of wines with different sweetness and concentration, and the grade, vintage and alcohol must be read together when buying.

## Producer and brand

The Prüm family estate is in Wehlen in the Middle Mosel, and public sources trace its modern form to 1911, when Johann Josef Prüm founded it, with the family running it across generations since. The estate centres on Riesling, with holdings mainly on steep slopes around Wehlen, Graach and Bernkastel.

Estate, brand and product are three levels: the estate answers for sites, equipment and technique, the brand answers for positioning and product line, and the product answers for grade, vintage, alcohol and volume. Family history is corporate narrative and cannot replace the grade and vintage on the label. Jos.

## Region and ingredients

The wine comes from the Middle Mosel in [Germany](ref:germany). The area is cool with a long ripening season; the Wehlener Sonnenuhr is a steep, south-facing site whose slate soils drain well, warm by day and release heat slowly at night, helping Riesling ripen in a cool environment. Steep slopes rule out mechanisation, so farming and picking are largely manual.

The raw material is [grape](ref:grape), of the [Riesling](ref:riesling) variety. Riesling ripens late and has high acidity, with aromas of citrus, green apple and minerality, and it suits cool regions. Slate shapes water and heat conditions, but positions within one site still differ, so this entry does not treat slate as a universal explanation for every Mosel wine.

## Winemaking

Picking is usually late, and some grades require selecting whole bunches or even individual berries. After pressing, yeast carries out [fermentation](ref:fermentation), and the estate's traditional practice ferments slowly in large old oak barrels with extended lees contact to add texture and stability while avoiding obvious new-oak flavour.

Whether fermentation finishes and whether residual sugar is kept decides if the wine is dry or sweet, and it also decides the alcohol. German Riesling grades are set by ripeness at harvest, not by chaptalisation or blending after fermentation, which differs from practice in many other regions. Specific parameters are estate information, and this entry gives no universal figures.

## Specification and label

The label should be checked for estate name, site name, variety, vintage, grade, alcohol, net volume and region.

## Flavour and tasting

Typical aromas are lime, white peach, green apple and wet-slate minerality, with honey, apricot and flowers at higher grades; the palate has clear acidity, a light body and sweetness that varies with grade, with a long finish of citrus peel and minerality. Low alcohol makes the aroma seem light, but that does not mean a thin wine, since acidity carries the structure.

Read Joh. Jos. Prüm Wehlener Sonnenuhr Riesling through the base and the stated strength, then the sweetness or bitterness, and finally how it is served. The sequence matters.

## Drinking and pairing

It suits steamed fish, white meat, salads, Asian sour-hot dishes and soft cheese; high acidity cuts fat and balances heat. Sweeter grades suit fruit desserts and blue cheese, but avoid layering with food that is sweeter still, or the wine will taste sour.

Most Kabinett and Spätlese are for drinking within a few years to a decade or more, and higher-grade sweet wines keep longer; the window depends on vintage, grade and estate technique. Excessive drinking harms health and minors should not drink.

## Buying and storage

For Joh. Jos. Prüm Wehlener Sonnenuhr Riesling, buy from reputable channels and check estate name, site name, grade, vintage and AP number. Prices vary widely with grade, vintage and estate reputation, so no figure is given here; different grades from the same site are different products, and one wine cannot stand for the whole site.

For Joh. Jos. Prüm Wehlener Sonnenuhr Riesling, unopened bottles should be kept away from light, lying flat in a cool place, avoiding heat and sharp temperature swings; low-alcohol sweet wines also suffer from heat. Cork-sealed bottles need humidity and can be stored lying down. After opening, refrigerate and drink soon; higher-grade sweet wines oxidise slowly, but aroma still fades.

## Common misconceptions

First, treating "Sonnenuhr" as a brand. It is a site name, and several estates own parcels there.
Second, assuming all German Riesling is sweet. Grade and fermentation result decide sweetness, and dry wines exist.
Third, equating low alcohol with a thin wine. Acidity and concentration carry structure too.
Fourth, treating grade as a quality ranking. Grade reflects ripeness at harvest, not personal preference.
Fifth, judging authenticity by price or packaging. Channel, AP number and back label are more reliable.
