# Brunello di Montalcino

Brunello di Montalcino is a red wine from the Montalcino appellation in Tuscany, Italy, made from Sangiovese and full-bodied with high acidity and firm tannin. It is commonly 750 mL at about 14.5% vol, and the DOCG rules require at least 5 years of ageing from the year after harvest, including at least 2 years in oak and 4 months in bottle.

## Definition and position

Start by separating the levels: [red wine](ref:red-wine) is the category, Sangiovese is the variety, Montalcino is the place, Brunello is the appellation (DOCG), and individual estates and wines are the producer level. Keeping these four apart is what stops "Brunello" from being read as a brand or as a grape.

Brunello is a place name and an appellation marker, not a trademark. Only red wine grown and made inside the delimited Montalcino area under the production rules, from Sangiovese, may be labelled Brunello di Montalcino; the everyday red of the same area is Rosso di Montalcino, a separate appellation.

## Producer and brand

The Montalcino area has family estates, cooperatives and merchants of clearly different scale. The bottler information on the label is the basis for identity: an estate with its own parcels and a merchant who buys grapes or wine play different roles, and one estate may make both Brunello and Rosso di Montalcino.

Estate, brand and product are three levels: the estate answers for sites, equipment and method, the brand answers for positioning and range, and the product answers for vintage, volume and alcohol. Family history and brand stories are company narrative and cannot replace the appellation and vintage on the bottle.

## Region and ingredients

The appellation lies around the town of Montalcino in Tuscany, [Italy](ref:italy), with marked differences in altitude and aspect; the south and the side closer to the coast are warmer, while the north and higher ground are cooler. The Mediterranean climate brings dry summers and the risk of autumn rain, and balancing ripeness with acidity is the key task each year.

The raw material is [grape](ref:grape), of the [Sangiovese](ref:sangiovese) variety, locally often called Sangiovese Grosso. Soils vary within the appellation, and parcels mixing sand, limestone and clay give different structure and aroma; site boundaries and soils follow the appellation's official materials.

## Winemaking

After harvest the grapes are destemmed and pressed, then [fermented](ref:fermentation) with a fairly long maceration to extract colour, tannin and aroma, followed by malolactic conversion to soften acidity. Brunello is usually not made with a high proportion of new oak, so as not to bury the sour cherry and herbal notes of Sangiovese.

After fermentation the wine goes into [oak ageing](ref:oak-ageing) and then continues in bottle. The production rules require at least 5 years of total ageing from the year after harvest, including at least 2 years in oak and 4 months in bottle, and the wine may be released only when these conditions are met. Barrel type and proportion are the estate's choice.

## Specification and label

The common volume is 750 mL, with [alcohol by volume](ref:abv) at about 14.5% vol, varying with vintage and estate. The label should be checked for the appellation name Brunello di Montalcino, the DOCG mark, vintage, bottler, net volume and importer information, and should not be confused with Rosso di Montalcino.

The Chinese back label, importer and anti-counterfeit information are the basis for checking identity; label languages differ by market, so the bottle on hand governs. On seeing the word "Brunello", also confirm that it is the full appellation name and not a name invented by an estate.

## Flavour and tasting

Typical aromas are sour cherry, red plum, violet, tomato leaf and herbs, with dried flowers, leather, tobacco and earth emerging with age. The palate has high acidity, firm tannin and a full body, and a young wine may seem tight before opening over time; compared with Nebbiolo, Brunello usually puts fruit further forward.

For tasting, serve at about 16–18 degrees Celsius in a large glass. Younger vintages can be decanted for one to several hours, while older bottles should be decanted for less time; too warm a temperature pushes alcohol forward, and too cold suppresses aroma.

## Drinking and pairing

It pairs well with braised and roasted meat, game, tomato-based dishes and hard cheese; high acidity and firm tannin cut fat and protein and echo the acidity of tomato. It does not suit steamed seafood, light vegetables or very sweet food, which can make the wine taste bitter or unbalanced.

After opening, refrigerate and finish within a few days, as prolonged air contact speeds oxidation. Most Brunello needs bottle age before it reaches its drinking window, and that window depends on vintage, site and estate method. Excessive drinking harms health and minors should not drink.

## Buying and storage

Buy from reputable channels and check the appellation name, vintage, bottler and importer information. Prices move with vintage, site and channel, so no figure is given here; Rosso di Montalcino and Brunello are different appellations with different positioning, and one wine cannot stand for all of Montalcino.

For Brunello di Montalcino, unopened bottles should be kept away from light, lying flat, in a cool place at an ideal 12–15 degrees Celsius, avoiding heat and sharp temperature swings. Cork-sealed bottles need humidity and can be stored lying down; after opening, refrigerate and drink soon.

## Common misconceptions

First, treating Brunello as a variety name. It is an appellation name; the variety is Sangiovese.
Second, confusing Brunello with Rosso di Montalcino. They are separate appellations with different ageing requirements.
Third, assuming all Brunello needs a decade or more in bottle. Vintages, sites and estate styles differ greatly.
Fourth, treating Montalcino as a single style. Altitude and soil vary noticeably within the appellation.
Fifth, judging authenticity by price or packaging. Channel, label and anti-counterfeit information are more reliable.

For Brunello di Montalcino, begin with the base and the stated strength. Then look at the sweetness or bitterness, and finally ask how it is served. The answers point to different choices, not to a single score.
