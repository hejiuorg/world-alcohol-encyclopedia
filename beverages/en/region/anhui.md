# Anhui

Anhui lies in eastern China, with the Yangtze and Huai rivers running west to east across the province and dividing it roughly into the Huaibei Plain, the Jianghuai hills and the mountainous south. Anhui is a major baijiu-producing province, with strong-aroma and mixed-aroma styles concentrated in the north; Gujing Town in Bozhou is the home of Gujing Gongjiu.

## Geography and climate

North of the Huai River the climate is warm-temperate and semi-humid, with cold dry winters and hot rainy summers. Between the rivers the climate is subtropical and humid with four distinct seasons, and the southern mountains receive more rain with pronounced vertical climate zones. The Huaibei Plain has deep soils and dense river networks, suited to sorghum, wheat and corn for brewing and transport.

Climate differences directly affect qu making and fermentation. Cold northern winters help control fermentation speed, while summer heat tests workshop cooling and hygiene. The riverine south is more humid, which raises the difficulty of microbial management in qu rooms and pits. To understand Anhui liquor, start with this north-south transitional geography.

## Industry structure

Anhui [baijiu](ref:baijiu) is mainly strong aroma, with mixed aroma and other types alongside. Northern Anhui is the core region: Bozhou, Huaibei and Fuyang host several large producers, while southern and riverine areas have local distilleries with smaller output and brand influence.

Across price bands, Anhui baijiu runs from everyday bottles to mid- and high-priced gift products; most products are low- to medium-strength, with some high-end products stronger. Competition within the province is intense, and producers emphasise channels and retail execution, which is the background for the province's reputation for marketing and distribution.

## Main production areas

Bozhou lies in the northwest. Gujing Town is the production base of Gujing Gongjiu and the site of the [Gujing Gongjiu](ref:gujing) brand and its wine-culture museum. Bozhou is also known as a centre for Chinese medicinal materials, so pharmacy and distilling together shape the local economy.

Huaibei and Suzhou form another baijiu cluster, where Kouzijiao and other producers are known for mixed aroma. Fuyang, Lu'an and Xuancheng also have representative brands. The areas are not stylistically uniform: water, soil, qu type and process route differ, and so do the wines.

## Representative brands and styles

Gujing Gongjiu is mainly strong aroma, stressing pit aroma, grain aroma and mellow sweetness. Kouzijiao is known for mixed aroma, combining some features of strong and sauce aroma. Yingjia Gongjiu emphasises an ecological-origin story, while Jinzhongzi and others cover mass price bands. Differences among brands come from both process and positioning.

When reading a brand page, separate company, brand and specific product. One brand may have several series with different raw materials, strengths, storage and blending. This region provides regional organisation and overview only and does not rank individual products.

## Raw materials and agriculture

The main raw material is sorghum, qu is made from wheat, and some products add rice, glutinous rice or corn. Grain production on the Huaibei Plain provides a base, but producers also buy across regions, so "local grain" should not be treated as the sole determinant of style.

Qu is a major source of flavour. Strong aroma generally uses medium-to-high-temperature qu, while mixed aroma makes different arrangements in qu making and fermentation. Grind size, cooking, pit-entry temperature and fermentation length all change the final aroma and texture. These are company process parameters with limited public detail, so labels and standards are the more reliable clues.

## Drinking and pairing

Anhui baijiu commonly appears at 40%–52% vol, suited to small-cup sipping. Northern Anhui food is savoury and oil-rich; with roast chicken, beef soup or iron-pot chicken, the sweetness and pit aroma of the spirit come forward. Huizhou cuisine in the south stresses slow cooking and fermented flavours, so stinky mandarin fish or hairy tofu need more careful matching to avoid the food and the wine cancelling each other.

Drink in moderation and avoid rapid drinking on an empty stomach. Low-strength products do not keep long and should be finished after opening; high-strength products should still be stored away from light, cool and free of odours and heat. Table customs around urging drinks are not a tasting standard, and individual tolerance should be respected.

## Relations with other regions

Anhui is a regional node under [China](ref:china), and city nodes below it carry specific places. It borders Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Jiangxi, Hubei, Henan and Shandong, and its baijiu is influenced by its neighbours: northern Anhui is close to the strong-aroma traditions of Henan and Shandong, while southern Anhui sits next to the huangjiu and rice-wine habits of Jiangsu and Zhejiang.

This transitional position means "Anhui liquor" is not one style. Treating it as a collection of regions and brands is more accurate than searching for a single Anhui flavour. For cross-province comparison, use samples of the same aroma type and price band.

## Common misconceptions

First, equating Anhui with one baijiu aroma type. Strong aroma dominates, but mixed and other types exist.
Second, assuming northern Anhui is always better. Regional scale is not quality.
Third, treating a brand name as a process. One brand has several series and process routes.
Fourth, that local raw materials determine all flavour. Qu, process and storage matter just as much.
Fifth, judging liquor by how hard people urge drinks. Drinking customs and product quality are separate.

For Anhui, begin with the appellation and its permitted varieties. Then look at the vintage and the producer, and finally ask how the wine is labelled. The answers point to different choices, not to a single score.

[China](ref:china)'s geographic boundary and [Strong-aroma baijiu](ref:strong-aroma)'s climate and soil form a useful pair: the contrast shows where one ends and the other begins.
