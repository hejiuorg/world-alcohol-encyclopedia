# Poetry

- [Asking Liu Shijiu: Bai Juyi's Cup of Wine on a Winter Night](bai-juyi-asking-liu.md) — Bai Juyi's “Asking Liu Shijiu” has only four lines and twenty characters: freshly brewed lüyi wine, a small red clay stove, an evening about to snow, and the question “can you drink a cup?” It turns an invitation into a warm little winter scene, and its sense of measure is more lasting than the wine.
- [Duan Ge Xing: Cao Cao Turns Wine into the Anxiety of Seeking Talent](cao-cao-duan-ge-xing.md) — Duan Ge Xing is an old yuefu title traditionally assigned to Cao Cao. It begins with “I face the wine and should sing; how long is a life?”, uses wine to dissolve sorrow, moves into the anxiety of seeking talent, and closes with the Duke of Zhou spitting out his food to receive guests. Wine is not pleasure here but the medium that places a short life and an unfinished enterprise on the same table.
- [Climbing High: Du Fu and a Cup of Rough Wine at Kuizhou](du-fu-deng-gao.md) — Du Fu wrote Climbing High in the autumn of 767 at Kuizhou. The eight lines of regulated verse give four lines to autumn scenery and four to his own life, ending on the words “I have just stopped the cup of rough wine”. Wine never appears at a banquet here; it appears as a stopping, a mark of age, illness and drift.
- [Arrival of a Guest: Du Fu Entertains at His Thatched Cottage](du-fu-ke-zhi.md) — Du Fu wrote “Arrival of a Guest” around the spring of 761 at his thatched cottage in Chengdu; the poem carries his own note that he was delighted by a visit from Magistrate Cui. From spring water and gulls to sweeping the path and opening the gate, from a distant market and plain food to poor wine, it makes a modest meal sincere and warm.
- [Qingming: Rain, a Traveller and the Direction of a Wine House](du-mu-qingming.md) — The four lines of Qingming give us rain, a traveller and a shepherd boy pointing the way; the wine house is asked about but never reached. The poem has long been attributed to Du Mu, yet it is absent from his collected works and first appears in a Southern Song reference book, so authorship remains disputed; Apricot Blossom Village is not necessarily a fixed place name either.
- [Yu Jia Ao: Fan Zhongyan and a Cup of Cloudy Wine at the Frontier](fan-zhongyan-yu-jia-ao.md) — Yu Jia Ao describes autumn at the north-western frontier: geese leave without lingering, border sounds rise, the lone city closes. The lower half sets “a cup of cloudy wine, home ten thousand miles away” against “Mount Yanran not yet inscribed, no way to return”, and ends on “the general's white hair, the soldiers' tears”. A single cup measures the distance between home and the frontier.
- [Drinking Alone under the Moon: Li Bai's Pot of Wine and Three Shadows](li-bai-drinking-alone.md) — Li Bai's “Drinking Alone under the Moon” turns solitude into a little theatre with the line “I raise my cup to invite the bright moon, and with my shadow we become three”. The moon and shadow cannot drink, yet the poet recruits them as company. Wine here is not a remedy for sorrow but a prop for an imaginative stage.
- [Written While Travelling: Li Bai's Lanling Wine and a Host Who Makes a Guest Forget Home](li-bai-kezhong-zuo.md) — Li Bai's “Written While Travelling” is a four-line poem about Lanling wine, a jade bowl and the amber glow of the liquor, and a host whose hospitality makes a guest forget he is far from home. “Turmeric” here means the rhizome used to scent wine, not the tulip; the wine is a low-alcohol fermented grain wine, not today's baijiu.
- [Bring in the Wine: Li Bai on drinking and time](li-bai-qiang-jin-jiu.md) — Bring in the Wine uses an old yuefu title and was probably written around the Tianbao era; Li Bai uses a drinking song to write about irreversible time, confidence in talent and the relief of disappointment. The received text differs from the Dunhuang manuscript Xizun Kong, and three hundred cups is literary exaggeration rather than a record of intake.
- [Sheng Sheng Man: Three Cups and Two Cups of Thin Wine against the Evening Wind](li-qingzhao-sheng-sheng-man.md) — Li Qingzhao's Sheng Sheng Man opens with fourteen repeated characters, then moves through a warm-cold autumn day, three cups and two cups of thin wine, fallen flowers and fine rain on the paulownia. Wine neither warms the body nor drowns sorrow; “thin” and “could not withstand” set the whole poem's helplessness.
- [Zui Hua Yin: Li Qingzhao and a Cup of Wine on the Double Ninth](li-qingzhao-zui-hua-yin.md) — Li Qingzhao's Zui Hua Yin follows a Double Ninth day from morning to dusk: thin mist and dense clouds, an incense burner, a jade pillow and gauze curtain, then a cup of wine by the eastern fence, ending on “the person is thinner than the yellow flower”. Wine appears once, yet it links the day's idle sorrow to the thinness of a body.
- [Yu Lin Ling: Liu Yong and a Farewell Cup He Could Not Drink](liu-yong-yu-lin-ling.md) — Yu Lin Ling describes a farewell after an autumn rain: a tent is set up outside the city gate and wine is laid out, but there is no heart for it; the boatman urges departure; the two hold hands and weep. The lower half imagines where the poet will wake from wine, turning a cup that was never enjoyed into the clear-headed scene of willows, morning wind and a fading moon. Wine here is not entertainment but evidence of parting.
- [Visiting West Mountain Village: Lu You and a Bowl of Farmhouse Wine](lu-you-you-shan-xi-cun.md) — Lu You's Visiting West Mountain Village describes a trip to a mountain village: the farmhouse winter wine is cloudy but the chicken and pork for a guest are ample; mountains and waters repeat until the road seems lost, then willows dark and flowers bright reveal another village; the spring sacrifice is near and flutes and drums follow; before leaving, the poet promises to lean on his staff and knock at the door at any hour. Wine is cloudy home brew, not a famous bottle, but it is where the meal begins.
- [Passing an Old Friend's Farm: Meng Haoran and an Ordinary Cup of Wine](meng-haoran-guo-gu-ren-zhuang.md) — Meng Haoran's Passing an Old Friend's Farm describes an ordinary visit to a farmhouse: the friend prepares chicken and millet, green trees ring the village, a blue mountain slopes beyond the wall, the window opens onto a threshing floor and vegetable plot, and wine is raised while farming is discussed, ending with a promise to return for chrysanthemums at the Double Ninth.
- [Ding Feng Bo: A Cloak of Mist and Rain for a Whole Life](su-shi-ding-feng-bo.md) — Su Shi's Ding Feng Bo records a rainstorm on the road to Shahu on the seventh of the third month: companions were wretched, he alone was unbothered; after the rain cleared he looked back and wrote “there is neither wind nor rain, nor clear sky”. Wine appears only once—when the chill spring wind sobers him—and turns the poem from rain toward clarity.
- [Nian Nu Jiao: Remembering the Red Cliff, Su Shi Pours Wine to the River Moon](su-shi-nian-nu-jiao.md) — Nian Nu Jiao was written in 1082, while Su Shi was exiled to Huangzhou. The song moves from the great river flowing east to Zhou Yu of the Three Kingdoms, then back to the poet with white hair, and ends with “I pour a cup of wine as a libation to the river moon”. Wine appears only at the end, not as a deep drink but as a cup poured out to the river and the moon.
- [Shui Diao Ge Tou: Su Shi Asks the Moon with a Cup of Wine](su-shi-shui-diao-ge-tou.md) — Su Shi wrote Shui Diao Ge Tou at the Mid-Autumn festival of 1076. The preface says he drank until dawn, was very drunk, and also missed his brother Ziyou. The song moves from raising a cup to ask the blue sky to moonlight on a sleepless person, and closes with the wish that we may live long and share the same moon a thousand miles apart.
- [Song of the Peach Blossom Hut: Tang Yin Pays for Wine with Peach Blossoms](tang-yin-tao-hua-an-ge.md) — The Song of the Peach Blossom Hut describes a “peach blossom immortal” living in a hut among peach trees: he plants the trees, picks the blossoms and trades them for wine money, sits before the flowers when sober and sleeps beneath them when drunk, and would rather die old among flowers and wine than bow before carriages and horses. It makes honest poverty sound exuberant, then asks what remains of the tombs of the great. The poem survives in widely differing versions, so the text used must be stated.
- [Drinking Wine, Number Five: Tao Yuanming Sees the Southern Mountain](tao-yuanming-drinking-wine-5.md) — Number Five of Drinking Wine describes building a hut among men without the noise of carriages and horses, picking chrysanthemums by the eastern fence and seeing the southern mountain, and ending on “there is a true meaning in this, but I have forgotten the words to explain it”. The poem itself never uses the word wine; wine belongs to the title and the preface of the sequence, and must be read within Tao Yuanming's return to farm life.
- [Drinking Wine, No. 7: Autumn Chrysanthemums, the Care-Forgotten Thing and the Eastern Gallery](tao-yuanming-drinking-wine-7.md) — Tao Yuanming's seventh poem in the Drinking Wine series begins with dew-wet autumn chrysanthemums floated in wine, uses the drink to push worldly feelings away, then moves through a solitary cup, a emptied pot, sunset and returning birds to a long whistle under the eastern gallery. It is a quiet poem about self-sufficiency.
- [Liangzhou Ci: Wang Han Sets a Cup of Grape Wine Beside the Battlefield](wang-han-liang-zhou-ci.md) — Wang Han's Liangzhou Ci has only four lines: grape wine, a luminous cup, a pipa played on horseback, and “do not laugh if I lie drunk on the battlefield; since ancient times how many have come back from war?” It places the most splendid drinking vessel and the cruellest battlefield in one scene and writes the cost of war in the tone of a drinking invitation. The last line is a question, not a boast.
- [Seeing Off Yuan the Second on His Mission to Anxi: One Cup Becomes a Song](wang-wei-song-yuan-er.md) — Wang Wei's “Seeing Off Yuan the Second on His Mission to Anxi” opens with morning rain at Weicheng and green willows by the guest house, then lands on “one more cup before you go: west of Yang Pass there are no old friends”. It compresses a farewell into a cup, and was already sung in the Tang before becoming the “Three Refrains on Yang Pass”.
- [Po Zhen Zi: Xin Qiji Trims the Lamp and Looks at His Sword](xin-qiji-po-zhen-zi.md) — Po Zhen Zi begins with “drunk, I trim the lamp and look at my sword”, moves into a dream of linked camps, a reviewed army and fast horses, keeps nine lines heroic, and then breaks the dream with “pitiful—white hair has grown”. Wine is the entrance to the dream and the night-time gesture of an ambition that cannot be fulfilled.
- [Huan Xi Sha: Yan Shu Sees Time in a Cup of Wine](yan-shu-huan-xi-sha.md) — Huan Xi Sha begins with hearing a new song and drinking a cup of wine; last year's weather and the old pavilion are still there, yet the setting sun is asked when it will return. The lower half sets “no way to stop the falling flowers” against “the returning swallows look familiar”, and ends with the poet pacing alone on a fragrant path. Wine is not a banquet here but a quiet daily act that lets a person face time.

- [In Praise of the Virtue of Wine: Liu Ling's drinking as political fable](liu-ling-jiu-de-song.md) — In Praise of the Virtue of Wine sets the Great Man against titled gentlemen and retired scholars who defend ritual law, turning drinking into a stance outside social judgement. Wine is not a party prop but a quiet refusal of Wei-Jin moralised politics.
- [The Pavilion of the Old Drunkard: Ouyang Xiu on exile, landscape and local government](ouyang-xiu-zuiweng-ting-ji.md) — Written in 1046 when Ouyang Xiu was magistrate of Chuzhou, the essay moves from Mount Langya and the Brewing Spring to the people of Chu and the magistrate's banquet, ending on the question of who the magistrate is. It is a landscape essay and also a text about exile, light government and shared joy.
- [Holding a Cup and Asking the Moon: Li Bai on people, time and the moon](li-bai-ba-jiu-wen-yue.md) — The poem moves from stopping the cup to ask when the moon began to the line that the people of today cannot see the ancient moon, though this moon once shone on the ancients. The moon is a constant reference, wine the act of asking, and the ending brings the cosmos back into the cup.
- [Song of the Eight Immortals of the Wine Cup: Du Fu's portrait gallery of High Tang](du-fu-yin-zhong-ba-xian-ge.md) — Eight sketches of He Zhizhang, the Prince of Ruyang, the former chancellor Li Shizhi, Cui Zongzhi, Su Jin, Li Bai, Zhang Xu and Jiao Sui. Wine lets each man step outside his official role; the poem descends from court to street and is neither a capacity ranking nor a simple hymn.
- [Linjiang Xian: A Door That Would Not Open, a River That Could Be Heard](su-shi-linjiang-xian-ye-yin-dongpo.md) — Written during Su Shi's Huangzhou exile, the lyric begins with a night of drinking at Dongpo and a return to Lingao where the servant is asleep and the door does not answer. The closing wish for a small boat is not an actual departure but an imagined exit heard through a locked door.
- [Ru Meng Ling: A Thirty-Three-Character Little Film](li-qingzhao-ru-meng-ling.md) — Li Qingzhao's lyric has only thirty-three characters yet layers time, place, drunkenness, losing the way and startled birds. "Deeply drunk" may be wine or scenery, and "zheng du" may be racing to cross or asking how to cross; the small lyric lives in those uncertainties.
- [Xi Jiang Yue: Pushing the Pine Away, a Joke and a Temper](xin-qiji-xi-jiang-yue-qian-xing.md) — Xin Qiji's lyric describes drunken laughter, falling by a pine, asking the pine how drunk he is, then pushing it away with a "go." On the surface a comic piece, underneath it is anger at the books of the ancients and at Southern Song politics.
