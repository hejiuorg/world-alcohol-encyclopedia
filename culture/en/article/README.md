- [Baijiu aroma systems: how to read strong, light, sauce and rice aromas](baijiu-aroma-systems.md) — Aroma type is the baijiu trade's vocabulary for describing a style of aroma and a tradition of craft, not a ranking of quality. This article explains where the idea came from, which processes and compounds shape it, and what to read on a label.
- [Beyond the bar: following a drink to where it is made](beyond-the-pub.md) — Beyond the bar lies the place of production. Visits to breweries, distilleries and wine estates break flavour down into raw materials, equipment, time and human choices. This article explains what each kind of visit shows, what to ask, and which claims not to trust.
- [Cellars and storage: temperature, humidity, light and time](cellar-and-storage.md) — A cellar is not a magic room that ages wine. Storage aims to let a drink change at a controlled pace. This article covers wine, beer and spirits, the differences between closures, common myths and practical home storage.
- [A concise history of Chinese alcohol: from grain fermentation to national famous wines](history-of-chinese-wine.md) — Using archaeology, agricultural manuals, urban records and modern evaluation systems as evidence, this article traces Chinese alcohol from early grain fermentation through Tang and Song taverns and Yuan-Ming distilled spirits to modern industry and the national famous-wine system, separating evidence, legend and later narrative.
- [A history of taverns: from wine shops and inns to bars](history-of-taverns.md) — The form of a tavern is shaped by cities, transport, taxation and regulation. Comparing Chinese wine houses, European inns and taverns, 19th-century bars and American saloons, this article explains why the same function took different shapes, and how to read an old tavern.
- [A history of drinking vessels: how the cup changed the drink](history-of-vessels.md) — A vessel does more than hold wine: it shapes temperature, aroma, measure and gesture. This article follows Chinese drinking vessels through pottery, bronze, lacquer, gold and silver, porcelain and glass, and shows how shapes in turn shaped drinking.
- [A concise history of world alcohol: from fermented drinks to a global industry](history-of-world-wine.md) — Using archaeological evidence, legal texts and industry records as evidence, this article traces three paths — wine, beer and distilled spirits — and how nineteenth-century industrialisation and twentieth-century standards and globalisation reshaped today's drinks world, while setting disputed origins and periods side by side.
- [A history of yellow wine: rice, qu, water and Shaoxing](history-of-yellow-wine.md) — Yellow wine is the main line of Chinese grain-fermented wine. From early grain wine and Tang-Song wine policy to the spread of Shaoxing wine and modern standards, craft and local conditions shaped today’s categories. This article marks the key turns and distinguishes a modern category from ancient wine names.
- [Hops and bitterness: what IBU can and cannot tell you](hops-and-bitterness.md) — Hops bring bitterness, aroma and keeping quality, and IBU is a measure of iso-alpha-acid concentration. This article explains how bitterness arises, how IBU is measured, and why “higher IBU means more bitter” is unreliable.
- [Why does a drink taste of a place?](place-and-process.md) — Place enters a drink through climate, soil, variety, microbes and craft; terroir is a clue to understanding origin, not a guarantee of quality. This article takes wine, whisky, baijiu and yellow wine to unpack the layers behind “a taste of place”.
- [Prohibition: bans change supply, not demand](prohibition.md) — Taking America’s national prohibition of 1920 to 1933 as the main case, with Russian, Finnish, Icelandic and Chinese experience alongside, this article explains enforcement, loopholes and consequences, and why prohibition rarely means no alcohol.
- [Reading a city through its bars](pub-and-city.md) — A bar is a slice of city life: its opening hours, regulars, drinks list and seating together reveal who a neighbourhood is made of, when it comes alive and how it treats guests. This article compares the pub, the café, the bar and the beer hall, and suggests how to observe them and how to read their history.
- [Sake and shochu: both Japanese, but different crafts](sake-and-shochu.md) — Sake is a brewed drink and shochu is a distilled one, yet Chinese-language readers often treat them as the same. This article explains their raw materials, fermentation, distillation, categories and drinking styles, and decodes common label terms.
- [The spread of distillation: from alchemy to global spirits](spread-of-distillation.md) — Distillation was not invented by one person or one country. Following Alexandria, Arabic alchemy, European medicinal distillation, Caribbean rum, East Asian spirits and 19th-century column stills, this article shows how technique travelled through translation, trade and empire.
- [Time in a cup](time-in-a-cup.md) — From the spring wine of the Book of Songs to the golden goblets of Tang poetry, the ancients wrote seasons, farm work and gatherings into their drinking. This article discusses only what the documents can support, separating text, edition and present-day imagination.
- [Wine and festivals: the calendar inside seasonal drinks](wine-and-festivals.md) — Almost every traditional Chinese festival has its own wine: tusu at New Year, realgar at Dragon Boat, osmanthus at Mid-Autumn, chrysanthemum at Double Ninth. This article traces their ingredients, documentary sources and meanings, and notes which practices are unsafe today.
- [Wine and painting: four ways to look at a drinking scene](wine-and-painting.md) — In painting, wine is rarely just a still life: it marks status, time and human relations. Using Along the River During Qingming, The Night Revels of Han Xizai, Caravaggio’s Bacchus and a Dutch genre scene, this article shows what to look at in a drinking picture.
- [Wine and poetry: drinking from the Book of Songs to Song lyrics](wine-and-poetry.md) — In Chinese poetry, wine is both a banquet prop and a marker of time, friendship and political circumstance. This article follows the shift from pre-Qin ritual, through Wei-Jin self-expression, Tang gatherings and Song lyrics, and sets out the limits of reading poems as historical evidence.
- [Wine and ritual: from village drinking rites to the modern toast](wine-and-ritual.md) — In Chinese ritual, wine assigns order and status. Drawing on the Rites of Zhou, the Yili and the Liji, this article explains how wine officials, village drinking rites and sacrificial wine made relationships visible, and separates classical rules from later custom.
- [Wine grading systems: geographical indications, national tiers and estate classifications](wine-grading-systems.md) — Abbreviations such as AOC, AOP, IGP and DOCG on a wine label are often confused. This article separates “grading” into geographical indication protection, national quality tiers and estate or plot classification, and explains what each can and cannot prove.
