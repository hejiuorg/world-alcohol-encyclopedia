- [A history of taverns: from wine shops and inns to bars](article/history-of-taverns.md) — The form of a tavern is shaped by cities, transport, taxation and regulation. Comparing Chinese wine houses, European inns and taverns, 19th-century bars and American saloons, this article explains why the same function took different shapes, and how to read an old tavern.
- [A history of drinking vessels: how the cup changed the drink](article/history-of-vessels.md) — A vessel does more than hold wine: it shapes temperature, aroma, measure and gesture. - [A concise history of world alcohol: from fermented drinks to a global industry](article/history-of-world-wine.md) — Using archaeological evidence, legal texts and industry records as evidence, this article traces three paths — wine, beer and distilled spirits — and how nineteenth-century industrialisation and twentieth-century standards and globalisation reshaped today's drinks world, while setting disputed origins and periods side by side.
- [A history of yellow wine: rice, qu, water and Shaoxing](article/history-of-yellow-wine.md) — Yellow wine is the main line of Chinese grain-fermented wine. From early grain wine and Tang-Song wine policy to the spread of Shaoxing wine and modern standards, craft and local conditions shaped today’s categories. This article marks the key turns and distinguishes a modern category from ancient wine names.
- [Hops and bitterness: what IBU can and cannot tell you](article/hops-and-bitterness.md) — Hops bring bitterness, aroma and keeping quality, and IBU is a measure of iso-alpha-acid concentration. This article explains how bitterness arises, how IBU is measured, and why “higher IBU means more bitter” is unreliable.
- [Why does a drink taste of a place?](article/place-and-process.md) — Place enters a drink through climate, soil, variety, microbes and craft; terroir is a clue to understanding origin, not a guarantee of quality. This article takes wine, whisky, baijiu and yellow wine to unpack the layers behind “a taste of place”.
- [Prohibition: bans change supply, not demand](article/prohibition.md) — Taking America’s national prohibition of 1920 to 1933 as the main case, with Russian, Finnish, Icelandic and Chinese experience alongside, this article explains enforcement, loopholes and consequences, and why prohibition rarely means no alcohol.
- [Reading a city through its bars](article/pub-and-city.md) — A bar is a slice of city life: its opening hours, regulars, drinks list and seating together reveal who a neighbourhood is made of, when it comes alive and how it treats guests. This article compares the pub, the café, the bar and the beer hall, and suggests how to observe them and how to read their history.
- [Sake and shochu: both Japanese, but different crafts](article/sake-and-shochu.md) — Sake is a brewed drink and shochu is a distilled one, yet Chinese-language readers often treat them as the same. This article explains their raw materials, fermentation, distillation, categories and drinking styles, and decodes common label terms.
- [The spread of distillation: from alchemy to global spirits](article/spread-of-distillation.md) — Distillation was not invented by one person or one country. Following Alexandria, Arabic alchemy, European medicinal distillation, Caribbean rum, East Asian spirits and 19th-century column stills, this article shows how technique travelled through translation, trade and empire.
- [Time in a cup](article/time-in-a-cup.md) — From the spring wine of the Book of Songs to the golden goblets of Tang poetry, the ancients wrote seasons, farm work and gatherings into their drinking. This article discusses only what the documents can support, separating text, edition and present-day imagination.
- [Wine and festivals: the calendar inside seasonal drinks](article/wine-and-festivals.md) — Almost every traditional Chinese festival has its own wine: tusu at New Year, realgar at Dragon Boat, osmanthus at Mid-Autumn, chrysanthemum at Double Ninth. This article traces their ingredients, documentary sources and meanings, and notes which practices are unsafe today.
- [Wine and painting: four ways to look at a drinking scene](article/wine-and-painting.md) — In painting, wine is rarely just a still life: it marks status, time and human relations. Using Along the River During Qingming, The Night Revels of Han Xizai, Caravaggio’s Bacchus and a Dutch genre scene, this article shows what to look at in a drinking picture.
- [Wine and poetry: drinking from the Book of Songs to Song lyrics](article/wine-and-poetry.md) — In Chinese poetry, wine is both a banquet prop and a marker of time, friendship and political circumstance. This article follows the shift from pre-Qin ritual, through Wei-Jin self-expression, Tang gatherings and Song lyrics, and sets out the limits of reading poems as historical evidence.
- [Wine and ritual: from village drinking rites to the modern toast](article/wine-and-ritual.md) — In Chinese ritual, wine assigns order and status. Drawing on the Rites of Zhou, the Yili and the Liji, this article explains how wine officials, village drinking rites and sacrificial wine made relationships visible, and separates classical rules from later custom.
- [Wine grading systems: geographical indications, national tiers and estate classifications](article/wine-grading-systems.md) — Abbreviations such as AOC, AOP, IGP and DOCG on a wine label are often confused. This article separates “grading” into geographical indication protection, national quality tiers and estate or plot classification, and explains what each can and cannot prove.

## Artworks

- [Brouwer's The Drinker: A Tavern and a Face](artwork/brouwer-the-drinker.md) — A tavern genre scene by the Flemish painter Adriaen Brouwer, represented here by the Mauritshuis's Drunken Peasants at an Inn and often discussed under titles such as The Drinker. Drink, tobacco and noise strip everyone of sobriety; the picture is both comic observation and a cool look at human weakness.
- [Caravaggio's Bacchus: A Glass of Wine and a Basket of Fruit](artwork/caravaggio-bacchus.md) — Caravaggio's Bacchus, painted around 1598 and now in the Uffizi Galleries in Florence, shows a young wine god offering a glass to the viewer beside a minutely painted basket of fruit. It reads both as an invitation and as a puzzle about drunkenness and classical models.
- [An Interior with a Woman Drinking and Two Men: A Glass of Wine in Delft](artwork/de-hooch-woman-drinking.md) — Pieter de Hooch's interior, painted around 1658 and now in the National Gallery, London, shows a woman with her back to us holding a glass while two men use pipes as instruments. The picture stays open between singing, drinking and suggestion, and infrared imaging reveals a figure the painter removed.
- [The Night Revels of Han Xizai: a banquet turned into a painting](artwork/han-xizai-night-banquet.md) — A silk handscroll attributed to Gu Hongzhong of the Southern Tang, 28.7 by 335.5 cm, in the Palace Museum, Beijing, graded first class A. The page reads the five scenes of listening, dancing, resting, piping and farewell, explains how wine warmers and cups pace the night, and reviews the Song-copy and surveillance debates.
- [Heda's Still Life with Drinking Vessels: A Roemer and a Watch](artwork/heda-still-life-drinking-vessels.md) — A still life by the Dutch painter Willem Claesz. Heda, represented here by the Mauritshuis's Still Life with a Roemer and Watch. A large drinking glass, silver, food and a watch are painted in a near-monochrome light; the picture is both a study of drinking vessels and a still-life tradition about time and moderation.
- [The Lantingji Xu: a floating-cup gathering and a preface](artwork/lanting-ji-xu.md) — On the third day of the third month in 353, Wang Xizhi and his companions held a purification gathering at the Orchid Pavilion, drank and wrote poems, and Wang composed a preface. The original is lost; the Shenlong copy, ink on paper, 24.5 by 69.9 cm, is in the Palace Museum, Beijing. The page reads the text, the calligraphy, the floating-cup rule and the copy debate.
- [Li Bai Drunk: a subject painted again and again](artwork/li-bai-zui-jiu-tu.md) — Starting from Su Liupeng's Li Bai Drunk, a hanging scroll of 1844 in the Shanghai Museum (ink and colour on paper, 204.8 by 93.9 cm), the page traces the subject across centuries and distinguishes it from Liang Kai's Li Bai Walking and Reciting in Tokyo and a Boston scroll of Li Bai drinking with Emperor Minghuang.
- [Along the River During the Qingming Festival: taverns and urban rhythm in Bianjing](artwork/qingming-shanghe.md) — Zhang Zeduan's Northern Song handscroll, 24.8 by 528.7 cm, in the Palace Museum, Beijing, object number 00087177. The page uses the Sun Yang shop, the Shiqian retail outlet and the Rainbow Bridge to explain the hierarchy of breweries and wine shops, and reviews the disputes over the meaning of Qingming, the season and the scroll's completeness.
- [The Seven Sages of the Bamboo Grove and Rong Qiqi: a brick relief in a Southern Dynasties tomb](artwork/seven-sages-of-bamboo-grove.md) — A moulded brick relief excavated in 1960 from a Southern Dynasties tomb at Xishanqiao, Nanjing. The Nanjing Museum's two groups measure 78 by 242.5 cm and 78 by 241.5 cm, with name labels beside the figures. The page reads the eight poses, the place of wine among them, and the disputes over tomb date, patron and lost model.
- [Velázquez's The Triumph of Bacchus: Bringing Myth into the Crowd](artwork/velazquez-triumph-of-bacchus.md) — Velázquez's painting of about 1628–1629, now in the Museo del Prado, places Bacchus among a group of Spanish peasants. Cups, crowning and drinking bring myth down to everyday life; the picture is at once a parody of the classical figure and a new telling of Bacchic culture.
- [Vermeer's The Glass of Wine: A Drink and a Window](artwork/vermeer-wine-glass.md) — Vermeer's interior, painted around 1658–1660 and now in the Gemäldegalerie in Berlin, shows a woman at a table holding a glass while a man pours from a jug. The picture places drinking, hospitality and Dutch Golden Age domestic life in the same window light.

## Books

- [Bencao Gangmu (wine sections)](book/bencao-gangmu.md) — Volume 25 of Li Shizhen’s Ming pharmacopoeia, in the fermented-and-brewed class of the grain section, collects entries on wine, shaojiu and grape wine with methods, properties and medicinal claims. It is a key text for Ming wine knowledge and for the “shaojiu began in the Yuan” account, but its pharmacology is not modern medicine.
- [Dongjing Menghua Lu: A Written Map of Bianjing, with Wine Houses as Coordinates](book/dongjing-menghua-lu.md) — Meng Yuanlao’s Dongjing Menghua Lu, completed in 1147, is a ten-juan memoir of Northern Song Bianjing. Wine houses, licensed breweries, petty wine shops, night markets and festival drinking customs are among its most important material clues. This article covers editions, author, wine content, historical value and common misreadings.
- [Dream of the Red Chamber](book/dream-of-the-red-chamber.md) — Cao Xueqin's novel, written in the mid-18th century and printed in a 120-chapter edition in 1791; wine runs through banquets, festivals and everyday hospitality, with yellow wine, Huiquan wine, shaojiu and acacia-flower infusion each belonging to a different occasion. Read the wine passages together with character, ritual and edition.
- [The Drunken Botanist](book/drunken-botanist.md) — A book that starts with plants and ends with drinks: wheat, corn, rice, sugarcane, agave, grapes, juniper and spices become spirits and wines through fermentation, distillation or infusion. It combines botany, craft and food history, and works best as an index and an introduction rather than a commercial distilling manual.
- [A History of Chinese Alcohol (Wang Saishi): From Prehistoric Remains to the Late Qing](book/history-of-chinese-alcohol-book.md) — Wang Saishi’s A History of Chinese Alcohol, published by Shandong University Press in 2010, is a 430,000-character survey from prehistoric remains to the late Qing and is presented as the first systematic history of alcohol in China. This article covers its editions, author, structure, arguments, limits and common misreadings.
- [Imbibe!: Turning Jerry Thomas from Legend into Bar History](book/imbibe-book.md) — David Wondrich’s Imbibe!, first published in 2007 and revised in 2015, uses Jerry Thomas’s 1862 How to Mix Drinks to reconstruct nineteenth-century American bar culture and the making of the classic cocktail. This article covers its editions, author, structure, arguments, limits and common misreadings.
- [Jin Ping Mei: Wine Tables, Trade Routes and Ming Life in a Novel of Manners](book/jinpingmei.md) — Jin Ping Mei, a Ming novel attributed to the pen name Lanling Xiaoxiaosheng, uses the rise and fall of Ximen Qing’s household to portray commerce, family, desire and urban life. Wine is both daily banquet fare and a tool of transaction, seduction and power. This article reads the novel through alcohol and food history, with editions, evidence limits and common misreadings.
- [Beishan Jiujing (Classic of Wine)](book/jiujing.md) — A three-volume brewing treatise by Zhu Gong of the Northern Song, also known as Beishan Jiujing; the upper volume discusses wine and brewing principles, the middle records qu-making, and the lower sets out the brewing process, making it a key text for understanding Song-era yellow wine.
- [The Joy of Mixology: Families and Ratios Instead of Memorized Recipes](book/joy-of-mixology.md) — Gary Regan’s The Joy of Mixology, first published in 2003 and revised in 2018, teaches cocktail structure through drink families and proportions rather than rote memorisation. This article covers its editions, author, method, influence, limits and common misreadings.
- [The Oxford Companion to Wine](book/oxford-companion-to-wine.md) — An alphabetical encyclopedia of wine covering viticulture, winemaking, law, regions, history and people. It is built for reference and cross-checking rather than linear reading, and its entries change with each edition, so the edition consulted should be cited.
- [Proof: The Science of Booze: From Yeast to Hangover, a Laboratory in a Glass](book/proof-science-of-booze.md) — Adam Rogers’s Proof: The Science of Booze, published in 2014, explains alcohol through fermentation, distillation, maturation, sensory perception and hangover science. This article covers its edition, author, structure, arguments, limits and common misreadings.
- [Qimin Yaoshu](book/qimin-yaoshu.md) — Jia Sixie’s Northern Wei agricultural treatise, completed between 533 and 544 in ten volumes and ninety-two chapters, gives volume seven to qu-making and brewing. This article covers the author, the editions, chapters 63 to 67, the wine-cultural value of the text and its limits.
- [Sake: A Modern Guide](book/sake-modern-guide.md) — An English-language introduction to sake that begins with rice, polishing, koji and multiple parallel fermentation, then moves to types, temperature, pairing and buying. It is useful for building a framework, but its 2006 brands and market details need to be updated from current sources.
- [The Savoy Cocktail Book: A 1930 Bar Ledger That Became a Classic](book/savoy-cocktail-book.md) — Harry Craddock’s The Savoy Cocktail Book, first published by Constable in London in 1930, gathered 750 recipes and Gilbert Rumbold’s Art Deco illustrations into a working record of the Savoy Hotel’s American Bar. This article examines its editions, structure, arguments, limits and common misreadings.
- [Classic of Poetry (Shijing)](book/shijing.md) — The oldest surviving anthology of Chinese poetry, with 305 poems in the received text; wine appears in sacrifices, banquets and village drinking rites, and “spring wine” links harvest, season and a wish for long life. Read the wine lines with their ritual setting and literary form in view.
- [Suiyuan Shidan (tea and wine list)](book/suiyuan-shidan.md) — Yuan Mei’s Qing food classic, first printed in 1792, runs to fourteen lists and some three hundred dishes, including a “tea and wine list” devoted to wine and tea. It is key to literati food aesthetics and pairing ideas, but it is personal criticism rather than a systematic brewing manual.
- [Tasting Beer: Turning “I Like It” into a Trainable Sensory Language](book/tasting-beer.md) — Randy Mosher’s Tasting Beer, first published in 2009 and revised in 2017, teaches sensory evaluation, styles, ingredients, pairing and draft service, turning beer tasting from preference into describable and comparable skill. This article covers its editions, structure, arguments, limits and common misreadings.
- [Tian Gong Kai Wu (chapter on qu and malt)](book/tian-gong-kai-wu.md) — Song Yingxing’s Ming technological compendium, first printed in 1637 in three volumes and eighteen chapters, devotes its seventeenth chapter to the making of wine qu and red qu. This article covers the author, editions, the qu-making process, its value for wine culture and its limits.
- [Water Margin: Wine as a Passport to the Jianghu and a Narrative Device](book/water-margin.md) — Water Margin, a Ming novel attributed to Shi Nai’an, turns wine into a ritual of brotherhood, a tool of trickery and a hinge of fate. This article reads “Three Bowls and You Cannot Cross the Ridge,” “Drunk Beating Jiang the Gate Guard” and “Cleverly Taking the Birthday Gifts” through alcohol history and narrative, with editions, limits and common misreadings.
- [Wine and War](book/wine-and-war.md) — A narrative history of how French winegrowers, merchants and estates tried to preserve their vineyards, stocks and trade during the Nazi occupation of 1940–1944. Wine appears here as both a commodity and a symbol of national identity, and therefore as an object contested by occupiers, the Vichy regime, resisters and ordinary families.
- [The Wine Bible: An American Wine Book Between Exam Syllabus and Dinner Table](book/wine-bible.md) — Karen MacNeil’s The Wine Bible, first published in 2001 and revised in 2015 and 2022, connects grape, place, tasting, food and storage in one accessible reference. This article covers its editions, author, structure, arguments, limits and common misreadings.
- [The World Atlas of Beer: A Map of Fermentation from Abbeys to Craft Breweries](book/world-atlas-of-beer.md) — Tim Webb and Stephen Beaumont’s The World Atlas of Beer, first published in 2012 and revised to a third edition in 2021, explains global beer through maps, styles, regions and food pairing. This article covers its editions, authors, structure, arguments, limits and common misreadings.
- [The World Atlas of Whisky: Distilleries, Places and Flavour on One Map](book/world-atlas-of-whisky.md) — Dave Broom’s The World Atlas of Whisky, first published in 2010 and fully revised in 2014, uses maps, more than 200 distilleries and 750 expressions to build a framework of place, process and flavour. This article covers its editions, author, structure, arguments, limits and common misreadings.
- [The World Atlas of Wine](book/world-atlas-of-wine.md) — Hugh Johnson and Jancis Robinson’s geographical reference on wine, first published in 1971 and revised to an eighth edition in 2019, explains climate, soil, regulation and style through maps and regional entries. This article covers its authors, editions, arguments, value and limits.

## Customs

- [Birthday wine](custom/birthday-wine.md) — Birthday wine is the honouring drink at birthday and milestone banquets, combining blessing, thanks and family gathering. This page covers timing, ritual sequence, documentary sources and modern change, with notes on urging drink and health.
- [Cold Food Festival and wine](custom/cold-food-festival-wine.md) — The Cold Food Festival falls a day or two before Qingming and centres on a fire ban, cold food and tomb visits; wine plays the role of a libation and a family-meal drink rather than a fixed seasonal recipe. This page covers timing, origins, vessels, food, modern practice and health limits.
- [Double Ninth chrysanthemum wine](custom/double-ninth-chrysanthemum-wine.md) — Chrysanthemum wine at Double Ninth goes with climbing heights, wearing zhuyu and honouring elders. This page covers the two documented methods, regional variation, the layered history, and the limits of health claims and home brewing.
- [Dragon Boat realgar wine](custom/dragon-boat-realgar-wine.md) — Drinking realgar wine, sprinkling realgar water and painting a character on children’s foreheads are documented Han festival customs. Realgar contains arsenic and heating produces an even more toxic compound; this page separates origins and symbolism from the safety boundary and offers safer substitutes.
- [Full-moon feast](custom/full-moon-feast.md) — The full-moon feast marks a baby's first month with announcement, thanks, naming and social exchange. This page links the Song “baby-washing gathering” to later banquets, compares regional customs, and sets out infant-safety and breastfeeding boundaries.
- [Harvest wine and grape-harvest customs](custom/harvest-wine.md) — Harvest wine is the drink of thanksgiving, new-taste tasting and village meals after the autumn harvest; in European vineyard regions the same impulse continues as the grape harvest festival. This page compares Chinese and European forms of harvest drinking, their timing, vessels, food and modern tourist packaging.
- [Housewarming wine](custom/housewarming-wine.md) — Housewarming wine is the meal and drink offered after a new home is finished or a family moves in; in the north it is often called “warming the pot” or “searing the pot bottom”, in the south “entering the household”. It confirms the joy of settling in, the neighbourhood relationship and social obligations, and raises real questions about pressure to drink, display and indoor pollution.
- [Mid-Autumn moon wine](custom/mid-autumn-moon-wine.md) — The combination of moon gazing, moon worship and drinking on the 15th day of the eighth lunar month; wine is at once an offering, a reunion drink, a literary image and a market event, and documentary records must be kept apart from present-day practice.
- [New Year Drinking: Pepper-and-Cypress Wine and Tusu Wine](custom/new-year-wine.md) — Drinking pepper-and-cypress wine and tusu wine on the first day of the first lunar month is a seasonal custom recorded at least as early as the Southern Dynasties; the Jingchu Suishi Ji also notes that “the order of drinking begins with the youngest”. Wine here combines plague protection, New Year blessing and family order, not merely festive cheer.
- [Sacrificial wine](custom/sacrificial-wine.md) — Sacrificial wine is offered to ancestors, spirits and sages, with stress on purity, vessels and order. This page compares household, grave and public sacrifices, explains the three offerings and libation, and discusses modern disputes and the double meaning of the term.
- [Wedding Wine Customs: From Hejin to the Cross-Cup Drink](custom/wedding-wine.md) — Hejin was a shared-drinking rite in the pre-Qin wedding ceremony. The Liji says “hejin er yin”, and the Yili records “the third ceremonial drink uses a jin”. Later practice developed into the cross-cup drink, but the ancient and modern rites, vessels and meanings are not identical.
- [Zhongyuan Festival and wine](custom/zhongyuan-festival-wine.md) — The Zhongyuan Festival on the fifteenth day of the seventh lunar month is observed as a Taoist festival and as the Buddhist Ullambana; its core is ancestor remembrance, paper offerings and river lanterns. Wine serves mainly as a libation and a family-meal drink, turning grief into a gesture.

## Historical events

- [The 1855 Bordeaux Classification: A List Produced for a World's Fair](historical-event/1855-bordeaux-classification.md) — On 18 April 1855, Bordeaux brokers delivered a classification requested by the Chamber of Commerce, ranking Médoc and Graves red wines and Sauternes and Barsac sweet wines into growths. It rested on long-term market prices and reputation, not a blind tasting, and has seen only two official changes since.
- [1952 National Wine Evaluation: the eight famous wines and the start of a system](historical-event/1952-national-wine-evaluation.md) — The first national wine evaluation, held in Beijing in September 1952, selected eight Chinese famous wines from 103 samples; the list included four baijiu, Shaoxing jiafan wine and three Zhangyu products, setting the framework for later national evaluations and famous-wine branding.
- [The 1963 second national wine evaluation](historical-event/1963-national-wine-evaluation.md) — The second national wine evaluation, held in Beijing in October 1963 and organised jointly by the Ministry of Light Industry and the Ministry of Commerce, selected 18 national famous wines and created a separate national quality award for the first time. This page explains the method, the changes in the list and what the meeting meant institutionally.
- [1976 Judgment of Paris: California wine beats French estates](historical-event/1976-judgment-of-paris.md) — On 24 May 1976 the British wine merchant Steven Spurrier organised a blind tasting in Paris; 20 wines were poured, 10 from California and 10 from France, and California wines took the top score in both the red and white groups. The event later called the Judgment of Paris changed the international standing of California wine and became a classic case of cross-region comparison.
- [The 1979 third national wine evaluation: blind judging and aroma groups](historical-event/1979-national-wine-evaluation.md) — The third national wine evaluation, held in Dalian, Liaoning, in 1979, introduced coded blind judging, examiner qualification and comparison by aroma type, saccharifying agent and alcohol strength, and selected 18 national famous wines. This page explains the methodological turning point and its effect on the famous-wine list.
- [The 1984 fourth national wine evaluation: the thirteen famous wines](historical-event/1984-national-wine-evaluation.md) — The fourth national wine evaluation, held in Taiyuan, Shanxi, in 1984 and organised by the China Food Industry Association, selected 26 national famous wines, 13 of them baijiu, later known as the thirteen famous wines. This page explains the background, the expansion of the list and the disputes.
- [The 1989 fifth national wine evaluation: seventeen famous wines and the end of the system](historical-event/1989-national-wine-evaluation.md) — The fifth national wine evaluation, held in Hefei, Anhui, in January 1989 and organised by the China Food Industry Association, selected 17 national famous wines, all baijiu; national evaluations were discontinued afterwards. This page explains the list, the background and why the system ended.
- [The French Absinthe Ban of 1915: Science, War and the Wine Industry](historical-event/absinthe-ban-1915.md) — In the early years of the First World War the Paris police banned the sale of absinthe, and on 16 March 1915 a French law prohibited the production, circulation and sale of absinthe and similar liqueurs. The ban is often blamed on the idea that absinthe caused hallucinations, but modern research shows the fear of thujone was exaggerated; war, nationalism, wine-industry lobbying and the temperance movement were equally important.
- [The French AOC System (1935): The Birth of Controlled Appellation of Origin](historical-event/aoc-1935.md) — On 30 July 1935 France created the Appellation d'Origine Contrôlée (AOC) by decree and set up a national committee for the appellations of wines and spirits. Half a century of phylloxera, overproduction, adulteration and boundary disputes led to this system, which tied a place name to varieties, yields and methods. It later grew into the INAO system and influenced European origin protection.
- [The 2013 restrictions on official consumption and the baijiu adjustment](historical-event/austerity-consumption-restriction-2013.md) — After the Central Committee's eight-point rules of late 2012, official spending and receptions were tightened in 2013, and official banquets and premium baijiu consumption contracted sharply. This page explains the policy background, the industry response and the structural changes of the adjustment period.
- [The 1982 Bordeaux Vintage: How a Year Became a Turning Point in the Modern Wine Market](historical-event/bordeaux-vintage-1982.md) — Bordeaux in 1982 had a mild winter, early budbreak, a hot July and a dry September, producing ripe, rich wines. Some American critics initially thought the wines were too ripe and low in acidity; Robert Parker championed the vintage in The Wine Advocate, and over time 1982 became a turning point for his reputation and for the Bordeaux en primeur market. It was not a uniformly perfect vintage, and differences among estates and appellations were large.
- [The Boston Molasses Flood of 1919: An Industrial-Alcohol Disaster](historical-event/boston-molasses-flood.md) — On 15 January 1919 a storage tank holding about 2.3 million gallons of molasses burst in Boston's North End. The molasses moved into the streets at an estimated 35 mph, killing 21 people and injuring 150. The tank belonged to Purity Distilling, a subsidiary of United States Industrial Alcohol, and supplied material for wartime industrial alcohol. The disaster prompted reflection on tank design, construction and urban safety.
- [The 1927 Champagne Delimitation: Names, Parcels and a Boundary War](historical-event/champagne-delimitation-1927.md) — On 22 July 1927 France passed a law formally delimiting the Champagne vineyard: 407 communes, based on the Marne and Aisne communes fixed by the 1908 decree, plus three communes in the Aube and those that had claimed the Champagne name in harvest declarations from 1919 to 1924. The law recognised only land already planted, or planted before the phylloxera crisis, turning a departmental dispute into a parcel dispute. Over-registration in 1928 and a review from 1951 eventually cut the area to about 34,000 hectares and 302 communes.
- [The Champagne Riots of 1911: Appellation Boundaries, Grape Prices and the Identity of Champagne](historical-event/champagne-riots-1911.md) — In 1911 Champagne was shaken by large-scale protest over appellation boundaries and grape prices. - [Harry Johnson](person/harry-johnson.md) — A nineteenth-century American bartender and author on bar management, usually dated to 1845; in 1882 he self-published the New and Improved Bartender's Manual, a systematic treatment of bar operation, equipment, staffing and standards that moved bartending from performance toward professional management.
- [Henri Jayer](person/henri-jayer.md) — Burgundy winemaker, 1922–2006; farming Vosne-Romanée parcels as a sharecropper, he used low yields, full destemming, cool maceration and restrained new oak to make precise Pinot Noir, and turned the abandoned Cros Parantoux into a premier cru.
- [Hugh Johnson](person/hugh-johnson.md) — A British wine writer born in 1939; he published The World Atlas of Wine in 1971 and has produced Hugh Johnson's Pocket Wine Book since 1977, shaping several generations of readers with maps, vintage tables and regional narrative.
- [Jack Daniel](person/jack-daniel.md) — American distiller (c. 1850–1911) who registered Distillery No. 1 in Lynchburg, Tennessee, in 1866 and standardised the sugar-maple charcoal filtration known as the Lincoln County Process, defining Tennessee whiskey; his brand won gold in St Louis in 1904 and became one of America's most recognisable whiskies.
- [James Buchanan](person/james-buchanan.md) — Scotch whisky blender and brand builder, 1849–1935; born in Canada and raised in Ireland, he set up on his own in London in 1884, built Buchanan Blend, later Black & White, and joined the Distillers Company in 1925.
- [Jancis Robinson](person/jancis-robinson.md) — A British wine writer and editor, born in 1950; she passed the Master of Wine examination in 1984, has written a weekly Financial Times column since 1989, edits The Oxford Companion to Wine and has co-authored The World Atlas of Wine with Hugh Johnson since 2001.
- [Jerry Thomas](person/jerry-thomas.md) — An American bartender, commonly dated 1825 to 1885 (some sources give 1830). In 1862 he published How to Mix Drinks in New York, turning word-of-mouth recipes into a book, and was known as the Professor. The Blue Blazer gave bartending a theatrical side, though he did not invent the classic cocktails.
- [Josef Groll](person/josef-groll.md) — Bavarian brewer (1813–1887) who was hired by the citizens of Plzeň in 1842 and brewed the first golden, clear Pilsner lager using soft local water, Saaz hops, Moravian barley and bottom-fermenting yeast, establishing the template for the modern pale lager.
- [Lao She](person/lao-she.md) — A modern Chinese novelist and playwright, 1899 to 1966, a Beijinger of Manchu descent. His fiction and drama turn teahouses, wine shops and courtyard compounds into slices of history, and Camel Xiangzi and Teahouse contain verifiable drinking scenes; reliable records of his own drinking are limited and should not be exaggerated.
- [Li Bai](person/li-bai.md) — A poet of the high Tang, 701 to 762; wine in his poetry is both a setting for banquets and farewells and a metaphor for solitude, time and ambition. Reading his wine poems means separating the literary figure from the drink actually consumed in Tang times.
- [Li Qingzhao](person/li-qingzhao.md) — Song poet, born 1084 and usually said to have died around 1155; her wine lyrics run from "so drunk I did not know the way home" to "three cups, two cups of thin wine", cooling as her life moves from Shandong to the south.
- [Louis Pasteur](person/louis-pasteur.md) — A French chemist and microbiologist, 1822 to 1895; he showed that fermentation is caused by microorganisms and proposed a method of heat treatment that kills them, with a lasting influence on wine, beer and food safety.
- [Lu Xun](person/lu-xun.md) — A modern Chinese writer, 1881 to 1936, from Shaoxing in Zhejiang. Kong Yiji and Upstairs in the Wine Shop put Shaoxing yellow wine, warming and tavern hierarchy into fiction, while his 1927 lecture explained Wei-Jin drinking through political pressure.
- [Lu You](person/lu-you.md) — Southern Song poet, 1125–1210; his many wine poems span army life, village retirement and old love, with named drinks and precise settings in “Touring West Mountain Village”, “The Phoenix Hairpin” and “Rain at Sword Gate”.
- [Max Schubert](person/max-schubert.md) — Australian winemaker (1915–1994) who joined Penfolds as a messenger in 1931, became its first chief winemaker in 1948 and created the experimental Grange in 1951; after the board ordered him to stop in 1957, he continued the 1957–1959 vintages in secret, and Grange was officially reinstated for 1960.
- [Michael Jackson (beer writer)](person/michael-jackson.md) — British beer and whisky writer (1942–2007) whose 1977 World Guide to Beer turned style, sensory description and cultural context into a shared language, and whose books and TV series helped Belgian beer, British ale and American craft brewing reach a global audience.
- [Michael Jackson (beer writer)](person/michael-jackson.md) — British beer and whisky writer (1942–2007) whose 1977 World Guide to Beer turned style, sensory description and cultural context into a shared language, and whose books and TV series helped Belgian beer, British ale and American craft brewing reach a global audience.
- [Michel Rolland](person/michel-rolland.md) — A Bordeaux wine consultant, 1947–2026; he combined ripeness assessment, fermentation and maceration control, oak and micro-oxygenation into a consulting process, with clients across many regions, and became a focus of the argument over internationalised style.
- [Miguel A. Torres](person/miguel-torres.md) — Spanish winemaker and family-business president (born 1941) who joined Familia Torres in 1962, made Mas La Plana in 1970, won the 1979 Paris Wine Olympics, expanded to Chile in 1979 and California in 1982, launched Torres & Earth in 2008 and co-founded the International Wineries for Climate Action in 2019.
- [Ouyang Xiu](person/ouyang-xiu.md) — Northern Song writer and statesman, 1007–1072; he called himself the Old Drunkard and made wine the vehicle of landscape pleasure, later listing it among the five objects of his retirement; Chuzhou and Yingzhou are the two places where his relationship with wine is deepest.
- [Baron Philippe de Rothschild](person/philippe-de-rothschild.md) — French estate owner, poet and entrepreneur (1902–1988) who took over Château Mouton Rothschild in 1922, pioneered château bottling, artist labels, Mouton Cadet and the 1973 promotion to First Growth, and co-founded Opus One with Robert Mondavi in 1979.
- [Qin Hanzhang](person/qin-hanzhang.md) — A Chinese food-industry and fermentation engineer, 1908 to 2019; he led the Fenjiu technical trial, took part in research on Maotai and taught at Jiangnan University, and is regarded as one of the founders of scientific winemaking in China.
- [Robert Mondavi](person/robert-mondavi.md) — American winemaker and winery owner (1913–2008) who founded Robert Mondavi Winery in Oakville, Napa Valley, in 1966; championed varietal labelling, French oak and wine tourism, and in 1979 co-founded Opus One with Baron Philippe de Rothschild.
- [Robert Parker](person/robert-parker.md) — An American wine critic, born in 1947; in 1978 he founded the independent newsletter The Wine Advocate, and his 100-point scale shaped the global wine market, while the “Parkerization” debate over homogenised taste made him a focus of the industry. He retired in 2019.
- [Steven Spurrier](person/steven-spurrier.md) — A British wine merchant, educator and writer, 1941–2021; he founded the Académie du Vin in Paris in 1973, organised the 1976 Judgment of Paris with Patricia Gallagher, and later served as a Decanter consultant editor, shaping international perceptions of California wine and the old hierarchy.
- [Masataka Taketsuru](person/taketsuru-masataka.md) — A pioneer of Japanese whisky, 1894–1979; he studied distilling and blending in Scotland from 1918 to 1920, helped build the Yamazaki distillery, and founded Dai Nippon Kaju, the predecessor of Nikka, in Yoichi, Hokkaido, in 1934 after parting ways with Shinjiro Torii.
- [Tang Yin](person/tang-yin.md) — A Ming dynasty painter and poet, 1470 to 1524, from Wuxian in Suzhou. After an examination scandal he gave up official ambition and sold paintings. The Peach Blossom Cottage Song and Drinking Wine Facing the Moon make flowers, wine and reclusion a position on life, and the official history also records him feigning madness through drink.
- [Tao Yuanming](person/tao-yuanming.md) — Eastern Jin poet, 365–427; he described himself as “addicted to wine” and made drinking part of the same choice as leaving office and returning to the fields.
- [Shinjiro Torii](person/torii-shinjiro.md) — Japanese entrepreneur (1879–1962) who founded Torii Shoten in Osaka in 1899, launched Akadama Port Wine in 1907, built the Yamazaki distillery in 1923, hired Masataka Taketsuru and released Japan's first commercial malt whisky, Shirofuda, in 1929.
- [Wang Wei](person/wang-wei.md) — Tang poet and painter, 701–761; “A Farewell to Yuan Er on His Mission to Anxi” turns one cup of wine into the definitive Chinese image of farewell, later set to music as the “Three Refrains of Yangguan”.
- [Wang Zengqi](person/wang-zengqi.md) — A contemporary Chinese writer, 1920 to 1997, from Gaoyou in Jiangsu and a student of Shen Congwen. His food essays treat wine as part of the table, valuing dishes, people and place over prices and display; “a cup of cloudy wine past midday” in “The Rain of Kunming” is his most concentrated passage on drinking.
- [William Grant](person/william-grant.md) — A Scottish whisky entrepreneur, 1839 to 1923; he worked his way up at Mortlach, built Glenfiddich with his family from 1886, ran the first spirit on Christmas Day 1887 and added Balvenie in 1892; the company remains family-owned.
- [Xin Qiji](person/xin-qiji.md) — Southern Song poet and soldier, 1140–1207; after crossing south from the Jin-occupied north in his twenties, he spent two long retirements writing ci in which wine opens memory, questions old age and mocks its own consolation.
- [Yu Dafu](person/yu-dafu.md) — A modern Chinese writer, 1896 to 1945, from Fuyang in Zhejiang and a founding member of the Creation Society. His classical-style poems and prose often mention wine, and in 1942 he ran the Zhao Yuji distillery in Sumatra under the alias Zhao Lian; he was killed in 1945.
- [Zhang Bishi](person/zhang-bishi.md) — A Chinese industrialist and overseas merchant, 1841 to 1916; in 1892 he invested in the Changyu wine company in Yantai, Shandong, bringing European grape varieties, equipment and winemakers to China and helping to establish a modern wine industry there.
- [Zheng Banqiao](person/zheng-banqiao.md) — A Qing dynasty painter, calligrapher and poet, 1693 to 1766; he served as magistrate of Fan and Wei counties, then sold paintings in Yangzhou as one of the Eight Eccentrics. His inscriptions and his published price list place wine, painting prices and livelihood on the same line.
- [Zhou Henggang](person/zhou-henggang.md) — A Chinese baijiu technologist, 1918 to 2004; he led the 1955 and 1962 Yantai trials and the 1964-1966 Maotai trial, summarised the Yantai method, and advanced research on fuqu, caproic-acid bacteria and pit mud.

- [André Simon](person/andre-simon.md) — A French-born British wine merchant, writer and bibliophile, 1877–1970; he moved to London in 1902 as Pommery's agent, co-founded the Wine & Food Society in 1933, and used trade records and bibliography to make wine history verifiable.
- [André Tchelistcheff](person/andre-tchelistcheff.md) — A Russian-born American winemaker, 1901–1994; chief winemaker at Beaulieu Vineyard from 1938, he brought cold fermentation, malolactic control and small French oak to Napa, and mentored generations of California winemakers.
- [Bruno Giacosa](person/bruno-giacosa.md) — A Piedmont winemaker, 1929–2018; in 1964 he labelled a single Barbaresco vineyard, Santo Stefano, later bought Falletto, and held to long cask ageing and strict vintage selection, refusing to bottle Barolo 2010.
- [Giacomo Conterno](person/giacomo-conterno.md) — A traditionalist Barolo producer in Monforte d'Alba; his long maceration, large Slavonian oak and Monfortino riserva made Barolo built for decades, and his family bought Cascina Francia in 1974.
- [Jim Barrett](person/jim-barrett.md) — An American winery owner and lawyer, 1926–2013; with Ernie Hahn he bought and revived Chateau Montelena in 1972, whose 1973 Chardonnay, made by Mike Grgich, won the white-wine flight of the 1976 Judgment of Paris.
- [Maynard Amerine](person/maynard-amerine.md) — An American viticulturist and UC Davis professor, 1911–1998; with Albert Winkler he built the growing-degree-day heat index, and he standardised wine sensory evaluation through trained panels, blind tests and statistics.
- [Michael Broadbent](person/michael-broadbent.md) — A British wine critic, auctioneer and writer, 1927–2020; he rebuilt Christie's wine department in 1966, recorded tens of thousands of wines in red notebooks from 1952, and wrote The Pocket Guide to Wine Tasting and Vintage Wine.
- [Piero Antinori](person/piero-antinori.md) — An Italian producer, born 1938 and the 26th generation of Marchesi Antinori; he took over in 1966, released Tignanello in 1971 with Sangiovese, Cabernet and small oak, and expanded the family estates internationally.
- [Warren Winiarski](person/warren-winiarski.md) — An American Napa winemaker, 1928–2024; he founded Stag's Leap Wine Cellars in 1973, won the red-wine flight of the 1976 Judgment of Paris with his 1973 Cabernet, and later worked on Napa's conjunctive labelling law.
- [Peter Lehmann](person/peter-lehmann.md) — An Australian Barossa Valley winemaker, 1930–2013; after Yalumba and Saltram he founded Masterson in 1979 to buy grapes from growers facing a vine-pull scheme, renamed the company Peter Lehmann Wines in 1982, and shaped modern Barossa with Stonewell and Eight Songs Shiraz.
- [Jimmy Russell](person/jimmy-russell.md) — An American bourbon master distiller at Wild Turkey; he joined in 1954 at eighteen, worked from the floor up, became master distiller in the 1960s, kept the 101 formula, and shaped Rare Breed, Kentucky Spirit and Russell's Reserve before forming a father-son master-distiller pair with Eddie in 2015.
- [Booker Noe](person/booker-noe.md) — An American sixth-generation master distiller of the Jim Beam family, 1929–2004; he took over the Boston, Kentucky plant in 1960, filled the millionth barrel in 1965, launched Booker's in 1987/1988 and built the Small Batch Bourbon Collection in 1992.
- [Fritz Maytag](person/fritz-maytag.md) — An American brewer and Anchor Brewing owner, born 1937; in 1965 he bought the failing Steam Beer brewery in San Francisco, rebuilt Anchor Steam with open fermentation and whole-cone hops, and became a founding figure of the post-Prohibition craft-beer movement.
- [Ken Grossman](person/ken-grossman.md) — An American founder of Sierra Nevada Brewing, born 1955; he opened a homebrew shop in Chico in 1976, built a brewery from scrap dairy equipment in 1980, and used Cascade hops and Pale Ale to drive the craft-beer revolution while keeping the company family-owned.
- [Jim Koch](person/jim-koch.md) — An American founder of Samuel Adams and The Boston Beer Company, born 1949; he brewed his great-great-grandfather's lager recipe at home in 1984, launched Boston Lager in 1985, listed the company in 1995 and created the Brewing the American Dream programme.
- [John Jameson](person/john-jameson.md) — A Scottish-born Irish merchant and distiller, 1740–1823; he opened the Bow Street distillery in Dublin in 1780, experimented with malted and unmalted barley under the malt tax, and laid the basis for Jameson's triple-distilled single-pot-still whiskey.
- [George Smith](person/george-smith.md) — A Speyside distiller in the Livet valley, died 1871; he obtained an Elgin licence in 1824, became the first legal distiller in the Glenlivet parish, built the plant amid threats from illicit producers, and laid the foundation for The Glenlivet single malt.
- [Lalou Bize-Leroy](person/lalou-bize-leroy.md) — A Burgundy merchant and estate owner, born 1932; she took over Maison Leroy in 1955, co-managed DRC from 1974 to 1992, founded Domaine Leroy in 1988 and converted it to biodynamics, influencing modern Burgundy through very low yields and massal selection.

## Poetry

- [Asking Liu Shijiu: Bai Juyi's Cup of Wine on a Winter Night](poem/bai-juyi-asking-liu.md) — Bai Juyi's “Asking Liu Shijiu” has only four lines and twenty characters: freshly brewed lüyi wine, a small red clay stove, an evening about to snow, and the question “can you drink a cup?” It turns an invitation into a warm little winter scene, and its sense of measure is more lasting than the wine.
- [Duan Ge Xing: Cao Cao Turns Wine into the Anxiety of Seeking Talent](poem/cao-cao-duan-ge-xing.md) — Duan Ge Xing is an old yuefu title traditionally assigned to Cao Cao. It begins with “I face the wine and should sing; how long is a life?”, uses wine to dissolve sorrow, moves into the anxiety of seeking talent, and closes with the Duke of Zhou spitting out his food to receive guests. Wine is not pleasure here but the medium that places a short life and an unfinished enterprise on the same table.
- [Climbing High: Du Fu and a Cup of Rough Wine at Kuizhou](poem/du-fu-deng-gao.md) — Du Fu wrote Climbing High in the autumn of 767 at Kuizhou. The eight lines of regulated verse give four lines to autumn scenery and four to his own life, ending on the words “I have just stopped the cup of rough wine”. Wine never appears at a banquet here; it appears as a stopping, a mark of age, illness and drift.
- [Arrival of a Guest: Du Fu Entertains at His Thatched Cottage](poem/du-fu-ke-zhi.md) — Du Fu wrote “Arrival of a Guest” around the spring of 761 at his thatched cottage in Chengdu; the poem carries his own note that he was delighted by a visit from Magistrate Cui. From spring water and gulls to sweeping the path and opening the gate, from a distant market and plain food to poor wine, it makes a modest meal sincere and warm.
- [Qingming: Rain, a Traveller and the Direction of a Wine House](poem/du-mu-qingming.md) — The four lines of Qingming give us rain, a traveller and a shepherd boy pointing the way; the wine house is asked about but never reached. The poem has long been attributed to Du Mu, yet it is absent from his collected works and first appears in a Southern Song reference book, so authorship remains disputed; Apricot Blossom Village is not necessarily a fixed place name either.
- [Yu Jia Ao: Fan Zhongyan and a Cup of Cloudy Wine at the Frontier](poem/fan-zhongyan-yu-jia-ao.md) — Yu Jia Ao describes autumn at the north-western frontier: geese leave without lingering, border sounds rise, the lone city closes. The lower half sets “a cup of cloudy wine, home ten thousand miles away” against “Mount Yanran not yet inscribed, no way to return”, and ends on “the general's white hair, the soldiers' tears”. A single cup measures the distance between home and the frontier.
- [Drinking Alone under the Moon: Li Bai's Pot of Wine and Three Shadows](poem/li-bai-drinking-alone.md) — Li Bai's “Drinking Alone under the Moon” turns solitude into a little theatre with the line “I raise my cup to invite the bright moon, and with my shadow we become three”. The moon and shadow cannot drink, yet the poet recruits them as company. Wine here is not a remedy for sorrow but a prop for an imaginative stage.
- [Written While Travelling: Li Bai's Lanling Wine and a Host Who Makes a Guest Forget Home](poem/li-bai-kezhong-zuo.md) — Li Bai's “Written While Travelling” is a four-line poem about Lanling wine, a jade bowl and the amber glow of the liquor, and a host whose hospitality makes a guest forget he is far from home. “Turmeric” here means the rhizome used to scent wine, not the tulip; the wine is a low-alcohol fermented grain wine, not today's baijiu.
- [Bring in the Wine: Li Bai on drinking and time](poem/li-bai-qiang-jin-jiu.md) — Bring in the Wine uses an old yuefu title and was probably written around the Tianbao era; Li Bai uses a drinking song to write about irreversible time, confidence in talent and the relief of disappointment. The received text differs from the Dunhuang manuscript Xizun Kong, and three hundred cups is literary exaggeration rather than a record of intake.
- [Sheng Sheng Man: Three Cups and Two Cups of Thin Wine against the Evening Wind](poem/li-qingzhao-sheng-sheng-man.md) — Li Qingzhao's Sheng Sheng Man opens with fourteen repeated characters, then moves through a warm-cold autumn day, three cups and two cups of thin wine, fallen flowers and fine rain on the paulownia. Wine neither warms the body nor drowns sorrow; “thin” and “could not withstand” set the whole poem's helplessness.
- [Zui Hua Yin: Li Qingzhao and a Cup of Wine on the Double Ninth](poem/li-qingzhao-zui-hua-yin.md) — Li Qingzhao's Zui Hua Yin follows a Double Ninth day from morning to dusk: thin mist and dense clouds, an incense burner, a jade pillow and gauze curtain, then a cup of wine by the eastern fence, ending on “the person is thinner than the yellow flower”. Wine appears once, yet it links the day's idle sorrow to the thinness of a body.
- [Yu Lin Ling: Liu Yong and a Farewell Cup He Could Not Drink](poem/liu-yong-yu-lin-ling.md) — Yu Lin Ling describes a farewell after an autumn rain: a tent is set up outside the city gate and wine is laid out, but there is no heart for it; the boatman urges departure; the two hold hands and weep. The lower half imagines where the poet will wake from wine, turning a cup that was never enjoyed into the clear-headed scene of willows, morning wind and a fading moon. Wine here is not entertainment but evidence of parting.
- [Visiting West Mountain Village: Lu You and a Bowl of Farmhouse Wine](poem/lu-you-you-shan-xi-cun.md) — Lu You's Visiting West Mountain Village describes a trip to a mountain village: the farmhouse winter wine is cloudy but the chicken and pork for a guest are ample; mountains and waters repeat until the road seems lost, then willows dark and flowers bright reveal another village; the spring sacrifice is near and flutes and drums follow; before leaving, the poet promises to lean on his staff and knock at the door at any hour. Wine is cloudy home brew, not a famous bottle, but it is where the meal begins.
- [Passing an Old Friend's Farm: Meng Haoran and an Ordinary Cup of Wine](poem/meng-haoran-guo-gu-ren-zhuang.md) — Meng Haoran's Passing an Old Friend's Farm describes an ordinary visit to a farmhouse: the friend prepares chicken and millet, green trees ring the village, a blue mountain slopes beyond the wall, the window opens onto a threshing floor and vegetable plot, and wine is raised while farming is discussed, ending with a promise to return for chrysanthemums at the Double Ninth.
- [Ding Feng Bo: A Cloak of Mist and Rain for a Whole Life](poem/su-shi-ding-feng-bo.md) — Su Shi's Ding Feng Bo records a rainstorm on the road to Shahu on the seventh of the third month: companions were wretched, he alone was unbothered; after the rain cleared he looked back and wrote “there is neither wind nor rain, nor clear sky”. Wine appears only once—when the chill spring wind sobers him—and turns the poem from rain toward clarity.
- [Nian Nu Jiao: Remembering the Red Cliff, Su Shi Pours Wine to the River Moon](poem/su-shi-nian-nu-jiao.md) — Nian Nu Jiao was written in 1082, while Su Shi was exiled to Huangzhou. The song moves from the great river flowing east to Zhou Yu of the Three Kingdoms, then back to the poet with white hair, and ends with “I pour a cup of wine as a libation to the river moon”. Wine appears only at the end, not as a deep drink but as a cup poured out to the river and the moon.
- [Shui Diao Ge Tou: Su Shi Asks the Moon with a Cup of Wine](poem/su-shi-shui-diao-ge-tou.md) — Su Shi wrote Shui Diao Ge Tou at the Mid-Autumn festival of 1076. The preface says he drank until dawn, was very drunk, and also missed his brother Ziyou. The song moves from raising a cup to ask the blue sky to moonlight on a sleepless person, and closes with the wish that we may live long and share the same moon a thousand miles apart.
- [Song of the Peach Blossom Hut: Tang Yin Pays for Wine with Peach Blossoms](poem/tang-yin-tao-hua-an-ge.md) — The Song of the Peach Blossom Hut describes a “peach blossom immortal” living in a hut among peach trees: he plants the trees, picks the blossoms and trades them for wine money, sits before the flowers when sober and sleeps beneath them when drunk, and would rather die old among flowers and wine than bow before carriages and horses. It makes honest poverty sound exuberant, then asks what remains of the tombs of the great. The poem survives in widely differing versions, so the text used must be stated.
- [Drinking Wine, Number Five: Tao Yuanming Sees the Southern Mountain](poem/tao-yuanming-drinking-wine-5.md) — Number Five of Drinking Wine describes building a hut among men without the noise of carriages and horses, picking chrysanthemums by the eastern fence and seeing the southern mountain, and ending on “there is a true meaning in this, but I have forgotten the words to explain it”. The poem itself never uses the word wine; wine belongs to the title and the preface of the sequence, and must be read within Tao Yuanming's return to farm life.
- [Drinking Wine, No. 7: Autumn Chrysanthemums, the Care-Forgotten Thing and the Eastern Gallery](poem/tao-yuanming-drinking-wine-7.md) — Tao Yuanming's seventh poem in the Drinking Wine series begins with dew-wet autumn chrysanthemums floated in wine, uses the drink to push worldly feelings away, then moves through a solitary cup, a emptied pot, sunset and returning birds to a long whistle under the eastern gallery. It is a quiet poem about self-sufficiency.
- [Liangzhou Ci: Wang Han Sets a Cup of Grape Wine Beside the Battlefield](poem/wang-han-liang-zhou-ci.md) — Wang Han's Liangzhou Ci has only four lines: grape wine, a luminous cup, a pipa played on horseback, and “do not laugh if I lie drunk on the battlefield; since ancient times how many have come back from war?” It places the most splendid drinking vessel and the cruellest battlefield in one scene and writes the cost of war in the tone of a drinking invitation. The last line is a question, not a boast.
- [Seeing Off Yuan the Second on His Mission to Anxi: One Cup Becomes a Song](poem/wang-wei-song-yuan-er.md) — Wang Wei's “Seeing Off Yuan the Second on His Mission to Anxi” opens with morning rain at Weicheng and green willows by the guest house, then lands on “one more cup before you go: west of Yang Pass there are no old friends”. It compresses a farewell into a cup, and was already sung in the Tang before becoming the “Three Refrains on Yang Pass”.
- [Po Zhen Zi: Xin Qiji Trims the Lamp and Looks at His Sword](poem/xin-qiji-po-zhen-zi.md) — Po Zhen Zi begins with “drunk, I trim the lamp and look at my sword”, moves into a dream of linked camps, a reviewed army and fast horses, keeps nine lines heroic, and then breaks the dream with “pitiful—white hair has grown”. Wine is the entrance to the dream and the night-time gesture of an ambition that cannot be fulfilled.
- [Huan Xi Sha: Yan Shu Sees Time in a Cup of Wine](poem/yan-shu-huan-xi-sha.md) — Huan Xi Sha begins with hearing a new song and drinking a cup of wine; last year's weather and the old pavilion are still there, yet the setting sun is asked when it will return. The lower half sets “no way to stop the falling flowers” against “the returning swallows look familiar”, and ends with the poet pacing alone on a fragrant path. Wine is not a banquet here but a quiet daily act that lets a person face time.
- [In Praise of the Virtue of Wine: Liu Ling's drinking as political fable](poem/liu-ling-jiu-de-song.md) — In Praise of the Virtue of Wine sets the Great Man against titled gentlemen and retired scholars who defend ritual law, turning drinking into a stance outside social judgement. Wine is not a party prop but a quiet refusal of Wei-Jin moralised politics.
- [The Pavilion of the Old Drunkard: Ouyang Xiu on exile, landscape and local government](poem/ouyang-xiu-zuiweng-ting-ji.md) — Written in 1046 when Ouyang Xiu was magistrate of Chuzhou, the essay moves from Mount Langya and the Brewing Spring to the people of Chu and the magistrate's banquet, ending on the question of who the magistrate is. It is a landscape essay and also a text about exile, light government and shared joy.
- [Holding a Cup and Asking the Moon: Li Bai on people, time and the moon](poem/li-bai-ba-jiu-wen-yue.md) — The poem moves from stopping the cup to ask when the moon began to the line that the people of today cannot see the ancient moon, though this moon once shone on the ancients. The moon is a constant reference, wine the act of asking, and the ending brings the cosmos back into the cup.
- [Song of the Eight Immortals of the Wine Cup: Du Fu's portrait gallery of High Tang](poem/du-fu-yin-zhong-ba-xian-ge.md) — Eight sketches of He Zhizhang, the Prince of Ruyang, the former chancellor Li Shizhi, Cui Zongzhi, Su Jin, Li Bai, Zhang Xu and Jiao Sui. Wine lets each man step outside his official role; the poem descends from court to street and is neither a capacity ranking nor a simple hymn.
- [Linjiang Xian: A Door That Would Not Open, a River That Could Be Heard](poem/su-shi-linjiang-xian-ye-yin-dongpo.md) — Written during Su Shi's Huangzhou exile, the lyric begins with a night of drinking at Dongpo and a return to Lingao where the servant is asleep and the door does not answer. The closing wish for a small boat is not an actual departure but an imagined exit heard through a locked door.
- [Ru Meng Ling: A Thirty-Three-Character Little Film](poem/li-qingzhao-ru-meng-ling.md) — Li Qingzhao's lyric has only thirty-three characters yet layers time, place, drunkenness, losing the way and startled birds. "Deeply drunk" may be wine or scenery, and "zheng du" may be racing to cross or asking how to cross; the small lyric lives in those uncertainties.
- [Xi Jiang Yue: Pushing the Pine Away, a Joke and a Temper](poem/xin-qiji-xi-jiang-yue-qian-xing.md) — Xin Qiji's lyric describes drunken laughter, falling by a pine, asking the pine how drunk he is, then pushing it away with a "go." On the surface a comic piece, underneath it is anger at the books of the ancients and at Southern Song politics.
- [The Wedding at Cana: A Wall-Sized Feast with a Miracle at the Edge](artwork/veronese-wedding-at-cana.md) — Veronese's 1562–1563 canvas for the refectory of San Giorgio Maggiore, now at the Louvre, turns the Gospel miracle of water into wine into a Venetian banquet; the wine moves through servants, cupbearers and guests.
- [The Absinthe Drinker: Manet Puts a Marginal Man on a History-Painting Scale](artwork/manet-absinthe-drinker.md) — Manet's early canvas of about 1859, now at the Ny Carlsberg Glyptotek, shows a single cloaked man, an empty bottle at his feet and a small glass on a ledge; rejected by the 1859 Salon, it became a founding image of modern-life painting.
- [L'Absinthe: Degas Paints Two People Sitting Together and Looking Past Each Other](artwork/degas-absinthe.md) — Degas's canvas of 1875–1876, originally titled In a Café, hangs in the Musée d'Orsay; an actress and a painter share a Paris café table with a glass of absinthe, and the subject is the solitude of the modern city.
- [The Night Café: Van Gogh Paints a Gaslit Room as an All-Night Fever](artwork/van-gogh-night-cafe.md) — Van Gogh's September 1888 canvas at the Yale University Art Gallery shows an all-night café in Arles: red walls, a green billiard table, four lemon-yellow gas lamps and exhausted patrons.
- [The Peasant Wedding: Bruegel Paints a Village Feast as a Cross-Section of Society](artwork/bruegel-peasant-wedding.md) — Bruegel's 1568 panel in Vienna shows a Flemish peasant wedding: the bride before a green cloth, the bridegroom absent by custom, porridge carried on a door and beer poured from a large jug.
- [Literary Gathering: Emperor Huizong Paints a Scholars' Dinner as an Ideal of Civil Order](artwork/wen-hui-tu.md) — Traditionally attributed to Song emperor Huizong, this ink-and-colour silk scroll in Taipei shows a garden gathering of scholars, with wine cups, tea ware and fruit on a large table.
- [The Eighteen Scholars: A Tang Political Portrait Turned into Four Scenes of Literati Life](artwork/eighteen-scholars.md) — A set of four hanging scrolls in Taipei, formerly labelled Song but now often dated to the Ming, depicts the Eighteen Scholars of Tang Taizong in garden gatherings organised around the qin, chess, calligraphy and painting.
- [Court of Master Sommeliers: turning restaurant wine service into an exam](organization/court-of-master-sommeliers.md) — The Court of Master Sommeliers held its first Master Sommelier examination in London in 1969 and was formally established in 1977. Its four levels turn a sommelier’s experience into examinable knowledge and service, but the title is neither a licence to practise nor a government certification.
- [Institute of Masters of Wine: a professional community born from an exam](organization/institute-of-masters-of-wine.md) — The Institute of Masters of Wine began with an examination in 1953, when 21 candidates sat and six passed; those six founded the Institute in 1955. Based in London but international in membership, it examines theory, blind tasting and a research paper and requires a code of conduct. It is not a regulator and does not grant a licence to practise.
- [Brewers Association: the trade body that defines small and independent](organization/brewers-association.md) — The Brewers Association took its present form in 2005 when the Association of Brewers merged with the Brewers’ Association of America. It represents small and independent US breweries, maintains the craft brewer definition, runs the Great American Beer Festival and advocates in Washington; it is not a government regulator.
- [China Alcoholic Drinks Association: from an industrial body to a cross-category trade organisation](organization/china-alcoholic-drinks-association.md) — Originally the China Brewing Industry Association, CADA was approved by the former Ministry of Light Industry and registered with the Ministry of Civil Affairs on 22 June 1992. Its registration authority is the Ministry of Civil Affairs and its business guidance comes from the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology; it covers baijiu, beer, wine and yellow wine through standards, exhibitions and industry data, but it is not an administrative agency.
