# Taipai Handmade Winter Brew

Taipai Handmade Winter Brew is a seasonal Shaoxing line from [Taipai](ref:brand), made by [Zhejiang Taipai Shaoxing Wine Co., Ltd.](ref:producer) in Hutong, Keqiao. The name puts the season on the label: ingredients are pitched between Lidong and Lichun, fermentation is stretched by the cold, and the wine is pressed, pasteurised and stored in jars. Inside the line sit three-, five-, six-, eight- and ten-year tiers in formats from 375 mL to 500 mL. The name describes a season and a method, not a grade in the national standard.

## One winter, one brew: from Lidong to Lichun

Jiangnan University's traditional fermented-food research centre states the window plainly: from Lidong, the start of winter, to Lichun, the start of spring, Shaoxing brewers make what they call winter brew. The reasons are cold air, which suppresses spoilage organisms, and the clarity of Jian Lake water in winter. Taipai's own account is more precise: the Winter Solstice begins the "nine nines" count, and 81 days later, at Jingzhe, the mash is ready to press, pasteurise and seal into jars. Taken together, winter brew is less a slogan than a production calendar fixed by climate.

## The Hutong plant and the limits of "handmade"

The plant stands in Hutong Subdistrict, Keqiao District. Taipai says it is 500 metres from the Jian Lake source, was founded in 1994 with investment from Zhejiang Cereals, Oils and Foodstuffs Import and Export Co., covers 450 mu, and runs 13 brewing workshops, two bottling workshops and more than 800 employees. It also calls itself "China's largest fully handmade yellow-rice-wine producer". Annual output and batch size are not disclosed. The figures describe capacity and scale; "largest" is a company claim, not an independent statistic. Its status as a provincial intangible-heritage workshop for Shaoxing wine refers to the craft, not to a particular batch.

## The temperature curve: 26–30 °C at pitching, below 25 °C later

The key to winter brew is not cold alone but a temperature curve. Jiangnan University gives two points: bring the mixture to 26–30 °C at pitching and keep every vat as even as possible; then slow the post-fermentation to below 25 °C for 15–20 days. Pitch too warm and the main fermentation runs away, pushing acidity up; pitch too cold and it struggles to start. The stirring step called kaipa exists to manage this curve by hand, adjusting temperature and oxygen. The first stirring usually comes more than ten hours after pitching, with the second about four hours later.

## Seventy to eighty days, pressed around Jingzhe

A virtual teaching project at Shaoxing University describes the traditional schedule as pitching around Xiaoxue, the minor snow solar term, and pressing by Lichun, with fermentation lasting 70–80 days. Taipai's own account puts the end at Jingzhe. The two are not contradictory: pitching falls inside the Lidong-to-Lichun window, and post-fermentation runs into late winter or early spring, with pressing timed to the weather and the maturity of the mash. The 70–80 days is an order of magnitude, not a fixed number for every vat. A longer fermentation gives starch conversion and fermentation more time, and the body usually gains weight.

## Hand versus machine: what actually changes

The handmade difference sits in judgement-heavy steps: when to stir, how many times, how to manage the temperature difference between the centre and the wall of the vat, and when to seal. Machines bring consistency and scale: temperature control is steadier and batch variation smaller. Both can make sound wine. Handmade is not automatically better, and machine-made is not automatically cheap. Taipai uses "handmade" as a product name to signal a method and its labour cost; the drinker still has to judge the raw materials, fermentation control and ageing on the specific bottle.

## Linfan starter and the tanfan method

Traditional Shaoxing wine uses a linfa starter to begin fermentation, then the tanfan method for the steamed rice: the rice is spread out or air-cooled, with the cooled grain temperature adjusted to the weather, commonly held in a 50–80 °C range, before being pitched in two stages with [wheat qu](ref:qu-making) made from [wheat](ref:wheat), linfa starter and slurry water. The starch in [glutinous rice](ref:glutinous-rice) is first [saccharified](ref:saccharification) into fermentable sugar and then converted by [fermentation](ref:fermentation) into alcohol and aroma. [Water](ref:water) runs through every stage; the quality of Jian Lake water shows up directly in the cleanliness of the finish. The linfa-plus-tanfan combination is one of the things that separates Shaoxing wine from many other huangjiu.

## The numbers in a single vat

The teaching material gives a per-vat recipe for jiafan wine: 144 kg glutinous rice, 25 kg wheat qu, 68.6 kg water, 50 kg slurry water, 8–10 kg linfa starter and 5 kg of 50% ABV distilled rice spirit, fermented in a vat of about 500 kg capacity. The ratios matter: rice to qu affects saccharification speed, while slurry water and starter affect acidity and the final yield. Recipes differ by producer and year. These figures are a reference point for the traditional process, not Taipai's commercial formula for any single product.

## Three to ten years: the winter-brew ladder

Taipai's site lists five tiers: three, five, six, eight and ten years, in formats including 375 mL × 12, 500 mL × 6, 500 mL × 8 and 418 mL × 6. The year figure is a product design tied to ageing. Under GB/T 17946, the declared age of a blend is the weighted average age, the base wine carrying the declared age must be at least 50%vol, and a wine aged three years or more should reach the superior grade. "Ten years" therefore does not mean every drop is ten years old; it is a weighted average. Read the age and sweetness type on the back label, not only the large type on the box.

## Why winter brew tastes rounder

A long, cool fermentation slows both saccharification and fermentation. The mash spends more time in the vat, and the finished wine usually feels fuller, with more sweetness from residual sugar and glycerol-like texture. Compared with [Kuaijishan Lanting](ref:lanting), winter brew is not necessarily more aged in aroma, but it is broader on the palate. Compared with [Taipai Benjiu](ref:benjiu), it does not sell "no caramel" freshness but the weight that time and a slow ferment build. The three coordinates are distinct: Lanting is aged aroma, Benjiu is freshness, winter brew is roundness.

## At the table: red-braised, braised and hot pot

A rounder semi-dry wine suits strongly flavoured food: red-braised pork, braised duck, soy-braised meats, lamb stew and hot pot. Its sweetness softens salt and chilli, and its body is not overwhelmed by sauce. Steamed fish and blanched prawns are better with the fresher Benjiu; hairy crab can go either way. Warm the wine gently in a water bath rather than boiling it, which scatters the aroma.

## Prices: about RMB 10 to RMB 73 a bottle

Retail prices captured in November 2026 give a checkable ladder: a six-pack of three-year handmade winter brew, 500 mL each, at about RMB 61, or roughly RMB 10 a bottle; an eight-year 500 mL bottle at about RMB 43; a ten-year 500 mL bottle at about RMB 73. The premium for age is visible but still leaves the line in the middle ground between everyday and banquet wine. Prices vary with channel, promotion and region, and gift boxes add packaging cost. Unit price alone is a poor guide; check age, sweetness type and format first.

## The "winter brew is a grade" mistake

"Winter brew" describes a seasonal window for pitching and fermentation. It is not a grade in the national standard, and it does not guarantee a better wine than a spring or autumn brew. Raw materials, qu quality, temperature control, a clean fermentation and storage conditions decide quality. A related error is to think the wine only ferments in winter: the main fermentation is in winter, but post-fermentation continues into Lichun and sometimes to Jingzhe before pressing. Another error is to treat "handmade" as a quality certificate. It is a method, not a grading system.

## Buying and keeping

Check the producer and origin first, then the tier, sweetness type, [alcohol by volume](ref:abv) and net content. The winter-brew line is usually semi-dry; check the ABV on the label. Jar, bottle and gift-box formats differ mainly in packaging and channel. Store unopened wine upright, away from light and heat; refrigerate after opening and finish it soon. Shaoxing wine is one protected branch of [Chinese](ref:china) [huangjiu](ref:yellow-wine), and good yellow-rice wine is made outside [Shaoxing](ref:shaoxing) too. Winter brew is not the only answer.
