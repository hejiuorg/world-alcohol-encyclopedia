# Terms

[返回English目录](../README.md)

- [Acidity](acidity.md) — Acidity describes the amount and strength of acids in a drink, commonly through total acidity in g/L and pH, two different measures; it supports freshness, balance and ageing potential, and affects sulfur dioxide effectiveness.
- [Alcohol by volume (ABV)](alcohol-by-volume.md) — ABV is the volume fraction of ethanol in a drink measured at 20 °C, usually written as % vol; it is a basic labelling and formulation parameter, not a quality score.
- [Angel's share](angels-share.md) — The angel's share is the portion of a spirit lost through the cask wall during maturation; the Scotch whisky trade often cites about 2% a year, but the real rate depends on temperature, humidity, cask size and warehouse type, and is neither a precise constant nor a quality grade.
- [Appellation](appellation.md) — An appellation is a legal designation that binds a geographical area to production rules, such as French AOC/AOP, Italian DOC/DOCG, Spanish DO and EU PDO/PGI; it states origin and rules, not a quality ranking.
- [Aroma](aroma.md) — Aroma is the volatile smell perceived by the nose, layered by origin into varietal, fermentation and maturation notes; it is not the same as flavour, and aroma is not a synonym for bouquet.
- [Barrel finishing](barrel-finishing.md) — Barrel finishing moves a drink from its main maturation container into a different cask for a further period to add or adjust flavour; it is secondary maturation, not full-term use of that cask, and not cask strength.
- [Blend](blend.md) — A blend combines different varieties, vintages, sites or base wines into a final product to balance structure, stabilise style or meet regulations; it is a normal craft, not a synonym for low quality or adulteration.
- [Body](body.md) — Body is the sensory impression of a drink's weight and fullness in the mouth, often described as light, medium or full; alcohol, glycerol, residual sugar, tannin, acidity and temperature all shape it.
- [Brix](brix.md) — Degrees Brix (°Brix) express the mass fraction of sucrose in a solution at 20 °C, where 1 °Brix is 1 g of sucrose per 100 g of solution; they measure sugar and soluble solids, not alcohol or sweetness.
- [Cask strength](cask-strength.md) — Cask strength means whisky bottled with little or no added water, retaining its natural strength from the cask, commonly around 50–65% vol; it is a bottling choice, not single cask or a quality grade.
- [Chill filtration](chill-filtering.md) — Cooling whisky so long-chain fatty-acid esters and proteins precipitate before filtration; it prevents cold haze, is an optional process, not a legal grade, and does not strip all flavour.
- [Clos](clos.md) — A clos is a vineyard plot enclosed by a wall, fence or natural boundary, common in Burgundy, the Loire and Alsace; it identifies a bounded site, is not a legal classification, and does not imply single ownership.
- [Column still](column-still.md) — A column still is a continuous distillation device that uses multiple plates or packing to create repeated vapour-liquid exchange and produce high-purity alcohol; plate count, reflux ratio and feed position decide purity and flavour retention.
- [Cork taint](cork-taint.md) — Cork taint is caused mainly by TCA and related haloanisoles, showing as damp cardboard and mustiness that mask fruit; it is perceptible at a few nanograms per litre but is not a safety issue and is not the same as cork crumbs.
- [Craft beer](craft-beer.md) — A small-scale, independent and tradition-minded beer production and consumption movement that emerged in the United States in the late 20th century; an industry and market concept, not a legal grade or a guarantee of quality.
- [Cuvée](cuvee.md) — Cuvée can mean the earliest, gentlest juice from sparkling-wine pressing, or a batch blended from several base wines; in most regions it is not a legal grade and often serves as a product name.
- [Decanting](decanting.md) — Decanting moves wine from its bottle into another vessel for two purposes — separating sediment and exposing the wine to air; old reds and young tannic wines have different goals, not every wine benefits, and timing depends on age and condition.
- [Disgorgement](disgorgement.md) — Disgorgement removes the yeast sediment from the neck of a traditional-method sparkling wine after riddling, by hand or with a frozen neck; dosage usually follows before the final closure.
- [Dosage](dosage.md) — Dosage is the topping liquid added after disgorging traditional-method sparkling wine, usually a blend of base wine and sugar; it replaces the volume lost at disgorging and sets the finished sweetness class, from brut nature to doux under EU rules.
- [Dry](dry.md) — Dry is a residual-sugar category on wine labels, usually no more than 4 g/L under EU rules and up to 9 g/L when acidity is high; it says nothing about fruit intensity or quality.
- [Fermentation pit](fermentation-pit.md) — A solid-state fermentation vessel for baijiu, usually built from yellow mud, stone, brick, concrete or wood and often rectangular. Pit type and pit-mud condition shape the microbial environment.
- [Filtration](filtration.md) — Filtration passes wine through a medium that physically retains particles and microorganisms; depth and surface filtration differ in structure, and filtration affects clarity, stability and flavour but is not the same as fining.
- [Fining](fining.md) — Fining adds bentonite, egg white, gelatin or synthetic polymers to wine so that they adsorb suspended or dissolved targets and settle out, removing protein, tannin, colour or off-flavours; it is a clarification and stabilisation step, not filtration, and raises vegan and allergen questions.
- [Finish](finish.md) — Finish is the taste, aroma and mouthfeel that persist after a drink is swallowed or spat out; length, layers and cleanliness determine its quality, and a long finish is not a quality guarantee.
- [Flor](flor.md) — Flor is the yeast film that forms on the surface of Sherry during biological ageing, shielding the wine from oxygen and metabolising alcohol and glycerol; Fino and Manzanilla depend on flor, while Oloroso follows an oxidative route.
- [Heads, hearts and tails](heads-hearts-tails.md) — A distillation run is split by volatility into heads, hearts and tails; hearts are the balanced middle cut and the body of the spirit, while heads and tails carry more impurities and are usually recycled, with cut points shaping style.
- [Hongqu](hongqu.md) — A purple-red qu made by culturing Monascus on rice; it is a saccharifying starter for huangjiu, a colouring material and a base for red-qu wine and foods, with citrinin risk requiring process control.
- [IBU (International Bitterness Units)](ibu.md) — IBU is the unit for beer bitterness, with 1 IBU roughly equal to 1 milligram of iso-alpha acids per litre; it measures hop-derived bitterness, not the bitterness felt on the palate, and not hop aroma.
- [Late harvest](late-harvest.md) — Late harvest is the practice of leaving grapes on the vine beyond normal ripeness so that water loss concentrates sugar and flavour; it can make sweet wine, but it is not the same as noble rot and not always sweet.
- [Lees](lees.md) — Lees are the solids, mostly spent yeast plus pulp residue and tartrates, that settle after fermentation; keeping fine lees and stirring them changes texture, aroma and stability.
- [Maceration](maceration.md) — Maceration is the contact of juice with skins, pulp, seeds or stems to extract colour, tannin and aroma; time, temperature and alcohol level decide how much, and in which direction, extraction happens.
- [Malolactic fermentation](malolactic-fermentation.md) — A secondary fermentation in which lactic acid bacteria convert malic acid into lactic acid and carbon dioxide, softening acidity; it differs from alcoholic fermentation and is not needed in every wine.
- [Mash](mash.md) — A mash is the mixture of milled malt and hot water in a mash tun, where enzymes convert starch into fermentable sugar. It is the precursor of wort, not wort itself, and its water-to-grist ratio, temperature and pH shape the sugar profile and the extract yield.
- [Mousse](mousse.md) — Mousse is the formation, bubble size and persistence of the foam in a glass of sparkling wine; bottle pressure is typically about 5–6 bar, and temperature, glassware, wine composition and lees ageing together shape the bubbles, which are not a direct quality score.
- [Must](must.md) — Must is the juice, pulp, skins and solids obtained from crushed or pressed grapes and the starting point of wine fermentation; its sugar, acid, solid content and clarity shape fermentation and style.
- [Noble rot](noble-rot.md) — Noble rot is infection of ripe grapes by Botrytis cinerea under humid nights and dry, sunny days, which shrinks the fruit and concentrates sugar, acid and flavour; it makes Sauternes and Tokaji-style sweet wines but differs from grey rot in persistently wet conditions.
- [Original gravity](original-gravity.md) — Original gravity is the concentration of soluble solids, mainly sugars and other extract, in wort before fermentation, usually expressed in °P (degrees Plato); it reflects raw-material extract and is not alcohol content.
- [Oxidation](oxidation.md) — Oxidation is the reaction of compounds in wine with oxygen, altering aroma, colour and tannin structure; a small controlled amount builds styles such as Oloroso, Madeira and vin jaune, while excess brings loss of fruit, browning and acetaldehyde.
- [Peat](peat.md) — Peat is partially decomposed plant matter formed in oxygen-poor bogs, and its smoke deposits phenols on kilned malt; peatiness is an optional whisky style, not a required feature of all Scotch whisky.
- [Peat phenol value (PPM)](peat-phenol-ppm.md) — Usually the total phenol content of malt, where 1 ppm is 1 mg/kg; it describes a malt smoking specification, not the concentration in the bottle or a legal peat grade.
- [Polishing ratio](polishing-ratio.md) — The polishing ratio is the percentage of sake rice remaining after milling, so 50% means half has been removed and half remains; it is tied to daiginjo, ginjo and honjozo grades but is not a direct quality score.
- [Pomace](pomace.md) — Pomace is the solid residue of skins, pulp, seeds and stems left after pressing grapes, distinct from lees; it can be distilled into grappa or marc and used to recover tartaric acid, but must be handled quickly to avoid acetification.
- [Pot still](pot-still.md) — A pot still is a batch-distillation copper vessel made of a heated pot, a neck and a condenser; pot shape, heating method and cut points decide the style of the spirit, trading efficiency for flavour retention against continuous distillation.
- [Standard strength and proof](proof-and-abv.md) — Standard strength is usually the volume fraction at 20°C; US proof doubles ABV at 60°F, so 100 proof is 50% ABV, while the old British system differs.
- [Reduction](reduction.md) — Reduction covers both reductive winemaking that keeps oxygen out and the hydrogen sulfide, thiols and disulfides formed in oxygen-poor conditions; the first is a style choice, the second a fault only at certain levels.
- [Residual sugar](residual-sugar.md) — Residual sugar is the sugar left after fermentation, usually measured in g/L; it sets sweetness labels, while perceived sweetness also depends on acidity, alcohol, temperature and bubbles.
- [Riddling](riddling.md) — Riddling (French remuage) gathers the yeast sediment of traditional-method sparkling wine at the bottle neck; hand riddling takes about 26–30 days, while a gyropalette can finish in a few days, with different trade-offs in consistency, cost and bottle-by-bottle judgement.
- [Semi-dry](semi-dry.md) — Semi-dry sits between dry and semi-sweet or sweet; the exact residual sugar varies by category and regulation, and sweetness cannot be judged by taste alone.
- [Single malt](single-malt.md) — Single malt means whisky from one distillery, made from 100% malted barley and distilled in pot stills; it differs from single grain and blended malt in raw material and scope, and while Scotland defines it in law, other markets mostly use the term as trade language.
- [Single varietal](single-varietal.md) — Single varietal describes a wine made mainly from one grape variety; whether the variety may appear on the label depends on national rules, with the EU and Australia usually requiring 85% and the United States 75%, so it is not a synonym for 100% of that variety.
- [Solera](solera.md) — A solera is a stacked system of fractional blending and ageing in which the oldest wine sits at the bottom and younger wine replenishes each level above; the finished wine has no single vintage and keeps a stable style, and it is not "padding with cheap wine".
- [Sweet](sweet.md) — Sweet is the highest residual-sugar category on wine labels, at 45 g/L or more for still wine under EU rules; sweetness can come from late harvest, noble rot, ice wine, fortification or dosage, and balance depends on acidity and alcohol.
- [Tannin](tannin.md) — Tannins are a group of polyphenolic compounds that bind proteins and create astringency; in wine they come mainly from skins, seeds, stems and oak.
- [Terroir](terroir.md) — Terroir is the combined effect of a place's natural conditions and long-standing human practice on the character of its produce; it is not a single soil measurement.
- [Vintage](vintage.md) — Vintage is the year the grapes were harvested and reflects that year's weather and ripening conditions; it is not age or bottling date, and non-vintage (NV) is not a sign of low quality.
- [Whole bunch](whole-bunch.md) — Whole-bunch fermentation puts intact grape clusters, stems included, into the fermenter; stems bring structure, spice and freshness but also raise pH and can add herbal notes, so the proportion depends on variety, ripeness and vintage.
- [Wort](wort.md) — Wort is the sugar-rich liquid separated from spent grain after mashing. It is the starting point of beer fermentation, not beer itself, and its composition depends on the grain, the mash schedule, the lautering and whether it is boiled with hops.
