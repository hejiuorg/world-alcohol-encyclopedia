# Langjiu

Langjiu's brand recognition comes from a single geographic sentence: the left bank of the Chishui River, at Erlang. It ties origin, aroma type and estate experience together, then divides the range into clear steps so that the words "Qinghua", "Honghua" and "Langpai" do much of the positioning work. The brand page explains how to read that product language, not who owns the group or how many pits it runs.

## Brand identity

Langjiu is a sauce-aroma [baijiu](ref:baijiu), and its trademark and product system are held by [Sichuan Langjiu Group Co., Ltd.](ref:producer). In speech the brand, the group and "Langjiu joint-stock company" are often mixed up, but the brand carries only market identity, not equity or production responsibility.

Recognising Langjiu needs three things: the trademark, the series name and the strength. One series can include several strengths, volumes and packs with clearly different prices. Bottle shape or colour alone easily confuses products from different tiers.

## The origin narrative

Erlang lies in Gulin county, Sichuan, with the Chishui River running past the town; the left bank is Langjiu's origin statement. The valley is humid and hot in summer, and its terrain and microbial environment suit the open qu-making and long fermentation of sauce-aroma baijiu.

The key word is "left bank". It separates Langjiu from Maotai town across the water, borrowing the wider recognition of the Chishui sauce-aroma belt while stressing an independent identity. Origin is a brand asset, not a quality guarantee; firms and workshops within one region still differ markedly.

## Aroma type and craft language

The basic sauce-aroma path is sorghum as raw material, qu making at the Dragon Boat Festival, grain addition at the Double Ninth Festival, high-temperature stacking, multi-round fermentation and [distillation](ref:distillation), followed by graded storage and blending. Langjiu's public description stresses "four highs and two longs" and multi-round spirit recovery.

That craft language means two things to consumers. First, it explains why sauce-aroma baijiu is costly and slow. Second, it offers a clue for judging authenticity: a product claiming sauce aroma at an extremely low price has usually compressed raw materials, rounds or storage.

## Qinghua Lang

Qinghua Lang is the brand's premium image product, carrying business banquets and high-end gifting. Its packaging centres on blue-and-white porcelain visuals, its price sits at the top of the brand ladder, and its communication stresses origin, storage and scarcity.

A premium product's value combines brand, liquid and occasion. Qinghua Lang carries a visible brand premium, so buyers should separate occasion value from drinking value: the first comes from face and gifting, the second from the wine. Mixing them leads to over- or under-rating a bottle.

## Hongyun Lang and Honghua Lang

Hongyun Lang and Honghua Lang cover the upper-mid band and are the mainstay for banquets and festivals. The Honghua series uses red packaging and the character "hong" to reinforce festive association; Honghua Lang 15 and Honghua Lang 10 use numbers to mark tiers, but the numbers are part of the product name, not the age in the bottle.

This band is fiercely competitive, with consumers sensitive to price, packaging and brand image. Hongyun Lang and Honghua Lang differ not only in price but in liquid design and channel position; buyers should compare individual bottles rather than classify by colour.

## Langpai Lang and the mass line

Langpai Lang carries the brand's base price and is the entry point for new drinkers. Its packaging is relatively plain and its price accessible, suiting everyday drinking and meals with friends.

A mass line widens the consumer base but can dilute a premium image. The brand separates price levels through series names and packaging, and consumers should confirm identity through producer, strength and standard rather than the word "Langjiu" alone.

## Jianxiang products

Beyond the sauce-aroma main line, Langjiu offers jianxiang products including Langpai Tequ, Shunpin Lang and Xiaolangjiu. Jianxiang combines characteristics of strong and sauce aroma, tastes softer, spans a wider price range and covers banquets, restaurants and younger occasions.

Jianxiang and sauce aroma are a difference of style and occasion, not of rank. Treating a jianxiang product as an inferior sauce-aroma baijiu is a common category error. Buyers should first decide the aroma type they want, then compare products within it.

## Price bands

Langjiu runs from small bottles in the tens of yuan to premium sauce-aroma products above a thousand yuan. Qinghua Lang holds the top, Hongyun Lang and Honghua Lang cover the upper-mid band, Langpai Lang holds the base, and jianxiang products fill the lower-mid and younger segments.

For Langjiu, the wider the ladder, the harder brand management becomes. Premium products need a scarcity story, mass products need distribution and value for money, and the marketing languages do not match. Consumers should read price as a positioning signal, not a quality grade.

## Packaging and visual identity

Langjiu's packaging divides clearly by colour: Qinghua Lang uses blue-and-white porcelain patterns, Honghua Lang uses red, Langpai Lang is plainer, and jianxiang products are lighter. Colour helps consumers locate a product quickly, but is also easy for counterfeiters to imitate.

Wording such as "estate", "cave storage" and "vintage" belongs to brand narrative, not legal grades. Judging a product means checking producer, strength, net content and standard, which are harder to fake than graphics.

## Advertising and channels

Langjiu's advertising has long centred on "the left bank of the Chishui River", the estate and sauce aroma, while channels cover distributors, specialist stores, supermarkets, restaurants and e-commerce. Premium products rely on bulk purchase and business channels; mass products rely on distribution and shelf display.

Advertising shapes perception; channels determine availability. Prices can differ widely between channels, and cheap channels warrant caution about cross-region dumping and counterfeits. Official flagship stores and authorised specialist outlets are safer places to verify a product.

## The estate and experience marketing

Langjiu Estate sits on the left bank of the Chishui River at Erlang and includes brewing, clay-jar storage and cave storage at Tianbao Cave and Dibao Cave. The estate turns production facilities into an experience space where visitors can see the "grow, raise, store" sequence.

Experience marketing strengthens credibility but can turn a visit impression into a quality conclusion. The estate displays origin and storage conditions; it does not mean every bottle has aged for the same time. After a visit, the label and product notes still decide.

## Consumer perception and misconceptions

First, conflating the brand with the group. The brand is a market mark; the group is a legal entity.
Second, treating the "jujiang" record as the founding year. A local-produce record and a company's management history are different things.
Third, treating cave storage as a guarantee of age. Cave storage is a method; age depends on the base-liquor composition.
Fourth, treating the numbers in Honghua Lang 10 or Honghua Lang 15 as the age in the bottle. The numbers are part of the product name.
Fifth, treating jianxiang as an inferior sauce aroma. They differ in style, not rank.
Sixth, treating price as a quality grade. Price is market positioning.

## Division of labour with the producer page

The brand page asks which bottle to buy, for what occasion and at what price; the [producer page](ref:producer) asks who owns the group, where it is registered and how restructuring and subsidiaries are arranged. Both say "Langjiu", but one faces shopping and the other corporate verification. Putting equity and capacity on the brand page, or flavour on the company page, makes readers look in the wrong place.
