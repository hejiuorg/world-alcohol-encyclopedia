# Xinomavro

Xinomavro (also spelled Xynomavro) is the most serious red grape of Greek Macedonia in the northern part of the country. Its name combines the Greek xino (acid) and mavro (black), meaning "sour black". High in acidity and phenols with firm tannin, it closes up when young and needs years in bottle to unfold red fruit, tomato, olive and dried herb layers; fine examples age for ten to thirty years, and the grape is often compared with Piedmont's Nebbiolo (ref:nebbiolo).

Two terroir lines should be read separately: the structured reds of the Naoussa hills and mountain slopes, with concentrated, hard tannin, and the cooler, cleaner expressions of the high Amyndeon plateau, which also extend into sparkling, rosé and blanc de noir styles. Rules, blends and ripening rhythm differ markedly across the PDOs, and the label name is only the entry point.

## Definition and naming

Xinomavro is a Greek native red Vitis vinifera variety. The original Greek name is Ξινόμαυro; English spellings Xinomavro and Xynomavro are both common. Historical local names include Mavro Naoussis, Niaoustiano, Mavro Goumenissas, Mavro Xino, Popolka and Xinomavro Kozanis; locals once called the Naoussa black grape Popolka. These names all refer to the same variety, though some folk names are borrowed by neighbouring regions.

The most common confusion is with Agiorgitiko of Nemea in the Peloponnese, a soft, open-fruited red that stands apart from Xinomavro's high acidity, firm tannins and long ageing direction. Xinomavro has medium, near-spherical berries with thin to medium skins in a deep blue-green; it buds and ripens late and needs sunny slopes and enough heat to finish ripening. Its wine is medium in colour, but a pale hue does not mean a light body. Under appellation rules, Naoussa and Amyndeon PDO wines must be 100% Xinomavro, while Goumenissa, Rapsani and others blend it with other varieties; the PDO name on the label can be used to check the variety proportion.

## Origin and spread

The variety is native to and mainly grown in the mountain districts of Greek Macedonia, extending south into central Greece and the edge of Thessaly, with small plantings in neighbouring North Macedonia. Archaeological records reach back thousands of years in Macedonia, but no definitive genetic conclusion exists on its parentage. It has survived for generations in the traditional mountain vineyards around Naoussa, Amyndeon and Goumenissa, including pre-phylloxera fragments.

By area, global plantings stood at 1,971 hectares in 2010, all of them in Greece; by 2013 this had grown to 2,239 hectares, with the first trial blocks appearing in Gansu, China. Outside Greece, commercial acreage remains small, and the core wines still come from the Macedonian PDO and PGI districts.

## Botany and cultivation

The vine is vigorous and fairly productive, suited to short pruning. Berries are medium and spherical, with a thin to medium inky-blue skin, soft flesh and colourless juice. It is relatively susceptible to downy and powdery mildew, and botrytis needs watching in damp years; stems break easily in wind, so site choice balances airflow and shelter. The variety prefers calcareous soils and is potassium-friendly; budburst and ripening are late, needing sunny slopes and enough heat to finish ripening.

The cultivation priorities are leaf-fruit balance and yield control: a dense canopy shades the fruit, lowers acidity and invites disease, while high yields give thin aroma and loose tannin. In the Naoussa appellation, yield for appellation wine may not exceed 10,000 kg per hectare; mountain slopes, old vines and low crops are the precondition for a concentrated structure. Altitude, soil and aspect shift acidity brightness, tannin texture and ripening rhythm, which is why the same variety reads differently in Naoussa and Amyndeon (ref:terroir).

## Style and palate

Typical Xinomavro reds are ruby with a moderate, often relatively pale colour rather than deep black. The nose carries violet, sour cherry, dark fruit, tomato, olive pulp and tapenade, with dried herb, mushroom and tobacco. On the palate acidity is high, tannin firm and the frame tight, with a long finish; young bottles are dry-tannined and restrained, and with age develop leather, dried fig, walnut, prune and a balsamic sweetness-acidity edge.

Winemaking routes diverge. The traditional route keeps more whole bunch and longer maceration for structure and ageing potential; the modern route picks earlier and controls extraction for a lighter, more open-knit fruit. Besides dry reds, the variety makes rosé, blanc de noir and sparkling wine, from dry through off-dry to sweet. Oak ranges from large casks to small barriques, with new-wood proportion setting the strength of vanilla and toast; the variety's acid and tannin are the frame, and process variables mainly adjust extraction and rhythm (ref:oak).

## Main regions and representative wines

Of the four PDOs linked to Xinomavro, Naoussa and Amyndeon use 100% Xinomavro; Goumenissa blends it with Negoska, and Rapsani with Krasato and Stavroto. Naoussa PDO was established in 1971, on the eastern foothills of Mount Vermio in Imathia, and its wines are structurally weighty. Its rules also tier ageing: the basic wine spends one year in French oak, the Reserve one year in cask plus two in bottle, and the Grande Reserve two years in cask plus at least two in bottle. Amyndeon lies on the Florina high plateau at about 620–710 m, a cooler, brighter-acid site and the source of sparkling rosé and blanc de noir. At PGI level, Velvendos, Siatista, Grevena, Pella, the Vertiskos slopes, Halkidiki, Epanomi and the Mount Athos area also use the variety.

Representative producers include Ktima Kir-Yianni, founded in 1997 by Yiannis Boutaris at Yianakohori on the Vermio foothills in Naoussa, with about 85 hectares split between Naoussa and Amyndeon. Its single-variety wines include Ramnista, Naoussa Cuvée Villages, Yianakohori Hills and the single-vineyard Diaporos; on the Amyndeon side, Kali Riza comes from 40–60 year old vines and Akakies is a Charmat-method sparkling rosé, with exports to about 54 countries. Alpha Estate was also founded in 1997, on the Amyndeon plateau, with about 220 hectares of estate vineyard. The Boutari family business began in Naoussa in 1879, and Vaeni Naoussa, with cooperative roots, is another local producer centred on Xinomavro.

## How to drink and pair

Structured Xinomavro is best at 16–18 °C; young, tannic vintages benefit from 30 minutes to an hour of decanting to soften the tannin and open the aroma. Do not serve it ice-cold, or the acidity and herbal notes tighten up. A medium-large bowl red glass gathers the tomato, olive and dried-herb complexity.

For food, it suits roast lamb, game stews, slow tomato-raised red meats and ragù, mushrooms, aged cheese and olive-led starters. Its acidity cuts fat, and the tannin meets savoury olives and preserved ingredients. Local dishes such as moussaka, tomato-based stews and roast lamb ribs work well; the rosé and sparkling versions go with seafood, white meat and cold cuts. Delicate seafood or sweet sauces clash with its tannin and acidity.

## Buying and common misreadings

The common analogy is "Greek Nebbiolo": both show pale colour, high acid, firm tannin and long ageing, but the aroma spectrum and terroirs differ, so the comparison is only an entry clue. A second mistake is reading pale colour as light body; Xinomavro's weight sits in acid and tannin structure, not heavy pigment. A third is treating all Xinomavro as one style: Naoussa is concentrated and hard, Amyndeon cleaner and higher-acid, while sparkling and rosé are different product types altogether.

On the label, separate PDO from PGI, single variety from blend, ageing tier and vintage. Vintage matters: cool vintages are brighter and tighter, warm vintages riper and higher in alcohol; quality PDO bottles have more than a decade of cellaring ahead, while plain young wines are for drinking within three to five years. Store bottles on their side, away from light and temperature swings; overseas it reaches market mainly through specialist importers, so check storage history when buying.
