# Arrival of a Guest: Du Fu Entertains at His Thatched Cottage

“Arrival of a Guest” is not about a feast but about a plain meal at which the host cannot offer good wine. Du Fu puts poverty and sincerity on the same table: the dishes are few, the wine is old, but the gate is opened for the guest, and a neighbour may be called in to finish the wine. The poem's virtue is that it neither hides the shabbiness nor lets it reduce the warmth.![Arrival of a Guest: Du Fu Entertains at His Thatched Cottage thematic illustration](/media/photos/theme-asian.jpg)

## Full text

> South and north of my house, spring water everywhere;
> only a flock of gulls comes day after day.
> The flower path has never been swept for a guest;
> the thatched gate opens today for you alone.
> The market is far, so the plates hold no second flavour;
> the family is poor, so the wine jar holds only old unfiltered wine.
> If you are willing to drink with the old man next door,
> I shall call him across the fence to finish the remaining cups.

In romanisation:

> She nan she bei jie chun shui,
> dan jian qun ou ri ri lai.
> Hua jing bu ceng yuan ke sao,
> peng men jin shi wei jun kai.
> Pan sun shi yuan wu jian wei,
> zun jiu jia pin zhi jiu pei.
> Ken yu lin weng xiang dui yin,
> ge li hu qu jin yu bei.

The poem has eight lines in the seven-character regulated form. Under the title stands the poet's own note: “Delighted by a visit from Magistrate Cui.”

## Line-by-line notes

**She**: the cottage, the thatched house by the Huanhua Stream in Chengdu. **Jie chun shui**: all spring water, the house surrounded north and south by the stream. **Dan jian**: only see. **Qun ou**: a flock of gulls. That only gulls come every day is a way of saying that people rarely do.

**Hua jing**: a path through flowers and trees. **Yuan**: because of. **Bu ceng yuan ke sao**: has never been swept for a guest. **Peng men**: a gate of tangled grass, a humble dwelling. **Jin shi**: only today. **Wei jun kai**: opens for you. The contrast between “never” and “only today” sets the usual solitude against this particular visit.

**Pan sun**: the food in the dish, also written “pan can”. **Shi yuan**: the market is far away. **Wu jian wei**: no second flavour, meaning no more than one dish. **Zun**: the wine vessel. **Jia pin**: the household is poor. **Jiu pei**: old wine that has not been filtered clear, as against newly made wine.

**Ken**: to be willing, to consent. **Lin weng**: the old man next door. **Xiang dui yin**: to drink face to face. **Ge li**: across the fence. **Hu qu**: to call him over. **Jin yu bei**: to finish the remaining cups.

## The wine image

“The wine jar holds only old unfiltered wine” is the turning point. The poet does not say “the wine is poor, please forgive me” in polite formulae; he states the fact. Because he is direct, the reader believes the hospitality is real and the feeling is real.

Old unfiltered wine is not spoiled wine, only wine that has stood for a time and has not been cleared, the everyday drink of ordinary households. Had the poet written “fine wine” or “precious vintage”, the meal would become ordinary social exchange; with “old unfiltered wine” the rank of the wine falls and the density of human feeling rises. Wine is not an object for display but an expression of “what I have, I give you”.

The closing couplet extends wine's work to the neighbourhood. “Finish the remaining cups” is not opening another jar but drinking what is there; calling the old neighbour across the fence widens the scene from host and guest to the whole hamlet. Wine becomes the medium that connects people: it is not abundant, yet it is enough to carry a plain meal through to its end.

## Background

Du Fu (712–770), courtesy name Zimei, was born in Gong County, Henan, to a family registered in Xiangyang. After the An Lushan rebellion he wandered as a refugee, entered Sichuan in 759, and in the spring of 760 built a thatched cottage by the Huanhua Stream in Chengdu, where life became somewhat more settled. This poem was probably written in the spring of 761, when the cottage had taken shape.

The “Magistrate Cui” of the headnote is identified only by surname: ming fu was the Tang honorific for a county magistrate, and his personal name is unknown. Old commentaries often guess that the Cui family was related to Du Fu's mother's kin, but there is no proof. What is certain is that the visitor was a local official of some standing, which is why the poet swept the path and opened the gate.

The cottage stood in the countryside by the stream, far from the market, which is the literal background of “the market is far”. Du Fu depended on relatives and friends in Sichuan and was not well off, so “the family is poor” is no polite formula either.

## Textual variants

“Pan sun” is sometimes written “pan can”, two characters close in sound and meaning; the standard text usually reads “pan sun”. “Zun jiu” is sometimes written with a different character for the wine vessel; the first is the proper word, the second a loan character. Commentators agree that “ken” expresses willingness and is not a question about whether the guest is willing or not.

The headnote appears in slightly different positions in different editions, sometimes under the title and sometimes folded into a small note on the title. Citations should name the edition and avoid mixing commentary with the main text.

## Later influence

“Arrival of a Guest” is one of the best known of Du Fu's Chengdu poems, widely included in later anthologies; the eighteenth-century selection Three Hundred Tang Poems places it among the regulated octaves.

“The flower path has never been swept for a guest; the thatched gate opens today for you alone” became a classic couplet of welcome, and later gardens and houses used “flower path” and “thatched gate” as self-deprecating terms. The Du Fu Cottage in Chengdu today has a “flower path” named after the poem.

In wine culture the poem offers the reverse of “green ant new wine”: Bai Juyi writes of new wine just ready and a stove burning warm, the warmth of inviting a friend; Du Fu writes of old unfiltered wine and a neighbour called across the fence, the sincerity of a poor household. Together the two show how elastic wine could be in daily life.
