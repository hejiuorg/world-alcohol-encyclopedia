# Kuaijishan Chunzheng 5

Kuaijishan Chunzheng 5 is a five-year aged Shaoxing huangjiu from [Kuaijishan](ref:brand), commonly 500 mL in jar or bottle, brewed from glutinous rice, wheat qu and Jian Lake water, and usually about 15% ABV. The "five" in the name is the company's statement of ageing and cannot be equated directly with the legal age of the liquid in the bottle.

## Definition and position

The wine belongs to the Shaoxing type of [huangjiu](ref:yellow-wine) and is produced by [Kuaijishan Shaoxing Rice Wine Co., Ltd.](ref:producer). Within the brand structure it sits between everyday and banquet products: priced above plain or low-age products and below premium series such as Lanting.

Three levels need separating: huangjiu is the broad category, Shaoxing wine is the region and traditional process, and Chunzheng 5 is a specific product. The words "five-year" and "chunzheng" in the product name belong to the company's naming practice, not to a national grade, so the label and company explanation should be read together.

## Producer and brand

The wine is produced by [Kuaijishan Shaoxing Rice Wine Co., Ltd.](ref:producer) and carried by the [Kuaijishan](ref:brand) brand. The company's historical starting point is traced to the Yunji winery of 1743, and it listed on the Shanghai Stock Exchange in 2014.

Company, brand and product are three levels: the brand answers "what is it called and who is it for", the company answers "who holds the trademark and who carries legal responsibility", and the product answers the specific age statement, volume and strength. Treating company history as a quality guarantee for a single bottle is a common confusion of levels.

## Origin and raw materials

Shaoxing lies in northern Zhejiang south of the Qiantang River, with a humid and rainy subtropical monsoon climate. The Jian Lake water system is an important water source for Shaoxing wine, and water body and climate together shape fermentation and style. Traditional practice values winter brewing, when low temperatures help control fermentation speed and unwanted microbes.

The main material is [glutinous rice](ref:glutinous-rice), requiring full grains and high starch. [Wheat](ref:wheat) is made into qu, responsible for saccharification and part of the aroma formation, while wine starter is used to start fermentation; wheat qu and starter together form a "dual qu" route. [Water](ref:water) runs through qu making, fermentation and strength reduction, and its quality affects the cleanliness of the palate.

Ingredient ratios, steeping time and rice steaming all affect the final style. The ingredient order and standard on the label are more reliable than promotional wording.

## Production

The typical flow is: steep rice, steam, cool, mix with qu, ferment in vats, stir (kaipa), press, pasteurise, fill jars and age. The starch in glutinous rice first undergoes [saccharification](ref:saccharification) into fermentable sugar, which [fermentation](ref:fermentation) then turns into alcohol and flavour compounds.

The temperature and duration of [qu making](ref:qu-making) affect enzyme activity and microbial composition, and therefore whether fermentation runs well and whether the wine is clean. Stirring regulates temperature and oxygen and is a key Shaoxing step. After fermentation the liquid is pressed off, pasteurised and stored in ceramic jars.

Ceramic jars are slightly permeable, and the wine slowly oxidises and esterifies during storage to form aged aroma. Age statements relate to time in jar, but time in jar, time in bottle and blending together decide the finished style, so the word "five-year" alone does not establish quality.

## Specification and label

The common volume is 500 mL, distributed in both jars and bottles. Strength is usually around 15% ABV, and the sugar type may be dry, semi-dry, semi-sweet or sweet, to be confirmed on the bottle label. Shaoxing wine commonly follows GB/T 13662 and the geographical-indication standard GB/T 17946.

The producer, product name, strength, net volume, sugar level, ingredients, standard and origin on the label are the basics for identifying a product. Some products may use caramel colour, and whether it is added should follow the label. Buy through regular channels and check anti-counterfeit marks and batch details.

## Flavour and tasting

The aroma is led by rice, wheat-qu and aged notes, with a soft entry, acidity giving structure and sweetness varying by product type. Five-year aged wine is usually rounder and more evidently aged than low-age products, but this is not an absolute rule, and batch and storage conditions both matter.

Taste it warm, at room temperature or chilled. Check the aroma for burnt, sour or sharply alcoholic notes, then judge whether sweet, sour and umami are in balance and whether the finish is clean. Overheating damages the aroma, so direct boiling should be avoided.

## Drinking and pairing

Traditionally served warm, heated in a water bath until warm to the hand rather than hot. Warm huangjiu suits hairy crab, braised pork, soy-braised duck and drunken chicken; its sweetness and umami soften richness and complement river fish. Semi-dry types suit savoury dishes, while sweeter types suit the end of a meal.

Huangjiu is also used in cooking, but drinking wine and cooking wine are different products and should not be substituted. After opening, refrigerate and finish reasonably soon, since long exposure to air turns the wine sour.

## Buying and storage

Buy through regular channels and check anti-counterfeit marks, producer and batch. Jar, bottle and gift-box versions should be checked against the label, and packaging differences should not be read as differences in the liquid. For full cases, also check seals and storage conditions.

Unopened huangjiu should be stored upright, away from light, in a cool place free of strong odours. Home storage cannot replicate a producer's jar-ageing conditions, so the year of purchase should not be equated simply with an improvement in quality.

## Common misconceptions

First, treating "five-year" as the legal age of the liquid. It is the company's age statement and should be read with the product explanation.
Second, treating "chunzheng" as a grade name. It is product naming, not a national standard grade.
Third, that older huangjiu is always better. Ageing has a drinking window, and overlong storage can bring excessive oxidation.
Fourth, equating Shaoxing wine with all huangjiu. Shaoxing wine is a geographical-indication product and only one branch.
Fifth, using cooking wine as drinking wine. The recipe and quality goals differ.

For Kuaijishan Chunzheng 5, begin with the base and the stated strength. Then look at the sweetness or bitterness, and finally ask how it is served. The answers point to different choices, not to a single score.
