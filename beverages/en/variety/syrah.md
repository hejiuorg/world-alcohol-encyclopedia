# Syrah

Syrah is a deeply coloured red grape with generous tannin and typical notes of blackberry, black pepper, violet and smoke. It originated in the Rhône Valley of France, is widely planted in Australia under the name Shiraz, and has become one of Australia's signature red varieties. Syrah can be made as a varietal wine or blended with Grenache and Mourvèdre.![Syrah photograph](/media/photos/q2-variety-syrah-dc13087a.jpg)

Figure: Syrah. Photograph: Syrah Grapes on the Vine. Credit: U.S. Geological Survey, CC0, via Wikimedia Commons ([source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?curid=114429512)).

## Definition and aliases

Syrah is the common name, written Shiraz in Australia and elsewhere. It belongs to the European grape Vitis vinifera and is a key Rhône variety alongside Grenache and Mourvèdre. Syrah and Shiraz usually refer to the same variety, though the different names also reflect stylistic differences: French Syrah tends toward spice and structure, Australian Shiraz toward riper fruit.

Syrah is not "a darker Grenache". The two differ in skin thickness, tannin and aroma; conflating them ignores the division of labour in Rhône blends.

## Botanical characteristics

Syrah clusters are medium and tight, skins are relatively thick, and pigment and tannin are high. Budbreak is late and ripening is mid-season, suiting temperate to warm climates. Thick skins give it good resistance to botrytis, but sufficient ripeness is still needed to soften tannin.

Its aroma is linked to pepper compounds, which are more obvious in cool regions; Syrah from warm regions tends toward black fruit and chocolate. Clone, yield and winemaking change the strength of pepper and tannin structure.

## Main regions

Syrah originates in the Rhône Valley. Hermitage and Côte-Rôtie in the northern Rhône use Syrah alone or as the dominant variety, with a tight, peppery and mineral style; the southern Rhône often blends Syrah with Grenache and Mourvèdre. Australia's Barossa Valley, McLaren Vale and New South Wales also have large plantings.

Syrah from the northern Rhône in [France](ref:france) is an important sample of cool-climate Syrah, while Shiraz from warm Australian regions is riper with higher alcohol and often chocolate and liquorice notes. Climate, soil and winemaking explain a style better than the variety name.

## Typical flavours

Typical Syrah aromas include blackberry, blueberry, black pepper, violet, smoke and olive. Tannin is high, acidity medium and the body medium to full. While young it is black fruit and pepper; with age it may develop leather, truffle and earthy notes.

Pepper is a recognisable Syrah trait, but not every Syrah shows it clearly. Cool regions show it more, while warm regions may mask it with ripe fruit. Judge Syrah by whether tannin is firm but not coarse, whether acidity supports the body, and whether the aroma has layers.

## Representative wines

[Penfolds Grange](ref:penfolds) is a representative Australian Shiraz, showing the ripe fruit and barrel-aged style of a warm region. Hermitage and Côte-Rôtie in the northern Rhône show a tighter, peppery and mineral Syrah. Looking at both helps explain the span between warm-fruit and cool-structure styles.

## Viticulture

The key viticultural focus is ripeness and tannin management. Syrah needs enough heat to ripen skins and seeds, but excessive heat lowers acidity and makes fruit dull. Producers balance sugar, acidity and tannin through yield control, canopy management and harvest timing.

Syrah behaves differently on different soils. Granite often gives tighter structure and minerality, while alluvial or clay soils may give a fuller body. Clone choice also changes pepper notes and berry size.

## Common misconceptions

First, that Syrah and Shiraz are completely different grapes. They usually refer to the same variety, with style shaped by region.

Second, that Syrah must be peppery. Pepper depends on region, ripeness and winemaking.

Third, that Syrah can only be a varietal wine. The Rhône often blends it with Grenache and Mourvèdre.

Fourth, that deep colour means a richer wine. Colour, tannin, acidity and aromatic complexity are different things.

Fifth, that Syrah does not suit cool regions. Northern Rhône Syrah shows structure precisely in a cool climate.

Syrah is a dark-skinned, tannic variety with black pepper, blackberry and violet notes. It originated in the northern Rhône and is widely planted in Australia under the name Shiraz. Variety and origin are not interchangeable: a northern Rhône Syrah and an Australian Shiraz can share the same grape while differing in climate, winemaking and labelling tradition. Read both the variety and the place. Northern Rhône Syrah often shows pepper, olive and mineral notes, while Australian Shiraz more often delivers ripe black fruit and chocolate. The same variety can move in different directions under different climates and winemaking. Read the variety and the origin together, because both belong on the label.

**Short definition:** A deeply coloured red grape with generous tannin and notes of black pepper, blackberry and violet; it originated in the Rhône Valley and is widely planted in Australia under the name Shiraz. 

Syrah is best read by starting with where it is grown, moving to how it is labelled, and ending with whether the style is dry or sweet.

[Wine](ref:wine) and [France](ref:france) answer different questions about Syrah: variety character and regional performance. Read them as a pair, not as alternatives.

A frequent error is to treat Syrah as the name of a wine. Establish what it is, then how the variety is labelled. Neither answer settles the question alone.

Syrah is the only red grape of Hermitage and the base of Côte-Rôtie; in Australia it is called Shiraz and gives riper, denser wines, so origin explains more than the name on the label.
