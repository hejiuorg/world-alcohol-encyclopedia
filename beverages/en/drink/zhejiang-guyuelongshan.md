# Guyuelongshan Huadiao

Guyuelongshan is a huangjiu brand from Shaoxing, Zhejiang, best known for huadiao and jiafan Shaoxing wines. It brews with glutinous rice, wheat qu and Jian Lake water, then presses, pasteurises and ages the wine. Strength is usually 15% to 18% ABV.

## Definition and position

Guyuelongshan is a company and brand name, and its products belong to the Shaoxing type of [huangjiu](ref:yellow-wine). Shaoxing wine is a geographical-indication product with defined requirements for origin, raw materials and process; a brand is a specific product system built on that regional tradition.

Three levels need separating: huangjiu is the broad category, Shaoxing wine is the region and traditional process, and Guyuelongshan is the brand. They are not interchangeable. Huadiao, jiafan, shanniang and xiangxue are product names within Shaoxing wine that point to different proportions and sugar styles, not four independent drink types.

## Origin and environment

Shaoxing lies in northern Zhejiang south of the Qiantang River, with a subtropical monsoon climate: four distinct seasons, humid and rainy. The Jian Lake water system is an important water source for Shaoxing brewing, and water body and local climate together affect fermentation and style. Traditional practice values winter brewing, when low temperatures help control fermentation speed and unwanted microbes.

Regional conditions are more than a narrative. Water quality, temperature, airborne microbes and rice supply all land in concrete fermentation outcomes. The geographical indication fixes these conditions and process requirements, but within one region producers and batches still differ, so the label and product information remain the guide.

## Raw materials and qu

The main material is glutinous rice, requiring full grains and high starch. Qu is mainly wheat-made bricks, responsible for saccharification and part of the aroma formation. The temperature and duration of [qu making](ref:qu-making) affect enzyme activity and microbial composition, and therefore whether fermentation runs well and whether the wine is clean.

Shaoxing wine usually also uses wine starter (a small qu) to start fermentation, and the combination of wheat qu and starter forms a "dual qu" route. Ingredient ratios, steeping time and rice steaming all affect the final style. Jiafan, as the name suggests, adds a higher proportion of rice, giving a fuller body; the difference shows directly in sugar, acidity and texture.

## Production

The typical flow is: steep rice, steam, cool, mix with qu, ferment in vats, stir (kaipa), press, pasteurise, fill jars and age. Stirring is a key Shaoxing step: it regulates temperature and oxygen and shapes yeast activity and flavour development.

[Oak ageing](ref:oak-ageing) is common for wine, while huangjiu mainly ages in ceramic jars. Ceramic jars are slightly permeable, and the liquid slowly oxidises and esterifies during storage to form aged aroma; the storage environment, time and blending together decide the finished style, so the word "aged" alone does not establish quality.

## Flavour and tasting

Shaoxing huangjiu commonly shows rice, wheat-qu and aged notes, with textures from semi-dry to semi-sweet. Huadiao is a traditional name, not a fixed sweetness; jiafan is usually semi-dry, shanniang sweeter and xiangxue sweeter still, and the label is the reliable guide to sugar. Acidity is an important part of the structure, and a wine without it tastes flat.

Taste warm, at room temperature or chilled. Warming releases aroma and softens the alcohol, but overheating damages the flavour. Check the aroma for burnt, sour or sharply alcoholic notes, then judge whether sweet, sour and umami are in balance and whether the finish is clean.

## Product lines and labels

Guyuelongshan's range includes huadiao, jiafan and shanniang series in various volumes, age statements and packaging. The producer, product name, strength, sugar level, standard and origin on the label are the basics for identity. Some products may use caramel colour, and whether it is added should follow the label.

Terms such as "vintage" and "aged" must be read with the company standard. Time in jar and time in bottle are not the same thing, and labelling practice involves trade-offs. Consumers easily read the number on the package as wine age, when the company explanation and product standard should be checked instead of the packaging.

## Drinking and pairing

Traditionally served warm at about 35–45°C, it can also be served at room temperature or chilled. Warm huangjiu suits hairy crab, braised pork, soy-braised duck and drunken chicken; its sweetness and umami soften richness and complement river fish. Semi-dry jiafan suits savoury dishes, while sweeter types suit the end of a meal.

Heat it in a water bath rather than boiling it directly, which would drive off aroma. After opening, refrigerate and finish reasonably soon, since long exposure to air turns it sour. Huangjiu is also used in cooking, but cooking wine and drinking wine are different products and should not be substituted.

## Common misconceptions

First, treating huadiao as a fixed-sweetness type. It is a traditional name, and sweetness depends on the product.
Second, that older huangjiu is always better. Ageing has a drinking window, and overlong storage can bring excessive oxidation.
Third, equating Shaoxing wine with all huangjiu. Shaoxing wine is a geographical-indication product and only one branch.
Fourth, that darker colour means greater age. Colour can come from caramel or raw materials, not necessarily from age.
Fifth, using cooking wine as drinking wine. The recipe and quality goals differ.

Guyuelongshan Huadiao is best read by starting with the base and the stated strength, moving to the sweetness or bitterness, and ending with how it is served.

A drink style or a brand is not a safe shorthand for Guyuelongshan Huadiao. Check which style it belongs to, then who makes it and which vintage or batch; where they diverge, prefer the specific source.
