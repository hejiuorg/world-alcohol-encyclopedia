# Zuccardi

Zuccardi is one of Mendoza's most familiar names and a case study of how a family business becomes a multi-brand system. The company was founded in 1963, with its commercial address at Ruta Provincial 33, Km 7,5 in Maipú, where the Santa Julia range grew up. Yet the visits and restaurant that the official site currently highlights are at Paraje Altamira in the Uco Valley. Understanding this winery means separating the family base from the current reception centre; otherwise two places on different maps become one confused point.

## 1963: the start of a family company

The Zuccardi family company was founded in 1963. Official and industry profiles describe it as a family operation that expanded into several brands and several districts: Santa Julia serves everyday consumption, the Zuccardi range covers a broader price span, and Zuccardi Valle de Uco carries the fine-wine and visitor side. That structure differs from a single-brand estate: it does not tell one flagship story for every market.

1963 is not an ancient date, but it gave the family time for generational succession and capital accumulation. With that in mind, the tourism layout becomes clearer: the Uco flagship is not the only entrance. The Maipú base remains part of production and brand history, but its visitor function has to be confirmed separately.

## The Maipú base: Santa Julia and La Agrícola

Maipú lies east of Mendoza city, with vineyards at about 650–850 metres, deeper clay-rich soils that hold water better, earlier ripening and usually rounder, more direct wines. Zuccardi's commercial address is Ruta Provincial 33, Km 7,5, Fray Luis Beltrán, Maipú; the Santa Julia plant and part of its fruit supply are in this area. For the company this is the engine of everyday wine: large volume, clear price tiers and a stable range.

A production base is not the same as a public visit. The official tourism page currently describes the Uco Valley experience, and it does not state with equal clarity whether the Maipú base receives individual visitors or offers tastings. This page therefore records the address and district without presenting the Maipú base as somewhere you can simply turn up; that is why operating status is marked unknown.

## The Uco Valley flagship: Piedra Infinita

Zuccardi Valle de Uco sits at Paraje Altamira in the Uco Valley, where the winery building and Finca Piedra Infinita vineyard form the current public face. The official tourism page lists visits daily at 09:30, 11:00, 12:30 and 15:00, all requiring booking; the Piedra Infinita restaurant opened in March 2016, with a menu built around local ingredients and the estate's fine wines and seating for about 50. The estate was recognised in the World's Best Vineyards list from 2019 to 2022, and the restaurant entered the Michelin Guide in 2024.

These honours explain why visitors equate "Zuccardi" with Uco. But the Uco experience and the Maipú base are two geographies and two booking systems. Calculate the drive, altitude and meal time separately, or you will spend the day shuttling between Mendoza city and the Uco Valley. For Uco's three altitude bands and Malbec background, see [Mendoza](ref:mendoza) and [Malbec](ref:malbec).

## Visit times, restaurant and booking

The official tourism page gives daily visit times of 09:30, 11:00, 12:30 and 15:00, all requiring advance booking; lunch runs from 12:30 to 14:00, and dinner must be enquired about separately. Book through the official site, WhatsApp or email, and confirm the length, whether food or tasting is included, and cancellation rules. Prices and available slots change with season, so this page does not turn one quote into a permanent price.

Restaurant and visit are separate arrangements: diners should confirm whether a winery tour is included, and tasters should confirm whether they can eat. Uco is high and sunny, and fatigue after lunch is common; do not plan a long night drive back. If returning to Mendoza city the same day, allow at least 1.5–2 hours.

## Why this entry lists Maipú

This entry follows the administrative city of Maipú and uses the commercial address in the Wines of Argentina profile: Ruta Provincial 33, Km 7,5, Fray Luis Beltrán, Maipú. That address is tied to the Santa Julia production base and was central to the family's early expansion; it is not the same as the Uco visitor centre currently promoted on the official site. Separating them is more useful for readers: those planning a Uco visit will not drive to the wrong place, and those interested in family history will not miss Maipú.

To be clear, this page does not present the Maipú base as confirmed open. The official home page returned no usable content during verification, and the tourism page describes only Uco; operating status is therefore marked unknown and no verified_at is given. The address and district come from an industry profile, while visit rules come from the official tourism page; both are listed in the sources so readers can check them.

## What to confirm before visiting

First, whether you are going to the Maipú base or the Uco flagship. They are far apart and have different booking systems and experiences. Second, whether the Maipú base receives individual visitors. The official site does not make this clear, so confirm through official channels and do not simply drive there. Third, whether the Uco visit and restaurant can be booked on the same day. High season fills quickly, so book the visit before the meal. Fourth, transport and return. Uco is at Paraje Altamira, about 80–130 km south of Mendoza city, and a private car or designated driver is safer. Fifth, price and cancellation rules, using the official site on the day.

The address is Ruta Provincial 33, Km 7,5, Fray Luis Beltrán, Maipú, Mendoza, Argentina; coordinates should be taken from official channels, and navigation can use the address. To pair this stop with [Catena Zapata](ref:catena) in one day, confirm both bookings and the drive before deciding whether to add Uco. ## Common misconceptions

First, Zuccardi is not only Piedra Infinita in Uco. The Maipú base and the Uco flagship are separate places with different visitor functions. Second, a commercial address does not mean a visit is open. It is business and logistics information, not tourism information. Third, Santa Julia is not merely a "low-end" line to be ignored; it is the cash flow and everyday-market core of the family company. Fourth, do not turn the World's Best Vineyards years into a permanent first place; that recognition covers 2019 to 2022, not a lifetime title. Fifth, do not skip booking; every Uco programme requires advance reservation.

Start with the address. From there, consider the current opening status, then what the place is known for. That is how a practical judgment about Zuccardi is built.
