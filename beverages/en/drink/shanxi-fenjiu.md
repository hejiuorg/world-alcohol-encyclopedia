# Fenjiu

Fenjiu is produced in Xinghuacun, Fenyang, Shanxi, and is the leading light-aroma baijiu. It uses sorghum with a barley and pea qu and the "clean steam, second clear" process. A common strength is 53% ABV.![Fenjiu image](/media/photos/drink-shanxi-fenjiu-3fd2e1c8.jpg)

Figure: Fenjiu. Image: N509FZ, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons ([source page](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fen_Jiu_and_Chu_Yeh_Ching_Chiew_at_a_supermarket_in_Beijing_(20241109164541).jpg)).

## Definition and position

Fenjiu is a brand within [baijiu](ref:baijiu) and the model for light-aroma baijiu. The "light" in light aroma refers to the clean steaming and firing and to earthen-jar fermentation, and also to a clean, crisp flavour. It is not the same evaluation system as strong or sauce aroma, and the difference should not be reduced to a quality ranking.

The product is distilled, with strength stated as [alcohol by volume](ref:abv), commonly 53% ABV. Different series and strengths change the entry heat and aroma release, and the label is authoritative.

## Origin and environment

Xinghuacun lies in Fenyang, Shanxi, in a temperate continental monsoon climate with four distinct seasons, low rainfall and relatively dry air. Dry conditions help qu making and storage and reduce the risk of unwanted microbes in open fermentation. Local water and sorghum quality are important conditions for the light style.

Light-aroma baijiu ferments in earthen jars rather than mud pits. The jars are buried, separating the fermenting mash from the soil, so the environment is cleaner and there is less composite aroma from pit mud. That process choice directly produces the "clean" direction of light aroma and explains its fundamental difference from strong aroma.

Xinghuacun's brewing tradition spread with Shanxi merchant trade in the Ming and Qing periods, and light-aroma methods influenced baijiu production across the north. When discussing Fenjiu, look at both the heritage and the raw materials and process execution of a specific product rather than deciding from origin stories alone.

## Raw materials and qu

The main material is sorghum, with full grains and high starch content. Rice husk is used to adjust the looseness and oxygen level of the mash. Qu uses barley and peas and usually includes clean-slice qu, after-fire qu and red-heart qu, with different culturing temperatures and proportions.

[Qu making](ref:qu-making) decides saccharifying power and aroma precursors. Light aroma stresses clean qu aroma without mustiness or excessive toast. Barley supplies enzymes and nutrients, peas add cohesion and aroma, and their ratio affects qu texture and fermentation behaviour.

## Production

The typical light-aroma process is "clean steam, second clear": steam the raw material, add qu and ferment in jars, then distil; the spent mash is then re-inoculated with qu for a second fermentation and distillation. The whole flow stresses clean steaming, clean firing and clean mash, avoiding contact between mash and pit mud. Jar fermentation makes temperature and humidity easier to control and lowers microbial risk.

During [distillation](ref:distillation), spirit is cut by segment: heads and tails are separated and the middle segment becomes the main base. New spirit is stored by grade and then blended. Light-aroma spirit usually does not need the long maturation of sauce aroma, but too little storage leaves a new-spirit feel, while too much can cost freshness.

## Flavour and tasting

Light-aroma baijiu stresses cleanliness and a crisp finish. Ethyl acetate leads the aroma, often with grain and light fruit notes, a mellow sweet entry and a clean finish. It differs clearly from the pit aroma of strong aroma and the toasted notes of sauce aroma. A good light-aroma spirit should not have a muddled aroma or leave obvious discomfort after drinking.

Taste in a small cup: smell the resting aroma and judge whether it is clean, with grain and light fruit notes; then sip and follow sweetness, body and finish. Light aroma is less layered than sauce aroma, so the evaluation focuses on purity, harmony and aftertaste rather than aroma intensity.

## Product lines and labels

Fenjiu has lines such as Qinghua Fen and Bofen at different price points and body designs. The producer, aroma type, strength, standard and volume on the label are the basics for identity. Counterfeits and similar names exist, so buy through official channels and check anti-counterfeit marks.

Terms such as "vintage" and "aged" must be read with company standards and labels. Light-aroma baijiu is not better the older it gets: long storage can cost the fresh aroma. Home storage should be away from light and heat and protected from odours.

## Drinking and pairing

Neat in a small cup is enough; some drinkers warm it gently, but the temperature should not be high. Do not judge light aroma by the standards of strong or sauce aroma, because style difference is not quality difference. At the table it suits light, savoury northern dishes such as noodles, roast chicken, cold dishes and instant-boiled mutton; heavy chilli and thick sauce cover the light aroma.

Fenjiu can be used in mixed drinks, but 53% ABV changes the balance. Ice lowers aroma release and suits a personal preference for crispness, but it is not traditional. Finish an opened bottle reasonably soon and avoid long exposure to air and light.

## Common misconceptions

First, that light aroma "has no flavour". Its strength is cleanliness and harmony, not aroma intensity.
Second, mixing Fenjiu with every Shanxi baijiu. Brand and origin are not the same concept.
Third, that all baijiu improves with age. Light aroma is more sensitive to storage time.
Fourth, that earthen-jar fermentation is a simplified process. It demands more of temperature, hygiene and handling.
Fifth, judging authenticity by price or packaging. Anti-counterfeit checks, channels and labels are reliable.
