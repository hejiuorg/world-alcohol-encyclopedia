# Mianzhu

Mianzhu is a county-level city administered by Deyang in Sichuan. It sits at the northwestern edge of the Sichuan Basin, where the Longmen Mountains meet the plain. To the southeast lies Deyang's Jingyang District; to the northeast, Anzhou District of Mianyang; to the southwest, Shifang across a river; and to the northwest, the mountain route toward Mao County in Aba Prefecture. The official profile gives an area of 1,246.2 square kilometres, a registered population of 474,293, and 10 towns, 2 subdistricts and 153 villages or communities. Those figures describe a complete county-shaped place, not a single industrial site built around a distillery.

## Location and access

Mianzhu's position at the mountain front matters. The Longmen Mountains rise to the northwest; the Chengdu Plain opens to the southeast. The city and its distilleries occupy the transition zone between them. That setting gives Mianzhu both mountain coolness and plain-level road access, but it also means heavy summer rain, flash-flood risk and occasional landslides in the uplands. Understanding this geography is more useful than remembering a compass direction from Chengdu.

Roads and railways provide the main access. Mianzhu is connected to Deyang, Chengdu and Mianyang by the Chengdu–Mianyang expressway and several national and provincial roads; rail travellers can change at Deyang or at stations near Mianzhu. Schedules, station choices and last departures change, so check railway and bus information on the day of travel. Driving from the Chengdu Plain is straightforward, but holiday traffic and mountain-road parking near scenic areas can consume more time than expected.

The walkable part of Mianzhu is concentrated in the old city and around Jiannan Old Street. The distillery area, the New Year print village and the Hanwang earthquake site lie in different directions and are not walking distance from one another. For a half-day visit, choose the old city plus one liquor-culture site. Do not try to combine the mountain district, the print village and a distillery in a single afternoon.

## Industry and production area

Mianzhu is best known as the home of [Jiannanchun](ref:jiannanchun). The group is based in the city and produces multi-grain strong-aroma [baijiu](ref:baijiu) across a range of prices and alcohol strengths. It would be inaccurate to reduce Mianzhu to one company, but it is fair to say that Jiannanchun sets the rhythm of the local economy: qu production, fermentation, storage, bottling, packaging, logistics and sales all cluster around it.

The Jiannanchun old distillery site is the city's most substantial liquor-heritage address. What survives is the space and equipment of a traditional workshop, not a modern bottling hall open to casual visitors. Its value lies in keeping a “workshop behind the shop” model of production inside the urban fabric. When visiting this kind of site, look for the fermentation pits, the arrangement of the workshop and the relationship between liquor making and the town, rather than imagining an accessible factory floor.

Mianzhu belongs to the northern edge of the strong-aroma zone in [Sichuan](ref:sichuan). Compared with [Yibin](ref:yibin) and [Luzhou](ref:luzhou), it sits closer to the northern end of the Chengdu Plain and feels the Longmen Mountains more directly; its distilleries and urban space are also more concentrated. It should not be written as “another Yibin”. Both places make strong aroma, but their grain formulas, pit histories, product styles and visitor routes differ.

## Flavour and craft

The shared language of [strong-aroma baijiu](ref:strong) is mud-pit fermentation, continuous use of the spent grain, solid-state fermentation and fractional distillation. Mianzhu's multi-grain style usually emphasises the combined grain aroma of sorghum, rice, glutinous rice, wheat and maize, alongside the cellar aroma from the pits. Individual brands and product lines vary widely; one bottle cannot represent a whole production area.

In craft terms, the continuous use and maintenance of mud pits shape the microbial community. Steaming the grain, cooling it, adding qu, loading the pit, fermenting, distilling and storing are linked steps; a small deviation in temperature or moisture can change the cleanliness and balance of the finished spirit. Mianzhu is humid in summer and damp-cold in winter, so workshops manage ventilation, cooling and insulation by season. These practical details often explain a batch better than the label “old cellar” does.

For an ordinary drinker, compare Mianzhu strong aroma with other areas through three questions: is the aroma clean, is the entry balanced, and is there a rough edge at the finish? Pour a small amount into a cup, smell the empty cup first, then take a sip. That reveals more than a banquet-style toast. To understand another route, read the pages on [sauce-aroma](ref:sauce) and [light-aroma](ref:light) baijiu, but do not let an aroma category stand in for a specific product.

## Visiting and itineraries

The most direct urban liquor-culture stops are Jiannan Old Street and the Jiannanchun old distillery site. Jiannan Old Street is listed among the city's 4A scenic areas and is useful for reading the old street, wine shops and local food. The old distillery site helps separate the traditional workshop from the modern company. Whether the working distillery is open, how to book, and which areas may be photographed depend on company notices and site rules; visitors should not enter production areas on their own.

For a one-day visit, spend the morning in the old city and on Jiannan Old Street, eat Mianzhu rice noodles or home-style Sichuan food at midday, then choose either the China Mianzhu New Year Print Village or the Hanwang earthquake memorial in the afternoon. The print village is about woodblock New Year pictures and their craft, not a distillery. Hanwang records the trauma and reconstruction after the 2008 Wenchuan earthquake; it deserves quiet attention rather than being treated as an ordinary photo stop. The Jiulong–Lutang mountain area suits a half-day only in stable weather; when it rains, safety comes first.

From Chengdu, Mianzhu works well as a day trip or an overnight short break, not a “ten-minute side stop”. Linking Mianzhu with Deyang and Shifang makes a more comfortable route than trying to cover several mountain attractions in one day from Chengdu. Opening hours, reservations and transport connections change from year to year, so check local government and scenic-area notices before departure.

## Food and seasons

Mianzhu's food is in a northwestern Sichuan home-style register: rice noodles, pressed duck, cured meat and seasonal mountain produce are common. The official city profile also lists Zhaopo tea and red-fleshed kiwifruit as local products. With strong-aroma baijiu, salty, savoury and moderately oily dishes support the alcohol better than a very heavy chilli heat, which amplifies the burn and blunts the aroma. At an ordinary meal, a small amount is enough; there is no need to prove “understanding” through forced drinking.

Spring and autumn are the best seasons for walking the city and taking short mountain trips. Summer brings concentrated rain to the Longmen foothills, so watch for flash-flood warnings and temporary closures. Winter is damp and cold, with a noticeable temperature gap between the old town and the mountains. Workshop temperatures differ from outdoors; ask whether access to hot or humid areas is allowed and dress in layers.

## Common misconceptions

First, Mianzhu is not only “the place where Jiannanchun is made”. It also has New Year prints, mountain landscapes, earthquake memory and a distinct local food culture; the city deserves half a day or more.

Second, “imperial liquor of the Tang dynasty” is not a verifiable vintage or recipe. It is a long-running brand narrative and cannot replace checking a specific product, production area and process.

Third, the old distillery site is not a modern production hall. Its value is the historic workspace and workshop system; whether a modern bottling line is open is a separate question.

Fourth, the print village, the Hanwang site and the distillery are not one walking route. They lie in different directions, and transport and visiting time must be planned separately.
