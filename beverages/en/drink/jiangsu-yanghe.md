# Yanghe

Yanghe is produced in Suqian, Jiangsu, and belongs to the strong-aroma family. Its modern identity is built around a "soft and mellow" style, with the Haitian, Tianzhi and Mengzhi Blue series covering strengths from 38% to 52% ABV.

## Definition and position

Yanghe is a brand within [baijiu](ref:baijiu), and its main style belongs to [strong-aroma baijiu](ref:strong-aroma). "Soft and mellow" describes the mouthfeel, not a legal aroma category: the process base is still strong-aroma solid-state fermentation, adjusted through raw materials, fermentation and blending to make the entry gentler and the alcohol bite lower.

The product is distilled, with strength stated as [alcohol by volume](ref:abv), covering versions from 38% to 52% ABV. Strength affects not only the heat but also aroma release and body structure, so different strengths cannot be compared on one scale.

## Origin and environment

Suqian lies in northern Jiangsu in a transitional climate between warm temperate and subtropical, with four distinct seasons, relatively plentiful rainfall and higher humidity than northern regions. Humidity helps qu making and pit fermentation and demands stricter workshop hygiene and temperature control. Local water and grain supply are basic conditions for the style.

The Huai River basin around Yanghe has a long brewing tradition, and the pits and workshops of the Suqian area form the region's accumulated practice. A region is not a marketing label: it means conditions created by raw materials, climate and long brewing experience, but a specific product still depends on process execution and blending.

## Raw materials and qu

Yanghe uses a multi-grain recipe: sorghum provides the frame, rice and glutinous rice add sweetness, wheat helps qu making, and corn brings weight. Multi-grain fermentation makes the aroma more composite and places higher demands on proportion and cooking control. Different series adjust the proportions to create their own aromatic centre, and those changes show directly in aroma and sweetness.

Qu is made mainly from wheat into medium-to-high-temperature bricks, and [qu making](ref:qu-making) quality affects saccharifying power and aroma precursors. Strong-aroma pit aroma comes from pit microbes and continued mash recycling, while qu provides the fermentation starting point. When the two work well together, the body has weight without coarseness.

## Production

The typical flow is: prepare the raw materials, mix, cook, cool, add qu, ferment in the pit, remove, distil, grade and store, blend and bottle. Yanghe often summarises its process direction as low-temperature pit entry, low-temperature fermentation and low-temperature distillation; these are company descriptions, and the specific parameters are not public.

Low-temperature entry and fermentation help control unwanted microbes and bitter compounds, while low-temperature distillation helps retain soft aromas. New spirit is stored by grade and then blended. The aim is a gentle entry, clear sweetness and a clean finish while keeping the pit and grain aroma of strong aroma.

## Flavour and tasting

Yanghe's typical style is a soft entry, obvious sweetness and low alcohol bite, with pit and grain aroma and a clean finish. Its difference from southern Sichuan strong aroma lies in the mouthfeel direction: Yanghe stresses softness and drinkability, while southern Sichuan stresses aromatic intensity and body weight.

Taste in a small cup: first check whether the aroma is clean, then sip and feel the softness of the entry and the mid-palate opening. Lower-strength products release aroma more slowly, and higher-strength products have more heat; confirm the strength before judging balance. Water changes the structure, so add it drop by drop if you try.

## Product lines and labels

Yanghe has Haitian, Tianzhi and Mengzhi Blue series with different positioning, price and body design. The producer, aroma type, strength, standard and volume on the label are the basics for identity. Counterfeits and similar names exist, so buy through official channels and check anti-counterfeit marks.

For Yanghe, terms such as "vintage" and "aged" must be read with company standards and labels. Bottle ageing and jar ageing change at different rates, and home storage cannot easily reproduce distillery conditions, so purchase year is not the same as quality. Price movements are market behaviour, and this page gives no collecting or investment advice.

## Drinking and pairing

Choose the serving style by strength: lower-strength products can be served neat or over ice, while higher-strength products suit small sips. With light Huaiyang dishes, sweetness and umami can change how the alcohol is perceived; light dishes make the heat of high-strength spirit more obvious, while lower-strength versions taste softer.

Yanghe can be used in mixed drinks, but the higher-strength versions change the balance. Ice lowers aroma release and suits a personal preference, but it is not traditional. After opening, store away from light in a cool place and finish within a reasonable time.

## Common misconceptions

First, treating "soft and mellow" as a legal aroma type. It describes mouthfeel, and the base is still strong aroma.
Second, that low-strength spirit is simply diluted. Low-strength products need dedicated body design and blending.
Third, that softness means weak aroma. Softness and aromatic intensity are different dimensions and must be judged separately.
Fourth, taking a series name as a quality grade. Series is product positioning, not a standard classification.
Fifth, judging authenticity by packaging or price. Anti-counterfeit checks, channels and labels are reliable.

For Yanghe, begin with the base and the stated strength. Then look at the sweetness or bitterness, and finally ask how it is served. The answers point to different choices, not to a single score.
