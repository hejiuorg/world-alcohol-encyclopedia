# Customs

- [Birthday wine](birthday-wine.md) — Birthday wine is the honouring drink at birthday and milestone banquets, combining blessing, thanks and family gathering. This page covers timing, ritual sequence, documentary sources and modern change, with notes on urging drink and health.
- [Cold Food Festival and wine](cold-food-festival-wine.md) — The Cold Food Festival falls a day or two before Qingming and centres on a fire ban, cold food and tomb visits; wine plays the role of a libation and a family-meal drink rather than a fixed seasonal recipe. This page covers timing, origins, vessels, food, modern practice and health limits.
- [Double Ninth chrysanthemum wine](double-ninth-chrysanthemum-wine.md) — Chrysanthemum wine at Double Ninth goes with climbing heights, wearing zhuyu and honouring elders. This page covers the two documented methods, regional variation, the layered history, and the limits of health claims and home brewing.
- [Dragon Boat realgar wine](dragon-boat-realgar-wine.md) — Drinking realgar wine, sprinkling realgar water and painting a character on children’s foreheads are documented Han festival customs. Realgar contains arsenic and heating produces an even more toxic compound; this page separates origins and symbolism from the safety boundary and offers safer substitutes.
- [Full-moon feast](full-moon-feast.md) — The full-moon feast marks a baby's first month with announcement, thanks, naming and social exchange. This page links the Song “baby-washing gathering” to later banquets, compares regional customs, and sets out infant-safety and breastfeeding boundaries.
- [Harvest wine and grape-harvest customs](harvest-wine.md) — Harvest wine is the drink of thanksgiving, new-taste tasting and village meals after the autumn harvest; in European vineyard regions the same impulse continues as the grape harvest festival. This page compares Chinese and European forms of harvest drinking, their timing, vessels, food and modern tourist packaging.
- [Housewarming wine](housewarming-wine.md) — Housewarming wine is the meal and drink offered after a new home is finished or a family moves in; in the north it is often called “warming the pot” or “searing the pot bottom”, in the south “entering the household”. It confirms the joy of settling in, the neighbourhood relationship and social obligations, and raises real questions about pressure to drink, display and indoor pollution.
- [Mid-Autumn moon wine](mid-autumn-moon-wine.md) — The combination of moon gazing, moon worship and drinking on the 15th day of the eighth lunar month; wine is at once an offering, a reunion drink, a literary image and a market event, and documentary records must be kept apart from present-day practice.
- [New Year Drinking: Pepper-and-Cypress Wine and Tusu Wine](new-year-wine.md) — Drinking pepper-and-cypress wine and tusu wine on the first day of the first lunar month is a seasonal custom recorded at least as early as the Southern Dynasties; the Jingchu Suishi Ji also notes that “the order of drinking begins with the youngest”. Wine here combines plague protection, New Year blessing and family order, not merely festive cheer.
- [Sacrificial wine](sacrificial-wine.md) — Sacrificial wine is offered to ancestors, spirits and sages, with stress on purity, vessels and order. This page compares household, grave and public sacrifices, explains the three offerings and libation, and discusses modern disputes and the double meaning of the term.
- [Wedding Wine Customs: From Hejin to the Cross-Cup Drink](wedding-wine.md) — Hejin was a shared-drinking rite in the pre-Qin wedding ceremony. The Liji says “hejin er yin”, and the Yili records “the third ceremonial drink uses a jin”. Later practice developed into the cross-cup drink, but the ancient and modern rites, vessels and meanings are not identical.
- [Zhongyuan Festival and wine](zhongyuan-festival-wine.md) — The Zhongyuan Festival on the fifteenth day of the seventh lunar month is observed as a Taoist festival and as the Buddhist Ullambana; its core is ancestor remembrance, paper offerings and river lanterns. Wine serves mainly as a libation and a family-meal drink, turning grief into a gesture.
