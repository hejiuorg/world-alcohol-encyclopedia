# Visiting West Mountain Village: Lu You and a Bowl of Farmhouse Wine

The lines most often remembered from this poem are mountains after mountains, waters after waters, I thought there was no road; willows dark, flowers bright, another village appears. But its opening is in fact a meal. “Do not laugh at the farmhouse winter wine for being cloudy; in a good year there is enough chicken and pork to keep a guest” first asks the reader to sit down: the wine is cloudy, the food is ample, and the host does not apologise for the cloudy wine. Wine here is not a symbol of refinement but the plain truth of farmhouse hospitality.

## The poem and a country visit

Lu You (1125–1210) was a Southern Song poet, styled Wuguan and known as Fangweng, a native of Shanyin. He argued for resistance to the Jin throughout his life, his official career rose and fell, and in later years he lived long at Shanyin. Visiting West Mountain Village was probably written during the Qiandao years, when he was out of office and living at Shanyin; the exact year and the location of the village have been variously explained. What is certain is that it describes a real visit to the countryside, not a study-room imagining.

West Mountain Village was a village near Shanyin, in what is now the Shaoxing area; west is a direction, and there is no need to fix it as a place name still traceable today. The poem has eight lines in seven-character regulated verse, moving from arriving and eating to the customs of the spring sacrifice, and then to a promise before parting.

## Text

> Do not laugh at the farmhouse winter wine for being cloudy; in a good year there is enough chicken and pork to keep a guest.
> Mountains after mountains, waters after waters, I thought there was no road; willows dark, flowers bright, another village appears.
> Flutes and drums follow one another as the spring sacrifice draws near; caps and robes are plain, and the old manners remain.
> From now on, if I may have the leisure to go out by moonlight, I shall lean on my staff and knock at your door at any hour.

This is the received text. Winter wine is wine brewed in the twelfth lunar month, cloudy means unfiltered and carrying sediment; pork here stands for pork generally; the spring sacrifice is the day after the beginning of spring when the earth god is honoured and a good harvest prayed for; if I may means if it is permitted; at any hour means at no fixed time.

## The first couplet: winter wine and chicken and pork

Do not laugh at the farmhouse winter wine for being cloudy speaks first for the host. Winter wine is brewed at home, the craft is rough, and the liquid is cloudy; to a city dweller or an official it is neither clear nor elegant. The poet says do not laugh, and this do not is both advice to the reader and a shield against a condescending gaze.

“In a good year there is enough chicken and pork to keep a guest” gives the other half: the harvest is good, and there is plenty of chicken and pork for a guest. The wine is not fine, but the food is substantial; the word enough shows that the host is not stingy. Together the two lines give a complete account of a meal: the wine has a flaw, the sincerity does not.

## The second couplet: mountain roads and a village

“Mountains after mountains, waters after waters, I thought there was no road” writes the feeling of walking. Mountain after mountain, water after water, the road seems to end; the word thought matters, because it means he believed there was no road, not that there truly was none. “Willows dark, flowers bright, another village appears” writes the turn: the willows are deep and dark, the flowers bright, and a village suddenly appears.

These two lines are often extended into the idea that a difficult situation always has a way out, and that has become a proverb-like usage. But in the poem they are first a real walk in the country: the road turns, the view changes, a village appears. Treating them only as an inspirational maxim loses their sense of space and movement.

## The third couplet: the spring sacrifice

“Flutes and drums follow one another as the spring sacrifice draws near” writes sound. The spring sacrifice is the day after the beginning of spring when the earth god is honoured; the village has flutes, drums, song and dance, and near means the season is approaching and the atmosphere has already risen. Follow one another writes the poet following the sound, and also the movement of the crowd.

“Caps and robes are plain, and the old manners remain” writes clothing and custom. Plain caps and robes are not an exhibition of poverty but a sign that an old way of life survives; the old manners remain is praise and also observation: in the village, ritual has not yet been diluted by city display.

## The final couplet: a promise to visit by night

From now on, if I may have the leisure to go out by moonlight is a condition: if in future I am allowed leisure, I shall walk out in the moonlight. I shall lean on my staff and knock at your door at any hour is a promise: I shall come with my staff and knock at your door at any time of night. At any hour does not mean no time but no fixed time, whenever it suits.

This ending is very close to Meng Haoran's [Passing an Old Friend's Farm](ref:meng-haoran-guo-gu-ren-zhuang): both poets turn a single visit into a lasting relationship and close with a return. But Lu You adds moonlight and a staff, a more concrete picture that also shows the rhythm of his retired life.

## Wine in this poem

First, wine is the everyday drink of the farmhouse. The winter wine is cloudy, which shows that it has not been finely filtered; this is not a defect but its identity. In today's terms it is closer to home-brewed rice wine than to a bottled brand.

Second, wine is part of hospitality. The poem does not say how much was drunk or that anyone got drunk; wine and chicken and pork together make up the condition for keeping a guest. The host's sincerity does not depend on the wine being famous but on enough.

Third, wine is evidence that the poet lowers his status. The words do not laugh show that the poet knows the wine is not fine, yet still sits down to eat. He has not come to judge farm life; he has come as a guest.

Fourth, wine does not stand alone. The poem mentions wine directly only in winter wine, and its focus is on people and scenery; this resembles the raised cups and talk of farming in [Passing an Old Friend's Farm](ref:meng-haoran-guo-gu-ren-zhuang): wine is the medium of conversation and company, not the subject.

It should be said that Song farmhouse drink was mostly low-strength grain wine, and the craft lineage of [yellow wine](ref:yellow-wine) is a useful reference; cloudy winter wine shows that it was unfiltered and should not be imagined as today's clear sake or strong baijiu.

## Common misreadings

First, taking cloudy winter wine as poor-quality wine. Cloudy means unfiltered, not spoiled; on a farm it is normal.

Second, taking the two lines about mountains and waters only as an inspirational maxim. They are first of all scenery and walking.

Third, taking the spring sacrifice as ordinary spring. It is a specific seasonal rite honouring the earth god after the beginning of spring.

Fourth, taking the old manners remain as praise of poverty. It praises plain ritual, not hardship.

Fifth, taking at any hour as having no time. It means whenever, at no fixed time.
