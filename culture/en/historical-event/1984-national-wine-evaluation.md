# The 1984 fourth national wine evaluation: the thirteen famous wines

In 1984 the fourth national wine evaluation was held in Taiyuan, Shanxi, organised by the China Food Industry Association. It selected 26 national famous wines, 13 of them baijiu, later known in the market as the thirteen famous wines. The expansion reflected growing output and more categories, and turned "famous wine" from a scarce title into a group with a clear hierarchy.

## Background

After 1979 alcohol output continued to grow, local plants expanded, and many products imitating famous-wine packaging and names appeared. The industry needed a new evaluation to reconfirm the quality hierarchy and finer categories to accommodate new products. Judging already used coded blind tasting and grouped samples by aroma type, saccharifying agent and alcohol strength, so the procedure was more mature than in the early years.

A second factor was the rising market value of famous wines. In the early reform period famous wines remained an important item in planned allocation and special supply, and prices were controlled, but the title already brought sales and local prestige. Provinces screened samples before submission, and firms sent their best batches, so the evaluation was at once a technical contest and a contest for local honour.

## Course and timeline

Samples were classified as baijiu, yellow wine, wine, beer, fruit wine and aromatised wine, and baijiu was further grouped by aroma type, saccharifying agent and alcohol strength. Samples were coded, scored independently and ranked. A common account says the meeting selected 13 national famous wines from 148 samples; some sources treat 148 as the number of baijiu samples.

The meeting selected 26 national famous wines, 13 of them baijiu, and a number of national quality wines, commonly recorded as 64. The thirteen baijiu were Moutai, Fenjiu, Wuliangye, Yanghe Daqu, Jiannanchun, Gujing Gongjiu, Dongjiu, Xifeng, Luzhou Laojiao Tequ, Quanxing Daqu, Shuanggou Daqu, special Huanghelou and Langjiu. Compared with the eight famous wines of 1979, Xifeng and Quanxing returned, while Shuanggou, Huanghelou and Langjiu entered for the first time.

The timeline runs as follows: 1984, provinces submit samples; the same year, judging and results; 1985 to 1988, famous-wine output and prices rise; 1988, famous-wine prices liberalised; 1989, the fifth evaluation builds on this base to select seventeen famous wines.

## Key people and institutions

The China Food Industry Association hosted the meeting, while light-industry departments and provincial light-industry and commercial systems organised sample submission. Baijiu technologists such as Zhou Henggang had long taken part in designing the evaluation system and promoted examiner qualification, aroma grouping and coded samples. The panel was made up of assessors recommended by different regions and had to pass a sensory test during the meeting.

At company level, [Luzhou Laojiao](ref:luzhou) Tequ remained on the list. Wuliangye, Jiannanchun and Quanxing from Sichuan; Yanghe and Shuanggou from Jiangsu; Moutai and Dongjiu from Guizhou; Fenjiu from Shanxi; Xifeng from Shaanxi; Gujing Gongjiu from Anhui; Huanghelou from Hubei; and Langjiu from Sichuan together formed the geography of the thirteen. That spread shows that the evaluation had become a national structure crossing provinces and aroma types.

## Concrete changes for the industry

First, "thirteen famous wines" became a market term. Firms printed it on packaging and advertising, and consumers used it to judge grade. Second, the famous-wine hierarchy expanded, institutionalising the gap between provincial and national famous wines. Third, the evaluation spread [strong-aroma](ref:strong-aroma) techniques, as plants around the country studied Sichuan pit, continuous-grain and blending methods. Fourth, it set the baseline for the 1989 fifth meeting, and most of the seventeen had already been selected here. Fifth, it pushed firms to set up laboratories and tasting teams, and sensory assessment began to be combined with chemical indicators.

Negative effects appeared too: the title brought a price premium, some firms valued competition over everyday quality management, imitative packaging and naming increased, and local protection and quota allocation affected how representative the results were.

## Disputes and later assessments

First, the count: 26 famous wines and 64 quality wines is the common account, but some sources count only baijiu, so the numbers do not match; other sources count 20 by gold award, a difference that comes from award level and scope. Second, the list details: the composition of the thirteen baijiu circulates in several versions, and whether individual brands were included is disputed. Third, fairness: blind judging reduced brand bias, but firms chose their own samples, and there was no independent check that the sample matched the product on sale. Fourth, later influence is exaggerated. The title did bring a short-term premium, but long-term performance depended more on ownership reform, capacity and distribution.

## Common misreadings

One: "The thirteen famous wines were all the national famous wines of 1984." There were 26 in total; the thirteen were only the baijiu part. Two: "The thirteen were all new brands chosen in 1984." Most had appeared in earlier meetings; this meeting kept them and added others. Three: "Selection depended on packaging and connections." Samples were coded and sensory quality remained central, though the representativeness of samples did affect results. Four: "A famous-wine title equals good wine today." The title is a historical honour and cannot replace judgement about current products and firms. Five: "Strong aroma dominated completely." Strong aroma was the largest group, but sauce, light, Feng and Dong aromas all had representatives.
